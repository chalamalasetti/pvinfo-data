17   I.      INTRODUCTION
18
19   This guidance is intended to assist sponsors, clinical investigators, and institutional review boards
20   (IRBs) involved in clinical investigations of investigational drugs and biological products. This
21   guidance applies to clinical investigations conducted under 21 CFR part 312 (investigational new
22   drug application (IND) regulations) and describes how to complete the Statement of Investigator
23   (Form FDA 1572).
24
25
26   II.     BACKGROUND
27

29   (FAQs) are answered in the information sheet guidance for sponsors, clinical investigators, and
30   IRBs Frequently Asked Questions—Statement of Investigator (Form FDA 1572) (May 2010) 2 (the
31   Form FDA 1572 FAQ Guidance).
32
33   This guidance, when finalized, partially revises the Form FDA 1572 FAQ Guidance and answers
34   additional questions received by FDA. Questions not answered either in this guidance or in the
35   Form FDA 1572 FAQ Guidance may be submitted to druginfo@fda.hhs.gov.

51   III.   GENERAL QUESTIONS (10, 11, AND 13)
52
53   10. Must investigators who conduct studies outside of the United States sign a 1572?
54   [Revised 2021]
55
56   If a clinical study is conducted at a foreign site under an IND, all FDA IND regulations,
57   including the requirement to obtain a signed 1572 (21 CFR 312.53(c)(1)), must be met unless
58   the sponsor requests and is granted a waiver that provides for specific exceptions. In the case
59   where a foreign investigator cannot or will not sign Form FDA 1572 (e.g., because regional,
60   national, or local laws or regulations prohibit its signing), the sponsor may submit a request for
61   a waiver of the 1572 signature requirement under 21 CFR 312.10 (see section [IV] of this
62   guidance); alternatively, the site may operate as a non-IND site, in which case the study would
63   be conducted as a non-IND study.
64
65   If a clinical study is conducted outside of the United States and the study is not under an IND,
66   then the investigator need not sign a 1572. If the study data from a non-IND site is to be
67   submitted to support a marketing application (e.g., a new drug application (NDA)), the study at
68   the non-IND site must be conducted in compliance with 21 CFR 312.120. (Also, see question
69   14 in the Form FDA 1572 FAQ Guidance.)
70
71   11. If a foreign clinical study is being conducted under an IND, what are the investigator’s
72   responsibilities with respect to regional, national, or local laws and regulations?
73   [Revised 2021]
74
75   Investigators are responsible for complying with the applicable laws and regulations of the
76   country in which the study is being conducted, regardless of whether the study is being
77   conducted under an IND. We recommend that sponsors obtain signed, written statements from
78   investigators acknowledging their commitment to comply with regional, national, or local laws
79   and requirements. In addition, if a foreign clinical study is being conducted under an IND, the
80   investigator must sign Form FDA 1572 (investigator statement) (21 CFR 312.53(c)(1)) and
81   ensure that the study is conducted in accordance with the investigator statement and all other
82   applicable regulations under 21 CFR part 312 unless the sponsor has requested, and FDA has
83   granted, a waiver of the signature requirement. If a waiver is granted, the sponsor and

 87   13. If a sponsor chooses to conduct a foreign clinical study (or operate non-U.S. sites in a
 88   multinational study) under an IND and the investigators at these non-U.S. sites follow the
 89   recommendations in the ICH E6 Good Clinical Practice Consolidated Guidance, 3 would the
 90   non-U.S. investigators also be in compliance with FDA’s IND requirements under 21 CFR
 91   part 312?
 92   [Revised 2021]
 93
 94   Yes, with three exceptions.
 95
 96   The first exception is that the FDA requirements for IRBs under 21 CFR part 56 are slightly
 97   different than ICH E6 with respect to membership and function. To address this issue, as
 98   described in question 12, 4 the sponsor can request that FDA provide a specific waiver from the
 99   part 56 IRB requirements, allowing an IEC that complies with good clinical practice to substitute
100   for the IRB. 5
101
102   The second exception is that the requirements for informed consent under 21 CFR part 50 for
103   particular clinical trials (e.g., emergency research under 21 CFR 50.24, clinical investigations
104   involving pediatric subjects under subpart D) are more extensive than ICH E6 with respect to
105   IRB responsibilities. Our experience has not revealed that this difference has caused a conflict,
106   but in the event of one, we would be willing to discuss a resolution with the sponsor on a case-
107   by-case basis.
108
109   The third exception is that ICH E6 does not require investigators to sign a Form FDA 1572. To
110   address this issue, the sponsor can request a specific waiver of the signature requirement on
111   Form FDA 1572 pursuant to 21 CFR 312.10. (See section [IV] of this guidance.)
112
113   If the investigator or sponsor concludes that there are other conflicting requirements, the sponsor
114   may request a waiver from FDA from the specific requirement under 21 CFR 312.10.

117   IV.  SECTION #9–11: COCOMMITMENTS, DATE, AND SIGNATURE OF
118   INVESTIGATOR — WAIVER OF SIGNATURE ON FORM FDA 1572
119
120   39. A sponsor may encounter situations in which investigators in foreign countries cannot or
121   will not sign Form FDA 1572, yet the sponsor wishes to conduct the study at these sites under
122   an IND. What can a sponsor do?
123   [New 2021]
124
125   Under FDA regulations, sponsors may submit requests to FDA for a waiver of an IND
126   requirement (21 CFR 312.10). Such submissions may include request for a waiver of the
127   requirement that sponsors obtain an investigator signature on Form FDA 1572 (21 CFR 312.53
128   (c)(1)). Waiver of the signature on Form FDA 1572, when granted, enables the study at foreign
129   sites to be and/or remain under an IND even though the investigator at those foreign sites cannot
130   or will not sign Form FDA 1572, provided that the sponsor complies with the terms of the
131   waiver. (See below for further information regarding the waiver process and commitments.)
132
133   40. What should a sponsor submit to the IND when requesting a waiver of the 1572 signature
134   requirement?
135   [New 2021]
136
137      1. A clear statement that the sponsor is requesting a waiver of the signature requirement on
138         the 1572
139
140      2. The study title, protocol number, study sites, and investigator name(s) that would be
141         subject to the waiver (Note: If multiple sites are identified, the submission should
142         identify which investigator(s) is associated with each site.)
143
144      A waiver request is required to contain at least one of the following (21 CFR 312.10(a)):
145
146      3. The reason(s) each investigator cannot or will not sign Form FDA 1572
147
148      4. The sponsor’s proposed alternative course of action to adequately satisfy the purpose of a
149         signed Form FDA 1572
150
151      5. Other information justifying a waiver
152
153   For FDA to consider granting the waiver request, it is important that the request addresses all of
154   the items above.
155
156   41. What documentation should a sponsor submit to show that they have an acceptable
157   alternative course of action to satisfy the purpose of a signed Form FDA 1572? Can you
158   provide an example?
159   [New 2021]

161   There may be multiple ways for a sponsor to assure FDA that its alternative course of action
162   adequately satisfies the purpose of a signed Form FDA 1572. Although there may be other
163   approaches, FDA recommends using the example and suggested templates found in the
164   Appendix of this guidance, which will likely result in the most efficient review of the waiver
165   request.
166
167   An alternative course of action could take the form of the submission of the following three
168   items:
169
170      1. Declaration by the sponsor that the regional (in the case of the European Union) or
171         national regulatory authority is a member of the International Council for Harmonisation
172         of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH) and has
173         implemented the ICH E6 Guideline for Good Clinical Practice. In addition, a
174         determination by the sponsor that an investigator’s compliance with the ICH E6
175         Guideline for Good Clinical Practice will ensure that the investigator and sponsor comply
176         with their respective obligations under 21 CFR part 312, other than the signature
177         requirement on Form FDA 1572. (See the sponsor commitment example in the
178         appendix.)
179
180      2. Commitment by the sponsor to collect from each investigator for whom the signature
181         requirement was waived, a completed but unsigned Form FDA 1572 that includes all
182         information in sections 1 through 8 of Form FDA 1572 and a signed statement containing
183         commitments equivalent to the commitments specified in Section 9 of Form FDA 1572.
184         (Note: In place of Form FDA 1572, the sponsor may choose to use an appropriate
185         alternative template of its choice.) (See the sponsor commitment example in the
186         appendix.)
187
188      3. Any templates or forms the sponsor intends to use to collect information and/or secure
189         signed commitments from the applicable investigators. (See the investigator commitment
190         example in the appendix.)
191
192   We encourage sponsors considering other approaches to discuss these with the appropriate
193   review division.
194
195   42. How and when should a sponsor submit the request to waive the signature requirement?
196   [New 2021]
197
198   The sponsor should submit the waiver request along with the necessary documentation
199   supporting the waiver request in an amendment to the IND under which the study will be
200   conducted.
201
202   The sponsor should submit the request to waive the signature on Form FDA 1572 when it
203   determines that an investigator(s) cannot or will not sign Form FDA 1572.
204

205   If after a waiver request is granted, new investigators are added to this IND protocol who refuse
206   or cannot sign the Form FDA 1572 and the sponsor wants to request a waiver of the Form FDA
207   1572 signature requirement, they will need to submit a new waiver request applicable to these
208   new investigators (21 CFR 312.53(c)(1); 21 CFR 312.10)). When doing so, the sponsor should
209   reference the protocol number, the date of the original request, and the date of any previous
210   correspondence with FDA regarding the original waiver in a cover letter.
211
212   43. Does the sponsor need to wait for the waiver request to be granted before initiating the
213   study at that site?
214   [New 2021]
215
216   Yes. The Agency will inform the sponsor in writing whether the Agency is granting each
217   request to waive the 1572 signature requirement under 21 CFR 312.10(b), whether the request
218   requires more information, or whether the request is denied. Only after either receiving a
219   granted 1572 signature waiver or obtaining an appropriately signed Form FDA 1572 may a
220   sponsor initiate the study at that site.
221
222   The sponsor should not assume that no response or a delayed response from the Agency means
223   that the request for waiver has been granted.
224
225   If a waiver is granted, the sponsor should ensure that a copy of the waiver is maintained by the
226   sponsor and the investigator so that it is available for review upon request.
227
228   44. What does it mean to get a “1572 signature waiver”?
229   [New 2021]
230
231   The waiver does not waive the sponsor’s obligation to ensure that the investigator complies with
232   the equivalent commitments expressed in the waiver request. Likewise, the waiver does not
233   waive the sponsor’s obligation to comply with all other applicable regulations.
234
235   Instead, after the Agency grants the waiver, the sponsor can conduct the study, under the IND, at
236   sites at which investigators cannot or will not sign Form FDA 1572.
237
238   As explained in detail within this section, it is important that the sponsor establish an alternative
239   course of action, acceptable to FDA, that will ensure that the investigator complies with
240   commitments equivalent to those listed on Form FDA 1572.
241
242   45. Is the waiver of the 1572 signature requirement a distinctly different waiver than an IRB
243   requirement waiver?
244   [New 2021]
245
246   Yes, they are distinct waivers. Waiving the 1572 signature requirement does not waive the IRB
247   requirement, and a waiver of the IRB requirement under 21 CFR 56.105 does not waive the 1572

248   signature requirement. If a sponsor wishes to request a waiver of the IRB requirement, refer to
249   question 12. 6
250
251   If a request for waiver of the 1572 signature includes the use of an independent ethics committee
252   (IEC) instead of an IRB, then the sponsor should obtain IRB waiver approval first or submit both
253   waiver requests in one submission. FDA recommends that if a sponsor intends to request both a
254   waiver of the 1572 signature requirement and a waiver of the IRB requirement, the sponsor does
255   so in one submission. In this submission, the sponsor should:
256
257            1. Explicitly state, in the cover letter subject line, that it is requesting both waivers
258               (waiver of the Form FDA 1572 signature and waiver of the IRB requirement); and
259
260            2. Ensure that both the request for waiver of the 1572 signature requirement and the
261               request for waiver of the IRB requirement contain all information required to support
262               each waiver request. The information supporting these requests should be separated
263               and clearly marked.
264
265   46. Are there any special considerations regarding Form FDA 1572 signature waivers and
266   pediatric studies?
267   [New 2021]
268
269   Sponsors requesting waivers of the Form FDA 1572 signature requirement for IND studies that
270   include pediatric subjects at foreign sites should take into consideration that any study that
271   includes pediatric patients must demonstrate that the proposed study satisfies the requirements of
272   21 CFR part 50, subpart D. We recommend sponsors consult with FDA regarding any such
273   request for a waiver under 21 CFR 312.10, as needed.

280
281   Sponsor XYZ declares that all the study sites under IND XXXX for which a waiver of the 1572
282   signature requirement is requested will be conducted in a country where the national regulatory
283   authority is a member of the International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements
284   for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH). The country has implemented the ICH E6 Guideline
285   for Good Clinical Practice (ICH E6) in regional and/or national legislation, norms, regulations,
286   or competent authority administrative processes.
287
288   Sponsor XYZ will obtain a signed commitment to follow the recommendations in ICH E6 from
289   each investigator at the study sites listed, using the form or template accepted by the FDA when
290   granting the waiver request. Additionally, Sponsor XYZ will comply with the terms of the
291   waiver. Further, Sponsor XYZ will ensure that each investigator at the study sites listed
292   complies with the commitments contained in this waiver request.
