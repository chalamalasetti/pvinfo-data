14   I.       INTRODUCTION
15
16   This guidance provides recommendations for the design and conduct of studies to evaluate the in
17   vivo skin irritation and sensitization potential of a proposed transdermal or topical delivery
18   system (collectively referred to as TDS 2). The recommendations in this guidance relate to
19   studies submitted in support of an abbreviated new drug application (ANDA). 3 This guidance is
20   a second revision to the revised draft guidance Assessing the Irritation and Sensitization
21   Potential of Transdermal and Topical Delivery Systems for ANDAs (April 2023). This revision
22   provides the following updates to the April 2023 revision of the draft guidance:
23       (1) Clarifies recommendations for the design and conduct of studies to evaluate the in vivo
24           skin irritation (and sensitization, if applicable) potential of a proposed TDS.
25       (2) Clarifies when an in vivo study to assess the sensitization potential of a TDS product may
26           not be needed.
27
28   In this guidance, the letter T (representing Test) will refer to proposed generic products that are
29   the subject of an ANDA, and the letter R (representing Reference) will refer to the reference
30   listed drug (RLD) and/or reference standard (RS) product.

51   II.      BACKGROUND
52
53   The components and composition of a TDS formulation, including the nature of the drug
54   substance and/or the degree to which the TDS materials occlude the transmission of water vapor
55   from the skin, in conjunction with other factors such as the environmental humidity or the
56   condition of the skin, may have the potential to irritate the skin or lead to a sensitization
57   reaction. 7 Such reactions can be unpleasant to the patient and may affect patient compliance,
58   skin permeability, and/or adhesion of the TDS to the skin. The collective consequence of these
59   potential effects could create uncertainty about the resulting drug delivery profile and the rate
60   and extent of drug absorption from the TDS. Therefore, when appropriate, applicants should
61   perform a comparative assessment of the T and R TDS products using an appropriately designed
62   skin irritation study or combined skin irritation and sensitization study with human subjects to

 68   III.    GENERAL CONSIDERATIONS
 69
 70   Skin irritation and sensitization studies are designed to compare the similarity between the T and
 71   R TDS products for the potential to cause irritation and sensitization reactions, respectively. A
 72   TDS may elicit these reactions in only some of the patients using the product, but even if the
 73   frequency of this occurrence were low, the adverse reactions could affect thousands of
 74   individuals.
 75
 76   Changes in environmental temperature or humidity, including the daily exposure of the TDS to
 77   heat and water during routine showering, may transiently affect the rate at which components of
 78   the TDS formulation are released and permeate through skin. Such changes may also affect
 79   entrapped moisture in and/or under the TDS, which could alter skin hydration and impact the
 80   bioavailability of formulation components, which may, in turn, change irritation and/or
 81   sensitization reactions. Therefore, applicants should consider any conditions of labeled use for
 82   the RLD that may impact the irritation or sensitization potential of a TDS product (e.g.,
 83   incidental exposure of the TDS to water, such as while bathing or showering, particularly for a
 84   TDS with a duration of wear that is up to or greater than 24 hours).
 85
 86   In addition to irritation and sensitization reactions that may arise from the corrosive or
 87   immunomodulatory nature of formulation components or from the pharmacodynamic response
 88   of the skin to the occlusion by the TDS, the skin may also become irritated in response to the
 89   physical insults that can occur during the removal of a TDS. For example, if the adhesive
 90   properties of the TDS are such that it strips away substantial portions of the stratum corneum
 91   during removal, the damage to the skin barrier may lead to irritation at the site of TDS removal.
 92   Accordingly, applicants should also consider the adhesive components of the TDS and physical
 93   insults from removal of the TDS that may impact the irritation potential of a TDS product.
 94
 95   FDA generally recommends applicants conduct an in vivo study to evaluate the irritation
 96   potential of a T product, unless a difference in an inactive ingredient as compared to the RLD
 97   warrants an in vivo study to evaluate both the irritation and sensitization potential of the T
 98   product. Specifically, if a T product contains an inactive ingredient not contained in the RLD
 99   that is known to be sensitizing, 8 or at an amount not previously used in an FDA-approved drug
100   product with a similar context of use, FDA recommends conducting a combined irritation and
101   sensitization study. FDA encourages an applicant who has questions related to if a sensitization

106   IV.     EVALUATIONS OF SKIN IRRITATION AND SENSITIZATION
107
108           A.       Study Design and Conduct
109
110                    1. Study Design Recommendations
111
112   In general, the Agency recommends that applicants conduct an evaluator-blinded, randomized
113   study to support their comparative evaluation of the skin irritation characteristics of the T and R
114   products. The study population should typically include healthy males and nonpregnant,
115   nonlactating females, unless product-specific considerations consistent with the RLD’s labeled
116   conditions of use for certain TDS products indicate otherwise. 10 In the study protocol, the choice
117   of TDS strength intended to be used should be prespecified and should be justified, as
118   appropriate, based upon the use of the TDS in the proposed study population. When evaluating
119   the irritation potential only, applicants should compare the T and R TDS products in a minimum
120   of 40 evaluable subjects, or a sufficient number of subjects to power the study at a level of 0.80
121   or higher, whichever sample size is greater. To evaluate the irritation potential only, the study
122   should consist of a single 21-day induction phase under provocative conditions (repeated
123   removal and reapplication of the TDS on the same skin site) to maximize the potential for the
124   occurrence of an irritation in accordance with the recommendations described below.
125
126   When a sensitization study is appropriate, the Agency recommends that applicants evaluate both
127   skin irritation and sensitization in a single study using the same evaluator-blinded, randomized
128   study design, study population, and prespecified choice of TDS strength as described in the
129   paragraph above. However, a combined irritation and sensitization study should compare the T
130   and R TDS products in at least 200 evaluable subjects and be conducted under provocative
131   conditions (repeated removal and reapplication of the TDS on the same skin site) to maximize
132   the potential for the occurrence of an irritation and/or sensitization reaction in the subject
133   population during the study. FDA recommends a combined irritation and sensitization study to
134   consist of both a 21-day induction phase and, after a 14- to 17-day rest period, a challenge phase
135   in accordance with the recommendations described below. 11

137   During the induction phase, applicants should simultaneously apply all TDS units (i.e., every
138   whole or partial 12 T product and every whole or partial R product) to each subject. T and R
139   products should be applied at contralateral locations of the same anatomical site (e.g., T product
140   on the left buttock and R product on the right buttock); applicants should select the anatomical
141   site based on the recommendations for dosing in the RLD labeling.
142
143              For 21 consecutive days, TDS units should be worn, removed, and replaced by a new
144              TDS unit, for repeated durations to the same skin site as the initial application; each
145              duration should be representative of the RLD’s labeled wear period, unless otherwise
146              noted within the relevant PSG. For example, a TDS with a 3-day wear period may be
147              removed every 3 days, assessed for irritation, and replaced to the same skin site every 3
148              days, for a total of 21 days.
149
150              Applicants should evaluate the subject’s irritation response at the time of each TDS
151              change by individually assessing the subject’s “dermal response” and “other effects”
152              scores (both of which are described below) by using a separate scoring scale.
153
154              For subjects who experience excessive irritation, 13 the TDS may be moved to a new site
155              to complete the 21-day induction phase and to continue with the sensitization part (when
156              appropriate) of the study. Applicants should predefine in their protocol what criteria
157              would trigger the movement of a TDS to a new site (due to skin reactions that are
158              determined to represent excessive irritation). For example, the criteria may specify that
159              TDS may be moved to a new site if the combined score is equal to or greater than 3.
160
161   When evaluating the sensitization potential is appropriate, completion of the induction phase
162   should be followed by a 14- to 17-day rest period before beginning the challenge phase of the
163   study. During the challenge phase, applicants should simultaneously apply all TDS units (i.e.,
164   every whole or partial T product and every whole or partial R product) to each subject. T and R
165   products should be applied at contralateral locations of the same anatomical site (e.g., T product
166   on the left buttock and R product on the right buttock); applicants should select the anatomical
167   site based on the recommendations for dosing in the RLD labeling.
168
169              The TDS units should be applied for a 48-hour duration at a naïve skin site (i.e., a site
170              onto which a TDS was not applied during the induction phase) and then removed.
171
172              Applicants should assess the subject’s skin reactions at 30 minutes, 24 hours, 48 hours,
173              and 72 hours after removal of the TDS.
174
175              Applicants should record any skin reactions observed with a narrative description of the
176              subject’s “dermal response” and “other effects” scores (both of which are described
177              below) by using a separate scoring scale for each.

188                       2. Study Conduct Considerations
189
190   Applicants should randomize their assignment of the T and R products to skin sites, describe
191   their method of randomization in the protocol, and provide the randomization schedule as a SAS
192   transport data set in XPT format.
193
194   A trained observer should score the TDS’s adherence and the subject’s skin reactions at each
195   TDS removal, and applicants should try to ensure that the same scorer is used for all
196   observations. If the same scorer is not used for all observations, applicants should provide
197   evidence to ensure that the scoring is consistent across different scorers. Because of likely
198   differences in the appearance of the TDS between the T product and the R product, blinding of
199   the observer may not be possible, especially for monitoring TDS adhesion, which requires direct
200   observation of the TDS. However, applicants should try to blind the evaluation of irritation and
201   sensitization when possible.
202
203   If applicants believe that use of tape or an overlay may be needed to maintain maximum contact
204   of the TDS with the skin throughout the relevant duration of an irritation or combined irritation
205   and sensitization study, then the use of tape or an overlay may be appropriate. Applicants should
206   prespecify, in their study protocol, their criteria for using tape or an overlay to reinforce any TDS
207   that is lifting. If a TDS is reinforced with tape or an overlay, skin irritation of the tape or overlay
208   area should be reported separately from the skin irritation associated with the TDS application
209   area.
210
211   Applicants should evaluate adhesion of the TDS to the skin at each time point before TDS
212   removal throughout the entire study period to monitor the adequacy of skin contact, which is
213   necessary for a suitably provocative induction of irritation and sensitization. Accordingly, even
214   where tape or an overlay is used, adhesion should be evaluated based on the surface area of the
215   TDS (not including any tape or overlay) to ensure that the TDS is adhering well throughout the
216   induction phase and challenge phase. FDA’s recommendations for evaluating the adhesion of
217   the TDS are described in the draft guidance for industry Assessing Adhesion with Transdermal
218   and Topical Delivery Systems for ANDAs (April 2023). 14
219

221   continue in the study. The subject should note the date and time of detachment as soon as it

327                          5. Calculating and Formatting Dermal Response Scores
328
329   FDA’s recommended primary endpoint for evaluating irritation is the mean irritation score
330   (MIS). At each assessment time point for each subject and for each product, applicants should
331   calculate a combined irritation score by adding the “dermal response” score and the numeric
332   equivalent for “other effects” letter score. For each subject and each product, applicants should
333   calculate the MIS as the sum of the combined irritation scores over the assessment time points
334   divided by the total number of assessments. A separate MIS should be calculated in the same

412   For the irritation evaluation, FDA also considers other clinically relevant data, including the
413   number of TDS unit applications that reach a maximum irritation score and the number of
414   subjects who discontinue product application because of excessive irritation. The same MIS
415   could be reached with a small number of high scores (e.g., 3 or higher), which may be of greater
416   clinical concern, or with a larger number of low scores (e.g., 1), which may be of lesser clinical
417   concern. Thus, it is difficult to determine the clinical meaningfulness of a given MIS or a given
418   difference between products with respect to their MIS.
419
420   Therefore, in addition to MIS, FDA recommends the applicant evaluate the proportion of
421   subjects with excessive irritation for each product. The proportion of subjects with excessive
422   irritation should be no higher for the T product than for the R product, and irritation should not
423   occur earlier in the application period for the T product than for the R product. The T product
424   should be noninferior to the R product with respect to the MIS, and the T product should show
425   no meaningful difference, compared to the R product, with respect to the degree of irritation.
426
427                      2. Sensitization Analysis
428
429   As previously noted, generally an in vivo sensitization evaluation of a TDS product may be
430   needed where there is a difference in an inactive ingredient between the T product as compared
431   to the RLD. Specifically, if a T product contains an inactive ingredient not contained in the RLD
432   that is known to be sensitizing, or at an amount not previously used in an FDA-approved drug
433   product with a similar context of use, a sensitization assessment may be needed to show that the
434   T product is not likely to be more sensitizing than the RLD.
435
436   When an in vivo sensitization evaluation is appropriate, a combined irritation and sensitization
437   study should compare the T and R TDS products in at least 200 evaluable subjects. Applicants
438   should define, in the protocol, the PP population for the sensitization analysis per TDS instead of
439   per subject.
440
441   The PP population for the sensitization analysis should include all TDS units worn (without any
442   period of detachment longer than 24 hours) for the full 21-day induction phase and the entire 48-
443   hour challenge phase. Each subject should return for at least one of the scheduled evaluations at
444   48 and 72 hours after removal of the challenge TDS. If a TDS unit is removed before the end of
445   the 48-hour challenge phase because of excessive irritation, the application site should be
446   evaluated at 24 hours, 48 hours, and 72 hours after TDS removal and be included in the
447   sensitization analysis using the LOCF from the original application site.

456
457          The subject has a combined irritation score of at least 2 at their last evaluation during the
458          challenge phase.

460          If the subject completed a rechallenge phase, the above two criteria were met during both
461          the challenge phase and the rechallenge phase.

463   Skin reactions that resolve before 48 hours are generally considered to be caused by irritation
464   instead of sensitization. For any potential sensitization reaction observed during the challenge or
465   rechallenge phase, applicants should provide a justification to support that the rate of
466   sensitization of the T product is comparable to that observed with the use of the R product.

484          C.      Vehicle TDS and Positive Control TDS
485
486   If safety concerns preclude the usual comparative studies, which include the use of the T and R
487   products, the evaluation of skin irritation (and sensitization, if applicable) by the T product can
488   be evaluated by testing a vehicle TDS versus a positive control TDS that produces mild irritation
489   (e.g., 0.1% sodium lauryl sulfate). The vehicle TDS should contain all the inactive ingredients
490   in the T product and be identical to the T product in every manner except for the absence of the
491   active ingredient. If the inactive ingredients in the vehicle TDS are different than those
492   contained in the T product or are in different amounts than in the T product, then the applicant

                                                 Draft — Not for Implementation


493   should clearly describe the differences and provide data to show that the differences will not
494   affect the safety of the T product or the applicant’s interpretation of the study results.
495
496   For a skin irritation (or combined irritation and sensitization) study that compares the vehicle
497   TDS to a positive control TDS, applicants should utilize essentially the same approach as is
498   recommended for the comparison of T and R products in sections IV.A. and B. of this guidance,
499   except that the vehicle TDS should serve as the T product and the positive control TDS should
500   serve as the R product.
501
502   Applicants should ensure that the positive control is consistently able to elicit and maintain an
503   irritation response during the induction phase. A positive control that is either unable to
504   consistently elicit an irritation response, or unable to maintain that response, may confound the
505   interpretation of study results and undermine the validity of the study.
506
507   It is not recommended to include multiple candidate positive control TDS products in the
508   irritation (or combined irritation and sensitization) study and post-hoc select one as the positive
509   control TDS to compare with the vehicle TDS in the statistical analysis. Rather, it is
510   recommended to preselect one appropriate positive control to use as the positive control TDS
511   prior to the irritation (or combined irritation and sensitization) study.
512
513              D.       Partial (Cut) TDS
514
515   If a safety concern prevents the simultaneous application of two whole TDS on the same subject
516   during the 21-day irritation-only study or 21-day induction phase of a combined irritation and
517   sensitization study, a matrix TDS can be cut to a smaller size. 17 In such situations, the T and R
518   products should both have designs that can be safely cut to a smaller size. Applicants should not
519   manufacture a separate batch of product to use a smaller TDS in this study. When using a cut
520   TDS, the general recommendations provided in sections IV.A and IV.B in this guidance apply.
