17   I.      INTRODUCTION
18
19   This guidance is intended to assist sponsors in monitoring growth and, when appropriate,
20   pubertal development in clinical trials that enroll pediatric participants with rare and common
21   diseases. This guidance provides recommendations for the most appropriate methods for
22   measuring and recording growth and evaluating pubertal development for evaluation of safety.
23
24   This guidance does not address use of growth or pubertal development data to support primary
25   evidence of efficacy in growth disorders (e.g., primary growth deficiency, disorders of pubertal
26   development such as precocious puberty or delayed puberty). Sponsors should further discuss
27   with the appropriate review division how to establish efficacy for such drugs. 2
28
29   This guidance does not address evaluation of nutritional status. This guidance does not address
30   statistical methods for analyzing growth or pubertal developmental data.
31
32   In general, FDA’s guidance documents do not establish legally enforceable responsibilities.
33   Instead, guidances describe the Agency’s current thinking on a topic and should be viewed only
34   as recommendations, unless specific regulatory or statutory requirements are cited. The use of
35   the word should in Agency guidances means that something is suggested or recommended, but
36   not required.

39   II.        BACKGROUND
40
41   In general, pediatric clinical trials should include accurate, serial measurements and recordings
42   of growth parameters if an investigational drug has the potential to affect growth or pubertal
43   development. Typically, growth is assessed using measurements of weight and linear growth
44   (length and height), and when appropriate, head circumference. Additional measurements and
45   calculations may be needed in certain pediatric age groups and disease populations, as discussed
46   further in section III., Measurements of Growth and Pubertal Development. Typically, pubertal
47   development is assessed using clinical phenotyping. Accurately identifying the onset and
48   progression of puberty is essential for accurate interpretation of growth data.
49

51   III.       MEASUREMENTS OF GROWTH AND PUBERTAL DEVELOPMENT
52
53              A.     Growth Measurements
54
55              1.     General Considerations
56
57   Sponsors should consider the following for measuring growth in pediatric participants enrolled in
58   clinical trials:
59
60          •   Develop a protocol for training investigators and trial site personnel responsible for
61              collecting growth parameters and conducting physical examinations and include
62              procedures for determining a trial participant’s age, obtaining growth measurements,
63              instrument calibration, and evaluation of pubertal development.
64
65          •   Avoid self-reported growth measurements because they may not be sufficiently accurate,
66              consistent, and reliable for data collection. Growth measurements assessed outside of
67              established trial sites may be acceptable if the measurements are taken by a trained health
68              care professional using appropriate and calibrated instruments.
69
70          •   In general, collect and record growth measurements for a minimum trial duration of 12
71              months. Sponsors should discuss alternative trial durations with the appropriate review
72              division and consider any potential safety concerns related to the drug for the intended
73              patient population.
74
75          •   Keep pediatric participants who discontinue the study treatment in the trial to continue to
76              obtain their growth measurements. Measurements obtained after treatment
77              discontinuation may be useful for ensuring the reliability and interpretability of analyses
78              and results.
79
80                     a.      Reducing measurement error
81
82   To reduce measurement error, the sponsor should include procedures and practices in the
83   protocol, such as the following:
84

 85      •   Outline procedures for calibration of measuring instruments (e.g., calibration timing
 86          relative to measurement, calibration frequency).
 87
 88      •   Document how measurements are obtained (e.g., for weight, use of tared weight,
 89          documenting type of scale, clothing; for height, use of recumbent or standing methods of
 90          measurement) and how to address challenges in obtaining measurements (e.g.,
 91          uncooperative child, assistive technology).
 92
 93      •   For trials involving multiple treatment arms, blind trial site personnel to each trial arm.
 94          For single-arm trials, the sponsor should discuss with the appropriate review division
 95          how the trial could be designed to avoid biased measurement of growth parameters.
 96
 97      •   Because of diurnal variations in height and weight, schedule study visits and/or perform
 98          growth measurements at the same time of day unless justification is provided.
 99
100                  b.     Determining age
101
102   Appropriately determining a participant’s age is important for accurate and consistent
103   documentation of growth. Sponsors should consider the following when determining age:
104
105      •   For participants 3 years of age and older, calculate and use the chronological age to the
106          nearest month and year.
107
108      •   For participants younger than 3 years of age, consider whether the participant was born at
109          term (37 weeks gestation or later) or preterm (before 37 weeks gestation).
110
111          For participants born at term, sponsors should consider the following calculations for age:
112
113             i.   For participants older than 12 months of age, calculate age to the nearest month.
114
115            ii.   For participants from three to 12 months of age, calculate age in completed weeks
116                  or months.
117
118           iii.   For participants less than 3 months of age, calculate age in completed weeks.
119
120          For participants born preterm, sponsors should consider the following for calculating age:
121
122             i.   Determine the gestational age (GA) at birth (e.g., based on the first day of
123                  mother’s last menstrual period (LMP), prenatal ultrasonography, history of
124                  assisted reproduction, postnatal physical exam), and document the method for
125                  determining GA.
126

127              ii.   Document both the chronological and corrected age 3 for each growth assessment
128                    until participants have transitioned from corrected to chronological age. Given a
129                    lack of consensus about thresholds of transition from corrected to chronological
130                    age, sponsors should provide data to support or justify the selected age correction
131                    strategy.

133                    c.       Using standardized charts
134
135   Sponsors should plot anthropometric measurements based on age and sex using the appropriate
136   standardized charts for the trial population.
137
138   Unless justification is provided to support using an alternative approach for growth
139   measurement, sponsors should consider the following for pediatric participants enrolled in U.S.
140   trial sites:
141
142       •   For participants born at term (37 weeks gestation or later):
143
144               i.   Two years of age and older, use the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention
145                    (CDC) growth charts. 4
146
147              ii.   Birth to younger than 2 years of age, use the World Health Organization (WHO)
148                    growth charts. 5
149
150       •   For participants born prematurely (before 37 weeks gestation):
151
152               i.   Born at 22 to 36 6/7 weeks GA, use the Fenton 6,7 preterm infant growth chart.

167            2.      Weight Assessment
168
169   Sponsors enrolling pediatric participants in clinical trials should consider incorporating the
170   following considerations in the trial protocol regarding weight assessment:
171
172        •   Use a scale with an electronic (i.e., digital) reading that allows for adjusting the scale to
173            zero prior to weighing the pediatric participant.
174
175        •   Place the scale on a flat, hard, and even surface.
176
177        •   Calibrate all scales used during trials at a standardized time before each study visit.
178
179        •   Record weight for participants weighing more than or equal to 5 kilograms (kg) to the
180            nearest 0.1 kg; for neonates weighing less than 5 kg, recording weight to the nearest 10
181            grams.
182
183        •   Address in the protocol how weight will be assessed in participants who are technology-
184            dependent (e.g., ventilator-dependent), device-dependent (e.g., gastrostomy tube), or
185            require assistive technology (e.g., orthotics, prostheses, wheelchairs). Techniques to
186            consider include the following:
187
188             If the technology can be safely removed 10, weigh the participant without the
189              technology.
190

224          the protocol.

228          3.     Linear Growth (Length and Height) Assessment
229
230   Sponsors should consider the following for measuring length and height in pediatric participants
231   enrolled in clinical trials:
232
233      •   Measure recumbent length in participants from birth to less than 2 years of age using a
234          length board (or infantometer) with a fixed head piece, horizontal backboard, and an
235          adjustable foot piece. The length board should be placed on a flat, stable surface.
236

237        •   Measure standing height in participants 2 years of age and older using a stadiometer
238            mounted at a right angle between a level floor and against a straight, vertical surface.
239
240        •   When transitioning from recumbent length to standing height measurements in
241            participants between 2 to 3 years of age, measure both length and height.
242
243        •   Measure linear growth to the nearest 0.1 centimeter (cm).
244
245        •   Before measuring linear growth, remove shoes, hats, hair ornaments, and braids
246            whenever possible because these items will interfere with accurate linear growth
247            measurement.
248
249        •   Measure linear growth three times at each study visit to improve accuracy and consider
250            the following:
251
252             Repeat measurements that are not clinically plausible (e.g., height measurement that
253              is lower than the height measured at the previous or prior study visits).
254
255             Calculate the mean of the three linear growth measurements.
256
257             Record individual growth measurements and the mean of the measurements.
258
259             Use the mean value in linear growth analyses.
260
261        •   Provide details in the protocol on any alternative strategies that will be used for
262            evaluating linear growth in trials enrolling pediatric participants with conditions that may
263            affect measurement of linear growth (e.g., contractures, genetic syndromes including
264            skeletal dysplasia, scoliosis). For participants who are not cooperative with linear growth
265            measurements, sponsors should consider an adjustment strategy based on
266            recommendations by WHO 11 or other professional health organizations.
267
268            4.      Head Circumference Assessment
269
270   Sponsors should consider the following for measuring head circumference in pediatric
271   participants enrolled in clinical trials:
272
273        •   Measure head circumference (HC) using a nonelastic tape measure.
274
275        •   Measure HC in all participants younger than 2 years of age or until sutures are fused.
276
277        •   Measure HC in participants 2 years of age and older in some clinical situations (e.g.,
278            neurocognitive impairment, microcephaly, macrocephaly, diseases that affect
279            neurodevelopment) and in trials investigating therapeutics that may cause neurotoxicity.

293          5.      Other Considerations
294
295   Sponsors should also consider the following when enrolling pediatric participants in clinical
296   trials:
297
298      •   Confirm diagnoses for genetic disorders affecting growth with molecular or cytogenetic
299          testing to ensure use of appropriate growth charts and accurate interpretation of
300          anthropometric data.
301
302      •   Discuss collection of relevant family history data (e.g., growth and puberty patterns) with
303          the appropriate review division.
304
305   Note that FDA currently does not recognize biomarkers as validated assessments of growth.
306
307          B.      Pubertal Development
308
309   Sponsors enrolling pediatric participants in clinical trials should use a sexual maturity rating
310   (e.g., Tanner Staging) to evaluate and document pubertal development at baseline (i.e., trial
311   entry) and at regular intervals based on the potential safety concerns associated with the drug and
312   the pubertal development stage of the pediatric participant. Sponsors should also consider the
313   following:
314
315      •   Discuss with the appropriate review division the appropriate intervals for evaluation of
316          pubertal development.
317
318      •   Sexual maturity ratings should be based on both breast and pubic hair changes in females
319          and on both genital and pubic hair changes in males. Evaluation of genital changes in
320          males should include an assessment of testicular volume using an orchidometer.
321
322      •   Discontinue or do not initiate pubertal development assessments when the participant has
323          completed puberty (i.e., Tanner 5). FDA also recommends the following:
324

325           Train investigators and examiners on how to conduct sexual maturity ratings.
326            Whenever possible, the same trial health care professional should evaluate pubertal
327            development.
328
329           Identify and record the age at menarche for female participants.

331          C.       Other Measurements
332
333          1.       Skeletal Age
334
335   Sponsors should consider the following recommendations when assessing skeletal age in
336   pediatric participants enrolled in clinical trials:
337
338      •   Evaluate skeletal maturation by validated assessments and tools to provide information
339          about skeletal maturity compared to chronological age. Potential bone age assessments
340          include imaging of the following:
341
342           For hand and wrist bones, using the following:
343
344                  X-ray (Greulich and Pyle method, Tanner-Whitehouse method, Gilsanz and
345                   Ratibin atlas, or computer-assisted skeletal bone age systems)
346
347                  Ultrasound
348
349                  Magnetic resonance imaging
350
351           For knee joints, using X-ray (O’Connor knee scale)
352
353      •   Discuss the appropriate bone age assessments for the trial participant population with the
354          appropriate review division.
355
356      •   Use the same validated methodology or instrument for all participants throughout the trial
357          to improve consistency and accuracy. Sponsors can consider alternative methods if
358          adequate validation is demonstrated.
359
360      •   For consistency, reassess all readings with a blinded, single central reader, when using
361          reader dependent methods, to limit interreader reliability issues intrinsic to these
362          methods. Sponsors should use the results from the single central reading as the primary
363          data source for analyses.
364
365      •   For body proportions, discuss with the appropriate review division the need for additional
366          measurements in pediatric participants with specific diseases known to be associated with
367          disproportionate growth (e.g., Marfan syndrome, skeletal dysplasia).

369          2.     Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry Scan
370
371   Depending on the study population, sponsors that enroll pediatric participants in clinical trials
372   can use dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) scans to monitor bone mineral density, bone
373   mineral content, and body composition. Sponsors should discuss the conduct of DXA scans with
374   the appropriate review division.
