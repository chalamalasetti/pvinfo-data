56   II.        BACKGROUND
57
58   As referenced above, a number of FDA’s statutory and regulatory provisions potentially impact
59   firms’ communications with payors. This guidance provides FDA’s thinking on frequently
60   asked questions regarding such communications in order to provide clarity to firms and payors.
61
62   Payors are a sophisticated audience with a range of expertise in multiple disciplines, as well as
63   established procedures for carefully considering evidence about medical products. Generally,
64   payors possess the financial resources and motivation to closely scrutinize information about
65   medical products as part of their decision-making process, including an evaluation of the
66   limitations and reliability of that information. Payors seek a range of information on the
67   effectiveness, safety, and cost-effectiveness of approved/cleared medical products, including
68   information from firms, to help support their product selection, formulary management, and/or
69   coverage and reimbursement decisions on a population basis. Often, this information differs
70   from — and can be provided in addition to — the information FDA reviews in making
71   approval/clearance decisions.
72
73   HCEI and product information provided by firms to payors about their medical products must be
74   truthful and not misleading. 12 This guidance provides FDA’s recommendations for firms to help
75   them ensure that their communication of HCEI to payors about approved/cleared medical
76   products is truthful and not misleading. Specifically, section III.A of this guidance provides
77   FDA’s thinking on frequently asked questions regarding communication of HCEI to payors
78   about approved/cleared medical products under section 502(a) of the FD&C Act (21 U.S.C.
79   352(a)). FDA considers firms’ communications of HCEI to payors that otherwise meet the
80   definition under section 201(m) of the FD&C Act (21 U.S.C. 321(m)) to be labeling. 13

129   Q1.      What is HCEI, and how can it be presented?
130
131   HCEI is defined in section 502(a)(2)(A) of the FD&C Act as “any analysis (including the clinical
132   data, inputs, clinical or other assumptions, methods, results, and other components underlying or
133   comprising the analysis) that identifies, measures, or describes the economic consequences,
134   which may be based on the separate or aggregated clinical consequences of the represented
135   health outcomes, of the use of a drug or device. Such analysis may be comparative to the use of
136   another drug or device, to another health care intervention, or to no intervention.” 15 HCEI
137   pertains to the economic consequences (including but not limited to monetary costs or resource
138   utilization) related to the clinical outcomes of treating a disease (or specific aspect of a disease)
139   or of preventing or diagnosing a disease. 16 HCEI, for example, may include comparative
140   analyses of the economic consequences of a medical product’s clinical outcomes to alternative
141   options (including the use of another medical product) or to no intervention.
142
143   HCEI can be presented in a variety of ways that can include but are not limited to an evidence
144   dossier, a reprint of a publication from a peer-reviewed journal, a software package comprising a
145   model with a user manual, a budget-impact model, a slide presentation, or a payor brochure.

147   Q2.          What is the appropriate scope of the audience for the communication of HCEI
148                about approved/cleared medical products under section 502(a)?
149
150   Section 502(a) specifies that HCEI can be provided to “a payor, formulary committee, or other
151   similar entity with knowledge and expertise in the area of health care economic analysis,
152   carrying out its responsibilities for the selection of drugs or devices for coverage or
153   reimbursement.” 17
154
155   This audience includes public and private sector payors, 18 formulary committees 19 (e.g.,
156   pharmacy and therapeutics committees), drug information centers, technology assessment
157   committees, pharmacy benefit managers, third party administrators, and other multidisciplinary
158   entities that, on behalf of health care organizations, 20 review scientific and/or technology
159   assessments to make medical product selection or acquisition, formulary management, and/or
160   coverage and reimbursement decisions on a population basis. 21
161
162   Such entities are constituted to consider HCEI (and other types of information) through a
163   “deliberative process” and should have the appropriate range of “knowledge and expertise in the
164   area of health care economic analysis” needed to interpret HCEI presented to them to inform
165   their population-based decision-making process. 22,23 Expertise in this area is critical to
166   understand and evaluate health care economic analyses and their limitations.
167
168   Because section 502(a) provides for communication of HCEI only to particular audiences,
169   dissemination of HCEI to other audiences is not covered by the recommendations of this
170   guidance. 24 Thus, this guidance does not apply to dissemination of HCEI to other audiences,
171   such as health care providers who are making individual patient prescribing decisions or

179   Q3.        How does FDA intend to implement this guidance for HCEI disseminated in
180              accordance with section 502(a)?
181
182   Section 502(a) provides that HCEI disseminated in accordance with the terms of that statutory
183   provision is not false or misleading. If a firm disseminates to an appropriate audience HCEI that
184   is of the type of information described in section 502(a), i.e., HCEI that relates to an approved
185   indication 25 and is based on competent and reliable scientific evidence (CARSE), as each of
186   these elements is described in this guidance, FDA does not intend to consider such information
187   false or misleading.
188
189   HCEI should clearly and prominently present the information discussed in Q7 and Q8 of this
190   section, including study design and methodology, generalizability, limitations, sensitivity
191   analyses, and information relevant to providing a balanced and complete presentation. If HCEI
192   includes material differences from the FDA-approved labeling (e.g., new or increased risks,
193   different dosing/use regimens, different endpoints, more-limited/targeted patient populations), it
194   must present “a conspicuous and prominent statement describing any material differences
195   between the health care economic information and the labeling approved for the drug or
196   device,” 26 as discussed in Q7.
197
198   In addition, FDA does not intend to use HCEI that is disseminated consistent with this guidance,
199   standing alone, as evidence of a new intended use.

236   Q5.    What evidentiary support should firms have for their HCEI under section 502(a)?
237
238   Section 502(a) states that HCEI shall not be considered false or misleading if, among other
239   things, it is “based on competent and reliable scientific evidence.” FDA considers HCEI to be
240   based on CARSE if the HCEI has been developed using generally accepted scientific standards
241   that are appropriate for the information being conveyed and that yield accurate, reliable results.
242   In evaluating whether the amount and type of evidence that forms the basis for a particular
243   communication of HCEI meets the generally accepted scientific standards for such information,
244   FDA will consider existing current good research practices for substantiation developed by
245   authoritative bodies. Examples of such bodies include but are not limited to the International
246   Society for Pharmacoeconomic and Outcomes Research (ISPOR), the International Society for
247   Pharmacoepidemiology (ISPE), the Patient-Centered Outcomes Research Institute (PCORI), and
248   the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (AHRQ). For example, when evaluating HCEI
249   based on indirect treatment comparisons in the absence of data from head-to-head controlled
250   clinical trials, FDA may refer to guidelines issued by external expert bodies regarding current
251   rigorous methodologies and best practices for such comparisons (e.g., network meta-analyses).
252
253   HCEI should clearly and prominently present the applicable information discussed in Q7 and Q8
254   of this guidance, including study design and methodology, generalizability, limitations,
255   sensitivity analyses, and information relevant to providing a balanced and complete presentation.
256
257   Q6.    Does the CARSE standard apply only to the economic components of HCEI, or does
258          it also apply to the other components?
259
260   Under section 502(a), HCEI includes the “clinical data, inputs, clinical or other assumptions,
261   methods, results, and other components underlying or comprising the analysis” of a medical
262   product’s economic consequences. FDA considers the CARSE standard in section 502(a) to
263   apply to all components of HCEI, including inputs and assumptions related to both economic
264   consequences and clinical outcomes (i.e., safety and/or effectiveness). As discussed previously
265   in Q4, such information must also relate to an approved indication in order to fall within the
266   provisions of section 502(a) regarding HCEI.

290      1.      Study Design and Methodology
291
292   Firms should include an accurate overview of the design of the economic analysis, including a
293   statement of the study objectives. For example, a clear description of the hypothesis tested
294   should be provided, and potential biases and/or confounders should be acknowledged. In
295   addition, the following information about the study and/or methodology should be presented
296   where relevant:
297
298   Type of Analysis: The type of economic analysis selected (e.g., cost-minimization analysis,
299   cost-effective analysis, cost-utility analysis, cost-benefit analysis, cost-consequence analysis,
300   budget impact analysis) should be stated, and the reason for its choice should be explained
301   (Drummond and Jefferson 1996).
302
303   Modeling: The type of modeling technique should be disclosed, with an explanation of the
304   model choice, its scope, and its key variables/parameters (Husereau et al. 2013). The rationale
305   and consequences of including and excluding specific variables in economic models should be
306   discussed in the analysis.
307
308   Patient Population: Details about the patient population should be specified, including the
309   number of patients and relevant demographic information, such as age, sex, race, clinical
310   characteristics, and socioeconomic status (Gold et al. 1996; Husereau et al. 2013).
311
312   Perspective/Viewpoint: The perspective or viewpoint of the economic analysis should be
313   clearly stated so that payors can understand the rationale for the selection of inputs (e.g.,
314   outcome measures, time periods, costs) and can therefore determine whether the HCEI is
315   relevant to their particular health care organizations. Possible viewpoints can include those of
316   the patient, employer, health care provider (e.g., clinician, institution), payor, regulatory body
317   (e.g., government agency), or society (i.e., everyone impacted by the treatment) (Gold et al 1996;
318   Drummond and Jefferson 1996).
319
320   Comparator: The choice of comparator (e.g., other medical products, other medical care, no
321   treatment) should be explained (Gold et al. 1996; Husereau et al. 2013).

620   investigational uses of approved/cleared medical products, while this guidance applies to
621   unapproved medical products and unapproved uses of approved/cleared medical products. To
622   the extent that there are products to which this guidance applies but section 502(gg) does not,
623   FDA recommends that firms provide the information set forth in section 502(gg)(1)(A).

625   Q14. What information does section 502(gg) require and section B of this guidance
626        recommend be excluded from “product information”?
627
628   Section 502(gg)(1)(B) of the FD&C Act provides that product information must not include
629   information that represents that an unapproved medical product:
630
631          •   Has been FDA-approved/cleared or
632
633          •   Has otherwise been determined to be safe or effective for the purpose(s) for which it
634              is being studied
635
636   Similarly, product information must not include information that represents:
637
638          •   That an unapproved use of an approved/cleared medical product is FDA-
639              approved/cleared or
640
641          •   That the medical product is safe or effective for the use(s) for which it is being
642              studied
643
644   To the extent that there are products to which this guidance applies but section 502(gg) does not,
645   FDA recommends that firms do not provide the information set forth in section 502(gg)(1)(B).
