15   I.      INTRODUCTION
16
17   The purpose of this guidance is to provide recommendations to applicants of approved new drug
18   applications (NDAs) and abbreviated new drug applications (ANDAs) for nonprescription drug
19   products on documenting minor labeling changes in the next annual report in accordance with 21
20   CFR §§ 314.70(d) and 314.97. This guidance also provides examples of such minor labeling
21   changes.
22
23   This guidance does not address labeling changes for prescription drug products. This guidance
24   also does not address major and moderate labeling changes to approved NDAs and ANDAs for
25   nonprescription drug products under §§ 314.70(b)-(c) and 314.97. 2 Nor does this guidance
26   address chemistry, manufacturing, and controls (CMC) postapproval manufacturing changes.
27
28   For additional information on making and reporting changes to an approved NDA or ANDA and
29   for distributing a drug product made with such changes, refer to §§ 314.70, 314.71, and 314.97.
30   In addition, an applicant should consider relevant CDER guidance documents for
31   recommendations on reporting categories and information that should be submitted to support a
32   change to an approved NDA or ANDA, including the guidances for industry Changes to an
33   Approved NDA or ANDA, CMC Postapproval Manufacturing Changes To Be Documented in
34   Annual Reports (March 2014), and ANDA Submissions – Prior Approval Supplements Under
35   GDUFA (October 2022).
36

38   Instead, guidances describe the Agency’s current thinking on a topic and should be viewed only
39   as recommendations, unless specific regulatory or statutory requirements are cited. The use of

44   II.        BACKGROUND
45
46   Certain nonprescription drug products that are new drugs within the meaning of section 201(p) of
47   the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act) are subject to approval on the basis of an
48   NDA or ANDA submitted under sections 505(b) or 505(j) of the FD&C Act, respectively. A
49   “nonprescription drug” is a drug that is not subject to the requirements of section 503(b)(1) of the
50   FD&C Act. 3
51
52   FDA evaluates whether the data and information submitted as part of an NDA or ANDA for a
53   nonprescription drug product demonstrate that the drug product is safe and effective for
54   nonprescription use under the conditions prescribed, recommended, or suggested in its proposed
55   labeling. 4 A nonprescription drug must be labeled with adequate directions for use. 5 Adequate
56   directions for use are the directions under which the consumer (layman) can use the drug safely
57   and for the purposes for which it is intended. 6 Therefore, labeling for a nonprescription drug
58   product 7 enables consumers to appropriately self-select and use the nonprescription drug product
59   safely and effectively without the supervision of a health care practitioner.
60
61   In order for FDA to approve an application for a nonprescription drug, particular studies may be
62   required to demonstrate that the drug product can be used safely and effectively in the
63   nonprescription setting. This may include consumer studies to demonstrate that the drug product
64   is safe and effective for use without the supervision of a health care practitioner (i.e.,
65   nonprescription setting), including label comprehension studies, 8 self-selection studies, 9 and
66   actual use studies. 10

 67   After FDA approves an NDA or ANDA, an applicant may make, or in certain cases propose to
 68   FDA, changes to the approved application. Section 506A of the FD&C Act and FDA regulations
 69   under 21 CFR §§ 314.70, 314.71, and 314.97 establish certain requirements for making and
 70   reporting to FDA changes to an approved NDA or ANDA, including an NDA or ANDA for a
 71   nonprescription drug product. The regulations address changes that must be approved by FDA
 72   before distribution of a drug product made with such changes, as well as changes for which FDA
 73   approval is not required before distribution and changes that must be submitted in annual reports.
 74   Changes to an approved NDA or ANDA, including labeling changes, are categorized into one of
 75   three reporting categories: major, moderate, or minor. 11
 76
 77   Minor changes include certain changes that have a minimal potential to have an adverse effect
 78   on the identity, strength, quality, purity, or potency of a drug product as these factors may relate
 79   to the safety or effectiveness of the drug product. 12 Minor changes to an approved NDA or
 80   ANDA may be implemented immediately by the applicant without the applicant submitting a
 81   supplement to FDA. 13 The applicant must document minor changes, including minor labeling
 82   changes, in its next annual report in accordance with 21 CFR § 314.81(b)(2) 14 (i.e., the annual
 83   report covering the period when the change or changes occurred) submitted to FDA. 15 The
 84   annual report must include a summary of any changes in labeling, including minor changes, that
 85   have been made since the last report listed by date in the order in which they were implemented,
 86   or if no changes have been made, a statement of that fact. 16
 87
 88   Determining the reporting category for a change to nonprescription drug labeling may present
 89   certain considerations that differ from changes to prescription drug labeling. Changes to the
 90   approved labeling for a nonprescription drug product may affect consumers’ ability to
 91   appropriately self-select and use the nonprescription drug product safely and effectively without
 92   the supervision of a health care practitioner. Thus, changes to nonprescription labeling may not
 93   be considered minor even though similar changes may be considered minor when applied to the
 94   labeling of a prescription drug product. For example, certain changes in the layout of the package
 95   or container label for a prescription drug product that are consistent with FDA regulations (e.g.,
 96   21 CFR part 201), without a change in the content of the labeling, might not affect the safe and
 97   effective use of the prescription drug product, because it is used under the supervision of a
 98   healthcare practitioner. In contrast, changes in the layout of the package or container label and
 99   other changes to nonprescription drug labeling could affect consumers’ ability to comprehend the
100   nonprescription drug labeling and to appropriately self-select and use the nonprescription drug
101   product such that the change would not be a minor change under 314.70(d).

217              B.      Additional Considerations for Minor Labeling Changes
218
219   Applicants should be aware of the following:
220
221          •   Generally, changes to the content in the DFL of an approved nonprescription drug
222              product are not considered minor changes and may not be documented in an annual
223              report. 27 However, certain changes to the DFL may be considered minor, including
224              changing color of DFL and moving the tamper-evident statement from the DFL to the
225              outer carton (see section III.A. of this guidance for examples).
226
227          •   In certain cases involving implementation of multiple minor changes, the totality of the
228              minor changes could affect consumers’ ability to understand the required labeling
229              information and to appropriately self-select and use the nonprescription drug product. 28
230              In such cases, applicants should submit a supplement, rather than an annual report.
231              Where appropriate, if an applicant includes multiple changes in its annual report,
232              especially changes to the PDP that cause the outer packaging to look new or different,
233              FDA may request that the applicant submit a supplemental NDA or ANDA.
234
235          •   When FDA reviews submitted annual reportable changes, it is FDA’s practice to
236              acknowledge the changes at the time of submission of the annual report. Labeling
237              changes submitted in annual reports are not FDA-approved and should not be considered
238              or described as such. FDA would consider the annual reportable changes for approval in
239              any subsequent supplemental NDA or ANDA that the applicant submits. 29 At times,
240              multiple annual reportable changes, especially to the PDP where the outer packaging
241              looks new or different, FDA will request a prior approval supplement be submitted to
242              approve that particular label with multiple changes.
243
244          •   FDA reviews previously submitted annual reports. Upon review of the annual report,
245              FDA may determine that a labeling change was inappropriate for documentation in an
246              annual report, notify the applicant of the correct supplement category, and request the
247              submission of such supplement. 30
