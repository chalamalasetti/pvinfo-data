36   II.       BACKGROUND
37
38   IC/BPS is a complex, poorly understood heterogeneous syndrome of unknown etiology.
39
40   Interstitial cystitis (IC) was first characterized over 100 years ago in patients as a symptom
41   complex with these two factors:
42
43         •   Bladder pain related to bladder filling
44         •   Historical pathognomonic cystoscopic finding of Hunner’s lesion 5 in the bladder
45
46   In the intervening years, beginning in the 1970s and 1980s, there began a gradual
47   acknowledgment that IC might encompass more diverse forms of the disorder than solely
48   identifying patients with Hunner’s lesions (mucosal lesions or ulcerations seen with or without
49   hydrodistention of the bladder). The disorder definition was expanded to include patients with
50   very similar symptoms but not necessarily requiring the presence of Hunner’s lesions on
51   cystoscopic examination.
52
53   Clinical IC management has evolved and expanded to include patients with symptoms of pain or
54   discomfort and accompanying urinary symptoms, and with or without obvious bladder
55   inflammation, which encompasses a very heterogeneous patient population, hereafter referred to
56   as IC/BPS.
57
58   Currently, there are no FDA-authorized diagnostic laboratory tests or biomarkers to establish IC/BPS
59   presence or response to therapy. There is wide variability in IC/BPS diagnostic criteria, which results
60   in patient population heterogeneity, complicating development of drugs for widespread use among
61   all patient subpopulations.
62
63   In general, current IC/BPS clinical diagnosis requires the following:
64
65         •   Chronic pain or discomfort localized to the bladder with the following variabilities:
66
67             – Can be related to bladder filling and/or voiding
68             – Can be constant, of variable intensity, or intermittent in nature

 81   III.       EARLY DRUG DEVELOPMENT PROGRAM FEATURES–KEY
 82              CONSIDERATIONS
 83
 84   Current IC/BPS clinical diagnosis criteria encompasses a very heterogeneous patient population
 85   who present with a common symptom: pain or discomfort localized to the bladder. Historically,
 86   this has resulted in difficulty developing drug products intended for use in a heterogenous patient
 87   population. Early clinical trials during drug development should focus on well-defined IC/BPS
 88   populations with similar clinical features in addition to pain or discomfort localized to the
 89   bladder. This approach may better inform the selection of clinically meaningful efficacy
 90   endpoints and outcomes for future studies.
 91
 92   Sponsors are encouraged to assess dosing strategies, including dose-finding. In addition, drug
 93   programs should explore multiple efficacy endpoints, and collect safety information during early
 94   drug development to inform design strategy and selection of clinically meaningful endpoints for
 95   later studies.
 96
 97   Patients’ experience with IC/BPS symptoms may vary (e.g., symptom type, severity, and
 98   duration). It is important to incorporate the patient’s voice during early drug development.
 99   Obtaining information from patients can better define the appropriate target population (e.g.,
100   IC/BPS patient subpopulations) and identify what matters most to patients regarding their
101   condition to inform endpoint selection. Sponsors should specify and define concepts (e.g.,
102   symptoms, functional impacts) early in development that are both relevant and important to
103   patients with IC/BPS. These concepts are more likely to lead to demonstration of clinically
104   meaningful and interpretable changes in later planned clinical trials. Identification of concepts
105   should come from patient input (e.g., conducting patient interviews, identifying literature related
106   to previously conducted patient qualitative studies). The Agency refers sponsors to the following
107   guidances for industry, FDA staff, and other stakeholders for more details about the collection of
108   evidence to inform the patient experience and obtain information from patients, caregivers, and
109   other relevant stakeholders:

147   IV.       CLINICAL TRIAL DESIGN FEATURES–KEY CONSIDERATIONS
148
149             A.     Enrollment Criteria
150
151   Sponsors of investigational drugs intended to treat IC/BPS should consider including the
152   following for subject enrollment criteria in clinical trials:
153
154         •   Subjects should report bladder pain and/or bladder discomfort and lower urinary tract
155             symptoms:
156
157             – Subjects should have at least 6 months’ duration of bladder pain and/or discomfort
158               symptom(s) before enrollment to exclude other disorders with similar presentations
159               that have a shorter time course.
160
161             – Subjects should have at least 6 months’ duration of one or more accompanying lower
162               urinary tract symptom(s), such as urinary frequency, urgency, or nocturia. This may be
163               intermittent or persistent.
164
165         •   The reported symptoms (bladder pain and/or bladder discomfort and lower urinary tract
166             symptoms) should be of sufficient severity (intensity or frequency) at baseline to allow
167             for measurement of a clinically meaningful improvement with the drug (i.e., a score
168             greater than the prespecified threshold or range of thresholds for clinically meaningful
169             within-patient change). Refer to section E, Selecting Potential PRO and Secondary
170             Endpoints for IC/BPS Clinical Trials, later in this guidance.
171
172         •   Subjects should have a cystoscopy at screening (if not obtained within the preceding 6
173             months) to exclude other conditions (e.g., transitional cell carcinoma and endometriosis,
174             etc.).
175
176         •   Subjects may have received prior treatment(s) for IC/BPS, including surgical procedures
177             such as fulguration. In general, FDA recommends including these subjects in the trials to
178             improve the generalizability of the results unless a compelling effectiveness or safety
179             reason exists for excluding them. During the protocol trial design phase, sponsors should
180             discuss with the Agency the extent to which such subjects should be included in the trials.
181
182         •   Subjects should undergo standard medical evaluation to exclude other conditions or
183             diseases that can cause similar symptoms, using information from medical history,
184             physical and pelvic examination findings, laboratory studies (e.g., negative urine bacterial
185             culture), and other previously performed procedures (e.g., gynecologic or prostate
186             examination, urodynamics, cystoscopy, laparoscopy, radiological studies).

188              B.       Clinical Trials Efficacy Endpoints
189
190   When developing efficacy endpoints for clinical trials, sponsors of investigational drugs intended
191   to treat IC/BPS should include both of the following as co-primary endpoints:
192
193          •   Improvement in bladder pain and/or bladder discomfort
194          •   Improvement in accompanying lower urinary tract symptom(s)
195
196   Sponsors are reminded that statistical improvement in these symptoms is not sufficient. Sponsors
197   should demonstrate that the changes reported are clinically meaningful to establish effectiveness
198   (refer to section E for more details about interpretation of PRO results).
199
200   If a drug is not expected to improve lower urinary tract symptoms 10 based on the mechanism of
201   action specific for IC/BPS, a single primary effectiveness endpoint related to symptoms of
202   bladder pain and/or bladder discomfort may be considered, but clinical meaningfulness should
203   still be demonstrated. However, lower urinary tract symptoms should still be captured as a
204   secondary endpoint as these symptoms should not be worsened by the drug.
205
206   Sponsors should discuss with the Agency early in the development program the endpoints,
207   analyses, and anchors 11 to ensure that study results are both clinically meaningful and
208   interpretable.
209
210              C.       Other Considerations
211
212   Sponsors of investigational drugs intended to treat IC/BPS should also consider the following for
213   trial design:
214
215          •   Treatment duration should be at least 6 months to adequately assess persistence of benefit
216              and capture intermittent symptomatic disease flares. For example, studies can be
217              designed with two treatment phases:
218
219              − Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled treatment phase of a minimum of 3
220                months or longer
221
222              − Extension treatment phase of a minimum of 3 months or longer following the initial
223                randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase. This extension phase could be
224                open-label, crossover, or active control.

255            D.      Hunner’s Lesions Subpopulation
256
257   Patients with Hunner’s lesions have been described in the literature as a “classic” IC patient
258   population, distinguishable from other IC/BPS populations. Hunner’s lesions have been
259   described as distinctive inflammatory bladder wall areas, found only at cystoscopy. 12 Patients
260   with Hunner’s lesions may be a subgroup of IC/BPS patients, and alternative endpoints, such as
261   healed lesion and regression of bladder inflammation, may be considered in designing clinical
262   trials in this subgroup. 13
263
264   FDA recommends that sponsors specify criteria for diagnosis and treatment benefits if patients
265   with documented Hunner’s lesions are included in studies. Sponsors may study patients with

291            E.     Selecting Potential PRO and Other Secondary Endpoints for IC/BPS Clinical
292                   Trials
293
294   IC/BPS is a symptomatic condition, so use of a PRO assessment is the most appropriate clinical
295   outcome assessment for evaluating changes in symptoms. A separate PRO assessment can also
296   be used to evaluate the impacts of IC/BPS symptoms on activities of daily living (e.g.,
297   functioning). Then these PRO assessments can be used to better assess impacts of the
298   investigational drugs on IC/BPS symptoms. It is critical that sponsors use fit-for-purpose PRO
299   instruments to assess IC/BPS symptoms (including lower urinary tract symptoms). 14 FDA
300   encourages sponsors to seek Agency input as early as possible and at important milestones
301   throughout the drug development process to ensure the inclusion of fit-for-purpose PRO
302   instruments in phase 3 trials.
