CHAPTER 9: SIGNAL MANAGEMENT
   9.1. Purpose
        Chapter 9 of the NAFDAC Good Pharmacovigilance Practice (GVP) Guideline provides the
        framework for the implementation of signal management activities in accordance with the legal
        requirements outlined in the Good Pharmacovigilance Practice Regulation, Section 15, as
        applicable to Marketing Authorization Holders (MAHs) and the Agency. It establishes the
        expectations for the systematic and proactive management of safety signals throughout the lifecycle
        of medicinal products authorized for human use in Nigeria.
        This chapter defines the roles and responsibilities and procedural aspects of the signal management
        process in Nigeria. The guidance is consistent with internationally recognised standards for signal
        management, including relevant ICH pharmacovigilance principles outlined in ICH E2E, and
        aligns with established best practices as described in EMA GVP Module IX. It is adapted to support
        effective implementation within the Nigerian regulatory framework.

   9.2. Scope and Applicability
   This chapter applies to:

      All medicinal products authorised for human use in Nigeria, including prescription medicines, over-
       the-counter medicines, biological products, vaccines, and other regulated therapeutic products,
       unless otherwise specified by the agency
      Marketing Authorization Holders (MAHs) and their local representatives, where applicable, with
       respect to their obligations for continuous signal detection, evaluation, documentation, and
       communication to the Agency.
      The Agency, in its role as the National Regulatory Authority, includes activities related to
       independent signal detection, validation, assessment, regulatory decision-making, and
       communication of safety information.
      The requirements outlined in this chapter are applicable throughout the lifecycle of a medicinal
       product, from the time of marketing authorisation and continuing for as long as the product remains
       on the market, unless otherwise determined by the Agency.

   This chapter should be implemented in conjunction with other relevant chapters of this GVP Guideline,
   particularly those relating to ICSR management, risk management systems, and safety communication.

   9.3. Definitions
           Signal: A signal is information arising from one or multiple sources, including observations
              and scientific investigations, that suggests a new potential causal association, or a new aspect
              of a known association, between a medicinal product and an event or set of related events,
              either adverse or beneficial. Signals may relate to:
                   All medicinal products containing the same active substance
                   Combination products
                   A specific product formulation
Effective Date: 16-07-2026                                                    Doc. Ref. No:PV-GDL-009-02
Review Date: 15-07-2031

                      A particular strength, indication, route of administration, or patient population.
                       In some circumstances, a signal may apply to an entire therapeutic class, while in
                       other situations it may be limited to a specific medicinal product.

               Signal Management Process: The signal management process refers to the structured set
                of pharmacovigilance activities undertaken to determine whether new risks are associated
                with a medicinal product or whether known risks have changed.

               Signal Detection: Signal detection is the process of systematically reviewing safety data
                from one or more sources to identify potential signals. Signal detection activities may be
                conducted by Marketing Authorisation Holders (MAHs) through their global
                pharmacovigilance systems and the Agency through national pharmacovigilance
                surveillance activities.

               Signal Validation: Signal validation is the process of evaluating the evidence supporting a
                detected signal to determine whether sufficient data exist to justify further investigation.

               Validated Signal: A validated signal is a signal for which the available documentation has
                been evaluated and determined to contain sufficient evidence suggesting a potential
                association that justifies further investigation and assessment. Validated signals may be
                subjected to structured regulatory review and further evaluation.

               Non-validated Signal: A non-validated signal is a signal for which the available evidence
                is considered insufficient at the time of evaluation to support further investigation. Non-
                validated signals may still be monitored over time as additional data become available.

               Confirmed Signal: A confirmed signal is a validated signal that has been formally accepted
                for structured regulatory assessment. Confirmed signals may be referred to expert bodies
                such as the NAFDAC National Drug Safety Advisory Committee (NNDSAC) for scientific
                review and recommendations on appropriate regulatory action.

               Signal Prioritisation: Signal prioritisation is the process of determining which signals
                require the most urgent attention and regulatory evaluation. Signals may be prioritised
                based on factors such as: Seriousness and clinical severity of the event; Impact on patients
                or public health; Strength and consistency of evidence; Preventability or potential for risk
                minimisation; Extent of population exposure to the product.
                Signals suggesting significant public health impact or major changes in the benefit-risk
                balance of a medicinal product should be prioritised for expedited evaluation.

               Signal Assessment: Signal assessment is the process of conducting a comprehensive
                scientific evaluation of a validated signal using all available evidence. The objective is to
                determine whether a causal relationship is likely and whether regulatory action is required.

               Refuted Signal: A refuted signal is a previously validated signal that, after comprehensive
                assessment, is determined not to represent a causal association between the medicinal
                product and the event based on available evidence. Refuted signals should continue to be
                monitored as new information becomes available.

                Emerging Safety Issue: An emerging safety issue is a safety concern that may require
                 urgent regulatory attention because of its potential major impact on the benefit-risk balance
                 of a medicinal product or on public health.
                Examples may include:
                     Unexpectedly high rates of serious or fatal adverse events identified in studies
                     New serious adverse reactions identified through spontaneous reports or literature
                     Safety concerns that may require restrictions of use, contraindications, or product
                        withdrawal
                     Significant safety-related regulatory actions taken by other regulatory authorities.
                Emerging safety issues should be promptly communicated to the Agency, and may require
                immediate regulatory evaluation and risk management actions.

             Closed Signal: A signal for which evaluation has been completed and regulatory
              conclusions reached.

   9.4. Governance of Signal Management
       9.4.1. Signal Management Structure and Coordination
           9.4.1.1.    Signal management in Nigeria is conducted through a coordinated framework
                  involving the Agency, Marketing Authorisation Holders (MAH), and the NAFDAC
                  National Drug Safety Advisory Committee (NNDSAC). MAHs are responsible for the
                  continuous monitoring of the safety of their authorised medicinal products and for
                  identifying and evaluating potential safety signals arising from all available data sources
                  throughout the product lifecycle. The Agency serves as the national regulatory authority
                  responsible for the detection, validation, prioritisation, and regulatory assessment of
                  safety signals using data from the national pharmacovigilance system and other relevant
                  sources.
           9.4.1.2.    Where appropriate, the Agency may refer validated and assessed signals to the
                  NNDSAC for independent scientific review and expert recommendations. Based on the
                  available evidence and expert advice, the Agency determines the appropriate regulatory
                  actions required to protect public health.

   9.5. Structure and processes
       9.5.1. Sources of Signals
   Signals may arise from a wide range of data sources including any scientific or clinical information
   concerning the use of a medicinal product and its associated outcomes. Signal detection activities shall
   therefore not be limited to spontaneous reports of suspected adverse reactions. MAHs and the Agency
Effective Date: 16-07-2026                                                     Doc. Ref. No:PV-GDL-009-02
Review Date: 15-07-2031

   shall ensure that signal detection activities systematically consider all relevant sources of safety
   information, as outlined in Chapter 5.4.2 Sources of Report
   Signal detection is frequently based on the periodic monitoring of databases containing reports of
   suspected adverse reactions. These databases may vary in scope and size and may include marketing
   authorisation holder global safety databases, national pharmacovigilance databases, and international
   pharmacovigilance databases such as those maintained under the WHO Programme for International
   Drug Monitoring (VigiBase).

      9.4.2. Signal Detection
           9.4.2.1.    Signal detection involves the systematic review of safety data to identify new
                  potential associations or new aspects of known associations between a medicinal product
                  and adverse events. Signal detection activities may include: Clinical review of individual
                  case safety reports; Statistical or disproportionality analyses where sufficient data exist;
                  Trend analysis over time; Review of case series or clusters of similar events; Monitoring
                  of scientific literature and emerging evidence

           9.4.2.2.    Detection activities shall be conducted in a structured and documented manner.
                  Criteria for prioritisation should consider factors such as: seriousness and severity of the
                  adverse event; potential impact on patients or public health; preventability of the event;
                  and strength and consistency of the available evidence.

           9.4.2.3.    Signal detection from Individual Case Safety Reports (ICSRs) may involve both
                  qualitative clinical review and, where applicable, quantitative data screening
                  methodologies. Clinical review should consider factors such as seriousness, clinical
                  pattern, temporal relationship between the medicinal product and the event, biological
                  plausibility, and recurrence of similar reports.
           9.4.2.4.    MAHs shall conduct routine signal detection using their global safety databases and
                  all relevant safety data available to them worldwide. Where an emerging signal is
                  identified that may affect products authorised in Nigeria, or that may have implications
                  for the Nigerian population, the MAH shall promptly notify the Agency and provide a
                  summary of the available evidence, including any proposed risk evaluation or risk
                  minimisation measures.
           9.4.2.5.    Where the volume of available data permits, statistical or disproportionality analyses
                  may be applied to support the identification of potential safety signals
           9.4.2.6.    While signal detection frequently originates from spontaneous reporting systems,
                  both the Agency and Marketing Authorisation Holders shall ensure that signal
                  management activities consider all relevant safety information. Reliance on a single data
                  source shall not be considered sufficient where additional data are reasonably available

       9.4.3. Signal Validation
                 Signal validation is the process of evaluating the available information supporting a
                 detected signal in order to determine whether the available evidence supports a new
                 potentially causal association or a new aspect of a known association between a medicinal
                 product and an adverse event, and therefore requires further in-depth assessment.
                 Validation should be conducted promptly after signal detection and documented as part
                 of the pharmacovigilance system.
                 Signal validation should be based on a structured review of the available evidence. When
                 validating a detected signal, the Agency and MAHs should consider the following
                 elements.

           9.4.3.1.   Previous Knowledge of the Association
                  Evaluation should determine whether the suspected association is already known or
                  previously assessed. The Agency or MAH should assess the extent to which the suspected
                  association is already known. This may include consideration of:
                       Whether the suspected adverse reaction is already described in the product
                           information (e.g., Summary of Product Characteristics or equivalent approved
                           Nigerian product information).
                       Whether the reaction has been identified for other medicinal products containing
                           the same active substance or belonging to the same pharmacological class.
                       Whether the association has previously been evaluated in regulatory procedures
                           such as:
                                the Risk Management Plan (RMP),
                                Periodic Safety Update Reports (PSURs), or
                                any other regulatory assessment procedure.
                       Whether the signal provides new information regarding a known adverse
                           reaction, such as changes in:
                                severity,
                                duration,
                                incidence,
                                clinical management.

           9.4.3.2.    Strength of the Initial Evidence
                  The available case reports or data should be reviewed to determine whether they provide
                  sufficient support for the signal. This may include:
                                The number of reported cases, after removal of duplicates.
                                The presence of supportive clinical features, such as:
                                      o plausible temporal relationship between drug exposure and event
                                      o positive dechallenge or rechallenge
                                      o absence of strong alternative explanations.
Effective Date: 16-07-2026                                                     Doc. Ref. No:PV-GDL-009-02
Review Date: 15-07-2031

                                 Consistency of clinical patterns across cases (e.g., similar time-to-onset
                                  or clinical presentation).
                                 Quality and completeness of the available case information.
                                 Presence of supporting signals from other sources, including other
                                  regulatory authorities.

           9.4.3.3.     Clinical Relevance
                   Initial validation should also consider the potential clinical importance of the suspected
                  reaction, including:
                                 seriousness or severity of the reaction
                                 outcome of the event
                                 potential impact on patient safety.

           9.4.3.4.   Decision
                  Following the review of available information, the signal should be classified as:
                       Validated signal where sufficient evidence exists to support further assessment
                         through signal assessment; or
                       Non-validated signal when available evidence does not support a causal
                         association at the current time.

           9.4.3.5.    Where validation supports further assessment, the signal shall be formally confirmed
                  and entered in the signal tracking system. Confirmation shall ensure structured
                  assessment, allocation of responsibilities, and defined timelines.
           9.4.3.6.    Validated signals should proceed to signal assessment where all available evidence
                  is assessed to determine whether the association represents a new risk or a change in a
                  known risk.
           9.4.3.7.    Both validated and non-validated signals, including the justification for the decision,
                  shall be documented as part of the signal management process.

       9.4.4. Signal Prioritisation and Public Health Impact
           9.4.4.1.   Throughout the signal management process, the Agency and MAHs shall consider
                  whether a detected or validated signal may represent a risk with significant implications
                  for patient safety, public health, or the overall benefit–risk balance of the medicinal
                  product.
           9.4.4.2.   Validated signals should therefore be prioritised for further assessment based on their
                  potential impact and urgency. In determining the level of priority for further signal
                  evaluation, the following factors should be considered.

           9.4.4.3.    The timeframe for further signal assessment should be determined based on the

                  communication measures before completion of the full signal assessment process.
           9.4.4.4.    Clinical judgement and scientific expertise should be applied throughout the signal
                  management process to ensure timely protection of public health.



       9.4.5. Signal Assessment
           9.4.5.1.    Signal assessment is the process of performing a comprehensive scientific evaluation
                  of a validated signal to determine whether a causal association between the medicinal
                  product and the event is likely, and whether regulatory or risk minimisation actions are
                  required. Signal assessment should consider all available sources of evidence, including
                  clinical, epidemiological, pharmacological, and regulatory information.
           9.4.5.2.    During signal assessment, the Agency and MAHs should conduct a structured
                  evaluation of the available evidence including:
               9.4.5.2.1.      Clinical Evidence from Case Reports: A detailed review of relevant
                        Individual Case Safety Reports (ICSRs) should be undertaken, considering:
                             clinical description and pattern of the reaction
                             time to onset after drug exposure
                             outcomes and reversibility
                             evidence of dechallenge or rechallenge
                             presence of alternative causes such as underlying disease or concomitant
                                medications
                             recurrence of similar cases across different populations.
Effective Date: 16-07-2026                                                     Doc. Ref. No:PV-GDL-009-02
Review Date: 15-07-2031


               9.4.5.2.2.     Biological and Pharmacological Plausibility: Assessment should examine
                        whether a credible biological mechanism may explain the association, including:
                            pharmacological properties of the medicinal product
                            known class effects
                            experimental or non-clinical evidence.

               9.4.5.2.3.     Epidemiological Evidence: Where available, additional analyses may be
                        undertaken to strengthen or refute the signal, including:
                            review of exposure data
                            epidemiological studies or pharmacoepidemiological analyses
                            data from clinical trials.

               9.4.5.2.4.      Evidence from External Sources: Additional information that may support
                        or refute the signal includes findings from the scientific literature; safety
                        information from other regulatory authorities; data from ongoing or completed
                        clinical studies; information from healthcare utilisation or observational databases.
               9.4.5.2.5.      Regulatory Considerations: In determining the regulatory significance of a
                        validated signal, consideration should be given to the seriousness and severity of the
                        reaction; frequency of occurrence relative to patient exposure; potential for
                        prevention or risk minimisation; impact on the overall benefit–risk balance of the
                        medicinal product; availability of alternative therapeutic options. Signals affecting
                        vulnerable populations, including children, pregnant women, older persons, or
                        patients with comorbidities, may warrant particular attention.
           9.4.5.3.    The outcome of signal assessment may include confirmation of a new or changed
                  safety risk; conclusion that the signal is refuted or unsupported; recommendation for
                  additional data collection or monitoring.
           9.4.5.4.    Where a new or changed risk is confirmed, the Agency may consider regulatory
                  actions such as updates to product information; additional risk minimisation measures;
                  post-authorisation safety studies; safety communications to healthcare professionals and
                  the public.
                9.4.5.5.       Validated signals requiring regulatory decision-making may be referred to
                        the NNDSAC for expert review and recommendations to the Agency

       9.4.6. Signal Detection and Notification by Marketing Authorisation Holders
           9.4.6.1.    Marketing Authorisation Holders are responsible for the continuous monitoring of
                  safety data related to their authorised medicinal products to identify potential safety
                  signals. This monitoring should include review and analysis of data from all relevant
                  sources available to the MAH.
Effective Date: 16-07-2026                                                    Doc. Ref. No:PV-GDL-009-02
Review Date: 15-07-2031

           9.4.6.2.    Where an MAH detects a potential signal during routine pharmacovigilance
                  activities, the MAH shall conduct signal validation to determine whether the information
                  suggests a new or changed risk associated with the medicinal product.
           9.4.6.3.    Signal validation should include a comprehensive review of available data sources,
                  which may include Individual Case Safety Reports available to the MAH; Data from the
                  MAH’s internal safety database; Scientific and medical literature; Clinical trial data;
                  Post-authorisation studies; Relevant information from international pharmacovigilance
                  databases or regulatory communications.
           9.4.6.4.    Where a signal is validated, the MAH should evaluate whether the safety issue
                  represents a refuted signal; a new or changed risk associated with the product; or a
                  potential safety issue that requires further evaluation by the regulatory authority.
           9.4.6.5.    Where the MAH concludes that a validated signal represents a new or changed risk,
                  or where the available data are insufficient to confirm or refute the signal, the MAH shall
                  promptly inform the Agency and provide the relevant supporting information for
                  regulatory assessment.
           9.4.6.6.    If the signal suggests that the safety profile of the medicinal product may require
                  modification, the MAH may be required to submit an application to update the product
                  information or implement additional pharmacovigilance or risk minimisation measures
                  as determined by the Agency.
           9.4.6.7.    Signals that are refuted following the MAH’s internal evaluation should be
                  documented and may be reported within the relevant periodic safety reports submitted to
                  the Agency.
           9.4.6.8.    All signal evaluations conducted by the MAH should be adequately documented and
                  made available to the Agency upon request.

         9.4.7.         Review by the NAFDAC National Drug Safety Advisory Committee
           9.4.7.1.    Where a signal has undergone validation and/or assessment and is considered to have
                  potential for regulatory action, the Agency may present the signal to the NNDSAC for
                  independent scientific review and expert recommendations.
           9.4.7.2.    The Committee shall evaluate the available evidence, consider the public health
                  implications, and provide recommendations to the Agency regarding appropriate
                  regulatory actions or risk minimisation measures.
           9.4.7.3.    Consultation with the Committee may occur during or after signal
                  Validation/assessment, depending on the urgency and complexity of the safety concern.
                  For urgent signals, an extraordinary NNDSAC session may be convened within 30
                  calendar days.
           9.4.7.4.    The Agency shall document signal summaries submitted to NNDSAC; Committee
                  deliberations, recommendations made and final regulatory decisions.
Effective Date: 16-07-2026                                                     Doc. Ref. No:PV-GDL-009-02
Review Date: 15-07-2031

           9.4.7.5.    Recommendations of the NNDSAC may include one or a combination of the
                  following actions, depending on the nature, strength, and public health relevance of the
                  signal:
               9.4.7.5.1.       Request for additional data: the MAH may be requested to provide additional
                        data or analyses to support further assessment of the signal within a defined
                        timeline.
               9.4.7.5.2.       Submission or review of data through periodic safety reporting: The MAH
                        may be requested to provide a comprehensive review of the signal in the next
                        Periodic Safety Update Report (PSUR) or to submit an ad-hoc PSUR where
                        necessary.
               9.4.7.5.3.       Update of product information: The MAH may be required to update the
                        product information (including the Summary of Product Characteristics, Patient
                        Information Leaflet, or labeling) through an appropriate regulatory variation to
                        reflect new safety information.
               9.4.7.5.4.       Submission or update of a Risk Management Plan (RMP): The MAH may be
                        requested to submit a Risk Management Plan or update an existing RMP to include
                        additional pharmacovigilance activities or risk minimisation measures related to the
                        identified signal.
               9.4.7.5.5.       Implementation of additional risk minimisation measures: The MAH may be
                        required to implement risk minimisation measures, such as educational materials
                        for healthcare professionals and patients, or the dissemination of a Direct Healthcare
                        Professional Communication (DHPC), where necessary to mitigate identified risks.
               9.4.7.5.6.       Post-authorisation safety studies: The MAH may be required to conduct a
                        post-authorisation safety study (PASS) or other pharmacoepidemiological study in
                        accordance with an agreed protocol and submit the results within a defined timeline.
               9.4.7.5.7.       Regulatory review or referral procedures: Where the signal raises significant
                        safety concerns, the Agency may initiate further regulatory review procedures or
                        refer the matter to appropriate national regulatory mechanisms for comprehensive
                        evaluation.
               9.4.7.5.8.       Collection of additional information: NAFDAC may undertake further data
                        collection activities, including targeted follow-up of individual case safety reports
                        (ICSRs), analysis of national or international pharmacovigilance databases, or
                        engagement with healthcare institutions to obtain additional information relevant to
                        the signal.
               9.4.7.5.9.       Consultation with experts: The agency may seek advice from external
                        experts, technical working groups, or relevant national committees where
                        specialised expertise is required.
               9.4.7.5.10.      Pharmacovigilance inspections: Where concerns relate to compliance with

                        verify adherence to pharmacovigilance requirements.

       9.4.8. Communication
           9.4.8.1.    Following the assessment of a validated signal and the issuance of recommendations
                  by the NNDSAC, the Agency shall determine the appropriate communication strategy
                  based on the level of risk identified, the urgency of the safety concern, and the population
                  potentially affected. The level and urgency of communication shall be proportionate to
                  the potential impact of the safety issue. Communication will generally follow the
                  principles outlined in Good Pharmacovigilance Practice guidelines chapter 4: Safety
                  Communication
           9.4.8.2.    Depending on the nature of the signal and the recommended regulatory action, the
                  agency may communicate signal outcomes through one or more of the following
                  mechanisms. In certain cases, the MAH may be required to support or implement specific
                  communication activities under the oversight and approval of the Agency
               9.4.8.2.1.       Direct Healthcare Professional Communications (DHPCs): Where urgent
                        communication to healthcare professionals is necessary to mitigate risks associated
                        with a medicinal product.
               9.4.8.2.2.       Public Alerts: These communications may be issued directly by the Agency
                        where immediate regulatory notification is required. The Agency may issue public
                        alerts to inform healthcare professionals, public health programmes, and the general
                        public of emerging safety concerns.
               9.4.8.2.3.       Updates to Product Information: Where signal assessment results in changes
                        to the safety profile of a medicinal product, the MAH may be required to submit an
                        application to update the product information, including the Summary of Product
                        Characteristics, Patient Information Leaflet, and product labelling. Following
                        approval by the Agency, the MAH shall ensure that the updated product information
                        is implemented and distributed appropriately.
               9.4.8.2.4.       Educational and Risk Minimisation Materials: Where additional risk
                        minimisation measures are required, the MAH may be required to develop and
                        disseminate educational materials or other communication tools aimed at healthcare
                        professionals and patients. Such materials shall be subject to prior review and
                        approval by the Agency.

       9.4.9. Signal Closure
           9.4.9.1.    A signal may be considered closed once the evaluation process has been completed
                  and an appropriate regulatory conclusion has been reached. Signal closure occurs when
                  the available evidence does not support a causal association between the medicinal
                  product and the reported adverse event, or when the signal has resulted in appropriate
                  regulatory action and the necessary risk management measures have been implemented.
           9.4.9.2.    Closure of a signal should be formally documented to ensure transparency and
                  traceability of the decision-making process. The documentation should include a
                  summary of the scientific assessment conducted, any recommendations provided by the
                  NNDSAC, the final regulatory decision taken by the Agency and details of any
                  communication or risk minimisation measures implemented. The rationale for closing
                  the signal should also be recorded in the signal tracking system to allow future reference
                  and regulatory review where necessary.

   9.5. Timelines for signal management activities
           The Agency and Marketing Authorisation Holders (MAHs) shall ensure that signal
           management activities are conducted within appropriate timelines that are proportionate to the
           level of potential public health risk.

       9.5.2. Frequency of Safety Data Monitoring
           9.5.2.1.    The Agency and MAHs shall ensure the continuous monitoring of safety data
                  sources, including spontaneous reports and other pharmacovigilance data.
           9.5.2.2.    The frequency of monitoring should be proportionate to the known or potential risk
                  profile of the medicinal product and should take into account factors such as:
                       Time since first marketing authorisation

                            Extent of patient exposure
                            Important identified or potential risks
                            Missing safety information
                            Frequency of periodic safety reporting
                            Volume of ICSRs received
                            Safety concerns identified during special programmes (e.g. vaccination
                             campaigns)

           9.5.2.3.   As a general principle, Safety data monitoring should be conducted at least every (6)
                  months for authorised products.
           9.5.2.4.   More frequent monitoring, including monthly or quarterly is required for:
                      Newly authorised medicinal products
                      Products under additional safety monitoring
                      Products associated with emerging safety concerns
                      Products used in large public health programmes
                      the seriousness of potential adverse reactions

           9.5.2.5.    MAHs and the Agency should determine the appropriate monitoring frequency for
                  each medicinal product or active substance based on a risk-based approach, taking into
                  account the factors listed above
           9.5.2.6.     The selected monitoring frequency, including any subsequent modifications and the
                  justification for such changes, should be clearly documented within the organisation’s
                  pharmacovigilance procedures and maintained as part of the signal management
                  documentation.

   9.6. Urgent Safety Signals
        Where available information suggests an emerging safety issue that may significantly affect the
        benefit–risk balance of a medicinal product, the MAH shall notify the Agency without delay.
        Urgent signals may arise from:
                Unexpected fatal or life-threatening adverse reactions
                Rapidly increasing reporting trends
                Safety findings from ongoing studies
                Major regulatory actions taken by other regulatory authorities.
        In such cases, the Agency may initiate immediate risk evaluation and regulatory measures,
        including safety communications to healthcare professionals and the public.

   9.7. Documentation and Quality Requirements for Signal Management
       9.7.2. Signal management is a critical pharmacovigilance process and should be conducted within
            a structured quality management system to ensure that activities are performed consistently,
            transparently, and effectively.
       9.7.3. All organisations involved in signal management, including the Agency and MAHs, should
            ensure that signal management processes are clearly documented. Documentation should
            describe the procedures, roles, responsibilities, and tasks associated with signal detection,
            validation, prioritisation, assessment, and regulatory decision-making.
       9.7.4. Appropriate standard operating procedures (SOPs) should be developed, implemented, and
            maintained to support signal management activities. These procedures should include the
            methodology used for signal detection, the frequency of signal review activities, and the
            criteria used for validation, prioritisation, and escalation of signals.
Effective Date: 16-07-2026                                                   Doc. Ref. No:PV-GDL-009-02
Review Date: 15-07-2031

       9.7.5. Organisations should maintain a tracking system that provides a clear audit trail of signal
            management activities. This system should ensure traceability of all steps in the signal
            management process, including signal detection, validation, assessment, decision-making, and
            regulatory actions taken. Records should include relevant dates, documentation of analyses
            conducted, decisions made, and the rationale for those decisions in order to demonstrate
            compliance with established timelines and procedures.
       9.7.6. Roles and responsibilities for signal management activities, including documentation,
            quality control, and review processes, should be clearly defined and assigned within the
            organisation. Mechanisms should also be in place to ensure the implementation of corrective
            and preventive actions where deficiencies in the signal management process are identified.
       9.7.7. Staff involved in signal management activities should possess the necessary qualifications
            and expertise and should receive appropriate training relevant to their roles and
            responsibilities. Training records should be maintained and periodically reviewed to ensure
            that personnel remain competent to perform signal management tasks.
       9.7.8. MAHs should document their signal management processes as part of their
            pharmacovigilance system documentation and ensure that records relating to signal detection,
            validation, evaluation, and regulatory communication are maintained and readily retrievable.
            MAHs should implement a record management system that ensures traceability of actions
            taken to investigate safety concerns, including documentation of the timelines, analyses
            performed, and decisions reached.
       9.7.9. Signal management systems should be subject to periodic review and audit to ensure that
            they are functioning effectively and in compliance with applicable pharmacovigilance
            requirements. This may include internal audits conducted by MAHs as well as inspections
            conducted by the Agency.
       9.7.10. Appropriate measures should also be implemented to ensure the confidentiality, security,
            and integrity of data and documents used in signal management activities. This includes
            ensuring that safety data are protected from unauthorized access and that data integrity is
            maintained when information is transferred or shared between organisations.
