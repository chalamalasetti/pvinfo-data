PURPOSE

    •   This MAPP describes the collaborative review (defined below) between the
        Office of Nonprescription Drugs (ONPD) and the specific subject matter review
        divisions (SSMRDs) in the Office of New Drugs (OND) and other Center for
        Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER) offices relating to the review of pre-
        investigational new drug applications (PINDs), investigational new drug
        applications (INDs), new drug applications (NDAs), and supplemental NDAs
        (sNDAs) for nonprescription drugs, and submissions to FDA under section 505G
        of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act) for over-the-counter
        (OTC) monograph drugs.

    •   This MAPP also describes the roles and responsibilities of review team members
        and the sign-off policies and procedures for nonprescription drugs and OTC
        monograph drugs.

    •   The procedures in this MAPP are intended to ensure quality and consistency in
        the reviews for nonprescription NDA and OTC monograph drugs.

BACKGROUND

    •    ONPD in OND has primary responsibility for overseeing the development, review,
         and regulation of nonprescription drug products marketed under an approved NDA
         or marketed without an approved drug application under section 505G of the
         FD&C Act (i.e., OTC monograph drugs).

    •    The review divisions in ONPD have oversight of nonprescription drug products in
         specific therapeutic areas.

     •   The ONPD review division may identify that additional therapeutic and/or
         subject matter expertise is needed to review data or information to support a
         nonprescription application or OTC monograph order. This scientific input is
         received through a collaborative process with one or more OND prescription
         clinical offices/review divisions and/or other CDER offices.

    •    All submissions to FDA under section 505G are referred to as “OTC monograph
         submissions.” Examples of OTC monograph submissions that may require
         collaborative review include, but are not limited to, the following:

         ‒   OTC monograph order requests (OMORs)1

         ‒   Public comments to a proposed administrative order (issued either on FDA’s
             initiative or at the request of one or more requestors) 2,3 or interim final
             administrative order 4

         ‒   Formal meeting requests and meeting packages 5

         ‒   Formal dispute resolution requests related to a final administrative order 6

POLICY

    •    The ONPD, Division of Nonprescription Drugs (DNPD) staff lead the review of
         PINDs, INDs, NDAs, and sNDAs for nonprescription drug products (hereafter
         collectively referred to as applications) and OTC monograph review activities.

    •    When additional subject matter expertise is needed, the appropriate ONPD
         division will obtain input from specific subject matter review divisions
         (SSMRDs), including but not limited to the relevant Office of New Drugs (OND)
         Clinical Offices; Office of Surveillance and Epidemiology (OSE); Office of
         Pharmaceutical Quality (OPQ); and the Office of Translational Sciences
         (OTS)/Office of Biostatistics (OB) and Office of Clinical Pharmacology (OCP)
         (collectively referred to as the review team).

    •    Attachment 1 provides a general representation of the primary review
         responsibilities between ONPD and the SSMRDs for various subject areas.

     •   In general, most outreach from ONPD to SSMRDs is handled under the
         collaborative review process. The collaborative review process is not the same as
         a consultative review process. A formal consult will not be issued for
         collaborative reviews. 9

Division of Regulatory Operations-Nonprescription Drugs (DRO-NPD) Regulatory

     •   Determines, along with the DNPD clinical reviewer, DNPD clinical team leader

     •   Participate in formal meetings with the sponsor, applicant, or requestor, as

     •   Drafts, coordinates clearance of, and issues the appropriate correspondence and
         action letters to sponsors, applicants, and requestors.

     •   Coordinates clearance of administrative orders.

         needed.

     •   Provides summary decisional review or concurs with cross-disciplinary team
         leader (CDTL) or integrated review.

     •   Signs correspondences for PIND and IND applications.

PROCEDURES

     1. Collaborative Review

         a. Through all stages of drug product development, all review team members
            will proactively collaborate and provide their opinions on matters related to
            their expertise, as well as the suitability and appropriateness of the drug
            product for nonprescription use.

         b. The DNPD and SSMRD reviewers draft their assigned section (if the review
            is integrated) or draft a separate review.

         c. The DNPD and SSMRD reviewers obtain clearance through their
            management and share the review with the DRO-NPD RPM by the agreed
            upon due date.

    2. Advisory Committee Meetings

    Nonprescription drug applications and OTC monograph orders may be considered for
    review by an FDA advisory committee panel that includes the full Nonprescription
    Drugs Advisory Committee (NDAC). Depending on the subject matter, the review
    team may choose to include a full committee from the representative SSMRD or
    certain members of those committees who can complement the discussion with their
    particular subject matter expertise. The meeting will be chaired by the NDAC
    chairperson.

        a. Members of the review team (ONPD and SSMRD) will collaborate in
           producing the AC backgrounder and slides, as well as participate in AC
           meetings as needed.

    3. Postapproval Oversight of Nonprescription Drug Products Marketed Under
       an Approved NDA

        a. Drug products approved under a nonprescription NDA will remain the
           responsibility of ONPD.

        b. Efficacy supplements, labeling supplements, and chemistry, manufacturing,
           and controls (CMC) supplements with labeling for nonprescription drug
           products approved under an NDA will remain the responsibility of ONPD.

        c. CMC supplements for nonprescription drug products approved under an NDA
           will remain the responsibility of OPQ.

        d. If an applicant requests additional nonprescription claims or variations on the
           original claim for a nonprescription drug approved under an NDA/sNDA, the
           review of such requests will be conducted by ONPD, in collaboration with the
           appropriate SSMRDs. The review will proceed as outlined in the Procedures
           section of this MAPP.

        e. ONPD will review postmarketing periodic safety reports and evaluate the
           need for revisions to the nonprescription drug product labeling.

        f. Whenever feasible, the same SSMRD reviewers and ONPD reviewers who
           served on the initial review team should be assigned to review postapproval
           supplements.

    4. Resolution of Differing Professional Opinions

        a. If there are disagreements between review team members, the disagreements
           will be resolved as described in MAPP 4151.8 Equal Voice: Collaboration
           and Regulatory Policy Decision-Making in CDER or as described in FDA
           Staff Manual Guides (SMG) 9010.1, 9010.2, and 9010.3.

DEFINITIONS

    •   Administrative Order: Under the process set forth in section 505G(b) of the
        FD&C Act, FDA has the authority to issue an administrative order (proposed,
        interim final, or final) that adds, removes, or changes generally recognized as safe
        and effective (GRASE) conditions for an OTC drug monograph.

    •   Collaborative Review: One or more divisions other than the lead
        nonprescription division contribute resources to an application or OTC
        monograph review team to review a specific portion of the submission (e.g.,
        chemistry, manufacturing, and controls; statistics; clinical pharmacology; clinical;
        human factors).

    •   Consultative Review: One or more CDER offices or Centers (e.g., CDRH)
        within FDA that require a formal consult process (e.g., Intercenter Consult
        Request (ICCR)) for expertise that resides in another office or center.

    •   Consumer Studies: Studies to evaluate how consumers will use the drug product
        in a nonprescription setting without the supervision of a health care provider.
        Consumer behavior studies may include: label comprehension, self-selection,
        actual use, and human factors studies.

    •   Direct-to-Nonprescription: Nonprescription marketing of a drug product that
        was never previously marketed as a prescription drug product.

    •   First-in-Class Nonprescription Drug Product: A proposed drug product that,
        if approved, would be the first in its pharmacological class to be marketed without

        legally marketed nonprescription drug product.

    •   Human Factors Study: A study conducted to assess the adequacy of the
        nonprescription combination product user interface design to eliminate or mitigate
        potential use-related hazards.

    •   New Molecular Entity: A new molecular entity is an active ingredient that
        contains no active moiety that has been previously approved by the FDA in an
        application submitted under section 505 of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic
        Act or has been previously marketed as a drug in the United States.

    •   Actual Use Study: A clinical trial to evaluate the use of the drug product under
        “real world” conditions to identify any issues with use that have not previously
        been recognized, and to confirm that consumers can self-diagnose and treat
        themselves appropriately without the intervention of a health care professional.

    •   Nonprescription Drug Product: A drug product approved for marketing which
        can be used safely and effectively by consumers without the supervision of a
        health care professional and does not meet the criteria for prescription-only
        dispensing.

    •   Label Comprehension Study: A study to evaluate the extent to which
        consumers understand the information on the proposed nonprescription drug
        product labeling. In such studies, no drug product is dispensed to subjects. Such
        a study is not a clinical investigation as defined under 21 CFR 312.3(b).

    •   OTC Monograph Drug: A nonprescription drug without an approved drug
        application which is governed by the provision of section 505G of the FD&C Act.
        An OTC drug monograph establishes conditions, such as active ingredients, uses
        (indications), doses, routes of administration, labeling, and testing, under which
        an OTC drug in a given therapeutic category (e.g., sunscreen, antacid) is generally
        recognized as safe and effective (GRASE) for its intended use.

    •   Prescription Drug Product: A drug product approved for marketing that can be
        obtained only with a prescription from an appropriately licensed health care
        professional.

    •   Prescription-to-Nonprescription Switch: A change in marketing status through
        the NDA process from a prescription drug product to a nonprescription drug
        product. There are two types of prescription-to-nonprescription switches: full or
        partial. A full switch, via submission of an efficacy supplement, is where the drug
        product switches all conditions of use in their entirety (e.g., indications,
        strengths). Upon approval, the drug will only be available as a nonprescription
        drug. A partial switch, via submission of an NDA, is where the drug product

        permit the simultaneous marketing of the same active ingredient in prescription

    •   Review Team: Typically includes ONPD/DNPD and SSMRD reviewers from
        clinical, biostatistics, clinical pharmacology, pharmacology/toxicology, and
        product quality; team leaders, cross-discipline team leads, ORO staff, and the
        office/division director(s) (or designees). Additional roles or disciplines may be
        included as needed, based on the nature or content of the application.

    •   Self-Selection Study: A study to assess the ability of consumers to apply drug
        labeling information to their personal health situation to make correct decisions
        about whether or not it is appropriate for them to use a drug product.

    •   Specific Subject Matter Review Division (SSMRD): The CDER office or
        OND prescription clinical review division with primary oversight of a group of
        prescription drug products directed at physiologically categorized diseases (e.g.,
        cardiovascular drug products).
