15   I.      INTRODUCTION
16
17   This guidance is intended to serve as a foundational guidance to assist applicants in determining
18   which one of the abbreviated approval pathways under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic
19   Act (FD&C Act) is appropriate for the submission of a marketing application to FDA. Many
20   potential drug product developers are not familiar with the different abbreviated approval
21   pathways for drug products under the FD&C Act described in sections 505(j) and 505(b)(2) of
22   the FD&C Act (21 U.S.C. 355(j) and 21 U.S.C. 355(b)(2), respectively) or the types of data and
23   information that are permitted to support approval under those pathways. In order to familiarize
24   potential drug product developers with these abbreviated pathways, this guidance highlights
25   criteria for submitting applications under the abbreviated approval pathways described in
26   sections 505(j) and 505(b)(2), identifies considerations to help potential applicants determine
27   whether an application would be more appropriately submitted under section 505(j) or pursuant
28   to section 505(b)(2) of the FD&C Act, and provides direction to potential applicants on
29   requesting assistance from FDA in making this determination.
30
31   This guidance revises the guidance for industry Determining Whether to Submit an ANDA or a
32   505(b)(2) Application issued in May 2019. When final, this guidance will replace the May 2019
33   guidance. Changes from the 2019 version include providing additional information on
34   duplicates and eligibility for approval under section 505(j) of the FD&C Act, as well as other
35   updates that are intended to clarify FDA’s recommendations to industry.
36
37   In general, FDA’s guidance documents do not establish legally enforceable responsibilities.
38   Instead, guidances describe the Agency’s current thinking on a topic and should be viewed only
39   as recommendations, unless specific regulatory or statutory requirements are cited. The use of
40   the word should in Agency guidances means that something is suggested or recommended, but
41   not required.

42   II.      BACKGROUND
43
44   The Drug Price Competition and Patent Term Restoration Act of 1984 (Public Law 98-417)
45   (Hatch-Waxman Amendments) added sections 505(b)(2) and 505(j) to the FD&C Act, which
46   describes abbreviated approval pathways under the FD&C Act for drug products regulated by the
47   Agency. The Hatch-Waxman Amendments reflect Congress’s efforts to balance the need to
48   “make available more low cost generic drugs by establishing a generic drug approval procedure”
49   with new incentives for drug development in the form of exclusivities and patent term
50   extensions. 2 With the passage of the Hatch-Waxman Amendments, the FD&C Act describes
51   different routes for obtaining approval of two broad categories of drug applications: new drug
52   applications (NDAs) and abbreviated new drug applications (ANDAs). 3
53
54   NDAs and ANDAs can be divided into the following four categories:
55
56         (1) A stand-alone NDA is an application submitted under section 505(b)(1) and approved
57             under section 505(c) of the FD&C Act that contains full reports of investigations of
58             safety and effectiveness that were conducted by or for the applicant or for which the
59             applicant has a right of reference or use. 4
60
61         (2) A 505(b)(2) application is an NDA submitted under section 505(b)(1) and approved
62             under section 505(c) of the FD&C Act that contains full reports of investigations of
63             safety and effectiveness, where at least some of the information required for approval
64             comes from studies not conducted by or for the applicant and for which the applicant has
65             not obtained a right of reference or use. 5
66
67         (3) An ANDA is an application submitted and approved under section 505(j) of the FD&C
68             Act for a drug product that is a duplicate 6 of a previously approved drug product. An
69             ANDA relies on FDA’s finding that the previously approved drug product, i.e., the
70             reference listed drug (RLD), 7 is safe and effective. An ANDA generally must contain

103   III.    ABBREVIATED APPROVAL PATHWAYS
104
105           A.       ANDAs 13, 14
106
107   As discussed in section II of this guidance, section 505(j) of the FD&C Act, together with its
108   implementing regulations, generally requires that an ANDA demonstrate that the proposed
109   generic drug product and the applicable RLD are the same with respect to their active
110   ingredient(s), dosage form, route of administration, strength, conditions of use, and labeling
111   (with certain exceptions). 15 An ANDA must also include sufficient information (1) to
112   demonstrate that the proposed drug product is bioequivalent to the RLD 16 and (2) to ensure the
113   drug’s identity, strength, quality, and purity. Consistent with any statutory provisions related to
114   the exclusivity of and patents listed for the RLD in FDA’s Approved Drug Products With
115   Therapeutic Equivalence Evaluations (the Orange Book), FDA must approve an ANDA unless
116   there is insufficient evidence that these requirements are met. 17 An ANDA relies on the
117   Agency’s finding of safety and effectiveness for an RLD and, as a result, that ANDA may be
118   approved without submission of the same type and extent of information as is required for an
119   NDA to establish the safety and effectiveness of the proposed drug product. 18
120
121   Also, as discussed in section II above, an ANDA may contain certain types of differences from
122   an RLD (e.g., a change approved in response to a suitability petition or other permissible
123   differences, such as certain differences in inactive ingredients, labeling, or container closure
124   systems), as long as clinical investigations are not necessary to establish the safety or
125   effectiveness of the drug product proposed in the ANDA.

158           A.       Regulatory Considerations for ANDAs and 505(b)(2) Applications
159
160           1.       Duplicates
161
162   FDA generally will refuse to file a 505(b)(2) application for a drug product that is a duplicate of
163   a listed drug and eligible for approval under section 505(j) of the FD&C Act. 24, 25
164
165   If FDA approves a drug product under section 505(c) of the FD&C Act before a 505(b)(2)
166   application is submitted for a proposed drug product, such that the proposed drug product would
167   be a duplicate of that previously approved drug product and eligible for approval under section
168   505(j) of the FD&C Act, FDA generally will refuse to file the application as a 505(b)(2)
169   application. However, if FDA approves a duplicate drug product after a 505(b)(2) application is
170   submitted but before the 505(b)(2) application is approved, that application would remain
171   eligible for approval as a 505(b)(2) application, and FDA would not require the applicant of the
172   pending 505(b)(2) application to withdraw the application and submit an ANDA.
173
174   When comparative in vivo and/or in vitro studies to demonstrate bioequivalence for a particular
175   drug product, that would otherwise be expected, 26 cannot be conducted in support of an ANDA
176   for a duplicate of a listed drug because (1) the RLD and reference standard 27 are listed in the
177   Orange Book’s Discontinued Drug Product List (commonly referred to as the Discontinued
178   Section) and (2) there is no other drug product listed in the Orange Book’s Prescription Drug
179   Product List (commonly referred to as the Active Section) that can be designated as a reference
180   standard for that RLD, it might be appropriate for a prospective applicant to consider whether
181   alternate methods to establish bioequivalence are adequate for the proposed drug product to be
182   eligible for approval under section 505(j) of the FD&C Act. In such circumstances, FDA
183   recommends that a prospective applicant contact the Office of Generic Drugs (OGD) prior to
184   submission of the application for the proposed drug product to discuss alternate methods of
185   establishing bioequivalence. See section V of this guidance for information about requesting
186   assistance from OGD.

228           3.       Bundling
229
230   In some circumstances, an applicant may seek approval for multiple drug products containing the
231   same active ingredient(s) when some of these drug products would qualify for approval under the
232   section 505(j) pathway and some would qualify for approval under the 505(b)(2) pathway. In
233   these circumstances, FDA has permitted an applicant to submit a single 505(b)(2) application for
234   all such multiple drug products that are permitted to be bundled in a single NDA. 39 For example,
235   an applicant seeking approval for multiple strengths of the same dosage form of a drug product,
236   only some of which are duplicates of previously approved drug products included in the Orange
237   Book as listed drugs, would not have to submit both an ANDA for the strengths listed in the
238   Orange Book and a 505(b)(2) application for the new strengths; instead, the applicant may
239   submit one 505(b)(2) application for all of the proposed strengths.
