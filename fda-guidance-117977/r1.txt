II.        BACKGROUND



HBV is an enveloped DNA virus belonging to the Hepadnavirus family. The highly stable covalently
closed circular viral DNA (cccDNA) func ons as a nonreplica ve minichromosome and persists
throughout the lifespan of infected hepatocytes. The cccDNA is not eliminated by currently approved
therapies that include drugs from the nucleoside/nucleo de reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NrtI) class,
and pegylated interferon.



Chronic HBV (CHB) infec on results in progressive liver disease ranging from asymptoma c to severe
disease with complica ons including cirrhosis, liver failure, and the development of hepatocellular

percent; and among those with cirrhosis, the 5-year cumula ve risk of hepa c decompensa on is 20
percent, and risk of HCC is 2 to 5 percent (Terrault et al. 2016). An eﬀec ve vaccine and an viral
therapies are approved for the preven on of HBV infec on and treatment of CHB, respec vely.



Currently available therapies achieve sustained suppression of HBV DNA while on treatment, but rates of
HBV surface an gen (HBsAg) loss with or without seroconversion to an -HBsAg (HBsAb) remain low.
Sustained HBV DNA suppression is associated with serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) normaliza on
and improvement in liver histology including regression of hepa c fibrosis and cirrhosis (Chang et al.
2010; Marcellin et al. 2013; Bu et al. 2015). Eﬀec ve an viral therapy for CHB reduces disease-related
complica ons, such as hepa c decompensa on and liver failure, and decreases risk of HCC (Lok et al.
2016; Papatheodoridis et al. 2017). Clearance of HBsAg is associated with reduced risk of hepa c
