       Purpose

       A. This Standard Operating Policy and Procedure (SOPP) serves as a guide for
          Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research (CBER) staff to define and
          develop Postmarketing Requirements (PMRs) and Postmarketing
          Commitments (PMCs) for drug and biological products.

       B. The SOPP provides procedures to discuss PMRs/PMCs with the applicant
          prior to the action goal date; document the rationale/justification for a
          PMR/PMC; ensure that the PMR/PMC is clearly written and is developed in
          accordance with Good Review Management Principles and Practices
                                                                                                                  Page 1 of 22
Center for Biologics Evaluation and Reseach                                    SOPP 8415


           (GRMP) recommendations.

       Scope

       A. This SOPP covers PMRs and PMCs issued to CBER-regulated human drugs
          approved with New Drug Applications (NDAs) and Biologics License
          Applications (BLAs) as well as supplements to a NDA (sNDA) or BLA (BLS).
          NOTE: Abbreviated New Drug Applications (ANDAs) managed in CBER
          could have a PMR/PMC. General principles of this SOPP would apply in
          those instances.

       B. This SOPP also covers PMCs agreed upon for devices approved under a
          BLA/BLS that meet the definition of a device under Section 201(h) of the
          Food, Drug, and Cosmetic (FD&C) Act.

       C. This SOPP applies to the development of:

           •   PMRs related to the Animal Efficacy Rule, accelerated approval, and
               safety studies or clinical trials required under the Food and Drug
               Administration Amendments Act (FDAAA);

           •   PMRs for deferred pediatric studies under the Pediatric Research Equity
               Act (PREA)

           •   Agreed-upon, reportable 506B PMCs; and

           •   Agreed-upon non-506B commitments (i.e., PMC for Chemistry,
               Manufacturing, and Controls (CMC) and certain product stability studies).

       D. This SOPP does not:

           •   Describe the criteria for determining whether a postmarketing study or
               clinical trial should be a PMR or a PMC;

           •   Describe the criteria for determining non-compliance with the conduct of
               PMRs or the required reporting for PMRs/PMCs;

           •   Include procedures and responsibilities for PMR/PMC tracking and review
               (i.e., after a PMR/PMC is issued/agreed upon). Those topics are
               addressed in SOPP 8413: Postmarketing Requirement/Commitment
               Related Submissions – Administrative Handling, Review, and CBER
               Reporting.

       Background

                                                                               Page 2 of 22
Center for Biologics Evaluation and Reseach                                      SOPP 8415


       A. In the past, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) used the term
          “postmarketing study commitments (PMCs)” to refer to studies or clinical trials
          conducted by the applicant after FDA approved or licensed a product for
          marketing. These studies, or clinical trials, were intended to further refine the
          safety, efficacy or optimal use of a product or to ensure consistency and
          reliability of product quality. These commitments were either agreed upon by
          the FDA and the applicant or required by FDA in the following situations:

           1. Accelerated approval confirmatory studies (21 CFR 314.510/Subpart H
              and 601.41/Subpart E), where subsequent studies are required to
              describe and verify clinical benefit.

           2. Deferred pediatric studies (21 CFR 314.55(b) and 601.27(b)), where
              studies are required under the Pediatric Research Equity Act (PREA)
              NOTE: Reference to PREA related PMRs are included to maintain
              background completeness.

           3. Animal Efficacy Rule, Clinical Efficacy and Safety Studies (21 CFR
              314.610(b)(1)/Subpart I and 601.91(b)(1)/Subpart H) where studies to
              demonstrate safety and efficacy in humans are required at the time of use.

       B. Section 130(a) of Title I of the Food and Drug Administration Modernization
          Act of 1997 (FDAMA) added a provision (section 506B) to the FD&C Act
          which mandates applicants to report annually on the status of PMRs and
          reportable PMCs, and obligated FDA to make certain information about these
          PMRs/PMCs is publicly available.

       C. In 2007, Section 901, in Title IX of FDAAA created section 505(o) of the
          FD&C Act which authorized FDA to require postmarketing studies or clinical
          trials at the time of approval or after approval if FDA becomes aware of new
          safety information.

           1. FDAAA states that studies and clinical trials may be required for one of
              three purposes:

               a. To assess a known serious risk related to the use of the drug,

               b. To assess signals of serious risk related to the use of the drug, or

               c. To identify an unexpected serious risk when available data indicate the
                  potential for a serious risk.

           2. In order to require a clinical trial or study, FDA must first make a
              determination that an analysis of spontaneous postmarketing adverse
              events reported under section 505(k)(1) of the FDCA will not be sufficient
              to identify a serious risk. Furthermore, FDA must make a determination
                                                                                Page 3 of 22
Center for Biologics Evaluation and Reseach                                      SOPP 8415


               that the pharmacovigilance system that FDA is required to maintain under
               section 505(k)(3) is not sufficient to assess this serious risk.
               NOTE: The pharmacovigilance system under section 505(k)(3) will be
               referred to as “CBER Sentinel.”

           3. Section 901 of FDAAA requires applicants to submit a milestone schedule
              for completion of each study/clinical trial and to report periodically on the
              status of any study or clinical trial required.

           4. Circumstances may arise where unresolved product quality issues can be
              related to the risk of a serious adverse drug experience and therefore may
              qualify as a safety-related product quality study PMR as opposed to a
              non-506B PMC. See the Postmarketing Studies and Clinical Trials—
              Implementation of Section 505(o)(3) of the Federal Food, Drug, and
              Cosmetic Act Guidance for Industry (2019) for safety-related examples.

       D. The 2022 Food and Drug Omnibus Reform Act (FDORA) allows, but does not
          mandate, the FDA to require that confirmatory accelerated approval
          studies/trials already be underway prior to accelerated approval or within a
          specified time from the date of approval. FDORA also requires applicants
          after approval to report on accelerated approval postapproval study progress
          every 180 days to ensure these studies are being done with due diligence
          until the final study report has been submitted to FDA.

       Definitions

       A. 506B-Reportable Postmarketing Commitment (506B PMC) –
          Postmarketing studies or clinical trials concerning clinical safety, clinical
          efficacy, clinical pharmacology, or nonclinical toxicology that applicants and
          the FDA have agreed, in writing, to conduct; and applicants are required to
          report on these PMCs in their PMR/PMC annual report (21 CFR
          314.81(b)(2)(vii)(a), 21 CFR 601.70(b), and Section 506B the FD&C Act,
          Reports of Postmarketing Studies).

       B. Clinical Trial – Any prospective investigation in which an applicant or
          investigator determines the method of assigning the investigational product or
          other interventions to one or more human subjects.

       C. Non-506B, Non-Reportable, Postmarketing Commitments (non-506B
          PMC) – Any Chemistry, Manufacturing, and Controls (CMC) study, agreed, in
          writing, to be conducted, to assess drug or biologic product quality data that
          was not required for approval; yet, the review committee felt was necessary to
          provide complete quality information; in addition, these commitments are not
          subject to 506B’s reporting requirements.

       D. PMR/PMC Schedule Milestones – The specific study dates for completing
                                                                                Page 4 of 22
Center for Biologics Evaluation and Reseach                                     SOPP 8415


           activities related to conducting a PMR/PMC.

       E. Postmarketing Commitment (PMC) – Any study or clinical trial that an
          applicant has agreed upon, in writing, to conduct after approval or licensing of
          a marketing application or supplement that is not a PMR.

       F. Postmarketing Requirement (PMR) – Any study or clinical trial that an
          applicant is required to conduct post-approval of a marketing application or a
          supplement. NOTE: Applicants may be subject to legal penalties for not
          conducting PMRs.

       G. CBER Sentinel Sufficiency Assessment – Under section 901 of FDAAA, a
          required determination by CBER of the sufficiency (i.e. capability) of CBER
          Sentinel to further characterize serious risk(s) of concern. Sentinel
          insufficiency serves as a justification for whether a Title IX PMR is warranted.

       H. Study – Any investigation other than a clinical trial, such as an investigation
          in humans (e.g., an observational epidemiologic study), an animal study or a
          laboratory experiment.

       I. Voluntary Postmarketing Study or Trial – A study or clinical trial conducted
          on an applicant’s own initiative without a formal request by FDA (i.e., not in an
          FDA letter).

       J. “Safety-related” Study (Title IX PMR under FDAAA or 506B safety-related
          PMC) - A study being conducted specifically to evaluate safety or further
          investigate a safety issue(s) associated with a product. NOTE: A study must
          have a primary safety endpoint to be considered a safety-related study.

       K. “Safety-related Product Quality Study” (Title IX PMR under FDAAA) - A
          quality (CMC) study conducted specifically to address unresolved product
          quality issues related to the risk of a serious adverse drug experience under
          FDAAA. NOTE: A study must have a primary endpoint related to product
          quality safety to be considered a safety-related product quality study. For
          example, in order to identify a serious risk of patient exposure to a virus and
          subsequent viral infection, a safety-related product quality study may be
          required to demonstrate clearance or inactivation of viruses.

       Policy

       A. CBER will:

           1. Use the CBER Safety Work Group (SWG) for review and feedback of all
              proposed PMRs invoking Title IX authorities and any proposed safety
              related 506B PMCs.

                                                                                Page 5 of 22
Center for Biologics Evaluation and Reseach                                     SOPP 8415


           2. Develop and communicate to applicants all PMRs/PMCs using a
              standardized process.

           3. Meet the targeted date for communication of any proposed PMCs as
              specified in the filing letter

           4. Meet the targeted communication date for any proposed PMR as specified
              in the filing letter:

               a. For standard new molecular entity (NME) NDAs and original BLAs,
                  CBER will communicate details on anticipated PMRs no later than 8
                  weeks before the PDUFA action goal date.

               b. For priority NME NDAs and original BLAs, CBER will communicate
                  details on anticipated PMRs no later than 6 weeks before the PDUFA
                  action goal date.

               c. CBER will use T 910.04: [8-] OR [6-] Week Postmarketing
                  Requirement (PMR) [and Postmarketing Commitments (PMCs)]
                  Communication to communicate the list of proposed PMRs (and PMCs
                  if ready).

             Note: An anticipated PMR is a PMR required as a condition of approval
             based upon information provided in the original application at the time of
             submission and not as a result of a a subsequent information request
             and/or an amendment. The pre-specified communication target date will not
             apply for unanticipated PMRs.

           5. Document discussions and agreements with the applicant relating to
              PMRs/PMCs in CBER’s Regulatory Management System as per the
              appropriate CBER SOPPs (see IX. References at end of this SOPP).

               Note: FDAAA gives FDA the authority to require PMRs without prior
               agreement from the applicant. Generally, CBER will work with the
               applicant on the design of a PMR study and to come to agreement on
               feasible milestones for completing the study. For PMCs, the applicant
               should submit a written agreement to conduct the PMCs.

           6. Review the rationale for a PMC.

               a. A PMC must be agreed upon, in writing, between FDA and the
                  applicant.

               b. A PMC will be requested only if the objectives are important to further
                  the knowledge of the safe and effective use of the product.

           7. Review PMR/PMC descriptions within the Office/Division to ensure that
              they are clearly written and contain the appropriate milestone schedule.
              The milestone schedule should include a final due date for the data that
              would result in a submission that addresses the issue for the stated
              PMR/PMC. Milestones include the final protocol submission date, the
              study completion date, and the final report submission date. NOTE:
              Additional interim milestones may be added to a PMR/PMC if necessary
              to provide enhanced oversight. For example, a pre-specified interim
              analysis report date.

           8. Collaborate with the applicant to develop the milestone schedule for the
              PMR/PMC. FDAAA gives FDA the authority to require PMRs without prior
              agreement from the applicant.

           9. Include a clear and concise justification for the determination of the need
              for any PMR/PMC, as well as whether the study/clinical trial will be 506B
              reportable or not. NOTE: Justification for Title IX-related PMRs will
              include reason(s) why CBER Sentinel is insufficient to further characterize
              serious risk(s) of concern.

           10. Strive to resolve all potential non-506B PMC issues during the review
               cycle and minimize the generation of non-506B PMC.

           11. Request status updates on non-506B PMC for BLA/BLS as
               “Postmarketing Study Commitments – Status Update” and for NDA/sNDA
               within a separate section of the NDA annual report (21 CFR
               314.81(b)(2)(viii)).

           12. Require the applicant to report periodically on the status of any study or
               clinical trial required under Section 505(o)(3)(E)(ii). In addition, require an
               applicant to report annually on the status of any PMC or PMR studies of
               clinical trials as per section 506B of the FDCA and 21 CFR 601.70.

           13. Ensure that all PMRs and PMCs in any approval letter are entered into the
               appropriate PMR/PMC tracking database within one business week.

       B. CBER will not:

           1. Include in the approval letter or otherwise record or track voluntary studies
              or trials.

           2. Request a PMC for a study/clinical trial that is covered by other
              regulations (e.g., safety data updates or adverse event reports under 21
              CFR 600.80).

           3. Require a Title IX PMR for a study/clinical trial that is covered by other

Center for Biologics Evaluation and Reseach                                      SOPP 8415


               regulations (e.g., periodic safety reports or adverse event reports under 21
               CFR 600.80 or studies in CBER Sentinel when CBER Sentinel is sufficient
               to further characterize the serious risk(s) of concern).

       C. Types of 506B PMC studies include:

           1. Clinical Efficacy – Human studies intended to further characterize the
              effectiveness or optimal use of a product.

           2. Clinical Pharmacology – Human studies to further determine the effects
              of the product on the body, including pharmacokinetics (what happens to
              the product while in the body), pharmacodynamics (the effects of the
              product, as pertaining to a subject’s biochemistry and physiology), toxicity,
              and drug interaction.

           3. Clinical Safety – Human studies intended to gather additional information
              on the safety of a product, e.g., at different dosage levels. These include
              immunogenicity studies intended to further define an immune response
              elicited by the introduction of an antigen (e.g., protein.)

           4. Non-Clinical Toxicology – Non-human studies (e.g., animal studies, in
              vivo animal models) to further characterize carcinogenicity, teratogenicity,
              reproductive toxicology, biopharmaceutic and pharmacokinetic properties
              of a product.

       D. Additional information concerning non-506B PMC:

           1. CMC studies may include manufacturing, stability, and other studies that
              do not have a direct safety endpoint

           2. Non-506B PMC studies are not subject to 506B reporting requirements
              described under 21 CFR 601.70 for BLAs. For NDAs, 21 CFR
              314.81(b)(2)(viii) requires an applicant to advise the FDA on the status of
              non-506B PMCs in a separate section of the NDA annual report.

       E. Additional information concerning voluntary studies:

           1. Voluntary studies or clinical trials are not required or agreed upon.

           2. Voluntary studies or clinical trials are not subject to 506B reporting
              requirements. Although for NDAs, 21 CFR 314.81(b)(2)(viii) requires the
              applicant advise the FDA on the status of voluntary studies or clinical trials
              in a separate section of the NDA annual report.

               a. There are no provisions to modify the original milestone dates as
                  stipulated under 21 CFR 314.81(b)(2)(vii)(a)(8) and 21 CFR
                                                                                 Page 8 of 22
Center for Biologics Evaluation and Reseach                                         SOPP 8415


                   601.70(b)(8). A revised schedule can be submitted along with reasons
                   for the revision; however, FDA will use the original study schedule to
                   determine the study progress.

               b. The following milestone dates should be included in the schedule and
                  in the approval letter or alternative correspondence (e.g., PMC Study
                  Schedule Notification letter) when the PMR/PMC is required or agreed
                  upon. These dates should be captured and updated as necessary in
                  RMS-BLA, and may include:

                   •   Final protocol submission date.

                   •   Study/clinical trial completion date or a date in relation to another
                       milestone listed above (e.g., X months after final protocol
                       submission).

                   •   Final report submission date or time related to the actual
                       completion date (e.g., within X months after study completion).

       Responsibilities

       A. Branch/Lab Chief

           1. Participates in discussion and provides input on the PMR/PMC;

           2. Ensures that a clear and concise justification for requiring or
              recommending a PMR/PMC is clearly described in the discipline review
              memo;

           3. Notifies the CBER Sentinel Team of serious risk(s) that may need to be
              further characterized in a safety postmarketing requirement study. NOTE:
              This refers to the pharmacovigilance discipline Branch Chief, in the
              Division of Pharmacovigalence/OBPV;

           4. Ensures that the PMR/PMC description clearly communicates the
              objectives of the PMR/PMC in order for the applicant to adequately
              address the PMR/PMC; and

           5. Ensures that the Review Committee Members resolve differences with the
              applicant.

           6. Ensures that the final milestone schedule is both feasible and reasonable;
              and provides concurrence on the PMRs/PMCs.

       B. CBER Sentinel Team

           1. Serves as the point of contact between the Review Committee Members
              and the CBER SWG Executive Secretary, and

           2. Participates in the SWG meeting.

           3. Participates in the SWG meeting.

           2. Ensures that staff are aware of and adhere to established policies

              justification for Title IX-related PMRs and the reason(s) why CBER
              Sentinel is insufficient to further characterize the serious risk(s) of concern
              (OBPV/DPV Division Director);

           4. Ensures that all PMRs/PMCs are applied consistently across the
              Office/Division and meets the general policy guidelines in this SOPP;

           5. Ensures that adequate resources are committed to review, track, and
              close each PMR or PMC;

SOPP 8415 Appendix A: Development of Non-506B Postmarketing Commitments
(PMC) – Questions and Answers

Non-Reportable Postmarketing Commitments (non-506B PMC) are also known as
Postmarketing Study Commitments. A non-506B PMC is any study to assess drug or
biologic product quality that is not required for approval; yet, the review committee
deems necessary to provide complete quality information. A non-506B PMC is agreed
upon, in writing, by the applicant and FDA. Studies may include:

       •   Stability – Submission of additional stability data that support the approved
           expiration date of the product or further supports the characterization of the
           product’s stability; or

       •   Chemistry, Manufacturing, and Controls (CMC) – Completion of studies and
           submission of associated data related to equipment qualification, process
           validation, product quality, and procedures.

The intent of non-506B PMCs is to obtain product and manufacturing related data not
available during the application or supplement review cycle, to obtain data that provides
additional assurance that manufacturing practices are adequate, to perform a study that
is only feasible to conduct post-approval or to further analyze a product or
manufacturing process. These studies are not subject to 506B PMC reporting
requirements, although New Drug Application (NDA) regulations require under 21 CFR
314.81(b)(2)(viii) that an applicant advise the FDA on the status of each of these types
of non-506B PMCs in a separate section of their annual report.

Questions:

       Q1.     What is a non-506B PMC?
       A1.     A Non-Reportable Postmarketing Commitment (non-506B PMC) is any
               study to assess drug or biologic product quality that is not required for
               approval; yet, the review committee deems necessary to provide complete
               quality information. A non-506B PMC is concurred, in writing, by the
               applicant and FDA. Studies may include manufacturing, stability, and
               other studies that do not have primary safety as an endpoint.

       Q2.     What should you consider when developing a non-506B PMC?
       A2.     There are a number of issues to consider:

               1. Is the generation of the non-506B PMC based on a risk/benefit model?
                  If the incomplete information is critical to “approving” an application or
                  supplement and cannot be obtained and reviewed within the review
                  cycle, the review committee should not approve the application or
                  supplement but should, instead issue a Complete Response (CR)
                  letter.

Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research                            SOPP 8415 Appendix A




               2. Are the scientific and regulatory rationales for agreeing-upon the non-
                  506B PMC included in the review memo (and SBRA)? NOTE: Do not
                  include the scientific and regulatory rationales in the non-506B PMC.

               3. Have you discussed and obtained concurrence for the non-506B PMC
                  with management before speaking with the applicant?

               4. Is the request necessary and does the request provide regulatory
                  information?

               5. Is the non-506B PMC clear, concise and not subject to interpretation?

               6. What type of data or report is required to fulfill the non-506B PMC?

       Q3.     What if the applicant volunteers to conduct a PMC?
       A3.     Evaluate whether the commitment would provide pertinent regulatory
               information.
               • If the review committee agrees with the commitment, then it is a non-
                   506B PMC and should be included in the approval letter.
               • If the review committee does not agree with the commitment, notify the
                   applicant that the commitment is considered unnecessary but can be a
                   voluntary study. Do not include the commitment in the approval letter.

               Note: If a pending advice or an information request was discussed prior to
               an approval action yet was not formally included in an approval letter as a
               commitment, then identify the submission type as a Product
               Correspondence. the Regulatory Project Manager (RPM) should inform
               the applicant that the submission was incorrectly identified as a
               commitment and the Division should discuss the status and the disposition
               of the submission.
