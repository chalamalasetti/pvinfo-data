14   I.       INTRODUCTION
15
16   This guidance is intended to assist applicants who are submitting abbreviated new drug
17   applications (ANDAs) in complying with FDA’s requirements for the submission of
18   bioequivalence (BE) data. FDA’s final rule on “Requirements for Submission of Bioequivalence
19   Data” (the BE data rule) requires an ANDA applicant to submit data from all BE studies the
20   applicant conducts on a drug product formulation submitted for approval, including studies that
21   do not demonstrate that the generic product meets the current bioequivalence criteria.2 All BE
22   studies conducted on the same drug product formulation must be submitted to the Agency as
23   either a complete study report or a summary report of the BE data.3 The amended regulations
24   include a definition of same drug product formulation (section 320.1(g)).
25
26   This guidance provides information on the following subjects:
27
28        •   Types of ANDA submissions covered by the BE data rule.
29
30        •   Recommended format for summary reports of BE studies.
31
32        •   Types of formulations the Agency considers to be the same drug product
33            formulation for different dosage forms based on differences in composition.
34
35   This guidance does not address which formulations the Agency considers to be the same drug
36   product formulation based on differences in methods of manufacture.

47   II.     BACKGROUND
48
49   The Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (the FD&C Act) and FDA regulations require that an
50   ANDA applicant submit, among other things, information showing that the applicant’s drug
51   product is bioequivalent to the approved product designated by FDA as the reference listed drug
52   (RLD) (section 505(j)(2)(A)(iv) of the FD&C Act (21 U.S.C. 355(j)(2)(A)(iv); sections
53   314.94(a)(7) and 320.21(b)(1)). In the past, ANDA applicants submitted only the BE studies that
54   demonstrated that a generic product met BE criteria,4 but they did not typically submit additional
55   BE studies conducted on the same drug product formulation, including studies that did not show
56   the product met bioequivalence criteria.
57
58   The BE data rule amended FDA’s regulations to require that an ANDA applicant submit data
59   from all BE studies the applicant conducts on the drug product formulation submitted for
60   approval (sections 314.81(b)(2)(vi), 314.94(a)(7), 314.96(a)(i), and 320.21(b)(1) and (c)). We
61   believe that data from any additional BE studies may be important in our determination of
62   whether a product is bioequivalent to the RLD and are relevant to our evaluation of generic
63   products in general.5 These data will increase our understanding of generic drug development
64   and how changes in components and composition may affect formulation performance, as well
65   as promote further development of science-based bioequivalence policies.
66
67   III.    SUBMISSION OF ALL BIOEQUIVALENCE STUDIES
68
69   FDA regulations, as amended by and clarified in the BE data rule, require that a complete report
70   be submitted for the BE studies upon which the applicant relies for approval and either a
71   complete or summary report be submitted for each additional study conducted on the same drug
72   product formulation (sections 314.81(b)(2)(vi), 314.94(a)(7), 314.96(a)(i), and 320.21(b)(1) and
73   (c)). This requirement includes both in vivo and in vitro testing conducted to demonstrate
74   bioequivalence. The regulations also provide that, if a summary report is submitted—and the
75   Agency believes that there may be bioequivalence issues or concerns with the drug product—the
76   Agency may request that a complete report be prepared and submitted to FDA.

 77              A.      What Types of ANDA Submissions Must Include All BE Studies?
 78
 79   Under the BE data rule, ANDA applicants are required to submit information from all BE studies
 80   conducted on the same formulation of the drug product contained in the following submissions:
 81
 82              •    ANDAs (section 314.94).
 83              •    ANDA amendments (section 314.96(a)).
 84              •    ANDA supplements that require BE studies under section 320.21(c).
 85              •    ANDAs submitted under a suitability petition (section 314.93).
 86              •    ANDA annual reports (section 314.81(b)(2)(vi)).
 87
 88              B.      What Format Should Be Used for a Summary Report?
 89
 90   For a suggested format for summary reports, go online to FDA’s Generic Drugs: Information for
 91   Industry web page. The Division of Bioequivalence has developed model data summary tables
 92   in a concise format consistent with a common technical document (CTD) formatted application.
 93   The tables are located under the heading “Generic Drug Development, Abbreviated New Drug
 94   Application (ANDA) Submissions, and Review Information.” The Agency recommends that
 95   these table formats be used to organize the data for summary reports required by the BE data
 96   rule.

 98   IV.        SAME DRUG PRODUCT FORMULATION
 99
100   FDA amended the regulations to require an applicant to submit data from all BE studies
101   conducted on the same formulation of the drug product submitted for approval. In section
102   320.1(g), FDA added a definition of the term same drug product formulation:
103
104              Same drug product formulation means the formulation of the drug product submitted for
105              approval and any formulations that have minor differences in composition or method of
106              manufacture from the formulation submitted for approval, but are similar enough to be
107              relevant to the FDA’s determination of bioequivalence.
108
109   The definition of same drug product formulation in section 320.1(g) applies regardless of whether
110   the products are manufactured at the same or different manufacturing sites.6
111
112   In the following sections, we discuss differences in composition to consider when comparing drug
113   product formulations. For immediate-release (IR) and extended-release (ER) drug products, we
114   discuss:
115
116         •    Minor differences in composition that are unlikely to have any detectable impact on
117              formulation quality and performance between the formulations being compared. These

119          formulation and for which BE studies must be submitted (sections 314.81(b)(2)(vi),

127          A.      Immediate-Release Drug Products
128
129          1.      Immediate-Release Formulations Considered to Be the Same
130
131   Minor differences that result in product formulations that are considered to be the same include
132   the following:
133
134          •    A difference in an ingredient intended to affect the color or flavor of the drug product.
135
136          •    A different approved ingredient of the printing ink.
137
138          •    A difference in the technical grade and/or specification of an excipient (e.g., Avicel
139               PH102 versus Avicel PH200).
140
141          •    A difference in particle size of the drug substance or excipients.
142
143   Formulations with different amounts of excipients are considered to be the same drug product
144   formulation if:
145
146          •    For an individual excipient, the difference in weight between the formulations being
147               compared is less than or equal to the percentage shown in Table 1, and
148
149          •    The cumulative total of all excipient weight differences is less than or equal to 10
150               percent.

157   Illustrative examples of IR formulations considered to be the same are provided below:
158
159          •    If the amount of a filler excipient in an experimental formulation (A) is 105 mg and
160               the same filler excipient in the formulation proposed to be marketed (B) is 100 mg,
161               the difference in the excipient weight is 5 percent. These two formulations would be
162               considered the same because the difference in weight of the filler excipient is less
163               than 10 percent.
164
165          •    In the case of multiple excipient changes, if an experimental formulation (A) contains
166               95 mg of a filler excipient and 103 mg of a disintegrant and the formulation proposed
167               for marketing (B) contains 100 mg of the same filler excipient and 100 mg of the
168               same disintegrant, the difference in weight for the filler excipient is 5 percent and the
169               difference in weight for the disintegrant is 3 percent. The cumulative change is 8
170               percent, which is less than 10 percent for all excipient differences. Therefore, these
171               formulations would be considered the same.

173          2.       Immediate-Release Formuations Considered Not to Be the Same
174
175   A difference that results in product formulations that are considered not to be the same would
176   include the addition or deletion of an excipient (with the exception of a difference in an
177   ingredient intended to affect the color or flavor of the drug product or a difference in an
178   ingredient of the printing ink).
179
180   Formulations with different amounts of the same excipients are considered not to be the same
181   drug product formulation if:

183          •    For an individual excipient, the difference in excipient weight between the
184               formulations being compared exceeds the percentages shown in Table 1, or

186          •    The cumulative total of all excipient weight differences exceeds 10 percent.

296          D.       Semisolid Dosage Forms
297
298   For the purposes of this guidance, formulations of semisolid dosage form products are
299   considered to be the same if the experimental formulation is in the same category as the
300   formulation proposed for marketing (e.g., the formulations being compared are both for creams)
301   and any differences between formulations are as described below.
302
303          •    If the difference in the amount of an individual excipient between the experimental
304               formulation and the formulation intended to be marketed is less than or equal to 5
305               percent, the two formulations are considered to be the same.

306          •    If more than one excipient amount is changed, and the cumulative total of differences
307               in the amount of all excipients is less than or equal to 7 percent, the two formulations
308               are considered to be the same.
309
310          •    Formulations with differences in particle size distribution of the drug substance, if the
311               drug is in suspension, are considered to be the same.
312
313   Formulations with differences in technical grade of a structure forming excipient are considered
314   not to be the same.
315
316          E.      Other Complex Dosage Forms
317
318   For other complex dosage forms (e.g., transdermals, injectable suspensions, and suppositories),
319   limited information is available regarding quantitative and qualitative changes that could have a
320   significant impact on the bioavailability of the product. Because of this lack of information, we
321   consider all experimental formulations that are pharmaceutically equivalent to the formulation of
322   the complex dosage form product intended to be marketed to be the same as the RLD.
323   Therefore, the Agency requests submission of either a summary report or a complete report of all
324   bioavailability or bioequivalence studies conducted during the development of the drug product.
325   This information will increase our understanding of the development of the generic product and
326   how changes in components, composition, and methods of manufacture have affected
327   formulation performance. Access to this information will promote further development of
328   science-based bioequivalence policies for complex dosage forms.
