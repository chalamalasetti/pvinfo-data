CHAPTER 6

PERIODIC SAFETY UPDATE REPORTS /PERIODIC BENEFIT RISK EVALUATION
REPORT
6.1 Purpose
This section outlines the requirements and procedures for the preparation, submission, and
regulatory assessment of Periodic Safety Update Reports (PSURs)/Periodic Benefit-Risk
Evaluation Reports (PBRERs).

A Periodic Benefit-Risk Evaluation Report (PBRER)/PSUR is a comprehensive, concise, and
critical assessment of new or emerging information on a medicine's benefits and risks, compiled
by the sponsor. These ongoing evaluations support both sponsors and regulators in maintaining
confidence in the medicine's benefit-risk balance, considering existing regulatory measures (e.g.,
approved indications, warnings, and labelling) and potential actions (e.g., restricting indications,
expanding warnings and precautions, introducing contraindications, rescheduling, relabelling, or
limiting use to specific populations).

The PBRER/PSUR supports the continuous evaluation of the safety, efficacy, and effectiveness
of medicinal products and helps determine whether their benefit-risk balance remains favourable
throughout the product lifecycle. It also specifies the information Marketing Authorisation
Holders (MAHs) must submit to the Agency to facilitate appropriate regulatory action and
protect public health.

6.2 Scope
This section applies to MAHs of human medicinal products registered or seeking registration in
Nigeria, and to relevant applicants where PSUR/PBRERs are required as part of a marketing
authorisation application. It covers reporting-cycle establishment, reporting frequency and
timelines, report format and content, safety and benefit–risk evaluation, Nigeria-specific safety
information, appendices, quality systems, submission and regulatory assessment. The
requirements apply according to the product’s risk category and any additional or ad hoc
reporting requirements specified by the Agency. Low-risk products are exempt from routine
submission unless a report is requested by the Agency or a new safety concern or change in risk
category warrants submission.

Definitions
For the purposes of this section, the following definitions apply:
Periodic Safety Update Report (PSUR)/Periodic Benefit-Risk Evaluation Report (PBRER):
A structured report prepared by the MAH that critically evaluates new or emerging safety,
efficacy and effectiveness information in the context of cumulative information, to assess
whether the benefit–risk balance of a medicinal product remains favourable.



                                                                                                    4
Marketing Authorisation Holder (MAH): The person or legal entity holding the marketing
authorisation or Certificate of Registration for a medicinal product and responsible for meeting
the applicable regulatory obligations.

International Birth Date (IBD): The date of the first marketing authorisation granted to any
company, in any country, for a medicinal product containing the active substance.

Data Lock Point (DLP): The date up to which data and information are included in a
PSUR/PBRER for a defined reporting interval.

Reporting interval: The period covered by a PSUR/PBRER, established in accordance with the
applicable reporting cycle and DLP.

Nigerian birth date: The date on which the Agency first granted a Certificate of Registration for
the medicinal product in Nigeria.

Reference Safety Information (RSI): The version of the designated reference product
information used to determine the expectedness of suspected adverse reactions during the
reporting period.

Adverse drug reaction (ADR): A noxious and unintended response to a medicinal product for
which a causal relationship is at least a reasonable possibility.
Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR): A report containing information about an individual
patient who experiences one or more suspected adverse reactions or other reportable safety
events associated with one or more medicinal products.

Risk Management Plan (RMP): A document describing the safety profile of a medicinal
product, its important identified and potential risks, missing information, and the
pharmacovigilance and risk-minimisation activities intended to address them.

Benefit–risk balance: An evaluation of the benefits of a medicinal product in relation to its
risks, considering the available evidence and the authorised conditions of use.


Key Elements of the Reporting Cycle

6.3 International Birth Date (IBD), Reporting Interval and Data Lock Point (DLP)
6.3.1      The reporting cycle for each PSUR/PBRER shall be established with reference to the
           International Birth Date (IBD) of the active substance. The IBD is the date of the
           first marketing authorisation granted to any company, in any country in the world, for
           a medicinal product containing the active substance.

6.3.2      The IBD, rather than the date on which the product was first registered or marketed in
           Nigeria (the Nigerian birth date), shall be used as the reference date for establishing



                                                                                                   5
          the reporting interval and Data Lock Point (DLP). The Nigerian birth date shall
          nevertheless be stated on the title page of the PSUR/PBRER (see Section 6.47).

6.3.3     The use of a common IBD and DLP facilitates preparation of a single PSUR/PBRER
          for submission to different regulatory authorities, while recognising that reporting
          frequencies may differ between jurisdictions in accordance with their respective
          regulatory requirements. This approach is consistent with ICH E2C(R2).

6.3.4     Where the IBD cannot be established despite reasonable efforts, the MAH shall use
          the earliest date of marketing authorisation for which reliable documentary evidence
          is available. The date used shall be stated on the title page, and the basis for its use
          shall be justified in the introduction to the report.

6.4 Establishment and Harmonisation of the DLP
6.4.1     The DLP is the date up to which data and information are included in a
          PSUR/PBRER. The DLP shall be established from the reporting cycle based on the
          IBD and the reporting interval applicable to the report.

6.4.2     To facilitate harmonisation across products containing the same active substance and
          across jurisdictions, the MAH may use an existing DLP established or agreed by
          another competent regulatory authority, if it is based on the same IBD. Where no such
          DLP has been established, the MAH shall establish the DLP in accordance with the
          IBD-based reporting cycle applicable to the product in Nigeria.

6.4.3     The DLP may correspond to the relevant anniversary of the IBD or to another DLP
          established within the IBD-based reporting cycle provided it is accepted by the
          Agency.

6.4.4     Once an IBD-based DLP convention has been established for an active substance, the
          MAH shall apply the same approach consistently to subsequent routine
          PSUR/PBRERs for that active substance, unless otherwise agreed with the Agency.
6.4.5     Where the reporting frequency required by the Agency differs from that applied in
          another jurisdiction, the MAH shall retain the same IBD as the reference date and
          establish the DLPs at the reporting milestones required to meet the Agency's
          applicable reporting frequency.(For more clarification, check ICH E2C(R2) guideline,
          which allows different regional reporting frequencies while retaining a harmonised
          IBD/DLP approach).

6.5 Products Registered but Not Yet Marketed in Nigeria
6.5.1     Where a medicinal product has been registered in Nigeria but has not yet been placed
          on the Nigerian market, the MAH shall submit PSUR/PBRERs in accordance with




                                                                                                     6
         the reporting frequency applicable to the product under Section 6.6, unless otherwise
         agreed in writing by the Agency.



6.6 Frequency of Submission of PSUR/PBRERs
6.6.1    The frequency of PSUR/PBRER submission in Nigeria shall be determined by the
         requirements of the Agency, taking into consideration the risk category assigned to
         the product (see Sections 6.11 and 6.12) and whether the product contains a new drug
         molecule.

6.6.2    The reporting frequency required by the Agency shall be applied independently of the
         age of the active substance calculated from its global IBD. The IBD shall continue to
         be used to establish the reporting cycle and DLP.
         Accordingly:

         a. New drug molecules: Where an active substance is being registered in Nigeria for
            the first time, the MAH shall submit PSUR/PBRERs at least every six (6) months
            for the first two (2) years following the first marketing authorisation in Nigeria,
            annually for the following three (3) years, and thereafter every five (5) years,
            unless the Agency requires more frequent submission. The reporting intervals and
            DLPs for these reports shall nevertheless be established with reference to the IBD
            in accordance with Sections 6.3 and 6.4.

         b. High-risk and medium-risk products that do not contain a new drug molecule:
            PSUR/PBRERs shall be submitted every five (5) years based on the IBD-
            established reporting cycle, irrespective of whether the reporting cycle aligns with
            the product renewal period, unless the Agency requires more frequent submission.

         c. Low-risk products: Low-risk products shall be exempt from routine submission of
            PSUR/PBRERs. The MAH shall nevertheless continue to collect, evaluate and
            report safety information in accordance with this Guideline and shall submit a
            PSUR/PBRER when requested by the Agency, when a signal or new safety
            concern arises, or when the Agency changes the risk category of the product.

6.6.3    Where a product is subject to a Nigerian reporting frequency that is more frequent
         than the frequency applicable to the same active substance in another jurisdiction, the
         MAH shall meet the Nigerian reporting requirement using DLPs established from the
         IBD-based reporting cycle. Where appropriate, the Agency may accept a
         PSUR/PBRER covering a longer interval, if it fulfils the applicable Nigerian
         reporting requirement and contains the required up-to-date information. (Read ICH
         E2C(R2) Q&A on managing different regional reporting frequencies for more
         information).


                                                                                               7
6.7 Authority to Require More Frequent or Ad Hoc Reporting
6.7.1    Irrespective of the routine reporting frequency assigned to a product, the Agency may
         at any time require more frequent submission or an ad hoc PSUR/PBRER where this
         is considered necessary for the protection of public health.
         Such a requirement may be made where:
         a. submission of PSUR/PBRERs is a condition of the marketing authorisation;

         b. the product is subject to a Risk Management Plan (RMP), additional
            pharmacovigilance activities or additional risk minimisation measures;
         c. there are concerns arising from pharmacovigilance data or other safety
            information;
         d. PSUR/PBRERs for the active substance have not been submitted where expected;

         e. regulatory action has been taken, or a significant new safety concern has arisen, in
            another jurisdiction; or

         f. other information indicates that more frequent monitoring of the benefit-risk
            balance is warranted.

6.7.2    For an ad hoc PSUR/PBRER, the Agency shall specify the reporting period and DLP,
         where these differ from the routine reporting cycle.

6.8 Products Already Marketed in Other Countries
6.8.1    Where a medicinal product has already been authorised and marketed in another
         country, the applicant shall submit the most recent available PSUR/PBRER for the
         active substance as part of the application for registration in Nigeria.

6.8.2    Following registration in Nigeria, the MAH shall submit PSUR/PBRERs in
         accordance with the reporting frequency applicable in Nigeria under Section 6.6. The
         first routine report due after registration shall be determined using the next applicable
         reporting interval/DLP in the IBD-based reporting cycle.
6.8.3    For listed medicinal products subject to provisional registration, the MAH shall
         submit a PSUR/PBRER every six (6) months during the two (2) year listing period
         where the product is classified as high risk, unless otherwise specified by the Agency.




                                                                                                 8
6.9 Timeline for Submission of PSUR/PBRERs

6.9.1     Each MAH shall be responsible for submitting PSUR/PBRERs for its own products
          to the Agency. For routine PSUR/PBRERs, Day 0 shall be the DLP. The completed
          report shall be submitted:

          a. within 70 calendar days of the DLP for PSUR/PBRERs covering reporting inter-
             vals of up to 12 months, including intervals of exactly 12 months; and

          b. within 90 calendar days of the DLP for PSUR/PBRERs covering reporting inter-
             vals exceeding 12 months.

          c. For an ad hoc PSUR/PBRER requested by the Agency, the submission timeline
             shall normally be specified in the Agency's request. Where no timeline is speci-
             fied, the report shall be submitted within 90 calendar days of the DLP.

6.10 Risk Categorisation of Medicinal Products for PSUR submission
6.10.1    The Agency shall establish and maintain a risk categorisation framework for
          medicinal products subject to PSUR/PBRER requirements. The framework shall
          define the criteria and process to be applied in categorising medicinal products as
          high, medium or low risk for the purpose of determining the appropriate level of
          PSUR/PBRER oversight.

6.10.2    The Marketing Authorisation Holder (MAH) shall assess each of its registered
          medicinal products against the Agency's risk categorisation framework and determine
          the applicable risk category. The MAH shall ensure that the categorisation is based on
          the most current and relevant safety, clinical, regulatory and product-related
          information available.

6.10.3    The MAH shall state the risk category determined in accordance with the Agency's
          framework on the title page of each PSUR/PBRER and in the accompanying
          covering letter.
6.10.4    The MAH shall periodically review the risk category of each product and shall
          reassess the categorisation where new information becomes available that may affect
          the product's risk profile. Such information may include, but is not limited to:
             i. new or emerging safety information;
             ii. identification, validation or confirmation of a safety signal;
             iii. a new or emerging safety concern;
             iv. a change in the known safety profile or benefit-risk balance of the product;




                                                                                                9
              v. significant changes to the Risk Management Plan (RMP), safety specification
                 or risk minimisation measures, where applicable; or

              vi. significant regulatory action or new safety information relating to the product
                  in another jurisdiction.

6.10.5     Where the MAH determines that a product's risk category has changed, the MAH
           shall update the categorisation and apply the corresponding PSUR/PBRER reporting
           requirements from the next report due, unless otherwise specified by the Agency.

6.10.6     The Agency may review the risk categorisation applied by an MAH as part of its
           regulatory oversight, including during PSUR/PBRER assessment, pharmacovigilance
           inspections, review of safety information or other regulatory activities. Where the
           Agency considers that the categorisation does not appropriately reflect the available
           evidence or the criteria in the Agency's framework, it may require the MAH to
           reassess and, where appropriate, revise the risk category.

6.10.7     The risk category determined by the MAH in accordance with the Agency's
           framework shall be used to determine the applicable routine PSUR/PBRER reporting
           requirements set out in Section 6.6. The risk categorisation does not remove or limit
           the MAH's obligations to continuously monitor the safety profile of its products,
           maintain an effective pharmacovigilance system, and promptly notify the Agency of
           significant safety information in accordance with this Guideline.

6.10.8     Irrespective of the risk category determined by the MAH, the Agency may require
           additional pharmacovigilance measures or more frequent or ad hoc PSUR/PBRER
           submission where this is considered necessary for the protection of public health.



6.11 Structures and processes
6.11.1 Objectives
6.11.1.1   The main objective of a PSUR/PBRER is to present a comprehensive, concise and
           critical analysis of the benefit-risk balance of the medicinal product considering new
           or emerging information in the context of cumulative information on risks and
           benefits. The PSUR/PBRER is therefore a tool for post-authorisation evaluation at
           defined time points in the lifecycle of a product.

6.11.1.2   For lifecycle benefit-risk management, it is necessary to continue evaluating the risks
           and benefits of a medicinal product in everyday medicinal practice and long-term use
           in the post-authorisation phase. This may extend to evaluation of populations and
           endpoints that could not be investigated in the pre-authorisation clinical trial. A
           different benefit-risk balance may emerge as pharmacovigilance reveals further safety



                                                                                                10
           information. The Marketing Authorisation Holder should therefore re-evaluate the
           benefit-risk balance of its own medicinal products in populations exposed. This
           structured evaluation should be undertaken in the context of ongoing
           pharmacovigilance and risk management (see Chapter 3) to facilitate optimisation of
           the benefit-risk balance through effective risk minimisation.

6.11.1.3   Urgent safety information should be reported through the appropriate mechanism. A
           PSUR/PBRER is not intended, in the first instance, for notification of significant new
           safety or efficacy information or to provide how new safety issues are detected. It is
           acknowledged that the review of the data in the PSUR/PBRER may lead to new
           safety issues being identified.



6.11.2 Principles
6.11.2.1   Benefit-risk evaluation should be carried out throughout the lifecycle of the medicinal
           product to promote and protect public health and to enhance patient safety through
           effective risk minimisation.

6.11.2.2   After a Certificate of Registration is granted, it is necessary to continue evaluating the
           benefits and risks of medicinal products in actual use and/or long-term use, to confirm
           that the benefit-risk balance remains favourable.

6.11.2.3   The analysis of the benefit-risk balance should incorporate an evaluation of the safety,
           efficacy and effectiveness information that becomes available, with reasonable and
           appropriate effort, during the reporting interval for the medicinal product in the
           context of what was known previously.

6.11.2.4   The risk evaluation should be based on all uses of the medicinal product. The scope
           includes evaluation of safety in real medicinal practice including use in unauthorised
           indications and use which is not in line with the product information (e. misuse, abuse
           or overdose). If use of the medicinal product is identified where there are critical gaps
           in knowledge for specific safety issues or populations, such use should be reported in
           the PSUR/PBRER (e.g. use in paediatric population or in pregnant women). Sources
           of information on use outside authorisation may include drug utilisation data,
           information from spontaneous reports and publications in the literature.


6.11.3 Scope of Information for the Benefit–Risk Evaluation
6.11.3.1   The scope of the benefit-risk information should include both clinical trial and real-
           world data in authorised indications.




                                                                                                    11
6.11.3.2   The integrated benefit-risk evaluation should be performed for all authorised
           indications and should incorporate the evaluation of risks in all use of the medicinal
           product (including use in unauthorised indications).
6.11.3.3   The evaluation should involve:

           a. Critically examining the information which has emerged during the reporting
              interval to determine whether it has generated new signals, led to the
              identification of new potential or identified risks or contributed to knowledge of
              previously identified risks.

           b. Critically summarising relevant new safety, efficacy and effectiveness information
              that could have an impact on the benefit-risk balance of the medicinal
              product.(including safety, efficacy and effectiveness information generated in
              Nigeria.)

           c. The PSUR/PBRER should include an overall benefit-risk evaluation covering all
              authorised indications using all cumulative data available since the Development
              International Birth Date (DIBD), which is the date the first clinical trial of the
              product was authorised in any country. If the DIBD is not known, or the
              Marketing Authorisation Holder does not have access to early clinical
              development data, the company should use the earliest date for which reliable
              information is available and use this as the starting point for the cumulative
              evaluation.

           d. Summarising any risk minimisation actions that may have been taken or
              implemented during the reporting interval, as well as risk minimisation actions
              that are planned to be implemented.
           e. Outlining plans for signal or risk evaluations including timelines and/or proposals
              for additional pharmacovigilance activities.



6.12 Principles for the preparation of PSUR/PBRERs
6.12.1     Unless the Agency instructs otherwise, the Marketing Authorisation Holder should
           submit one PSUR/PBRER covering all its products that contain the same active
           substance. The report should include all approved indications, routes of
           administration, dosage forms, and dosing regimens, even if the products are registered
           under different brand names.

6.12.2     Where necessary, the PSUR/PBRER should present separate information for different
           indications, dosage forms, routes of administration, or dosing regimens, and any
           related safety concerns should be discussed clearly.



                                                                                                12
6.12.3    In special cases, separate PSUR/PBRERs may be allowed for the same active
          ingredients, for example when different formulations are used for completely
          different indications. In such situations, the Marketing Authorisation Holder should
          obtain agreement from the Agency, preferably at the time of product authorisation.


6.12.4    Case narratives should be provided in the relevant risk evaluation section of the
          PSUR/PBRER where integral to the scientific analysis of a signal or safety concern.
          In this context, the term “case narratives” refers to clinical evaluations of individual
          cases rather than the CIOMS narratives.

6.12.5    If the Marketing Authorisation Holder receives safety information from a partner
          (such as a distributor, licensee, affiliate, or co-marketing company), and the
          information could affect how safe the product is considered or could lead to a change
          in the product information, the Marketing Authorisation Holder must review it and
          include it in the PSUR/PBRER. The Marketing Authorisation Holder remains
          responsible for evaluating all available safety information for the product, even if the
          data was not originally generated by them.
6.12.6    All PSUR/PBRERs shall be prepared in the format and with the table of contents of
          the ICH E2C(R2) Periodic Benefit-Risk Evaluation Report, as described in section
          6.14 to 6.37. Each report should include interval as well as cumulative data.

6.13 Reference information
6.13.1    Risk minimisation activities evaluated in the PSUR/PBRER include updates to the
          product information.

6.13.2    The reference product information for the PSUR/PBRER should include “core safety”
          and “authorised indications” components. In order to facilitate the assessment of
          benefit and benefit-risk balance by indication in the evaluation sections of the
          PSUR/PBRER, the reference product information document should list all authorised
          indications in Nigeria.

6.13.3    The basis for the benefit evaluation should be the important baseline efficacy and
          effectiveness information summarised in the PSUR/PBRER section 6.34.2
          (“Important baseline efficacy and effectiveness information”).

6.13.4    Information related to a specific indication, formulation or route of administration
          should be clearly identified in the reference product information.

6.13.5    The following possible options can be considered by the Marketing Authorisation
          Holders when selecting the most appropriate reference product information for a
          PSUR/PBRER:



                                                                                                 13
6.13.5.1 Company core data sheet (CCDS)
          a. Many Marketing Authorisation Holders prepare a Company Core Data Sheet
             (CCDS). The CCDS contains important product information such as safety,
             approved uses (indications), dosage, pharmacology, and other product details. The
             safety section within the CCDS is called the Company Core Safety Information
             (CCSI). For the PSUR/PBRER, the Marketing Authorisation Holder should
             preferably use the CCDS version that was valid at the end of the reporting period
             as the reference product information. This document should be used to assess both
             the safety (risk) sections and the benefit of the authorised indications.
          b. If the CCDS does not include the approved indications, the Marketing
             Authorisation Holder must state in the PSUR/PBRER which document is being
             used as the reference for the authorised indications (for example, the approved
             SmPC or national product label).

6.13.5.2 Other options for the reference product information
6.13.5.2.1 If a product does not have a CCDS or CCSI (for example, an older or generic
           product), the Marketing Authorisation Holder must state which reference safety
           information they are using for the PSUR.his reference may be an approved national or
           regional product information document, such as the Summary of Product
           Characteristics (SmPC).

6.13.6    The company must attach the reference safety document used to evaluate
          expectedness of adverse reactions (e.g., CCDS/CCSI or approved SmPC) as an
          appendix. If the approved indications are contained in a different document, that
          document must also be attached. The version attached must be the one valid at the
          Data Lock Point.

6.13.7    During the reporting period, the Marketing Authorisation Holder should regularly
          review any new safety information to determine whether the reference product
          information or reference safety information needs to be updated. Any important
          updates made during the reporting period must be described in the “Changes to the
          reference safety information” section 4 of the PSUR/PBRER and, where applicable,
          further discussed in the “Signal and risk evaluation” section 16.
          These changes may include:
          a. A change to contraindications, warnings/precautions sections;
          b. Addition to adverse reactions and interactions;
          c. Addition of important new information on use in overdose; and
          d. Removal of an indication or other restrictions for safety or lack

6.13.8   The Marketing Authorisation Holder must attach a clean copy of the reference
         product information to the PSUR/PBRER as an appendix. This should include all
         versions that were valid at the end of the reporting period (for example, where

6.14 Format and contents of the PSUR/PBRER
6.14.1   The PSUR/PBRER should be based on all available data including those generated
         from within Nigeria and should focus on new information which has emerged since
         the data lock point of the last PSUR/PBRER. Cumulative information should be
         considered when performing the overall safety evaluation and integrated benefit-risk
         assessment.
6.14.2   Safety information from studies carried out after the product has been registered must
         be included in the PSUR/PBRER. This includes results from post-authorisation
         studies and clinical trials, even if they involve uses, patient groups, or indications that
         are not yet approved.

         Safety information obtained from off-label use should also be considered and
         discussed in the risk evaluation where it is relevant to the product’s safety.

6.14.3   The PSUR/PBRER should provide summaries of data relevant to the benefits and
         risks of the medicinal product, including results of all studies with a consideration of
         their potential impact on the Certificate of Registration.

6.14.4   Examples of sources of efficacy, effectiveness and safety information that may be
         used in the preparation of PSUR/PBRERs include the following:
         a. Non-clinical studies;
         b. Spontaneous reports (e.g. on the marketing authorization
         c. Active surveillance systems (e.g. sentinel sites);
         d. Investigations of product quality;
         e. Product usage data and drug utilisation information;
         f. Clinical trials, including research in unauthorised indications or populations;

         g. Observational studies, including registries;
         h. Patient support programs;
         i. Systematic reviews and meta-analysis;

            presented at scientific meetings;
         l. Unpublished manuscripts;
         m. Licensing partners, other sponsors or academic institutions and research networks;
         n. Competent authorities (worldwide).
6.14.5   The data sources listed above are not the only sources that may be used. The

         the safety, benefits, and overall benefit-risk balance of the product, depending on the
         product and its known or emerging risks.

6.14.6   The PSUR/PBRER shall be prepared following the format described in sections 6.14

6.14.7   The Marketing Authorisation Holder should use all safety information about the
         active substance or product that they can reasonably access and that is relevant to
         assessing safety and the benefit-risk balance.

         All PSURs should follow the same format, but the amount of information provided
         may differ depending on what data the Marketing Authorisation Holder can obtain.

         may only have access to the published study report.

6.14.8   The amount of detail included in each section of the PSUR/PBRER should be based
         on the importance of the product’s known or new safety and benefit information and
         should be proportionate to the risk category of the product. More detailed discussion
         is expected in sections that evaluate safety, efficacy, effectiveness, safety signals, and
         the overall benefit-risk balance, especially when important risks or new findings are
         identified.

6.23.6 Cumulative summary tabulations of serious adverse events from clinical trials
6.23.6.1   This PSUR/PBRER section should provide background for the appendix that provides
           a cumulative summary tabulation of serious adverse events reported in the Marketing
           Authorisation Holder's clinical trials, from the DIBD to the data lock point of the
           current PSUR/PBRER. The Marketing Authorisation Holder should explain any
           omission of data (e.g. clinical trial data might not be available for products marketed
           for many years). The tabulation(s) should be organised by MedDRA SOC (listed in
           the internationally agreed order), for the investigational drug, as well as for the
           comparator arm(s) (active comparators, placebo) used in the clinical development
           programme. Data can be integrated across the programme. Alternatively, when useful
           and feasible, data can be presented by trial, indication, route of administration or
           other variables.

6.23.6.2   This section should not serve to provide analyses or conclusions based on the serious
           adverse events.
6.23.6.3   The following points should be considered:



                                                                                                26
           a. Causality assessment is generally useful for the evaluation of individual rare
              adverse drug reactions. Individual case causality assessment has less value in the
              analysis of aggregate data, where group comparisons of rates are possible.
              Therefore, the summary tabulations should include all serious adverse events and
              not just serious adverse reactions for the investigational drug, comparators and
              placebo. It may be useful to give rates by dose.

           b. In general, the tabulation(s) of serious adverse events from clinical trials should
              include only those terms that were used in defining the case as serious and non-
              serious events should be included in the study reports.
           c. The tabulations should include blinded and unblinded clinical trial data.
              Unblinded serious adverse events might originate from completed trials and
              individual cases that have been unblinded for safety-related reasons (e.g.
              expedited reporting), if applicable. Sponsors of clinical trials and Marketing
              Authorisation Holders should not unblind data for the specific purpose of
              preparing the PSUR/PBRER.

           d. Certain adverse events can be excluded from the clinical trials summary
              tabulations, but such exclusions should be explained in the report. For example,
              adverse events that have been defined in the protocol as “exempt” from special
              collection and entry into the safety database because they are anticipated in the
              patient population, and those that represent study endpoints, can be excluded (e.g.
              deaths reported in a trial of a drug for congestive heart failure where all-cause
              mortality is the primary efficacy endpoint, disease progression in cancer trials).

6.26.1 Other clinical trials
6.26.1.1   This PSUR/PBRER section should summarise information relevant to the benefit-risk

6.26.2 Medication errors
6.26.2.1   This section should summarise relevant information on patterns of medication errors

6.28 Literature

6.31.1     The MAH should summarise in this PSUR/PBRER section the potentially important

6.32 Overview of signals: new, ongoing, or closed
6.32.1     The purpose of this section is to provide a high-level overview of signals that were
           closed (i.e. evaluation was completed) during the reporting interval as well as
           ongoing signals that were undergoing




                                                                                                  33
         evaluation at the end of the reporting interval. For the purposes of the PSUR/PBRER,
         a signal should be included once it has undergone the initial screening or clarification
         step, and a determination made to conduct further evaluation by the MAH. It should
         be noted that a safety signal is not synonymous with a statistic of disproportionate
         reporting for a specific medicinal product/event combination, as a validation step is
         required. Signals may be qualitative (e.g., a pivotal individual case safety report, case
         series) or quantitative (e.g. a disproportionality score, findings of a clinical trial or
         epidemiological study). Signals may arise in the form of an information request or
         inquiry on a safety issue from a competent authority (worldwide).

6.32.2   Decisions regarding the subsequent classification of these signals and the conclusions

         available data, which is presented in section 6.33“Signal and risk evaluation” of the
         PSUR/PBRER.
6.32.3   A new signal refers to a signal that has been identified during the reporting interval.
         Where new clinically significant information on a previously closed signal becomes
         available during the reporting interval of the PSUR/PBRER, this would also be
         considered a new signal on the basis that a new aspect of a previously refuted signal
         or recognised risk warrants further action to verify. New signals may be classified as
         closed or ongoing, depending on the status of signal evaluation at the end of the
         reporting interval of the PSUR/PBRER.
6.32.4   Examples of new signals would therefore include new information on a previously:
         a. Close and refute a signal, which would result in the signal being re-opened.

            outcome such as hepatic failure is received, or neutropenia is an identified risk
            and a well-documented case report of agranulocytosis with no presence of
            possible alternative causes is received).

         c. Identified risk for which a higher frequency or severity of the risk is newly found
            (e.g. in an indicated subpopulation).

         d. Potential risk which, if confirmed, would warrant a new warning, precaution, a
            new contraindication or restriction in indication(s) or population or other risk
            minimisation activities.

          b. seriouness of the signal

          g. A brief summary of the key data;
          h. Plans for further evaluation; and
          i. Actions taken or planned.

6.33 Signal and risk evaluation
6.33.1    The purpose of this section of the PSUR/PBRER is to provide:

          a. A succinct summary of what is known about important identified and potential
             risks and missing information at the beginning of the reporting interval covered
             by the report (section 6.33.4).
          b. An evaluation of all signals closed during the reporting interval (section 6.33.5)

          c. An evaluation of new information with respect to previously recognised identified
             and potential risks (section 6.33.6).




                                                                                                  35
           d. An updated characterisation of important potential and identified risks, where
              applicable (section 6.33.7).

           e. A summary of the effectiveness of risk minimisation activities in any country or
              region which may have utility in other countries or regions (section 6.33.8).

6.33.2     The Marketing Authorisation Holder may present the mapping of signals and risks to
           the relevant sections of the PSUR/PBRER in tabular form or as a flow chart (see
           6.37).

6.33.3     These evaluation sections should not summarise, or duplicate information presented
           in previous sections of the PSUR/PBRER but should rather provide interpretation and
           critical appraisal of the information, with a view towards characterising the profile of
           those risks assessed as important. In addition, as a general rule, it is not necessary to
           include individual case narratives in the evaluation sections of the PSUR/PBRER but
           where integral to the scientific analysis of a signal or risk, a clinical evaluation of
           pivotal or illustrative cases (e.g. the first case of suspected agranulocytosis with an
           active substance belonging to a class known to be associated with this adverse
           reaction) should be provided (see section 6.12).


6.33.4 Summary of safety concerns
6.33.4.1   The purpose of this section is to provide a summary of important safety concerns at
           the beginning of the reporting interval, against which new information and
           evaluations can be made. For products with an existing safety specification, this
           section can be either the same as, or derived from the safety specification summary
           that is current at the start of the reporting interval of the PSUR/PBRER. It should
           provide the following safety information:
           a. Important identified risks;
           b. Important potential risks; and
           c. Missing information.


6.33.4.2   The following factors should be considered when determining the importance of each
           risk:
           a. (medical )seriousness of the risk, including the impact on individual patients;
           b. Its frequency, predictability, preventability, and reversibility;
           c. Potential impact on public health (frequency; size of treated population); and



                                                                                                 36
           d. Potential for avoidance of the use of a medicinal product with a preventive benefit
              due to a disproportionate public perception of risk (e.g. vaccines).

6.33.4.3   For products without an existing safety specification, this section should provide
           information on the important identified and potential risks and missing information
           associated with use of the product, based on pre- and post-authorisation experience.
           Important identified and potential risks may include, for example:
           a. Important adverse reactions.
           b. Interactions with other medicinal products.
           c. Interactions with food and other substances.
           d. Medication errors (e.g Overdose).
           e. Effects of occupational exposure; and
           f. Pharmacological class effects.

6.33.4.4   The summary on missing information should take into account whether there are
           critical gaps in knowledge for specific safety issues or populations that use the
           medicinal product.


6.33.5 Signal evaluation
6.33.5.1   This section of the PSUR/PBRER should summarise the results of evaluations of all
           safety signals (whether or not classified as important) that were closed during the
           reporting interval. A safety signal can be closed either because it is refuted or because
           it is determined to be a potential or identified risk, following evaluation. The two
           main categories to be included in this section are:

           a. Those signals that, following evaluation, have been refuted as “false” signals
              based on medical judgement and scientific evaluation of the currently available
              information.

           b. Those signals that, following evaluation, have been categorised as either a
              potential or identified risk, including lack of efficacy.

6.33.5.2   For both categories of closed signals, a concise description of each signal evaluation
           should be included in order to clearly describe the basis upon which the signal was
           either refuted or considered to be a potential or identified risk by the Marketing
           Authorisation Holder.
6.33.5.3   It is recommended that the level of detail provided in the description of the signal
           evaluation should reflect the medical significance of the signal (e.g. severe,


                                                                                                  37
           irreversible, lead to increased morbidity or mortality) and potential public health
           importance (e.g. wide usage, frequency, significant use outside the recommendations
           of the product information,effect on vulnarable population.) and the extent of the
           available evidence. Where multiple evaluations will be included under both categories
           of closed signals, they can be presented in the following order:
           a. Closed and refuted signals.
           b. Closed signals that are categorised as important potential risks.
           c. Closed signals that are categorised as important identified risks.
           d. Closed signals that are potential risks not categorised as important.
           e. Closed signals that are identified risks not categorised as important.

6.33.5.4   Where applicable the evaluations of closed signals can be presented by indication or
           population.

6.33.5.5   The description(s) of the signal evaluations can be included in this section of the
           PSUR/PBRER or in an appendix. Each evaluation should include the following
           information as appropriate:
           a. Source or trigger of the signal;
           b. Background relevant to the evaluation;

           c. Method(s) of evaluation, including data sources, search criteria (where applicable,
              the specific MedDRA terms (e.g. PTs, HLTs, SOCs, etc.) or Standardised
              MedDRA Queries (SMQs) that were reviewed), and analytical approaches;

           d. Results - a summary and critical analysis of the data considered in the signal
              evaluation; where integral to the assessment, this may include a description of a
              case series or an individual case (e.g. an index case of well documented
              agranulocytosis or Stevens Johnson Syndrome);
           e. Discussion;
           f. Conclusion.

6.33.5.6   Marketing Authorisation Holder's evaluations and conclusions for refuted signals
           should be supported by data and clearly presented.

6.33.8 Effectiveness of risk minimisation
6.33.8.1   Risk minimisation activities are public health interventions intended to prevent the

           minimisation activities may consist of routine risk minimisation (e.g. product
           labelling) or additional risk minimisation activities (e.g. Direct Healthcare
           Professional Communication/educational materials).

6.33.8.2   The PSUR/PBRER should contain the results of assessments of the effectiveness of
           risk minimisation activities relevant to the benefit-risk assessment.

6.33.8.5   When required for reporting in a PSUR/PBRER, results of evaluations that became

6.34.4.4   When there is new positive benefit information and no significant change in the risk

6.35 Integrated benefit-risk analysis for authorised indications
6.35.1     The Marketing Authorisation Holder should provide in this PSUR/PBRER section an
           overall appraisal of the benefit and risk of the medicinal product as used in clinical
           practice. Whereas section 6.33.7 (“Characterisation of risks”) and section 6.34.4
           (“Characterisation of benefits”) present the risks and benefits, this section should
           provide a critical analysis and integration of the key information in the previous
           sections and should not simply duplicate the benefit and risk characterisation
           presented in the sections mentioned above.



6.35.2 Benefit-risk context – medicinal need and important alternatives
6.35.2.1   This section of the PSUR/PBRER should provide a brief description of the medicinal
           need for the medicinal product in the authorised indications and summarised
           alternatives (medicinal, surgical or other; including no treatment).

6.35.3 Benefit-risk analysis evaluation
6.35.3.1   A benefit-risk balance is specific to an indication and population. Therefore, for
           products authorised for more than one indication, benefit-risk balances should be
           evaluated and presented by each indication individually. If there are important
           differences in the benefit-risk balance among populations within an indication, the
           benefit-risk evaluation should be presented by population, if possible.

6.35.3.2   The benefit-risk evaluation should be presented and discussed in a way that facilitates
           the comparison of benefits and risks and should consider the following points:

           a. Whereas previous sections should include all important benefit and risk
              information, not all benefits and risks contribute importantly to the overall
              benefit-risk evaluation, therefore, the key benefits and risks considered in the
              evaluation should be specified. The key information presented in the previous
              benefit and risk section should be carried forward for integration in the benefit-
              risk evaluation.




                                                                                                   44
           b. Consider the context of use of the medicinal product: the condition to be treated,
              prevented, or diagnosed; its severity and seriousness; and the population to be
              treated (relatively healthy; chronic illness, rare conditions).

           c. With respect to the key benefits, consider its nature, clinical importance, duration,
              and generalizability, as well as evidence of efficacy in non-responders to other
              therapies and alternative treatments. Consider the effect size. If there are
              individual elements of benefit, consider all (e.g. for therapies for rheumatoid
              arthritis: reduction of symptoms and inhibition of radiographic progression of
              joint damage).
           d. With respect to risk, consider its clinical importance, (e.g. nature of toxicity,
              seriousness, frequency, predictability, preventability, reversibility, impact on
              patients), and whether it arose from clinical trials in unauthorized indications or
              populations, off-label use, or misuse.

           e. The strengths, weaknesses, and uncertainties of the evidence should be considered
              when formulating the benefit-risk evaluation. Describe how uncertainties in the
              benefits and risks impact the evaluation. Limitations of the assessment should be
              discussed.

6.35.3.3   Provide a clear explanation of the methodology and reasoning used to develop the
           benefit-risk evaluation:

           a. The assumptions, considerations, and judgement or weights that support the
              conclusions of the benefit-risk evaluation should be clear.

           b. If a formal quantitative or semi-quantitative assessment of benefit risk is
              provided, a summary of the methods should be included.

           c. Economic considerations (e.g. cost-effectiveness) should not be considered in the
              benefit-risk evaluation.
6.35.3.4   When there is important new information or an ad hoc PSUR/PBRER has been
           requested, a detailed benefit-risk analysis should be presented based on cumulative
           data. Conversely, where little new information has become available during the
           reporting interval, the primary focus of the benefit-risk evaluation might consist of an
           evaluation of updated interval safety data.

6.36 Conclusions and actions
6.36.1     A PSUR/PBRER should conclude with the implications of any new information that
           arose during the reporting interval in terms of the overall evaluation of benefit-risk
           for each authorised indication, as well as for relevant subgroups, if appropriate.

6.36.2   Based on the evaluation of the cumulative safety data and the benefit-risk analysis,
         the Marketing Authorisation Holder should assess the need for changes to the
         reference product information and propose changes as appropriate.

6.36.3   In addition, and as applicable, the conclusions should include preliminary proposal(s)
         to optimise or further evaluate the benefit-risk balance for further discussion with the
         Agency. This may include proposals for additional risk minimisation activities.

6.36.4   For products with a pharmacovigilance or risk management plan, the proposals
         should also be considered for incorporation into the pharmacovigilance plan and/or

6.36.5   Based on the evaluation of the cumulative safety data and the benefit-risk analysis,

         need for changes and/or actions, including implications for the approved summary of
         product characteristics for the product(s) for which the PSUR/PBRER is submitted.

6.36.6   Proposed changes to the reference product information should be described in this
         section of the PSUR/PBRER. The appendix should include proposals for summary of
         product information and package leaflet together with information on ongoing
         changes when applicable.

6.38 Quality systems for PSUR/PBRERs at the level of Marketing Authorisation Holders
6.38.1    The information on signals and risks should be mapped in a flow chart.

6.38.2    Marketing Authorisation Holders should have in place structures and processes for
          the preparation, quality control, review and submission of PSUR/PBRERs including
          follow-up during and after their assessment. These structures and processes should be
          described by means of written policies and procedures in the Marketing Authorisation
          Holder's quality system (see Chapter 1).

6.38.3    There are a number of areas in the pharmacovigilance process that can directly impact
          the quality of PSUR/PBRERs, some examples are case management of spontaneous
          and study reports, literature screening, signal management, additional
          pharmacovigilance and post-marketing research activities, procedures for integration
          of information on benefits and risks from all available data sources and maintenance
          of product information. The quality system should describe the links between the
          processes, the communication channels and the responsibilities with the aim of
          gathering all the relevant information for the production of PSUR/PBRERs. There
          should be documented procedures including quality control checks in place to check
          the accuracy and completeness of the data presented in the PSUR/PBRERs.

6.39 Submission and assessment of PSUR/PBRERs by the Agency
6.39.1   PSUR/PBRERs shall be submitted electronically [through the NAFDAC
         pharmacovigilance portal / to the e-mail address designated by the Agency],
         accompanied by a covering letter signed by the QPPV in Nigeria that states the name,
         strength and dosage form of the product exactly as registered, the NAFDAC
         registration number, the IBD, the DLP, the reporting interval, the sequence number of
         the report and the risk category of the product. Submissions that do not meet these
         requirements, or that do not follow the format in 6.3 to 6.37, will not be accepted as
         valid and the Marketing Authorisation Holder will be notified.

6.39.2   The Agency shall acknowledge receipt of a valid PSUR/PBRER within [10] working
         days of submission.

6.39.3   The Pharmacovigilance Directorate shall assess the PSUR/PBRER within [ 30 ]
         working days of acknowledgement. During the assessment, the Agency may request
         additional information or clarification from the Marketing Authorisation Holder, who
         shall respond within [10] calendar days of the request or within the period specified in
         the request; the assessment period is suspended until the response is received.
6.39.4   On completion of the assessment, the Agency shall communicate one or more of the
         following outcomes to the Marketing Authorisation Holder in writing:
         a. The benefit-risk balance remains favourable, and no further action is required;
         b. A request for further information or evaluation, to be provided in the next
            PSUR/PBRER or within a specified period;

         c. A variation to the Certificate of Registration, including changes to the approved
            product information (summary of product characteristics and patient information
            leaflet)

         d. Submission of a new or updated risk management plan, or of additional risk
            minimisation measures;



                                                                                              49
          e. Additional pharmacovigilance activities, including post-authorisation safety
             studies;

          f. A change in the risk category of the product or in the frequency of submission of
             PSUR/PBRERs; or

          g. Referral for regulatory action, including suspension, withdrawal or non-renewal
             of the Certificate of Registration, where the benefit-risk balance is no longer
             favourable.

6.39.5    The Marketing Authorisation Holder shall implement the outcome of the assessment
          within the timelines communicated by the Agency and shall report on its
          implementation in the next PSUR/PBRER. Requests made by the Agency during the
          assessment of a PSUR/PBRER shall be addressed in the subsequent report.

6.39.6    Failure to submit a PSUR/PBRER when due, submission of a report that does not
          meet the requirements of this Chapter, or failure to implement the outcome of an
          assessment constitutes non-compliance with this Guideline. The Agency may in such
          cases issue a compliance directive to the Marketing Authorisation Holder, conduct a
          pharmacovigilance inspection, refuse to renew the Certificate of Registration, and
          take any other regulatory action provided for under the NAFDAC Act and the
          relevant regulations.

6.40 Contractual arrangements and third parties
6.40.1    When Marketing Authorisation Holders are involved in contractual arrangements
          (e.g. licensor-licensee) respective responsibilities for preparation and submission of
          the PSUR/PBRER to the Agency should be clearly specified in the written agreement.

6.40.2    When the preparation of the PSUR/PBRER is delegated to third parties, the
          Marketing Authorisation Holder should ensure that they are subject to a quality
          system compliant with the current legislation. Explicit procedures and detailed
          agreements should exist between the Marketing Authorisation Holder and third
          parties. The agreement may specifically detail the options to audit the PSUR/PBRER
          preparation process.

6.41 Training of staff members related to the PSUR/PBRER process
6.41.1    For all organisations, it is the responsibility of the person responsible for the
          pharmacovigilance system to ensure that the personnel, including pharmacovigilance,
          medicinal and quality personnel involved in the preparation, review, quality control,
          submission and assessment of PSUR/PBRERs are adequately qualified, experienced
          and trained according to the applicable guidelines (e.g. ICH E2C(R2) and Chapter 6).
          The appropriate, specific training for the different processes, tasks and responsibilities
          relating to the PSUR/PBRER should be in place.
