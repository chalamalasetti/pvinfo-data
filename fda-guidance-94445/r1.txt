18   I.       INTRODUCTION
19
20   This guidance is intended to help small businesses understand and comply with FDA’s
21   implementation of the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) and the Council on
22   Environmental Quality (CEQ) regulations for classes of actions for tobacco products as amended
23   by this final rule. FDA has prepared this Small Entity Compliance Guide in accordance with
24   section 212 of the Small Business Regulatory Enforcement Fairness Act (Public Law 104-121).
25
26   FDA’s guidance documents, including this guidance, do not establish legally enforceable
27   responsibilities. Instead, guidances describe the Agency’s current thinking on a topic and should
28   be viewed only as recommendations, unless specific regulatory or statutory requirements are
29   cited. The use of the word should in Agency guidances means that something is suggested or
30   recommended, but not required.
31
32   II.      BACKGROUND
33
34   NEPA and the CEQ Regulations require each Federal Agency to assess, as an integral part of its
35   decision-making process, the environmental impacts of any proposed Federal action to ascertain
36   the environmental consequences of that action on the quality of the human environment and to
37   ensure that the interested and affected public is appropriately informed (42 U.S.C. 4332(2); 40

38   CFR 1506.6). FDA regulations governing its responsibilities under NEPA are codified at 21
39   CFR part 25, and the CEQ regulations are codified at 40 CFR parts 1500 to 1508.
40
41   CEQ oversees FDA’s compliance with NEPA. For major Federal actions that may have a
42   significant environmental impact, FDA can prepare either an environmental impact statement
43   (EIS) or an environmental assessment (EA). An EA provides sufficient information and analysis
44   for FDA to determine whether to prepare an EIS or issue a finding of no significant impact
45   (FONSI) (21 CFR 25.20; 40 CFR 1501.4). FDA is responsible for the scope and content of an
46   EA and generally requires an applicant to prepare an EA and make necessary corrections to it (21
47   CFR 25.40(b)).
48
49   Categorically excluded actions refer to a category of actions that have been found not to
50   individually or cumulatively have a significant effect on the quality of the human environment
51   and which do not normally require the preparation of an EA or EIS (40 CFR 1508.4). However,
52   as required under 21 CFR 25.21 and 40 CFR 1508.4, FDA will require preparation of at least an
53   EA for any specific action that normally would be excluded if extraordinary circumstances are
54   present such that the specific proposed action may have the potential to significantly affect the
55   quality of the human environment.
56
57   In the Federal Register of January 23, 2014 (79 FR 3742), FDA issued a notice of proposed
58   rulemaking (NPRM) to amend 21 CFR part 25 to provide categorical exclusions for certain
59   actions related to substantial equivalence (SE) reports, SE exemption requests, and tobacco
60   product applications, and the rescission (order withdrawing an order) or suspension of orders
61   regarding the marketing of tobacco products under the Family Smoking Prevention and Tobacco
62   Control Act (Tobacco Control Act). FDA also proposed to amend its NEPA-implementing
63   regulations to include tobacco products, where appropriate, in light of its new authority under the
64   Tobacco Control Act. In the Federal Register of September, 24, 2015 (80 FR 57531), FDA
65   published the final rule, codified at 21 CFR part 25.

67   III.     QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
68
69            A.    What are the specific sections of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR)
70            which are impacted by this rule?
71
72   This rule amends §§ 25.15, 25.20, 25.30, 25.35, 25.50, and 25.52 of title 21 of the CFR.
73
74            B.       How does this rule affect part 25 of title 21 of the CFR?
75
76   Part 25 of title 21 provides that all applications or petitions requesting agency action require the
77   submission of an EA or a claim of categorical exclusion. It also clarifies that FDA may refuse to
78   file or approve an application or petition if an applicant provides an insufficient EA. This final
79   rule (1) expands the list of actions that ordinarily require at least the preparation of an EA, to
80   include certain orders relating to tobacco product applications; (2) creates categorical exclusions
81   for certain classes of actions involving tobacco products; and (3) requires applicants to submit an
82   EA or claim of categorical exclusion with their tobacco product applications.

 84            C.    What classes of actions for tobacco products ordinarily require at least the
 85            preparation of an EA?
 86
 87   Section 25.20 lists the following classes of actions that ordinarily require at least the preparation
 88   of an EA, unless categorically excluded under § 25.35 (see III. D. for those classes of actions for
 89   tobacco products that are categorically excluded):
 90
 91                •    Issuance of an order finding a tobacco product substantially equivalent under the
 92                     Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, or granting of a request for an exemption
 93                     under 21 CFR part 1107 from the requirement of demonstrating substantial
 94                     equivalence.
 95                •    Issuance of an order authorizing marketing of a new tobacco product under
 96                     section 910 of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act or an order authorizing
 97                     marketing of a modified risk tobacco product under section 911 of the Federal
 98                     Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act.
 99
100            D.     What classes of actions for tobacco products qualify for a categorical
101            exclusion?
102
103   Section 25.35 lists the following classes of actions that are categorically excluded and, therefore,
104   normally do not require the preparation of an EA or an EIS:
105
106                •    Issuance of an order finding a tobacco product substantially equivalent under
107                     section 910(a)(2)(B) of the FD&C Act (provisional SE order);
108                •    Denial of a request for an exemption under 21 CFR part 1107 from the
109                     requirement of demonstrating substantial equivalence;
110                •    Issuance of a not substantially equivalent (NSE) order under section 910(a) of the
111                     FD&C Act;
112                •    Issuance of an order finding that a product may not be introduced or delivered for
113                     introduction into interstate commerce under section 910(c) of the FD&C Act;
114                •    Issuance of an order finding that a modified risk tobacco product may not be
115                     introduced or delivered for introduction into interstate commerce under section
116                     911 of the FD&C Act;
117                •    Rescission (order withdrawing an order) or temporary suspension of an order
118                     authorizing the marketing of a new tobacco product;
119                •    Rescission of a modified risk tobacco product (MRTP) authorization order; and
120                •    Rescission of an order granting an exemption from the requirement of
121                     demonstrating substantial equivalence.
122
123            E.     What information must be provided to FDA to request a categorical
124            exclusion?
125
126   A claim of categorical exclusion must be submitted in accordance with 21 CFR 25.15. Section
127   25.15 requires that the claim of categorical exclusion include: (1) A statement of compliance
128   with the categorical exclusion criteria and (2) a statement that, to the submitter's knowledge, no
129   extraordinary circumstances exist.

131            F.     What if I qualify for a categorical exclusion but I have already submitted my
132            application and CTP has not yet made a determination?
133
134   You may amend your application. Contact the assigned regulatory health project manager for
135   your application for further details.
136
137            G.     Under what circumstances is an application prohibited from requesting
138            categorical exclusion?
139
140   If extraordinary circumstances exist, such that the specific proposed action may have the
141   potential to significantly affect the quality of the human environment, an EA must be submitted
142   for that particular application. To learn more about what qualifies as an extraordinary
143   circumstance, refer to CEQ’s, "Final Guidance for Federal Departments and Agencies on
144   Establishing, Applying, and Revising Categorical Exclusions Under the National Environmental
145   Policy Act," 76 FR 3843, January 21, 2011.
146
147            H.       When will applicants have to submit an EA
148
149   Section 25.15 requires that all applications or petitions requesting agency action include the
150   submission of an EA or a claim of categorical exclusion. Thus, applicants must include an EA in
151   their applications seeking the actions listed in III.C or a claim of categorical exclusion. The EA
152   must be prepared in accordance with 21 CFR 25.40, unless the action qualifies for a categorical
153   exclusion. Under § 25.15, an applicant’s failure to submit an adequate EA for an application or
154   petition requesting agency action of a type specified in § 25.20, unless the agency can determine
155   that the action qualifies for exclusion under § 25.35, is sufficient grounds for FDA to refuse to
156   file or approve the application or petition. Per § 25.15, an EA adequate for filing is one that
157   addresses the relevant environmental issues and an EA adequate for approval is one that contains
158   sufficient information to enable the agency to determine whether the proposed action may
159   significantly affect the quality of the human environment. More information on environmental
160   assessments can be found in 21 CFR part 25.
