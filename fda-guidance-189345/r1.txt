42   II. Background
43   FDA has historically recognized the benefits of harmonization with other regulatory authorities
44   and, over time, has taken a number of actions to promote consistency with its international
45   regulatory counterparts.
46
47   The QS regulation included requirements related to the methods used in, and the facilities and
48   controls used for, designing, manufacturing, packaging, labeling, storing, installing, and
49   servicing of devices intended for human use. These requirements have been effective in
50   providing assurance that devices are safe and effective and otherwise in compliance with the Act.
51
52   In 1996, ISO issued the first version of ISO 13485, “Quality Systems—Medical Devices—
53   Particular Requirements for the Application of ISO 9001,” as a voluntary consensus standard to
54   specify, in conjunction with the application of ISO 9001, the QMS requirements for the design
55   and development and, when relevant, installation and servicing of medical devices.
56
57   Over time, the requirements in ISO 13485 have become more closely aligned with the
58   requirements set forth in FDA’s QS regulation. This alignment and similarity are particularly
59   true for the 2016 version of ISO 13485. Recognizing this progression, FDA realized an
60   opportunity for regulatory harmonization by amending 21 CFR part 820 to incorporate by
61   reference the QMS requirements of ISO 13485 (2016 edition) and, thereby, replace the QS
62   regulation with the new QMSR. As such, the QMSR incorporated by reference the 2016 version
63   of ISO 13485 and the 2015 version of Clause 3 of ISO 9000. 6 FDA finalized a rule entitled,
64   Medical Devices; Quality System Regulation Amendments on February 2, 2024. Effective
65   February 2, 2026, 21 CFR part 820, will be revised to the following sections: 7

 86   III. Overview
 87   This draft guidance document is intended to assist medical device manufacturers both to prepare
 88   and maintain a QMS and to provide the information required to be included in marketing
 89   submissions regarding a QMS, in line with the QMSR. In addition, this draft guidance is
 90   intended to make recommendations regarding information that may help FDA determine
 91   compliance with the QMSR.
 92
 93   The Act requires that certain marketing submissions contain a “full description” of the methods
 94   used in, and the facilities and controls used for, the manufacture, processing, and when relevant,
 95   packing and installation of such device. 8 This information needs to be in sufficient detail so that
 96   FDA can make a knowledgeable assessment of the quality controls used in producing the
 97   medical device. FDA shall deny approval of an application if, based upon the submission and
 98   any other information before FDA with respect to the device, FDA finds that the methods used
 99   in, or the facilities and/or controls used for, the manufacture, processing, and when relevant,
100   packing or installation of the device do not conform to good manufacturing practices. 9
101
102   In general, FDA recommends that submitters provide copies of written procedures, summaries,
103   reports, or lists of items related to the QMSR as part of their FDA submissions. The QMSR
104   requires firms to implement, establish, and maintain many of the items described in this draft
105   guidance. Where the QMSR does not explicitly require such information to be documented, FDA
106   considers the information to be the type that submitters are likely to create and maintain as part
107   of the QMS, and as such recommends that it be included in a marketing submission that is the
108   subject of this draft guidance. Including such information in the submission may reduce or
109   eliminate the need for FDA to request additional information to determine compliance with the

224            A.      Quality management system
225   ISO 13485 Clause 4 and its subclauses 19 describe the basic expectations for how an organization
226   establishes its QMS and emphasizes that risk-based decision making is critical to implementing
227   the requirements of ISO 13485. FDA expects that the documents described in Clause 4 and its
228   subclauses will provide a full description of the QMS and will enable FDA to evaluate a
229   manufacturer’s ability to comply with the QMSR.
230                    (1)      Basic expectations for establishing a QMS
231   ISO 13485 Subclause 4.1 establishes the general requirements for a QMS. Subclauses 4.1.1-4.1.6
232   set forth the basic elements that a manufacturer must document in its QMS. A full description of
233   a QMS in a marketing submission will address how the manufacturer meets those requirements.
234   FDA recommends that a submitter include the following information in its marketing
235   submissions:
236
237        •   A summary of the risk-based approach(es) used to control the processes that make up the
238            organization’s QMS. This summary should explain how the submitter addresses the
239            following:
240                • The risk-based approach(es) used to control the appropriate processes that make
241                   up the organization’s QMS, as described in Subclause 4.1.2(b),
242                • The organization’s documented methods for monitoring and ensuring control over
243                   outsourced processes that affect product conformity to requirements. As described
244                   in Subclause 4.1.5, the controls should be proportionate to the risk involved and
245                   the ability of the external party to meet the requirements, and
246                • The risk-based approach and activities the organization uses for initial validation
247                   and revalidation after QMS software changes, as described in Subclause 4.1.6.
248
249                    (2)      General QMS Documentation
250   ISO 13485 Subclause 4.2 requires an organization to document certain general QMS information
251   and processes. FDA expects that a full description of that documentation and the processes that
252   maintain the documents in a marketing submission will include:
253
254        •   Documented statements of its quality policy and quality objectives,
255        •   A quality manual, which should address the scope of the QMS, describe documented or
256            referenced QMS procedures, provide a description of the interactions between QMS
257            processes, and include an outline of the QMS documentation structure,
258        •   Medical device files, and
259        •   A full description of the document and record control processes that maintain the
260            documentation described in Subclause 4.2.
261
262   Note: 21 CFR 820.35 sets forth additional requirements for the content of specific records.
263   Please see Section VIII of this draft guidance.

305                     (2)      Work Environment and Contamination Control
306   ISO 13485 Subclause 6.4 and its subclauses set forth an organization’s responsibilities for
307   documenting the work environment needed to achieve conformity to product requirements and
308   contamination control processes.
309
310   The controls for the work environment and contamination control may vary depending on the
311   type of product, the associated manufacturing processes, sterility status, and with consideration
312   to the extent to which the product contact with the environment may adversely impact product
313   cleanliness.
314
315   For example, for an implanted device, the final cleanliness of the implant surface affects medical
316   safety performance and is crucial for that device to perform as intended. For other devices, such
317   as electronically controlled medical devices, protections in the work environment against
318   electrostatic discharge, or other sources of damage or contamination of the electronic
319   components may be crucial to the device. For many devices, controls for (1) the production
320   environment for air cleanliness, including particulate and bioburden, may be applied in the form
321   of a defined "cleanroom" or controlled environment and classification, and (2) personnel (such as
322   gowning, gloves, personal hygiene) may apply, because preventing contamination with
323   microorganisms or particulate matter may prevent contamination of the product. Therefore, as
324   for other sections of this draft guidance, the risk management documentation should clearly
325   define the type and extent of controls to be exercised for the subject device.
326
327   As such, a marketing submission containing a full description of the documentation required by
328   Subclause 6.4 should contain the following information:
329
330        •   Documents that identify the requirements for the work environment needed to achieve
331            conformity to the product requirements, as described in Subclause 6.4.1.
332        •   Documents that describe the plan, procedures, and arrangements to control contaminated
333            or potentially contaminated product, including control of contamination with
334            microorganisms or particulate matter, to prevent conditions that could adversely affect
335            the device, consistent with Subclause 6.4.2.
336        •   The overarching (main or top level) documents that apply to the device, if there are
337            multiple applicable procedures. Additionally, submitters should identify (list) the others
338            (i.e., next document tier or layer down). FDA may interactively request select examples
339            of next layer/tier documents, where appropriate, to complete/facilitate its review.
340        •   Either within this section, or under Subclauses 7.5.6 and 7.5.7, as discussed in this draft
341            guidance, the related validation plan, associated validation protocol and report to
342            represent an example 21 of the environmental and/or contamination control
343            systems/processes (e.g., cleaning process, cleanroom qualification) applicable for the
344            subject device.

812   IX. 21 CFR 820.45 Device labeling and packaging controls
813   The QMSR describes device labeling and packaging controls required in addition to those noted
814   in ISO 13485 Subclause 7.5.1 Control of production and service provision. More specifically, as
815   set forth in ISO 13485, manufacturers shall document and maintain procedures that provide a
816   detailed description of the activities to ensure the integrity, inspection, storage, and operations
817   for labeling and packaging during the customary conditions of processing, storage, handling,
818   distribution, and, as appropriate, use of the device. As such, in its marketing submission, the
819   submitter should provide the procedure(s) that fulfill the requirements for device labeling and
820   packaging control described in 21 CFR 820.45(a)-(c) for the subject device.
