13   I.      INTRODUCTION
14
15   This guidance provides recommendations to sponsors about using minimal residual disease
16   (MRD) and complete response (CR) in multiple myeloma as primary endpoints in trials
17   evaluating drug and biological products intended to treat patients with multiple myeloma (MM)
18   to support approval under accelerated approval. 2 For the purpose of this guidance, the MRD
19   endpoint refers to MRD negativity rate as assessed in the bone marrow by either flow cytometry-
20   or sequencing-based methods in patients who have achieved a CR. 3 The definition of CR
21   includes patients who achieved CR or stringent CR. 4
22
23   This guidance does not address the use of MRD or CR for patient selection, enrichment,
24   stratification in clinical trials or to guide treatment decisions. This guidance also does not address
25   the use of MRD or CR in disease settings other than MM. General recommendations on the use
26   of MRD as a biomarker to support marketing approval of drug and biological products for
27   treating specific hematologic malignancies have been discussed in the guidance for industry
28   Hematologic Malignancies: Regulatory Considerations for Use of Minimal Residual Disease in
29   Development of Drug and Biological Products for Treatment (January 2020). In addition, this
30   guidance does not address the statutory or regulatory standards for accelerated approval. The
31   draft guidance Accelerated Approval-Expedited Program for Serious Conditions (December
32   2024) and the guidance Expedited Programs for Serious Conditions—Drugs and Biologics (May
33   2014) discuss these topics.
34
35   In general, FDA’s guidance documents do not establish legally enforceable responsibilities.
36   Instead, guidance documents describe the Agency’s current thinking on a topic and should be
37   viewed only as recommendations, unless specific regulatory or statutory requirements are cited.

41   II.     BACKGROUND
42
43   MM is a plasma cell malignancy and accounts for 18% of all hematologic malignancies in the
44   United States. 5 Since 2003, there have been more than 20 new drug or biological products
45   approved for the treatment of multiple myeloma. These new therapeutics combined with
46   advancements in supportive care have significantly improved outcomes for patients with
47   multiple myeloma. As of 2022, the median overall survival (OS) is anticipated to be 7-10 years
48   for patients with newly diagnosed multiple myeloma. 6 In multiple myeloma, accelerated
49   approval based on an endpoint of overall response rate (ORR, defined as a partial response or
50   better) supported by duration of response has expedited access to new therapies. However, the
51   overall response rates observed with new therapeutics have surpassed 60-70% in the relapsed or
52   refractory setting 7,8,9 and 90% in the newly diagnosed setting. 10 With the improved outcomes
53   observed in this disease area, demonstrating statistically significant difference in overall response
54   rates may require infeasibly large clinical trials. Additionally, more sensitive response
55   assessments will allow for continued expeditious drug development.
56
57   MRD, which is generally assessed in the bone marrow by either flow cytometry- or sequencing-
58   based methods with a minimum sensitivity to detect one tumor cell in 100,000 normal cells, can
59   further quantify the depth of response to treatment beyond ORR or CR. MRD is a recognized
60   prognostic biomarker in MM; patients who attain MRD-negativity 11 have improved long-term
61   outcomes. The 2016 International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG) response criteria
62   incorporated standardized definitions of MRD-negative response, which has resulted in greater
63   inclusion of these assessments in clinical trials. 12,13 MRD has been assessed in numerous MM
64   trials, often as an exploratory endpoint or as a secondary endpoint with control of Type I error. 14
65
66   In this treatment landscape, there has been interest in the use of MRD as a primary endpoint for
67   clinical trials intended to support regulatory decision-making.
68
69   Multiple groups have performed pooled analyses of clinical trial data to assess the relationship
70   between MRD and long-term outcomes (i.e., Progression-Free Survival (PFS) and Overall
71   Survival (OS)). FDA also performed a pooled analysis of data submitted to the Agency to
72   evaluate this relationship. These analyses were discussed at an Oncology Drug Advisory

 81        III.        MRD AS AN ENDPOINT FOR ACCELERATED APPROVAL
 82
 83               A.       General Drug Development Considerations
 84
 85                    •   MRD can be used as an endpoint to support accelerated approval in MM based on
 86                        single-arm trials or randomized trials. Randomized trials are preferred, as they
 87                        provide robust assessments for comparative safety. Given that time-to-event
 88                        endpoints such as PFS and OS are also interpretable in randomized trials,
 89                        sponsors can conduct one trial evaluating MRD for accelerated approval, with the
 90                        trial continuing to evaluate later time-to-event endpoints (e.g., PFS/OS) to support
 91                        traditional approval. Randomized trials are recommended for evaluating
 92                        combination regimens.
 93
 94                    •   The control arm should ensure equipoise and be consistent with standard of care
 95                        in the United States.
 96
 97                    •   The randomized trial should be designed to adequately assess long-term clinical
 98                        endpoints such as PFS or ORR as key objectives. Additionally, even if not a key
 99                        objective, OS should be evaluated as a secondary endpoint or as a safety endpoint.
100
101                    •   In a randomized trial, prior to conducting an analysis of the response endpoint
102                        used to support accelerated approval, the trial should be completely enrolled
103                        to prevent circumstances that may jeopardize the trial results or trial integrity. For
104                        example, there may be excessive drop outs in the control arm(s) if results are
105                        inadvertently unblinded at the time of the response endpoint analysis.
106
107                    •   Sponsors should adequately justify the assumed magnitude of treatment
108                        difference for MRD negativity rate between the arms in randomized trials. This
109                        justification can be based on data derived from a meta-analytic approach or data
110                        from the literature and should also consider the toxicity of the therapy to inform
111                        benefit-risk.
112
113                    •   Cross-trial comparisons to historical controls to assess whether the observed
114                        treatment effect represents an improvement over available therapy is

131              B.       Trial Design and Statistical Considerations
132
133                       1.     Patient Population
134
135              •    Use of MRD as an endpoint should be appropriately justified in the specific MM
136                   patient population/disease setting. Currently, there are insufficient data to consider the
137                   use of MRD as an endpoint to support accelerated approval in the maintenance setting
138                   and in populations such as smoldering MM, monoclonal gammopathy of
139                   undetermined significance [MGUS], extramedullary disease.
140
141              •    If the sponsor plans to use MRD as an endpoint to support accelerated approval in
142                   MM trials based on a biomarker-selected population, whether to select patients with a
143                   higher risk of recurrence (i.e., prognostic biomarker) or to select patients more likely
144                   to favorably respond (i.e., predictive biomarker), additional information may be
145                   needed regarding the natural history, anticipated outcomes, etc., in that patient
146                   population, to aid in determination of the appropriateness of using MRD as an
147                   endpoint.
148
149
150                       2.     Assessments
151
152              •    Trials designed with MRD as a primary endpoint should enroll patients with
153                   measurable disease as per standard criteria.
154
155              •    The protocol should clearly indicate the assessment schedule for MRD endpoints and
156                   include justification for the proposed schedule.
157

158   •   The protocol should ensure robust data collection for the MRD endpoints with
159       minimal missing data.
160
161   •   In randomized trials, the assessment schedule and frequency should be similar across
162       the arms to minimize bias.
163
164   •   Bone marrow aspirate assessment is required to assess achievement of CR to
165       determine MRD negativity. The protocol should include clear instructions to ensure
166       adequate collection of bone marrow aspirate samples to permit a robust assessment of
167       MRD.
168
169   •   In general, we recommend an independent review committee for assessment of CR.
170
171   •   MRD-negative response should be assessed at the time of CR or within a specific
172       time window of CR response (e.g., achievement of MRD negativity within +/- 3
173       months of CR).
174
175   •   The timepoint for the analysis of the primary endpoint for MRD negativity should be
176       prespecified and justified (e.g., 9-month or 12-month timepoint, or best MRD).
177
178   •   MRD negativity should be assessed at a threshold of at least 1 in 105 residual tumor
179       cells; alternate thresholds for assessing MRD negativity should be appropriately
180       justified.
181
182   •   Using bone marrow MRD-based definitions to identify relapse can be challenging
183       since it would require frequent marrow sampling. It may be more practical to monitor
184       for progressive disease based on standard disease response criteria.
185
186   •   Currently, there are limited data on the use of imaging based MRD response
187       endpoints or sustained MRD negativity rate to support their use as a primary MRD
188       endpoint. Sponsors may consider including imaging based MRD response or
189       sustained MRD negativity rate as secondary or exploratory endpoints.
190
191          3.      Statistical Considerations
192
193   •   The primary analysis of MRD should be based on the stated study objectives and
194       should be detailed in a study protocol and the statistical analysis plan (SAP) to define
195       the primary metric for decision-making based on this endpoint.
196
197       o The primary analysis should be based on a prespecified threshold (e.g., ≤1 in 105)
198       o The summary measure to be used for the primary analysis should be prespecified.
199         Generally, the point estimate of the MRD negativity rate at a prespecified
200         timepoint should be calculated, along with its exact 95% confidence interval, for
201         the pre-specified cohort or treatment arm.
202       o In a randomized trial, the difference in rates between treatment arms should be
203         assessed, along with the 95% confidence interval. Any other measures to quantify

205               prespecified, and methods for analyzing these treatment effect measures should be
206               described and justified.
207
208                      Appropriate Type I error control should be prespecified, to include all
209                       statistical tests for MRD or any additional endpoints in the trial for which
210                       claims are intended. The use of one- or two-sided tests of statistical

213                       or an exact version with stratification factors used at randomization.

215       •   Key assumptions and justification should be detailed in the SAP, as well as any other
216           key considerations for statistical analyses, such as the following:
217
218           o The hypotheses that are to be tested and/or the treatment effects that are to be
219             estimated.
220           o The denominator used for the rate calculations. The denominator for the analysis
221             of MRD response should be all treated patients (single-arm trial) or the intent-to-
222             treat (ITT) population (randomized trial), and the numerator should be all patients
223             who achieved the required level of MRD negativity.
224           o The timepoint of the primary MRD analysis and any other key analyses.
225           o Strategies for handling of missing data and any intercurrent events.
226           o Known subgroups should be prospectively identified in a study, and a plan for
227             adequate data collection within the subgroups should be specified. These
228             subgroups should be justified, and assumptions about MRD within these
229             subgroups should be included.
230           o In a randomized trial, analyses of PFS and OS should be pre-specified. If MRD is
231             to be assessed at an earlier timepoint, OS and PFS data at that time point may be
232             immature; however, an evaluation of PFS to assess futility (with prespecified
233             stopping boundaries) and OS to assess safety, may be appropriate at the time of
234             the primary MRD assessment. Justification should be provided for appropriate
235             timing of the PFS and OS analyses, which may occur at later timepoints.
236
237   •   Provide multiple supplementary and/or sensitivity analyses of MRD based on different
238       methods and/or assumptions to evaluate the robustness of the primary analysis results as
239       applicable. For example:
240
241           o Alternate thresholds for negativity (e.g., ≤1 in 104, ≤1 in 106).
242           o Patients in CR or better as the denominator for MRD negativity instead of ITT
243           o MRD negativity regardless of attaining CR
244
245   •   Patients with missing MRD information should be considered as missing in the primary
246       analysis and included in the denominator for assessment of MRD response.
247
248           o Imputation methods may be used to address missing MRD data for exploratory
249             purposes, but these methods should be prespecified and justified in the SAP.

252            C.      Assay Considerations for MRD Evaluation
253
254        •   The assay used to assess MRD (e.g., Next Generation Sequencing (NGS), Next
255            Generation Flow (NGF)) should be appropriately validated to support regulatory
256            decisions.
257
258        •   When including an MRD assay in a clinical study protocol for the purposes of using
259            MRD as an endpoint, sponsors should consider the MRD assay methodology and
260            validation data supporting the assay. Sponsors should also consider the proposed context
261            of use of the MRD assay in the study and the analytical performance information
262            supportive of such use such as accuracy, precision, limit of detection, limit of blank, limit
263            of quantitation, linearity, reagent and sample stability, analytical specificity, and as
264            applicable, appropriate DNA input.
265
266        •   The assay analytical sensitivity should generally be at least one log below the pre-
267            specified MRD negativity threshold; however, the precision of the assay at the MRD
268            negativity threshold should be considered when determining which MRD negative
269            threshold is acceptable for the assay technology (e.g., NGS). The proposed MRD
270            negativity threshold cutoff should be discussed with FDA.
271
272        •   With sequencing assays (e.g., NGS), high baseline calibration failure rates can impact
273            data interpretation. Calibration failure may be due to low disease burden, insufficient
274            DNA sample, and hemodilution. Sponsors should ensure robust data collection to reduce
275            calibration failures and missing MRD data.
276
277        •   Requirements under 21 CFR Part 812 may apply when investigational devices are used to
278            detect MRD in the trial. For additional information regarding investigational device use
279            and requirements under the IDE regulation, see the guidance for industry In Vitro
280            Diagnostic (IVD) Device Studies – Frequently Asked Questions. 18
281
282        •   FDA encourages sponsors to meet with the therapeutic product center (i.e., CDER or
283            CBER) early in development regarding the MRD assay to ensure adequate data collection
284            and analytical validation.
285
286            For additional information on the use of MRD as an efficacy endpoint, see the guidance
287            for industry Hematologic Malignancies: Regulatory Considerations for Use of Minimal
288            Residual Disease in Development of Drug and Biological Products for Treatment
289            (January 2020).

291        IV.     CR AS AN ENDPOINT FOR ACCELERATED APPROVAL
292
293   CR allows for a quantification of the depth of response to treatment beyond ORR. CR is a
294   recognized prognostic biomarker; patients who attain CR have improved long-term outcomes. 19
295   The 2016 International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG) response criteria incorporate
296   standardized definitions of CR 20 and CR rate has been assessed in numerous MM trials, often as
297   a secondary endpoint with control of Type I error.
298
299   FDA conducted a pooled analysis of clinical trial data, which demonstrated an association
300   between CR and long-term outcomes (i.e., PFS and OS). Like MRD, CR rate can be used as an
301   endpoint to support accelerated approval in trials evaluating drug and biological products
302   intended to treat patients with multiple myeloma.
303
304   The general principles for the design and analysis of clinical trials that use MRD as an endpoint
305   for accelerated approval outlined in Section III A and III B also apply to trials that propose CR
306   rate as an endpoint for accelerated approval.
307
308   Below are additional considerations specific to using the CR endpoint:
309
310                •    The CR endpoint should be assessed as overall CR rate rather than CR rate at a
311                     specific timepoint.
312                •    Since the potential utility of CR as an endpoint is similar to that of ORR, adequate
313                     follow-up is needed in order to establish that the durability of CR is meaningful.
314                •    Durability for CR should generally be assessed from time to achievement of CR
315                     to progression or death.
316
317
318        V.      REGULATORY CONSIDERATIONS
319
320   For products granted accelerated approval based on MRD or CR endpoints, verification of
321   clinical benefit will generally be required, and timely verification of clinical benefit is critical.
322   Generally, the confirmatory trial(s) to verify the anticipated clinical benefit of a product should
323   be underway prior to accelerated approval. 21 Sponsors may take one of the following approaches
324   to verify clinical benefit:
325
326                •    Two-trial model for accelerated approval – this approach involves use of a single
327                     arm trial using MRD and CR endpoints for accelerated approval, followed by a
328                     randomized study using an endpoint that directly measures clinical benefit (e.g.,
329                     PFS or OS) to verify the anticipated clinical benefit for traditional approval.
