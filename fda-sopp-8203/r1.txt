I.           Purpose

             This Standard Operating Policy and Procedure (SOPP) serves as a guide for the
             Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research (CBER) staff for the administrative
             processing and review management of charging requests (i.e., cost recovery
             requests) for an investigational product that is the subject of an investigational new
             drug application (IND) or investigational device exemption (IDE) under 21 Code of
             Federal Regulations (CFR) 312 and 21 CFR 812, respectively.

II.          Scope

             A. This SOPP applies to charging for investigational drugs under INDs or charging
                for investigational medical devices under IDEs.

             B. This SOPP does not address general IND policy and procedures. Refer to SOPP
                8217: Administrative Processing and Review Management Procedures for
                Investigational New Drug Applications for policies and procedures applicable to
                all INDs.

             C. This SOPP does not address general IDE policy and procedures. Refer to SOPP
                8732: Administrative Processing and Review of Investigational Device Exemption
                (IDE) and Amendments to Original Applications for policies and procedures
                applicable to all IDEs.

       D. This SOPP does not apply to clinical trials that are exempt by law from the IND

          Application).

III.   Background

       A. Background for INDs

          1. The FDA's rules regarding charging for investigational drugs under INDs have
             evolved from the 1987 charging rule to the current final rule published in
             2009. The 2009 final rule sets the current charging regulations in 21 CFR
             312.8.

          2. The charging regulation for INDs aims to:

              a. Address the situation in which there is a very high cost associated with
                 making a drug that has a potential significant advantage over available
                 products available to clinical trial subjects and that the drug cost prevents
                 continued development unless the cost of the drug can be recouped
                 during development.

              b. Provide a reasonable incentive for sponsors to make investigational drugs
                 available for treatment use under the expanded access INDs.

              c. Ensure charges for investigational drugs do not restrict patient access or
                 compromise the clinical studies.

              d. Prevent sponsors from inappropriately shifting the normal financial cost
                 associated with new drug development onto patients.

              e. Prevent commercialization of unapproved drugs and unapproved
                 indications.

       B. Background for IDEs

          1. IDE regulations allow sponsors to charge for an investigational device,
             however, the charge cannot exceed an amount necessary to recover the
             costs of manufacture, research, development, and handling of the
             investigational device (21 CFR 812.7(b)). A sponsor must justify the proposed
             charges for the device in the IDE application, state the amount to be charged,
             and explain why the charge does not constitute commercialization (21 CFR
             812.20(b)(8)). The informed consent must include a description of any
             additional costs to the subject that may result from participation in the
             research (21 CFR 50.25(b)(3)).

          2. Likewise, as noted in the preamble (45 FR 3751) to these regulations,
             finalized in 1980, the FDA/CBER is implicitly empowered to determine

  Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research                                      SOPP 8203


             whether the charge sought by the IDE sponsor is “excessive”. The preamble
             (45 FR 3751) suggests FDA/CBER staff should understand device market
             realities but also remain cognizant of ethical considerations, stating that
             “[m]any devices, unlike most drugs, are expensive to manufacture, and it is
             unrealistic to prohibit a sponsor from recovering costs. It is a well-established
             ethical principle, however, that no profit should be made on experimental
             devices.

         3. Medical devices regulated under IND/NDA/BLA follow the policy and process
            for IND cost recovery.

IV.   Definitions

      A. Definitions related to IND charging:

         1. Charging request (Cost Recovery Request) – A request from an IND
            sponsor to seek FDA’s prior authorization to charge for an investigational
            drug used in a clinical trial under an IND, or used for treatment under an
            expanded access IND.

         2. Direct Cost – Costs incurred by a sponsor that can be specifically and
            exclusively attributed to providing the drug for the investigational use for
            which FDA has authorized cost recovery. Direct costs include costs per unit to
            manufacture the drug (e.g., raw materials, labor, and non-reusable supplies
            and equipment used to manufacture the quantity of drug needed for the use
            for which charging is authorized) or costs to acquire the drug from another
            manufacturing source, and direct costs to ship and handle (e.g., store) the
            drug (21 CFR 312.8(d)(1)(i)).

         3. Expanded Access IND – A request to use an investigational drug or
            biological product to diagnose, monitor, or treat a serious or immediately life-
            threatening disease or condition when there is no comparable or satisfactory
            alternative therapy to diagnose, monitor, or treat the disease or condition.
            There are three categories of expanded access INDs: individual patient,
            including individual non-emergency and individual emergency use,
            intermediate-size patient populations, and treatment use (21 CFR 312,
            Subpart I).

         4. Extraordinary cost – Under the context of 21 CFR 312.8(b)(1)(iii), the cost of
            a drug may be considered extraordinary to a sponsor because of
            manufacturing complexity, scarcity of a natural resource, the large quantity of
            the drug needed (e.g., based on the size or duration of the trial), or some
            combination of these or other extraordinary circumstances (e.g., resources
            available to a sponsor) (21 CFR 312.8(b)(1)(iii)).

         5. Independent Certified Public Accountant – Certified public accountant who
            is qualified to make the required determinations for charging and not an

                                                                                   Page 3 of 16
Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research                                       SOPP 8203


           employee of the company or institution seeking to charge for an
           investigational drug (see FDA’s Guidance for Industry: Charging for
           Investigational Drugs Under an IND, Questions and Answers).

       6. Indirect Cost – Costs incurred primarily to produce the drug for commercial
          sale (e.g., costs for facilities and equipment used to manufacture the supply
          of investigational drug, but that are primarily intended to produce large
          quantities of drug for eventual commercial sale) and research and
          development, administrative, labor, or other costs that would be incurred even
          if the clinical trial or treatment use for which charging is authorized did not
          occur (21 CFR 312.8(d)(ii)).

       7. Investigational New Drug (Investigational Drug) – A new drug or biological
          drug that is used in a clinical investigation. The term also includes a biological
          product that is used in vitro for diagnostic purposes. The terms
          “investigational drug” and “investigational new drug” are deemed to be
          synonymous for purposes of this part (21 CFR 312.3).

       8. Investigational New Drug Application (IND) – A request under 21 CFR 312
          from a sponsor to FDA seeking permission to use an unapproved drug or
          biological product for the purpose of clinical investigation or clinical treatment.

       9. Sponsor – A person who takes responsibility for and initiates a clinical
          investigation. The sponsor may be an individual or pharmaceutical company,
          governmental agency, academic institution, private organization, or other
          organization. The sponsor does not actually conduct the investigation unless
          the sponsor is a sponsor-investigator. A person other than an individual that
          uses one or more of its own employees to conduct an investigation that it has
          initiated is a sponsor, not a sponsor-investigator, and the employees are
          investigators (21 CFR 312.3(b)).

   B. Definitions related to IDE charging

       1. Investigational Device Exemption (IDE) Application – An application
          submitted to FDA (21 CFR 812) by a sponsor to use a significant risk device
          in an investigation(s) or to conduct an investigation that involves an exception
          from informed consent under 21 CFR 50.24. An application may also be
          submitted by a sponsor if they were notified by FDA that an IDE application is
          required for an investigation.

       2. Sponsor - A person who initiates, but who does not actually conduct, the
          investigation, that is, the investigational device is administered, dispensed, or
          used under the immediate direction of another individual. A person other than
          an individual that uses one or more of its own employees to conduct an
          investigation that it has initiated is a sponsor, not a sponsor-investigator, and
          the employees are investigators (21 CFR 812.3(n)).

V.   Policy

     A. Policy for Charging INDs

        1. Charging request:

              a. In accordance with 21 CFR 312.8(a), an IND sponsor who wishes to
                 charge for investigational drugs under an IND, including investigational
                 use of its approved drug, must seek FDA’s prior authorization by
                 submitting a charging request to FDA, with the following exceptions:

                i.   Sponsors do not need to obtain FDA authorization to charge for an
                     approved drug obtained from another entity not affiliated with the
                     sponsor for use as part of the clinical trial evaluation (e.g., in a clinical
                     trial of a new use of the approved drug, for use of the approved drug
                     as an active control, or as concomitant therapy) (21 CFR 312.8(a)(1)).

               ii.   Per the preamble to the 2009 final rule: “Costs incurred at a clinical trial
                     site, including pharmacy costs (e.g., the cost to reformulate a drug for
                     infusion), nursing costs (e.g., costs associated with administering a
                     drug and monitoring study subjects), equipment costs (e.g.,
                     intravenous administration sets), and costs for study-related
                     procedures (e.g., chemistry labs, radiographic procedures), can be
                     recovered without prior authorization from FDA.”

              iii.   Sponsors do not need to obtain FDA authorization to charge for its own
                     approved drug when that drug is used as concomitant therapy for an
                     approved use and is not part of the clinical trial evaluation (i.e., the
                     approved drug itself is not being evaluated for an investigational use).

              b. Charging requests must be submitted by IND sponsors.

              c. IND sponsors must obtain written authorization from FDA before charging
                 for INDs.

              d. Charging requests for drugs used in a clinical trial are submitted in an IND
                 amendment to the original IND. Charging requests for an expanded
                 access IND may be submitted as part of an original IND or as an
                 amendment.

              e. A charging request for drugs used in a clinical trial must contain all the
                 information required under 21 CFR 312.8(a), 21 CFR 312.8(b), and 21
                 CFR 312.8(d), including the following information:

                i.   Provide evidence that the drug has a potential clinical benefit that, if
                     demonstrated in the clinical investigation(s), would provide a significant
                     advantage over available products in the diagnosis, treatment,


                                                                                       Page 5 of 16
Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research                                           SOPP 8203


                        mitigation, or prevention of a disease or condition (21 CFR
                        312.8(b)(1)(i)).

             ii.        Demonstrate that the data to be obtained from the clinical trial would
                        be essential to establishing that the drug is effective or safe for the
                        purpose of obtaining initial approval of a drug, or would support a
                        significant change in the labeling of an approved drug (e.g., new
                        indication, inclusion of comparative safety information) (21 CFR
                        312.8(b)(1)(ii)).

            iii.        Demonstrate that the clinical trial could not be conducted without
                        charging because the cost of the drug is extraordinary to the sponsor.
                        The cost may be extraordinary due to manufacturing complexity,
                        scarcity of a natural resource, the large quantity of drug needed (e.g.,
                        due to the size or duration of the trial), or some combination of these or
                        other extraordinary circumstances (e.g., resources available to a
                        sponsor) (21 CFR 312.8(b)(1)(iii)).

            iv.         Justify the amount to be charged in accordance with the requirements
                        specified in 21 CFR 312.8(d)(1) and 21 CFR 312.8(d)(3).

                        a) Per 21 CFR 312.8(d)(1), only the direct costs incurred by a sponsor
                           may be recovered.

                        b) Per 21 CFR 312.8(d)(3), supporting documentation for cost
                           recovery calculation must be provided to show that the calculation
                           is consistent with the requirements of 21 CFR 312.8(d)(1). The
                           documentation must be accompanied by a statement that an
                           independent certified public accountant has reviewed and approved
                           the calculations.

             v.         Provide the intended duration for the charge and demonstrate the
                        understanding that unless FDA specifies a shorter period, charging can
                        only continue for the length of the trial (21 CFR 312.8(b)(2)).

           f. A charging request for expanded access use under 21 CFR 312, subpart
              I, must contain all the information required by 21 CFR 312.8(a), 21 CFR
              312.8(c) and 21 CFR 312.8(d), including the following information:

                   i.      Provide reasonable assurance that charging will not interfere with
                           developing the drug for marketing approval (21 CFR 312.8(c)(1)).

                        a) For expanded access under 21 CFR 312.320 (treatment IND or
                           treatment protocol), such assurance must also include information
                           required by 21 CFR 312.8(c)(2), including evidence of sufficient
                           enrollment in any ongoing clinical trial; evidence of adequate
                           progress of the drug for marketing approval; and milestones the
                           sponsor plans to meet in the next year.

                                                                                       Page 6 of 16
Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research                                       SOPP 8203


                 ii.   Justification for the amount to be charged in accordance with 21
                       CFR 312.8(d).

                   a) For an expanded access IND for individual patient under 21 CFR
                      312.310, only the direct costs incurred by a sponsor may be
                      recovered (21 CFR 312.8(d)(1)).

                   b) For an expanded access IND under 21 CFR 312.315 (intermediate-
                      size patient populations) and 21 CFR 312.320 (large patient
                      populations), in addition to the direct costs described in 21 CFR
                      312.8(d)(1)(i), a sponsor may recover the costs of monitoring the
                      expanded access IND or protocol, complying with IND reporting
                      requirements, and other administrative costs directly associated
                      with the expanded access IND (21 CFR 312.8(d)(2)).

                       Note: FDA interprets § 312.8(d)(2) as permitting the sponsor to
                       recover the cost of the fees paid to a third party for administering an
                       intermediate-size patient population or treatment IND or protocol,
                       including any profit for the third party that may be included in the
                       fees. The fees paid to the third party should be included in the
                       calculation for cost recovery that the sponsor provides in its request
                       to charge. In addition, FDA recommends that the sponsor disclose
                       to the patients any relationship it may have with the third party (See
                       FDA’s Guidance for Industry: Charging for Investigational Drugs
                       Under an IND, Questions and Answers Q18).

                   c) Per 21 CFR 312.8(d)(3), supporting documentation for cost
                      recovery calculation must be provided to show that the calculation
                      is consistent with the requirements of 21 CFR 312.8(d)(1) and if
                      applicable 21 CFR 312.8(d)(2). The documentation must be
                      accompanied by a statement that an independent certified public
                      accountant has reviewed and approved the calculations.

                       Note: When the amount to be charged for a drug is simply the
                       amount charged to the expanded access sponsor by a third party
                       who provides the drug to the expanded access sponsor, such that
                       there is no calculation of cost made by the sponsor to which the
                       requirement under § 312.8(d)(3) applies, the expanded access
                       sponsor should provide a copy of the receipt or invoice from the
                       source that provided the drug to the expanded access sponsor to
                       justify the amount to be charged for the drug (see FDA’s Guidance
                       for Industry: Charging for Investigational Drugs Under an IND,
                       Questions and Answers Q13 & Q21)

                iii.   Specify that the charge is limited to the number of patients
                       authorized to receive the drug under the treatment use, if there is a
                       limitation.

Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research                                         SOPP 8203


                   iv.   Provide the intended duration for the charge. Demonstrate the
                         understanding that unless FDA specifies a shorter period, charging
                         for expanded access to an investigational drug may continue for 1
                         year from the time of FDA authorization per 21 CFR 312.8(c)(4)).

           g. If participants in a clinical trial or patients being treated under expanded
              access will be charged for the investigational drug, this information must
              be disclosed in the informed consent document (21 CFR 50.25(b)(3)).
              CBER review team may request this information when needed.

           h. Sponsors must notify FDA of changes to an authorized charge, including
              any requests to increase charges, through an information amendment (21
              CFR 312.31).

           i. If a sponsor for expanded access use wishes to continue charging beyond
              the authorized duration, the sponsor must submit an IND amendment to
              FDA to request reauthorization. FDA recommends that the sponsor submit
              a request to reauthorize charging at least 60 days prior to the expiration of
              the existing authorization. The sponsor must receive written
              reauthorization from FDA before it can continue to charge for the
              investigational drug beyond the period previously authorized.

       2. FDA Review & Authorization

           a. There is no statutory requirement on the time frame for FDA to respond to
              a charge request for INDs. CBER intends to respond to charge requests
              within 30 days of receipt when possible.

           b. Upon review of a charge request submitted by an IND sponsor, CBER will
              authorize the charge if the request meets all applicable requirements
              stated in 21 CFR 312.8.

           c. Duration of authorized charge:

              i.     Charging for an investigational drug for a clinical trial may continue for
                     the entire length of the clinical trial unless FDA specifies a shorter
                     duration. 21 CFR 312.8(b)(2) gives FDA the discretion to specify a
                     duration shorter than the length of the trial. FDA may specify a shorter
                     duration if, for example, there is a particular concern that the charge
                     has the potential to delay the development of a drug for marketing
                     approval. Where there is a concurrent clinical trial still enrolling
                     subjects, the charging rule provides FDA the option to specify a shorter
                     period.

             ii.     Unless FDA specifies a shorter period, charging for expanded access
                     to an investigational drug for treatment use under subpart I of this part
                     may continue for 1 year from the time of FDA authorization (21 CFR
                     312.8(c)(4)).

                                                                                    Page 8 of 16
Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research                                     SOPP 8203


            iii.   If the need for charging will likely change over time, CBER may specify
                   a shorter duration for charging from the outset. It is usually less
                   burdensome to limit the initial charging duration than to subsequently
                   withdraw the charge authorization.

          d. For expanded access, the authorization to charge is limited to the number
             of patients authorized to receive the drug under the treatment use, if there
             is a limitation (21 CFR 312.8(c)(3)).

          e. Although FDA determines whether a sponsor may charge for an
             investigational drug used in a clinical trial or for expanded access, FDA
             does not decide whom the sponsors may charge, and how that charging is
             to be carried out. FDA anticipates that the sponsor would ordinarily charge
             a patient directly or charge a third-party if reimbursement is available.
             FDA’s authorities do not extend to insurance/reimbursement policy for
             investigational drugs.

       3. Reauthorization

          a. Charging for an investigational drug for expanded access use may continue
             for 1 year from the time of FDA authorization unless FDA specifies a shorter
             period. (21 CFR 312.8(c)(4). A sponsor may submit an IND amendment
             and request that FDA reauthorize charging for additional time periods.

          b. When reviewing sponsor requests to reauthorize charging, CBER will
             ensure that the sponsor’s justification for charging continues to meet the
             requirements stated in 21 CFR 312.8, and that the sponsor continues to
             make good faith progress toward FDA/CBER’s marketing approval for the
             product under investigation.

       4. Recordkeeping and Record Retention

          a. Sponsors must comply with documentation requirements specified in 21
             CFR 312, including 21 CFR 312.8 and 21 CFR 312.57.

       5. Withdrawal:

          a. FDA will withdraw authorization to charge if it determines that charging is
             interfering with the development of a drug for marketing approval or that the
             criteria for the authorization are no longer being met per 21 CFR
             312.8(a)(4) (e.g., A small company or academic sponsor were acquired by
             a much larger company, it may no longer face “extraordinary
             circumstances” requiring cost recovery for its product during a clinical study
             and CBER may then reconsider its cost recovery authorization).

   B. Policy for Charging IDEs

  Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research                                     SOPP 8203


         1. Sponsors are prohibited from commercializing any investigational device by
            charging the subjects or investigators for a device a price larger than that
            necessary to recover costs of manufacture, research, development, and
            handling (21 CFR 812.7(b)).

         2. If the device is to be sold, the sponsor shall include in the IDE application the
            amount to be charged and an explanation of why sale does not constitute
            commercialization of the device within the IDE application per 21 CFR
            812.20(b)(8). For IDE for treatment use, if the device is to be sold, the
            sponsor must include in the IDE application, the price to be charged and a
            statement indicating that the price is based on manufacturing and handling
            costs only per 21 CFR 812.36(c)(1)(x).

         3. The sponsor must include a description of any additional costs to the subject
            that may result from participation in the research within the informed consent
            (21 CFR 50.25(b)(3)).

         4. The IDE regulations (21 CFR 812) do not dictate a specific time limit or
            expiration date for recovering authorized costs.

VI.   Responsibilities

      A. Branch Chief (BC) - Ensures that the overall content of reviews is appropriate
         and timelines are met, reviews and approves employee’s review memo’s and
         other documents related to a charge request (e.g., letters to sponsors). Advises
         employees on review issues and keeps upper management informed of
         important issues.

      B. Regulatory Project Manager (RPM)

         1. Notifies the review team, including the clinical and chemistry, manufacturing,
            and controls (CMC) reviewers, that a cost recovery request has been
            submitted.

         2. Communicates with the sponsor on behalf of the review team if more
            information is needed, including required information omitted from the cost
            recovery request.

         3. Coordinates the review of the request by the assigned reviewer(s).

      C. Reviewer(s) for Charging Request (Reviewer)

         1. Evaluates the sponsor’s justification as to why charging is necessary to
            undertake or continue the clinical trial or provide expanded access. Note: The
            clinical and/or CMC reviewers are often assigned this role.

         2. Involves other colleagues/experts as needed (e.g., bioethicists, Office of Chief
            Counsel).

          3. Seeks concurrence from supervisors about whether to approve or deny the
             cost recovery request.

       D. Office Director

          1. Concurs with decision to approve or deny cost recovery requests based on
             staff recommendations or initiates further discussion of the request.

VII.   Procedures

       A. Procedures for IND Charging Request

          1. Follow SOPP 8217: Administrative Processing and Review Management
             Procedures for Investigational New Drug Applications for IND receipt,
             processing, routing, assignment, and characterization. [RPM, Reviewer]

          2. Review the charging request submitted by IND sponsor; ensure the request
             contains all the applicable information and documents required by 21 CFR
             312.8. [RPM, Reviewer]

              a. For a charging request for drugs used in a clinical trial, ensure the request
                 contains all the information required under 21 CFR 312.8(a), 21 CFR
                 312.8(b), and 21 CFR 312.8(d), including the following information:

                 i.   Evidence that the drug has a potential clinical benefit that, if
                      demonstrated in the clinical investigations, would provide a significant
                      advantage over available products in the diagnosis, treatment,
                      mitigation, or prevention of a disease or condition (21 CFR
                      312.8(b)(1)(i)).

                ii.   Information demonstrating that the data to be obtained from the clinical
                      trial would be essential to establishing that the drug is effective or safe
                      for the purpose of obtaining initial approval of a drug, or would support
                      a significant change in the labeling of an approved drug (e.g., new
                      indication, inclusion of comparative safety information (21 CFR
                      312.8(b)(1)(ii)).

               iii.   Information demonstrating that the clinical trial could not be conducted
                      without charging because the cost of the drug is extraordinary to the
                      sponsor. The cost may be extraordinary due to manufacturing
                      complexity, scarcity of a natural resource, the large quantity of drug
                      needed (e.g., due to the size or duration of the trial), or some
                      combination of these or other extraordinary circumstances (e.g.,
                      resources available to a sponsor) (21 CFR 312.8(b)(1)(iii)).

               iv.    Justification for the amount to be charged in accordance with 21 CFR
                      312.8(d)(1) and 21 CFR 312.8(d)(3).
