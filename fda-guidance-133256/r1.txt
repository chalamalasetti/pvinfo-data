18   I.       INTRODUCTION
19
20   The purpose of this guidance is to assist sponsors in the clinical development of locally applied
21   corticosteroid products (including suppositories or products that require an applicator) for the
22   short-term treatment of symptoms associated with internal or external hemorrhoids. Specifically,
23   this guidance describes FDA’s current thinking regarding the recommended attributes of patients
24   for enrollment, efficacy assessments, and safety assessments. 2
25
26   This guidance does not address the clinical development of drugs for the chronic treatment of
27   signs and symptoms associated with internal or external hemorrhoids or drugs for the treatment
28   of an underlying disease.
29
30   In general, FDA’s guidance documents do not establish legally enforceable responsibilities.
31   Instead, guidances describe the Agency’s current thinking on a topic and should be viewed only
32   as recommendations, unless specific regulatory or statutory requirements are cited. The use of
33   the word should in Agency guidances means that something is suggested or recommended, but
34   not required.

37   II.        BACKGROUND
38
39   Hemorrhoids represent one of the most common medical and surgical disease conditions in the
40   United States, accounting for over 2.5 million outpatient evaluations per year. 3 Hemorrhoids are
41   dilated arteriovenous connective tissues that are normally present between the anal mucosa and
42   underlying internal sphincter and are usually categorized as internal or external hemorrhoids
43   based upon their location with respect to the dentate line.
44
45   Because the pathogenesis of hemorrhoids involves anatomic structural changes in the vascular
46   cushions (hemorrhoidal arteriovenous plexus), surrounding supportive muscles, connective
47   tissues, and skin, rectal bleeding commonly associated with internal hemorrhoids is unlikely to
48   be improved with locally applied corticosteroid products. However, many patients have one or
49   more additional perirectal symptoms (e.g., itch, discomfort, pain, or burning) because of
50   secretions from inflamed hemorrhoids that irritate the skin. These perirectal symptoms are
51   common in patients with both external and internal hemorrhoids and may be mitigated by locally
52   applied corticosteroid products.
53
54   Fit-for-purpose 4 patient-reported outcome (PRO) instruments 5 for evaluating symptomatic
55   treatment response in internal or external hemorrhoids should be identified and agreed upon with
56   FDA for regulatory use.

 92   IV.       DEVELOPMENT PROGRAM
 93
 94             A.     Trial Design
 95
 96   Sponsors developing locally applied corticosteroid products for the short-term treatment of
 97   symptoms associated with internal or external hemorrhoids should consider the following for
 98   clinical trial design:
 99
100         •   We recommend a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, parallel
101             group trial design with a prespecified screening period before randomization of patients
102             to confirm eligibility criteria.
103
104         •   The trial duration and timing of efficacy assessments should be guided by the mechanism
105             of action of the drug and its expected onset of action and the time frame in which a
106             clinical benefit is expected to be observed. We recommend a treatment period of at least
107             2 weeks’ duration to assess efficacy.

117          B        Efficacy Considerations
118
119   Sponsors developing locally applied corticosteroid products for the short-term treatment of
120   symptoms associated with internal or external hemorrhoids should consider the following
121   regarding a drug’s efficacy:
122
123      •   For the primary endpoints in phase 3 trials, sponsors should identify a response that
124          represents meaningful improvement from baseline in symptoms, compared with vehicle
125          control, using a well-defined and reliable PRO instrument’s endpoint scores.
126
127      •   Statistically significant but small group-level mean differences may not establish whether
128          the effect is clinically meaningful.
129
130          –    To aid in interpreting the PRO endpoint results, sponsors should propose a range of
131               thresholds that would constitute a clinically meaningful within-patient score change
132               using anchor-based methods (e.g., patient global impression of symptom severity
133               scale as an anchor) in conjunction with empirical cumulative distribution functions
134               (eCDFs) of individual patient changes in scores using data pooled across trial arms
135               with separate curves for each anchor response category.
136
137                  Additionally, sponsors should submit for review a supportive graph (i.e., eCDF)
138                   of within-patient changes in scores from baseline with separate curves for each
139                   treatment arm. The graph will be used to assess whether the treatment effect
140                   occurs in the range that patients consider to be clinically meaningful.

142          C.       PRO Endpoints
143
144   Sponsors developing locally applied corticosteroid products for the short-term treatment of
145   symptoms associated with internal or external hemorrhoids should consider the following when
146   selecting a fit-for-purpose PRO instrument:
147
148      •   FDA encourages sponsors to seek FDA input as early as possible and at important
149          milestones throughout the drug development process to ensure that fit-for-purpose PRO
150          instruments are included in phase 3 trials. For general recommendations regarding PRO
151          instruments and the documents to be provided to FDA for review, see the guidance for
152          industry relevant to patient-reported outcome measures or clinical outcome assessments.

154   •   FDA recommends selecting a PRO instrument based on patient input regarding the most
155       important and bothersome perirectal symptoms of internal or external hemorrhoids (e.g.,
156       itch, discomfort, pain, or burning) and choosing for assessment symptoms that are
157       expected to improve with the treatment. FDA encourages sponsors to first explore using
158       existing PRO instruments for assessing patients’ symptoms before developing a de novo
159       PRO instrument.
160
161       — Examples of patient-reported symptom severity assessment scales that may be fit-for-
162         purpose include an 11-point (i.e., 0 to 10) numeric rating scale or a verbal rating scale
163         (e.g., none, mild, moderate, severe) that asks patients daily to rate their worst
164         experience of one or several specific perirectal symptoms (e.g., itch, discomfort, pain,
165         or burning) over the past 24 hours. Additionally, a frequency scale for one or more of
166         these items may also be considered (e.g., ranging from “none of the time” to “all of
167         the time”).
168
169       — To minimize patient recall error and measurement error, sponsors should use
170         instruments that are administered daily (e.g., 24-hour recall period) and that focus on
171         capturing patients’ symptoms. Patients should complete the PRO instruments at the
172         same time each day (e.g., evening before bedtime). Improvement in, or resolution of,
173         symptoms should be demonstrated for a meaningful, prespecified duration of time
174         (e.g., one week), not based solely on a single day’s assessment.
175
176             For newly developed instruments, or existing instruments that have not been
177              tested in the target patient population, it may be useful for sponsors to test the
178              appropriateness and clarity of any proposed PRO instruments’ instructions, recall
179              period, items, and response options by conducting cognitive interviews with a
180              number of patients (e.g., 8 to 10 patients) matching the target population to
181              decrease the possibility of introducing measurement error because of patients’
182              misunderstanding or incomplete understanding of the PRO instruments.
183
184             When modifying an existing instrument or developing a new PRO, sponsors
185              should consider that phase 2 trials should help inform finalization of scoring
186              algorithms and endpoint definitions. Piloting the proposed PRO instrument in
187              phase 2 trials can provide sponsors an opportunity to evaluate the instrument’s
188              psychometric properties and performance (reliability, validity, and ability to
189              detect change) as well as provide guidelines for interpreting clinically meaningful
190              within-patient change in scores and confirm the endpoint definition. Pilot results
191              can further inform plans for implementing the proposed instruments in phase 3
192              trials.
193
194             We recommend that sponsors analyze PRO endpoints as continuous or ordinal
195              variables, with the choice justified based on the nature of the data, using baseline
196              values as covariates. FDA does not recommend a responder analysis endpoint or
197              a percentage change from baseline endpoint unless the targeted response is
198              complete resolution of symptoms. The statistical analysis plan should include

202           D.       Safety Considerations
203
204   Sponsors developing locally applied corticosteroid products for the short-term treatment of
205   symptoms associated with internal or external hemorrhoids should consider the following
206   regarding safety in clinical trials:
207
208       •   For products being studied over a 2-week course of treatment, we recommend at least one
209           interim assessment to capture potential adverse events (which can be completed by
210           telephone) and a minimum follow-up period of 2 to 3 weeks after treatment is
211           discontinued to adequately assess safety (duration, severity, progression or resolution of
212           any reported adverse events).
213
214       •   Safety assessment at the end of the trial or at drop-out should include assessing changes
215           in the perirectal skin, such as thinning or atrophy of skin.
216
217       •   We recommend that hypothalamic pituitary adrenal (HPA) axis suppression potential be
218           studied by an adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) stimulation test. The ACTH
219           stimulation test should be performed at preestablished time points with at least a 4-week
220           interval between the pre- and posttreatment assessments. For trials lasting less than 4
221           weeks, the ACTH stimulation test for determining baseline status should be completed
222           early in the screening period to permit an adequate amount of time between the pre- and
223           posttreatment assessments. The ACTH stimulation test may be performed in a dedicated
224           study or during phase 2 or phase 3 trials. Patients showing signs of HPA axis
225           suppression or having abnormal ACTH stimulation test results at the end of treatment
226           should be followed closely until complete resolution.
227
228           Sponsors intending to rely on another approved corticosteroid product to support absence
229           of HPA axis suppression from the systemic absorption of the proposed product should
230           demonstrate that the relative bioavailability of the proposed product is no greater than
231           that of the approved corticosteroid product that has been shown to cause no HPA axis
232           suppression.
233
234           E.       Additional Considerations
235
236   If the proposed drug product will be copackaged with a device (e.g., an applicator), it would
237   probably be considered a drug-device combination product as defined in 21 CFR 3.2(e).
238   Sponsors should meet with the Division about combination product status considerations early in
239   the development process; sponsors are encouraged to consult the guidance for industry and FDA
240   staff Current Good Manufacturing Practice Requirements for Combination Products (January
241   2017). 7
