  1.0   Introduction

             This guideline was developed by PBSL in line with the Pharmacy and Drugs Act
             2001 and other applicable legislations aligned with the legislation and the
             guidelines from the international organizations and standards developed by WHO,
             the European Union and other ICH documents with relevant information that
             include:

             1. ICH E2A
             2. Guideline on good pharmacovigilance practices (GVP) Module VI – Collection,
                management and submission of suspected adverse reactions to medicinal
                products (Rev 2)
             3. EU Directive 2001/83/EC
             Due to increased availability and use of medicinal products and vaccines,
             including medical devices, by the population for the management and control
             of diseases in Sierra Leone there is the need to monitor quality and safety of
             these products as part of a functioning pharmacovigilance (PV) system.
             Detecting and monitoring adverse reactions (ARs) of medicinal products and
             devices, also known as Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs), and Adverse Events
             Following Immunisation (AEFIs) are one main part of securing health of the
             population. In accordance with Act, the Pharmacy Board of Sierra Leone,
             (hereafter referred to as PBSL), functions as the National Pharmacovigilance
             Centre (NPC). The regulation of a medicinal product and device in Sierra
             Leone is governed by the provisions and requirements of the Act by which
             PBSL was established as the regulatory body for medicinal products and
             devices. The PBSL is mandated to ensure that evidence of existing and new
             adverse events, interactions and information about pharmacovigilance of
             medicinal products and devices being monitored globally, are analysed and

  2.0 Objective

             The objective of this guide is to promote early detection of drug safety
             problems, improve selection and rational use of drugs by HCPs and to reduce
             medicinal product-/ device-induced morbidity and mortality. Effective PV
             activity will enable Sierra Leone to develop a good record keeping habit and
             build a useful safety information database that will improve the quality of
             health care offered to the patient.
             All reporters stand to benefit as follows:
              improvement on the quality of care offered to patients;
              reduction of medicinal product-related problems leading to better treatment
               outcomes;
              improved patient confidence in the professional’s practice and consequently
               professional growth;
              improved knowledge, access to feedback information on drug related
               problems reported within the country and internationally;
              satisfaction for the fulfillment of moral and professional obligation.


             Safety monitoring of medicinal products and devices by PBSL ensures
             continuity of safety for patients and the general public

  3.0   Scope

             This document provides guidance to all relevant stakeholders on reporting and
             requirements of reporting of ADRs of medicinal products including vaccines
             and medical devices in humans and animals. This guideline applies to all
             medicinal products and devices as defined in the Act and the Regulations

  4.0   Requirements and general principles of efficient ADR reporting

              All healthcare professionals (HCPs)/workers, including doctors, dentists,
              pharmacists,   pharmacy     technicians,   nurses,   community   health   officers,
              traditional medicine practitioners and other health professional are requested
              to report all suspected adverse reactions. Consumers should report suspected
              adverse reactions to their HCPs or directly to PBSL. This should be done
              without regard to a possible alternative causality or existing lack of
              information. It applies to all medicinal products and medical devices including
              orthodox medicines, vaccines, biological products, X-ray contrast media,
              cosmetics, traditional and herbal remedies, household chemical agents, and
              nutritional agents. This includes all healthcare institutions such as primary,
              secondary and tertiary healthcare facilities, public health programmes as well
              as patients/consumers, MAH and manufacturers.

       4.6     Reporting in Special Situations

               4.6.1 Overdose, abuse, misuse and medication error

             If overdose, abuse, misuse or medication error is associated with a suspected
             serious adverse reaction, the adverse reaction should be submitted to PBSL as
             an ICSR.
             The reports of overdose, abuse, misuse or medication error should be
             routinely followed-up by PBSL to ensure that information is as complete as
             possible with regard to the symptoms, suspected medicinal products/ devices,
             outcomes and context of occurrence (e.g. error in prescription, administration,
             dispensing, dosage, unauthorised indication or population, etc.).

               4.6.2 Lack of efficacy or effectiveness

             HCPs, MAHs, or their national representatives of MAHs and other stakeholders
             should notify PBSL when they are aware of lack of efficacy or vaccination
             failure.
             In case the medicinal product/ device is used in critical conditions or for the
             treatment of life-threatening diseases or contraceptives, lack of therapeutic
             efficacy should be reported as ICSRs, unless the reporter has specifically
             stated that the outcome was due to disease progression and was not related
             to the medicinal product or device.
             Clinical judgement should be used when considering if cases of lack of
             therapeutic efficacy is qualified for submission or not.
             For vaccines, cases of lack of prophylactic efficacy should always be submitted
             as ICSRs, in particular with the view to highlight potential signals of reduced
             immunogenicity in a sub-group of vaccines, waning immunity, or strain
             replacement.

             A drug or medicinal product
             A pharmaceutical product, used in or on the human body for the prevention
             (prophylaxis) mitigation, diagnosis and or treatment of disease, or for the
             modification of physiological function.
             This definition includes prescribed medicines, over-the counter medicines,
             vaccines, herbal medicines, traditional medicines and biologicals (including blood
             and blood-related products e.g. sera, plasma) and cosmetics, medical devices and
             nutritional agents.


             Abuse
             The persistent or sporadic, intentional excessive use of a medicinal product, which
             is accompanied by harmful physical or psychological effects.


             Active Substance
             Any substance or mixture of substances intended to be used in the manufacture of
             a medicinal product and that, when used in its production, becomes an active
             ingredient of that product intended to exert a pharmacological, immunological or
             metabolic action with a view to restoring, correcting or modifying physiological
             functions or to make a medical diagnosis.


             Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR)
             A response to a medicinal product which is noxious and unintended and which
             occurs at doses normally used in man for the prophylaxis, diagnosis of disease, or
             for the modification of physiological function.


             Adverse Event

             Any untoward medical occurrence which follows immunisation and which does not
             necessarily have a causal relationship with the usage of the vaccine. The adverse
             event may be any unfavourable or unintended sign, abnormal laboratory finding,

             An adverse event of special interest (serious or non-serious) is one of scientific
             and medical concern specific to the sponsor’s product or programme, for which
             ongoing monitoring and rapid communication by the investigator to the sponsor
             can be appropriate. Such an event might warrant further investigation in order to
             characterise and understand it. Depending on the nature of the event, rapid
             communication by the trial sponsor to other parties (e.g., regulators) might also
             be warranted.


             Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) Case Report
             A case report in pharmacovigilance is a notification related to a patient who has
             experienced an adverse medical event or laboratory test abnormality suspected to
             be induced by a medicine.

  WHO-UMC

  Causality term      Assessment criteria*

  Certain              Event or laboratory test abnormality, with plausible time relationship
                         to drug intake
                       Cannot be explained by disease or other drugs
                       Response to withdrawal plausible (pharmacologically, pathologically)
                       Event definitive pharmacologically or phenomenologically (i.e. an
                         objective and specific medical disorder or a recognised
                         pharmacological phenomenon)
                       Rechallenge satisfactory, if necessary
  Probable / Likely    Event or laboratory test abnormality, with reasonable time
                         relationship to drug intake
                       Unlikely to be attributed to disease or other drugs
                       Response to withdrawal clinically reasonable
                       Rechallenge not required
  Possible             Event or laboratory test abnormality, with reasonable time
                         relationship to drug intake
                       Could also be explained by disease or other drugs
                       Information on drug withdrawal may be lacking or unclear
  Unlikely             Event or laboratory test abnormality, with a time to drug intake that
                         makes a relationship improbable (but not impossible)
                       Disease or other drugs provide plausible explanations
  Conditional/         Event or laboratory test abnormality
  Unclassified         More data for proper assessment needed, or
                       Additional data under examination
