PURPOSE

This Manual of Policies and Procedures (MAPP) describes the organization, membership, policy,
responsibilities, and procedures of the Medical Policy and Program Review Council (MPPRC) in
the Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER or Center).


BACKGROUND

To improve coordination and consistency across the Center, the Office of Medical Policy
(OMP)-led Medical Policy Council and the Office of New Drug (OND)-led Program Review
Council were combined in 2017 into one entity and renamed MPPRC.

The MPPRC provides a senior-level forum to discuss and recommend medical policy in CDER
and its application to the new drug applications (NDA), investigational new drug applications
(INDs), abbreviated new drug applications (ANDAs), and biosimilars and biologic review
processes. MPPRC helps ensure that medical policy is implemented in a consistent manner
throughout the Center.

The MPPRC provides a forum for senior staff and management engagement on medical policy
development and drug development program-related issues, including:

POLICY

The MPPRC will meet on a regular basis to advise on medical policy and programmatic issues
that are complex or precedent-setting and would benefit from senior-level input.

Topics on the meeting agenda will be identified as either a medical policy or a program issue.
Medical policy topics will be chaired by OMP, and program topics will be chaired by OND.

For the purposes of the MPPRC, the term “medical policy” encompasses broad issues related to
application of statute or regulation to new, complex, or challenging situations or circumstances
where application has been unclear or inconsistent. Policy issues may involve health care
professional and patient labeling, human subject protections, counterterrorism drug development
(such as application of the animal rule (21 CFR 314.600)), prescription drug promotion, good
clinical practice, and novel drug development pathways, among others.

Programmatic issues discussed under the MPPRC may include approvability of a drug product or
therapeutic biological product or other challenging scientific/regulatory issues, as identified by
the review team, team leaders, or divisional or office leadership across the Center.

Although the issue discussed by the MPPRC may have been prompted by a specific product or
situation, this may lead to policy development (with due consideration of FDA’s responsibilities
under its good guidance practices regulations) that may be more broadly applicable.

To be considered by the MPPRC, an issue typically should meet one or more of the following
criteria:

    •   A novel medical policy issue requiring senior-level input.
    •   A medical policy issue on which CDER has not taken a consistent position.
    •   An existing medical policy position that should be reconsidered in light of scientific or
        regulatory advances.
    •   Strategies for implementation of a new medical policy.
    •   A complex programmatic issue that would benefit from multi-disciplinary discussion
        with senior management to inform a decision.

RESPONSIBILITIES

MPPRC
  • Provides senior-level consultations and recommendations, as needed, on issues involving
    medical policy or programmatic review. The goal of MPPRC review is to facilitate
    consistent communication and implementation of policies and procedures related to
    medical policy for internal and external use (e.g., IND drug development application
    review, post-approval management, labeling).
  • Establishes and oversees subcommittees and working groups on medical policy and
    programs to accomplish specific assessments and projects
  • Reviews work products (e.g., documents and recommendations) of subcommittees and
    working groups on cross-CDER medical policy before circulating for clearance
  • Promotes and coordinates internal and/or external communication of recommendations
    when appropriate
  • Develops Agency-wide communications on policy when appropriate

MPPRC Members
  • During discussions, share expertise on issues under consideration by the MPPRC
  • Identify relevant stakeholders and their concerns to the issue under discussion
  • Nominate representatives from their organizational unit to participate in working groups
    to implement activities deemed necessary by the Council to meet goals and objectives
  • Identify agenda items
  • Attend meetings regularly

MPPRC Chair(s)
  • Provides leadership and direction to the MPPRC
  • Promotes involvement and balanced participation of all members
  • Reviews proposals and determines selection and prioritization of issues for consideration
    in conjunction with Council members when appropriate
  • Reviews nominations for positions on the Council

Project Manager
   • Reviews and prioritizes proposed agenda items
   • Schedules meetings and communicates agenda and any background material prior to each
       meeting
   • Drafts and disseminates the meeting minutes
   • Holds subcommittees and working group members accountable for completion of tasks
   • Prepares documents and papers as directed by the Chair(s)
   • Serves as a primary point of contact for the MPPRC, subcommittees, and/or working
       groups
   • Maintains the rosters of the subcommittees and/or working groups
   • Maintains a repository that includes meeting minutes, a log and status of issues discussed
       and actions assigned, and copies of Council recommendations and actions

Subcommittees
   • Establish a Charter detailing the organization, membership, roles and responsibilities, and
     procedures of the subcommittee for Council approval
   • Confirm objectives with the Council and projected life span of subcommittee
   • Provide quarterly updates to the Council of subcommittee activities
   • Provide work products to the Council in a timely manner
   • Respond to questions from the Council on specific issues
   • Advise and assist the Council in responding to Agency staff and other queries

Working Groups
  • Develop project plans that include timelines and update MPPRC at regular intervals as
     designated by MPPRC
  • Confirm working group objectives, goals, and due dates with the Council
  • Define working group member responsibilities
  • Provide work products to the Council in a timely manner
  • Respond to questions from the Council on specific issues
  • Advise and assist the Council in responding to Agency staff and other queries

Individual and/or Office Requesting MPPRC Meeting
   • Submits a topic to the Project Manager for consideration
   • Prior to the meeting:
       o Provides a brief, focused background document (usually 3 to 5 pages) to the Project
          Manager at least 5 days before the scheduled MPPRC meeting. The background
          document (see attachment 2) should provide all the necessary information to
          understand the issue to be discussed. The background document should also include
          questions for the panel members to consider and provide resolution. A PowerPoint
          presentation may also be provided.
       o Identifies a lead speaker or speakers from the office seeking MPPRC evaluation who
          will present during the MPPRC meeting.
       o Submits to the Project Manager a proposed list of attendees representing viewpoints
          on the issue to be discussed.
   • After the MPPRC meeting is held, addresses any action items assigned and provides
       update to the Council as necessary

PROCEDURES

    1. Identification of topics and issues

        •    Medical policy and programmatic issues may be submitted by any Center staff
             member. If the discussion at the MPPRC meeting suggests that the issue has broad
             policy implications, typically a working group is initiated to develop
             recommendations, and these are brought to MPPRC (under this MAPP) for discussion
             and agreement.

    2. Requests for discussion at an MPPRC meeting should be no more than one or two
       paragraphs to include the following:

        •    The medical policy or programmatic issue to be resolved
        •    The trigger that raised the issue
        •    The date by which a meeting is needed

    3. The Project Manager will review proposed topics and prioritize issues for consideration,
       with appropriate input from Council members.

    4. A pre-meeting may be scheduled between the Chair(s) and the individual or office
       seeking Council evaluation to assist in refining the issue to be discussed.

    5. The issue will be summarized in a Medical Policy and Program Review Council
       background document (see attachment 2) and brief slide presentation prepared by the
       requester and submitted at least 5 days in advance of the meeting.

    6. A MPPRC meeting will be convened.

        •    Experts from CDER and Agency staff will be sought and invited to participate in the
             discussion at the Chair’s discretion.

        •    The requester will provide a brief overview of the issues at the beginning of the
             meeting.

        •    Recommendations will be established through deliberation among all Council
             members attending the meeting.

        •    If the MPPRC aligns on recommendations for a medical policy issue discussed at the
             scheduled meeting:

             o The Council will recommend appropriate action items. This could include, but is
               not limited to, the following:

                 - MAPP (new or revision to current MAPP)
                 - Guidance (new, revision to current guidance, or addendum to current
                   guidance)

                 Until such documents are drafted and distributed, the Council will determine the
                 appropriate communication strategy to disseminate changes in policy to CDER
                 staff.

It is the purview of the Council to determine whether a current MAPP, guidance, or other
document is sufficient to communicate the information, and in some cases, the Council may
determine that additional training or other supplemental measures should be used.

        •    If the Council does not resolve an issue at the scheduled meeting:

             o The Council may establish a working group to explore the question further and
               return to the Council with recommendations for Council discussion on how to
               proceed; and
             o The Council may identify specific questions/concerns for an individual and/or
               office to research and provide answers, returning to the Council at a future
               meeting for further discussion.

        •    If the Council establishes a working group, lead offices will be identified and
             included in the action items.

        •    If the Council establishes a subcommittee, a charter will be adopted for the
             subcommittee.

        •    Medical policy and action items will be archived in an electronic database accessible
             to all CDER staff.

    7. If the requester identifies concerns or challenges in implementing the recommendations
       of the Council, the requester can request further deliberation.

        •    Such requests must be accompanied with an explanation on how implementing the
             recommendation from the Council would affect CDER decisions.

        •    The Council, at the discretion of the Chair, may respond to the request using one of
             the following two options:

             o The Chair, with appropriate input from Council members and experts from
               CDER, may respond to the request in writing.
             o The agenda item may be re-introduced to the Council at a future meeting, with the
               additional material in support of the request.

        •    The request and the response will be archived with the initial recommendations
             provided.

    8. The Chair will meet with appropriate CDER staff to debrief on the Council meeting and
       to coordinate action items when needed.

    9. Action items will be completed according to statutory or organizational deadlines where
       applicable, or otherwise in a timely manner. The Project Manager is responsible for
       tracking action items and following up with the lead contact(s).

AUTHORITY

The MPPRC will have the following authority:

    •   Make recommendations on medical policy and programmatic issues that may impact
        additional therapeutic and discipline areas across the Center
    •   Establish subcommittees
    •   Establish working groups
    •   Provide direction and feedback to subcommittees and working groups
    •   Ratify subcommittee and working group recommendations

ORGANIZATION

Membership

The CDER Medical Policy and Program Review Council includes the following:

    •   Policy Chair: Director, Office of Medical Policy
    •   Program Chair: Director, Office of New Drugs
    •   Members:
        o CDER Center Director
        o CDER Deputy Center Director(s)
        o Director, Office of Surveillance and Epidemiology
        o Deputy Director, Office of Surveillance and Epidemiology
        o Director, Office of Translational Sciences/Office of Biostatistics
        o Director, Office of Translational Sciences/Office of Clinical Pharmacology
        o Deputy Director, Office of New Drugs
        o Director, Office of Regulatory Policy
        o A staff member from the Oncology Center of Excellence, which will be on a one-year
            rotation
        o Two staff from the Office of New Drugs review divisions to participate on the
            Council for a 2-year term

Any Council member may send a representative to participate on his or her behalf when the
member is unavailable for the Council meeting.

CDER Executive Committee members will be advised on Council meeting agendas and will be
invited by the Council to attend and/or send a representative with expertise within their offices
on the medical policy issue to be discussed.

members selected to serve on the MPPRC for their 2-year term. The selection process is at the

Subcommittees
Subcommittees may be established by MPPRC to perform ongoing activities and create work
products with oversight by the Council. These subcommittees typically perform ongoing
activities that are more detailed and/or directed to a specific medical policy issue.

Subcommittees will be disbanded when they have successfully completed their goal or their
purpose no longer meets the needs of the Council, as determined by the MPPRC.

Working Groups
Working groups may be established and directed by the MPPRC to facilitate work on a short-
term project not being addressed by a standing subcommittee.

The working groups will adjourn when they have successfully completed their goal or additional
work is not required as determined by the MPPRC.

ATTACHMENT 1

The following are examples of medical policy and programmatic issues that the CDER Medical
Policy and Program Review Council may address:

    1. Labeling implications of prescription drug-use related software

    2. Demonstration of contribution of components for fixed-dose combination products and
       labeling implications

    3. Standards for use of the animal rule

    4. Approval of products that may benefit the community but not the individual (e.g.,
       combinations to prevent emergence of microbial resistance, transmission blocking
       vaccines, interventions to reduce transmissibility of tuberculosis)

    5. Standards of evidence for prophylaxis where efficacy may not be testable in humans
       (e.g., prevention of anthrax, countermeasures to prevent poisoning)

    6. Recommendations for breakthrough therapy designation recissions

    7. Requests for breakthrough therapy designation that review divisions require leadership
       input

    8. Post-market review release reconsiderations

    9. Approaches to novel clinical trial designs, including new biostatistical analysis methods
       proposed by sponsors or applicants

    10. Life Cycle Management and impact on generic drug products

 ATTACHMENT 2

                          CDER Medical Policy and Program Review Council
                                Background Document Template


Purpose - The CDER Medical Policy and Program Review Council background document is a
stand-alone document of medical policy or programmatic issue(s) that require resolution from
the CDER Medical Policy and Program Review Council. It should convey the medical policy or
programmatic issue to be resolved, the trigger that raised the issue, and the date by which a
response is needed. The document should follow the CDER Style Guide, be no more than three
to five pages, and provide all the necessary information to understand the issue to be discussed.

Introduction - Provide an overview of the issue to be resolved.

Background - Describe scientific, clinical, and regulatory areas that address the issue to be
resolved. If applicable, include any areas and issues that have been raised; the regulation,
MAPP, and/or guidance that has an impact on the issue; any considerations and advice already
provided in discussions with the sponsor; and any other important aspect, such as previous
advice and precedent given to other sponsors or staff, that would affect the resolution to be
reached. The information should contain ideas, including any differing opinions, on how the
medical policy should be implemented.

Questions to be Considered by the Council - List the questions that the Council should
consider in response to the issue to be resolved. The questions should be general, applying to all
drugs and/or biological products or a group of drugs and/or biological products.

It is recommended that the questions include options with the preferred outcome highlighted, if
possible.

Presentation - Provide all presentation materials to be used by the individual or an identified
lead from the Office seeking Council evaluation. The presentation will be limited to no more
than 10 minutes and may only reference materials provided in the background document or
attachments. A PowerPoint presentation is not required; however, at least one slide should be
provided to display the question(s) for the MPPRC.

Attachments - Attach any additional background material, such as reviews, guidance, MAPPs,
or regulations. Attachments are not required, but such attachments are supplementary to the
Council background document.
