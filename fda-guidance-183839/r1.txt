49   I.      INTRODUCTION
50
51   This guidance provides recommendations to sponsors regarding measurement of ovarian toxicity
52   using clinical measures and biomarkers of ovarian function in relevant cancer clinical trials that
53   enroll premenopausal adults. Specifically, this guidance applies in the cancer setting where life
54   expectancy based on tumor type is of a sufficient time where ovarian toxicities may be relevant.
55   Ovarian toxicity should be considered as a safety endpoint in trials including premenopausal
56   adults and should be considered an integral part of a drug 2 development program when the
57   intended therapy will be used in premenopausal adults.
58
59   Recommendations regarding ovarian function in pediatric patients with cancer and nonclinical
60   recommendations are outside the scope of this guidance and are not addressed. For
61   recommendations regarding the nonclinical assessment of reproductive toxicities of anticancer
62   pharmaceuticals, refer to the following guidances for industry: ICH S9 Nonclinical Evaluation
63   for Anticancer Pharmaceuticals (2010), ICH S9 Nonclinical Evaluation for Anticancer
64   Pharmaceuticals Questions and Answers (2018), and FDA guidance Oncology Pharmaceuticals:
65   Reproductive Toxicity Testing and Labeling Recommendations (2019). For cancer prevention
66   trials in otherwise healthy individuals, refer to nonclinical recommendations in the guidance ICH
67   M3(R2) Nonclinical Safety Studies for the Conduct of Human Clinical Trials and Marketing
68   Authorization for Pharmaceuticals (2010). For nonclinical recommendations for cell and gene
69   therapy products, refer to the final guidance for industry Preclinical Assessment of
70   Investigational Cellular and Gene Therapy Products (2013).

 79   II.        BACKGROUND
 80
 81   Loss of ovarian function is a potentially irreversible toxicity associated with systemic anti-cancer
 82   agents. Ovarian dysfunction may result in infertility and long-term morbidities related to early-
 83   onset menopause and estrogen deficiency, including vasomotor symptoms, sexual dysfunction,
 84   osteoporosis, and earlier onset of cardiovascular disease. Routine collection of ovarian toxicity
 85   data in cancer clinical trials of investigational agents is currently lacking. For example, from
 86   2008 to 2019 only 9% of phase 3 (neo)adjuvant breast cancer trials prespecified ovarian function
 87   as an endpoint, and only 20% collected pre- and post-intervention ovarian function data, mostly
 88   just menstrual status 3. Assessment of ovarian toxicity in cancer trials is essential to ensure that
 89   patients and clinicians have information about the possible long-term impacts of cancer
 90   treatments on ovarian function and thus facilitate informed decision-making regarding anti-
 91   cancer agents.

 94   III.       RECOMMENDATIONS
 95
 96   FDA encourages sponsors to discuss their drug development plan early in development with the
 97   relevant review teams in CDER or CBER, as applicable, when enrolling premenopausal adults in
 98   trials that are likely to enroll premenopausal patients with high survival rates (e.g., adjuvant
 99   therapy). FDA recommends the following:
100
101          •  Sponsors should assess ovarian toxicity in relevant clinical trials of anti-cancer agents
102             that enroll premenopausal adults with ovaries.
103          • Sponsors should evaluate ovarian function with the following clinical measures and
104             laboratory biomarkers:
105                 o Gynecologic history: clinical gynecologic data including menstrual history (e.g.,
106                     date of last menstrual period, cycle length and duration of menstrual bleeding, and
107                     prior history and cause of irregular cycles, if applicable), prior pregnancies, and
108                     live births.
109                 o Laboratory biomarkers: serum anti-Mullerian hormone (AMH), serum follicle
110                     stimulating hormone (FSH), and serum estradiol (E2) levels, preferably on days
111                     3-5 of the follicular phase of the menstrual cycle.
112                          The concomitant measurement of serum AMH, serum FSH, and serum E2
113                             in the early follicular phase provides the most comprehensive information
114                             regarding the ovarian effects of exposure to anti-cancer therapies, and
115                             potential reversibility of impaired ovarian function.
116          i) Sponsors should specify during trial design the type of assay used to measure AMH,
117             FSH, and E2 and a standardized laboratory methodology across sites is preferable.

128       and confounders) at baseline, every 6-12 months while on treatment, at the end of
129       treatment, and at 12-24 months after completion of treatment, and consider additional

131            o For agents with limited existing data on ovarian toxicity, where the mechanism of

133                from ovarian toxicity are not known, sponsors should collect these data at
134                additional timepoints (e.g., within 30 days of the end of treatment and beyond 12-
135                24 months).
136   •   If there is an identified risk of ovarian toxicity associated with an anti-cancer agent, for
137       example based on nonclinical data or previous clinical data that is suggestive of ovarian
138       toxicity, the sponsor should assess ovarian toxicity in at least a subset of premenopausal
139       study participants (e.g., N=40) during pre-market trials intended to support a marketing
140       application.
141   •   If the sponsor justifies why it is not able to assess ovarian toxicity during pre-market
142       trials, the sponsor should discuss a plan with FDA to evaluate ovarian function in the
143       post-marketing setting.
144            o When a post-marketing study to assess ovarian toxicity is appropriate, the sponsor
145                should incorporate the following recommendations:
146                     A minimum of 40 premenopausal adults should be enrolled and have
147                        follow-up data collected.
148                     Ensure characterization of the incidence and severity of ovarian toxicity
149                        after a minimum of 12 months post-treatment.
150                     Follow-up assessments including reproductive hormone measurements
151                        (Serum AMH, FSH, and E2) should be obtained at regularly scheduled 6-
152                        month intervals and at 12 months post-treatment.
