 5   Referenced Biomarker(s): Galactomannan in serum and bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL)
 6   fluid.
 7
 8   Galactomannan is a heat-stable and water soluble cell wall polysaccharide that is released by
 9   Aspergillus species during fungal growth.
10
11   This guidance describes a qualified context of use for galactomannan detection in serum
12   and/or BAL fluid. The experimental conditions and constraints (below) describe conditions
13   for galactomannan use in the qualified context. Galactomannan use in drug development
14   not conforming to the complete context of use will be considered on a case-by-case basis in
15   regulatory submissions (e.g., investigational new drug application (IND), biologics licensing
16   application (BLA), or new drug application (NDA)); in such cases, additional information
17   relevant to the expanded use may be requested by the Center for Drug Evaluation and
18   Research product review team.
19
20   This guidance applies to research use only in investigational studies of treatments for invasive
21   aspergillosis (IA), and does not change any regulatory status, decisions, or labeling of any test
22   used in medical care of patients.
23
24   Section I: Context of Use (COU)
25
26   A.      Use Statement: Galactomannan in serum and/or BAL fluid is qualified as a sole
27           microbiological criterion to classify patients as having probable IA as defined by the
28           European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer/Invasive Fungal Infections
29           Cooperative Group and Infectious Diseases Mycoses Study Group (EORTC/MSG) in

33   B.      Conditions for Qualified Use:
34
35           1.       Assay: A validated assay (see section III), such as the Bio-Rad Platelia
36                    Aspergillus enzyme immunoassay (EIA), should be used for galactomannan
37                    detection as a biomarker.
38
39           2.       Patient populations:
40
41                     a. The use of galactomannan, as the sole microbiological criterion for diagnosis
42                        of IA, is restricted to patients with hematologic malignancies or recipients of
43                        hematopoietic stem cell transplant (HSCT) who also have clinical and
44                        radiologic features consistent with invasive fungal infection as defined by
45                        EORTC/MSG in 2008 (see footnote 1).
46
47                     b. Subjects enrolled in a treatment trial who satisfy the clinical and radiologic
48                        criteria and the following microbiological criteria can be classified as
49                        EORTC/MSG category probable IA when using the Bio-Rad Platelia
50                        Aspergillus EIA:
51
52                         Serum: A positive result should be based on a cut-off galactomannan index ≥
53                         0.5 based on testing of two separate serum samples or a single sample with a
54                         value of ≥ 1.0; and/or
55
56                         BAL fluid: A positive result should be based on a cut-off galactomannan
57                         index ≥ 1.0 based on testing of two aliquots of a single BAL fluid sample.
58
59                         The quantitative value of the galactomannan index should be documented in
60                         the case report forms and included in the electronic datasets.
61
62                    c.   For other assays, appropriate cut-off parameters (for example, values or
63                         indices) should be determined and discussed with FDA prior to initiating the
64                         clinical trial. The quantitative value of the galactomannan measures should
65                         be documented in the case report forms and included in the electronic
66                         datasets.
67

 69
 70                  a. Use of fluids that contain sodium gluconate should be avoided when
 71                     performing a BAL. The presence of electrolyte solutions containing sodium
 72                     gluconate, such as PlasmaLyte, may yield false positive results in the
 73                     galactomannan assay.
 74
 75                  b. Piperacillin/tazobactam may yield false positive assay results. Patients who
 76                     have recently received or are receiving this antibacterial drug can be enrolled
 77                     in clinical trials after samples for galactomannan testing are obtained;
 78                     however, the timing of administration of the antibacterial drugs in relation to
 79                     sample collection and name of the manufacturer should be recorded so that a
 80                     sensitivity analysis can be performed.
 81
 82                      FDA is aware of studies in progress to evaluate whether newer preparations of
 83                      piperacillin/tazobactam exhibit cross-reactivity. If these studies do not
 84                      eliminate concerns of cross-reactivity with piperacillin/tazobactam, then
 85                      patients enrolled based upon the galactomannan findings as the sole
 86                      microbiologic criterion after receiving piperacillin/tazobactam should not be
 87                      considered evaluable in the primary efficacy analyses. Sponsors are advised to
 88                      discuss this issue with FDA.
 89
 90                  c. The antibacterial drug amoxicillin/clavulanate has been reported to cause false
 91                     positive results of the galactomannan assay. Patients who recently received or
 92                     who are receiving this antibacterial drug should not be enrolled into a study
 93                     based upon positive galactomannan assay results as the sole microbiologic
 94                     criterion.
 95
 96                  d. Infection with certain other fungal pathogens, such as Penicillium,
 97                     Paecilomyces, Geotrichum, and Histoplasma, may lead to a false positive
 98                     galactomannan assay result for Aspergillus caused by cross-reactivity.
 99                     Patients known to be infected with these pathogens should not be enrolled,
100                     and if enrolled, should be excluded from the analysis of efficacy.
101
102      4. Considerations for sample acquisition and documentation:
103
104                  a. Investigators should comply with the standard practice of obtaining a fungal
105                     smear and culture on all clinically relevant samples. All positive and negative
106                     results should be documented on case report forms and included in the datasets.
107                     This is especially important for BAL samples so that the presence of Aspergillus
108                     species or rare fungal pathogens (such as Penicillium, Paecilomyces,
109                     Geotrichum, and Histoplasma) that may cross-react with the galactomannan
110                     assay are documented.

112                  b. A BAL fluid sample should be collected as soon as it is feasible because
113                     exposure to mold-active antifungal agents may affect the sensitivity of the

116      5. Analysis of study results:
117
118                  a.   When a noninferiority study design is used, the appropriate noninferiority
119                       margin should be determined in discussion with FDA at the time the study is
120                       proposed within an IND.
121
122                  b.   The primary efficacy analysis should be performed on the modified intent-to-
123                       treat population, which is defined as all proven and probable invasive IA.
124                       Proven and probable IA is defined as per the 2008 EORTC/MSG criteria.
125                       However, the use of a positive galactomannan assay (as described above) to
126                       classify a patient as probable IA is restricted to patients with hematological
127                       malignancies or HSCT recipients.
128
129                  c.   A subset analysis should be done for patients that are diagnosed based on a
130                       positive culture and/or histopathology, excluding those whose
131                       microbiological diagnosis is based only on a galactomannan positive result.
132
133                  d.   As stated above in section 2.a, patients with hematologic malignancy or
134                       recipients of HSCT who have a positive serum galactomannan assay without
135                       clinical or radiologic findings consistent with invasive fungal disease should
136                       not be enrolled, or if enrolled, should be excluded from analysis of efficacy.
137
138   Section II: Supportive Information
139
140   The data to support qualification were obtained with use of the Bio-Rad Platelia Aspergillus EIA.
141   The criteria for a positive galactomannan index described here for use in investigational studies
142   of treatments for IA are different from the FDA-cleared Bio-Rad Platelia Aspergillus EIA device
143   labeling. The recommendations of this guidance do not alter the labeling recommendations of the
144   galactomannan assay for use in the medical care of patients, and cut-off values specified in the
145   device labeling should continue to be used. The use of different criteria for research is to
146   increase the positive predictive value of the Bio-Rad Platelia Aspergillus EIA.

160      A. Other galactomannan assays:
161
162          Sponsors using non-FDA-cleared galactomannan assays for studies to be submitted
163          within a drug development regulatory submission to FDA should determine the
164          performance characteristics of the assay using clinical studies to assess performance
165          against defined reference standards and should ensure that the assay is adequate for the
166          COU. For this performance assessment, analytical parameters such as precision,
167          reproducibility, and cross-reaction with non-Aspergillus fungal species should be
168          determined. Sponsors should discuss their plans with FDA prior to conducting clinical
169          studies.
