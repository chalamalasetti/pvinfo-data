16   I.         INTRODUCTION
17
18   This guidance provides sponsors and investigators with considerations for approaches on how
19   common symptoms related to the Coronavirus Disease 2019 (COVID-19) can be measured and
20   analyzed in clinical trials evaluating drugs or biological products 2 for the prevention or treatment
21   of COVID-19 in outpatient adult and adolescent 3 subjects. 4 This guidance is not intended for
22   development programs evaluating products to treat or prevent postinfectious COVID-19
23   conditions (e.g., long COVID, multisystem inflammatory syndrome) in children and adults, or
24   development programs for preventative vaccines. This guidance does not address considerations
25   for clinical trial design other than those pertaining to the measurement and analysis of COVID-
26   19-related symptoms among outpatients. Considerations for patients with COVID-19 who
27   require hospitalization are out of scope for this guidance.
28
29   FDA is implementing this guidance without prior public comment because the Agency has
30   determined that prior public participation is not feasible or appropriate (see 21 CFR 10.115(g)(2)

45   II.     BACKGROUND
46
47   Sponsors continue to conduct outpatient prevention or treatment clinical trials in adult and
48   adolescent subjects that incorporate assessments of COVID-19-related symptoms; however,
49   challenges persist. These challenges include but are not limited to the identification of methods
50   to assess the numerous and heterogenous symptoms across subjects, patient burden, poor
51   compliance with diary completion, and potential missing data. To address these challenges,
52   sponsors should identify key common symptoms for daily assessments based on the program-
53   specific context of use (e.g., target population, mechanism of action of the investigational
54   product, underlying pathophysiology of COVID-19, symptomatology associated with the
55   currently circulating variants) to optimize the measurement strategy for symptoms most likely to
56   change while lessening the burden on trial subjects. 5 To assist sponsors, this guidance provides
57   an exemplary set of common COVID-19-related symptoms as well as an approach to their
58   measurement for use in clinical trials (see Table 1). The symptom items are derived from
59   existing literature, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), 6 and clinical research.

62   III.       DISCUSSION
63
64              A.       General Recommendations
65
66   FDA recommends the following for sponsors initiating clinical trials evaluating drugs for the
67   prevention or treatment of COVID-19 in outpatient adult and adolescent subjects:
68
69          •   Use patient-reported outcome (PRO) instruments 7 to assess COVID-19-related
70              symptoms; the use of PRO instruments is advised when measuring signs and symptoms best
71              known by the patient or best measured from the patient perspective.
72
73          •   Consult with the appropriate FDA review division regarding PRO instruments proposed
74              for use.
75
76          •   Conduct PRO assessments of COVID-19-related symptoms at least every 24 hours and
77              conduct assessments at the same time each day.
78
79          •   Use electronic data collection systems with reminders to trial subjects to complete the
80              PRO instrument to minimize missing data and provide time stamps of completion.
81              Alternatively, if a paper-based diary is used, sponsors should send reminders (e.g., phone
82              calls, text messages, email) to trial subjects.
83
84          •   Include a set of common COVID-19-related symptoms (for an example, see Table 1) in
85              the daily PRO assessments of all trial subjects regardless of which symptoms a subject
86              had at baseline, as new symptoms may appear following the baseline assessment.
87
88          •   Consider whether a comprehensive set of COVID-19-related symptoms, beyond the set
89              of common COVID-19-related symptoms assessed in the daily PRO instrument, is useful
90              when collected at study baseline and again at landmark time points 8 and also whether a
91              transition to weekly PRO assessments later in the study period would be useful,
92              depending on the program-specific context of use (e.g., study duration).

 99            B.      Example Assessment of Key COVID-19-Related Symptoms
100
101   Given the heterogeneous nature of COVID-19-related symptoms, key COVID-19-related
102   symptoms should be assessed systematically to provide an accurate evaluation of benefit in
103   outpatients. In this guidance we provide an exemplary set of 14 common COVID-19-related
104   symptoms to consider in Table 1 (as described below). Although we recommend assessing these
105   items to capture change in common COVID-19-related symptoms (e.g., emergence,
106   improvement, or worsening) during the clinical trials, a subset of key COVID-19-related
107   symptoms can be used to derive symptom-based efficacy endpoints. Determination of which
108   subset to investigate will depend on what aspect(s) of the condition the study drug is expected to
109   improve as well as symptomatology associated with the currently circulating COVID-19
110   variant(s) of concern. The final assessment plan should be informed by qualitative evidence
111   from patient interviews and/or the literature.
112
113        •   In the example in Table 1, the following applies for rating each symptom:
114
115            – Items 1 to 10, 13, and 14: Trial subjects rate symptom severity at its worst over a
116              specified recall period (e.g., 24 hours) using a verbal rating scale for severity.
117
118            – Items 11 (vomiting) and 12 (diarrhea) are each recorded promptly after a vomiting or
119              diarrhea episode occurs.
120
121        •   In the example in Table 1, there is no total score. Each symptom is scored individually
122            using the following response options and/or scoring values:
123
124            – Items 1 to 10: None = 0, Mild = 1, Moderate = 2, and Severe = 3.
125
126            – Items 11 and 12: Record the time of event occurrence.
127
128            – Items 13 and 14: Sense of smell/taste same as usual = 0, Sense of smell/taste less
129              than usual = 1, No sense of smell/taste = 2.

158   When designing and implementing PRO instruments, sponsors should consider the following
159   recommendations:
160
161        •   FDA recommends using response scales that include verbal descriptors (e.g., none, mild,
162            moderate, severe) because the absence of verbal descriptors may create difficulty in
163            interpretation. Accordingly, response scales such as visual analog scales and 0-to-10
164            numeric rating scales are not directly interpretable and may result in interpretation
165            difficulties in this context.

167        •   FDA recommends avoiding an excessively large number of items in the PRO
168            instrument(s), which may lead to unnecessary patient burden, poor compliance with diary
169            completion, and missing data.

171            C.      Considerations for Outpatient Clinical Trial Endpoint Selection
172
173            1.      Endpoint Selection
174
175   Sponsors should consider the following:
176
177        •   The selection of time point(s) for clinical endpoint assessments in prevention or treatment
178            trials in outpatient adult and adolescent subjects is dependent on up-to-date knowledge of
179            the time course of COVID-19-related symptom onset or resolution. For example, clinical
180            findings show that certain symptoms (e.g., cough, fatigue, or alterations in taste and
181            smell) may take longer to resolve in comparison to other symptoms. 12,13 Furthermore,
182            the CDC has reported findings of COVID-19 rebound, which may be part of the natural
183            history of SARS-CoV-2 infection in some individuals. The rebound included a
184            recurrence of COVID-19 symptoms, but the severity of the illness was mild. 14

195            2.      Trials of Drugs for Treatment of COVID-19
196
197   Sponsors should consider the following:
198
199        •   As described in the guidance for industry COVID-19: Developing Drugs and Biological
200            Products for Treatment or Prevention, an appropriate endpoint could be the time to
201            sustained symptom alleviation or resolution assessed over an appropriate duration.
202
203        •   Sustained symptom alleviation or resolution can be defined as occurring when no key
204            COVID-19-related symptom scored higher than a prespecified threshold over a clinically
205            meaningful time period (as documented using a PRO instrument).
206
207        •   To evaluate clinical benefit, sponsors should include trial entry criteria defining the
208            minimal baseline severity score for COVID-19-related symptoms (e.g., at least two
209            symptoms with a score of 2 or higher using the scoring system shown in Table 1 15).
210
211   FDA does not recommend the following:
212
213        •   Defining endpoints based on adding scores of the items within a set of common COVID-
214            19-related symptoms to form an aggregate score. Given the heterogeneity of COVID-19-
215            related symptoms, any single trial subject may only experience a small subset of the
216            common COVID-19-related symptoms described in this guidance.
217
218        •   Calculating an area under the curve of COVID-19-related symptoms, as this metric is not
219            easily interpretable in this context of use.
220
221            3.      Trials of Drugs for Prevention of COVID-19
222
223   Sponsors should consider the following:
224
225        •   As described in the guidance for industry COVID-19: Developing Drugs and Biological
226            Products for Treatment or Prevention, there is interest in ascertaining whether the
227            severity of COVID-19-related symptoms is milder in trial subjects receiving prophylaxis
228            compared with subjects not receiving prophylaxis.

235            D.      Handling Data
236
237   Sponsors developing drugs for the prevention or treatment of COVID-19 and investigators
238   conducting related clinical trials should consider the following regarding missing data and other
239   related issues for clinical trials:
240
241        •   Sponsors and investigators should make efforts to minimize the amount of missing data.
242            These efforts should generally include providing reminders (e.g., phone calls, text
243            messages, email) to trial subjects to complete PRO instruments, monitoring adherence
244            with PRO instrument completion throughout the assessment period, following up with
245            trial subjects who are not successfully completing PRO instruments (and, where
246            permissible, close contacts if the trial subject is not responding), supplemental training of
247            trial subjects, and recording verbal responses for those who are unable to self-record
248            because of illness or other circumstances. FDA recommends obtaining contact
249            information for close contacts of trial subjects for use in case of nonresponse. If the
250            investigator or a member of the study team plans to contact a family member or other
251            close contact when subjects do not respond to follow-up, this should be described in the
252            informed consent document approved by the institutional review board. The reasons for
253            missing data should be documented. We recommend employing risk-based approaches,
254            including centralized monitoring in clinical trials, to ensure data quality and integrity. 17
255
256        •   The informed consent process 18 and informed consent document should include
257            information to educate prospective subjects about the continued scientific importance of
258            their follow-up data even if they choose to discontinue treatment.
259
260        •   The sponsor should prospectively plan appropriate methods for handling missing data in
261            the development of endpoints and conduct of analyses, considering the reason for the

272            E.       Additional COVID-19-Related Assessments
273
274   In addition to assessment of key COVID-19-related symptoms, such as by using the assessments
275   provided as an example in Table 1, FDA recommends that sponsors standardize the collection
276   and reporting of other clinical trial assessments for trial subjects. Additional assessments and
277   their associated methods that sponsors can consider include the following:
278
279        •   Use of any medications to treat some of the COVID-19-related symptoms (e.g.,
280            analgesics, antipyretics): The name of medication, dose, dosage form, and date and
281            time(s) of administration should be reported.
282
283        •   Body temperature: The timing and the route of the body temperature assessment method
284            (e.g., oral) should be specified in the protocol, and sponsors should provide thermometers
285            to trial subjects.
286
287        •   Oxygen saturation: Pulse oximetry data should be collected, and the sponsor should
288            provide that equipment and provide training or instruction on its use to subjects.
289
