   Purpose

   A. This Standard Operating Policy and Procedure (SOPP) serves as a guide for
      Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research (CBER) staff to ensure that the
      provisions of the Pediatric Research Equity Act (PREA) & the Best
      Pharmaceuticals for Children Act (BPCA) are met.

   B. This SOPP serves as a resource for product offices/review divisions to ensure
      that sponsors/applicants as well as CBER staff are following the required
      procedures and timeframes under PREA & BPCA.

   Scope

   A. This SOPP applies to Investigational New Drugs (INDs) and New Drug
      Applications (NDAs) under section 505 of the Federal Food, Drug, & Cosmetic
      (FD&C) Act, Biologics License Applications (BLAs) under section 351 of the
      Public Health Service Act (42 USC 262), and related NDA and BLA supplements
      for a new active ingredient, new indication, new dosage form, new dosing
      regimen, or new route of administration.

       NOTE: It also applies to initial pediatric study plans (iPSPs) submitted in the
       absence of an IND when clinical programs have been conducted outside the
       United States in support of a BLA.

   Background

   A. The Pediatric Research Equity Act (Public Law 108-155), enacted in December
      2003, amended the FD&C Act by adding section 505B (21 U.S.C. 355B) to give
      FDA the authority to require pediatric studies for certain drugs and biological
      products. The goal of these studies is to obtain pediatric labeling for the product.
      PREA was re-authorized as part FDAAA in 2007 and made permanent as part of
      FDASIA in 2012.

   B. PREA requires that all applications, and supplements to an application, for drugs
      submitted under section 505 of the FD&C Act (21 U.S.C. 355) or biologics under
      section 351 of the Public Health Service Act (42 U.S.C. 262) for a new active
      ingredient, new indication, new dosage form, new dosing regimen, or new route
      of administration, contain pediatric assessments, unless the applicant has
      obtained a waiver or deferral (section 505B(a) of the FD&C Act).

   C. In 2010, the Biologics Price Competition and Innovation Act of 2009 extended
      provisions of the BPCA to biological products. The BPCA defines pediatric
      studies to mean at least one clinical investigation in “pediatric age groups
      (including neonates in appropriate cases) in which a drug is anticipated to be
      used, and, at the discretion of the Secretary, may include preclinical studies.”
      The BPCA and PREA are designed to work together to encourage the
      development of data to inform the safe and effective use of drugs in pediatric
      populations.

   Definitions

   A. Pediatric Review Committee (PeRC) - A CDER led committee, established
      under FDAAA, with additional membership from CBER, Office of Pediatric
      Therapeutics (OPT) and Office of the Chief Council (OCC), that regularly meets
      to provide consultations to review divisions on all PREA-required studies to
      include iPSP, agreed upon iPSP, assessments, deferrals, deferral extensions
      (DE) and waivers; also reviews BPCA submissions: Proposed Pediatric Study
      Requests (PPSR), inadequate letters, and Written Requests (WR).

   B. Oncology subcommittee of PeRC - A committee established by the Oncology
      Center of Excellence (OCE) to implement provisions under FDARA regarding the
      development of safe and effective new drugs and biologics to treat cancer in
      children.

   C. CBER Pediatrics Working Group (PWG) - A collaborative forum used in CBER
      to discuss and develop both policy and procedures regarding product
      development in the pediatric population in a manner which will provide for
      participation by all CBER offices. This working group is overseen by the
      Associate Director for Policy (ADP) and the Associate Director for Medicine
      (ADM) Office of the Center Director (OCD) or designee.

   D. PREA Post Marketing Requirement (PREA PMR) - A deferred pediatric study
      required under PREA that is implemented as a PMR.

   E. Pediatric Deferral Extension (DE) Request - A request from an applicant to
      extend the final study report submission date (milestone) of an outstanding
      PREA PMR.

   F. Pediatric Study Plan (PSP) - An outline of the pediatric study or studies that the
      sponsor plans to conduct (including, to the extent practicable, study objectives
      and design, age groups, relevant endpoints, and statistical approach); any
      planned request for a deferral, partial waiver, or waiver if applicable, along with
      any supporting information; and other information specified in the regulations
      issued by the FDA.

   G. Initial PSP (iPSP) - The initial submission under the pre-IND/IND of a sponsor’s
      plans for addressing PREA for any planned new application or supplement.

   H. Agreed iPSP - FDA’s agreement to an iPSP.

   I. Amended iPSP - Sponsor’s proposed changes made to an agreed iPSP.

   J. Agreed Amended iPSP - FDA’s agreement to the sponsor’s changes made to
      the amended iPSP (30 day review clock).

   K. PREA Non-Compliance Letter - Issued when FDA has determined that the
      applicant has failed to submit a pediatric assessment for a PREA PMR or has
      failed to submit a request for deferral extension of that PREA PMR.

   L. Written Request (WR) - A document issued by FDA requesting submission of a
      study or studies intended to provide meaningful health benefits in the pediatric
      population. The WR specifies the elements of the study or studies that the
      sponsor or application holder must complete to qualify for pediatric exclusivity.
      FDA can issue a WR at the request of an interested party or on its own initiative.
      Completion of studies described in a WR is voluntary.

   M. Proposed Pediatric Study Request (PPSR) - A sponsor's submission
      describing proposed pediatric studies to obtain a WR under BPCA. The PPSR
      should describe the studies the sponsor or application holder proposes to
      conduct to qualify for pediatric exclusivity. The PPSR should include:

        1. A background section,

        2. Nonclinical studies,

        3. Drug information,

        4. Clinical studies,


                                                                              Page 4 of 19
Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research                                     SOPP 8421


        5. Known drug safety concerns and monitoring,

        6. Statistical information, including power of studies and statistical
           assessments, and

        7. Time frame for submitting reports of the study or studies.

   N. Pediatric Exclusivity - A 6-month extension of existing patent or exclusivity
      protections granted for completing studies in response to a WR. For biologics,
      pediatric exclusivity only attaches to reference product and orphan drug
      exclusivity periods. In addition, unlike other drug products, pediatric exclusivity
      does not attach to patents for biological products.

   O. Pediatric Exclusivity Board (PEB) - The Pediatric Exclusivity Board reviews
      pediatric studies that can be submitted in response to a Written Request (WR),
      issued by FDA, that addresses a public health need. The Board reviews the
      materials provided by the review division to make a determination. The
      determination must be made within 180 days of receipt of the application.

   Policy

   A. Product Offices will engage sponsors/applicants early in product development
      (i.e., under the pre-IND, if submitted without an IND or IND phase) regarding any
      required plans for developing products subject to PREA. Specifically, Product
      Offices will ensure that the following key points are discussed:

       1. Rationale for the sponsor’s plan to request either a waiver or deferral for
          pediatric studies in accordance with PREA and the PSP guidance.

       2. Reasonably agreed upon timelines for completing planned pediatric studies.

   B. In the absence of an IND (e.g., when the clinical development program is
      conducted outside the United States), sponsors are strongly encouraged to
      contact the appropriate CBER Office as early as possible to avoid any delay to
      ensure that an agreed iPSP is included in any future NDA, BLA, or supplement
      that is required by PREA to include pediatric assessments. All applicable review
      timelines, PeRC review requirements, and communication procedures outlined in
      this SOPP and in JA 851.05: Instructions for Processing Pediatric Study Plans
      remain in effect regardless of a submitted iPSP and are reviewed without an IND.

   C. The pediatric assessments shall contain data gathered using appropriate
      formulations for each age group for which the assessment is required that are
      adequate to:

            1. Assess the safety and effectiveness of the drug or the biological product
               for the claimed indications in all relevant pediatric subpopulations.


                                                                                 Page 5 of 19
Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research                                     SOPP 8421


           2. Support dosing and administration for each pediatric subpopulation for
              which the drug or the biological product has been assessed to be safe and
              effective.

           3. Applicants must provide an assessment of the data gathered to support
              the safety and effectiveness of the product in the entire pediatric
              population (from birth through younger than 17 years of age).

               NOTE: While the entire pediatric age range must be addressed,
               depending on the product, not all age groups (neonates, infants, children,
               and adolescents) will necessarily require completed studies.

   D. A sponsor of an IND subject to PREA is required to submit an initial pediatric
      study plan (iPSP), typically no later than 60 days after an end-of-phase 2
      meeting, to identify needed pediatric studies.

   E. Under PREA, an applicant may request and be granted a:

       1. Waiver for some/all age groups if (in most cases only one of the following is
          applicable):

           a. Studies in pediatric age group(s) are impossible or highly impracticable
              (because, for example, the number of patients is so small, or the patients
              are geographically dispersed);

           b. There is evidence strongly suggesting that the product would be
              ineffective or unsafe in some/all pediatric age groups. If the Secretary
              grants a full or partial waiver because there is evidence that a drug or
              biological product would be ineffective or unsafe in pediatric populations,
              the information shall be included in the labeling for the drug or biological
              product;

           c. The product does not represent a meaningful therapeutic benefit over
              existing therapies AND is not likely to be used in a substantial number of
              pediatric patients; or

           d. The applicant can demonstrate that reasonable attempts to develop a
              pediatric formulation necessary for a specific age group have failed (partial
              waiver only).

       2. Deferral for some/all age groups if (in most cases only one of the following is
          applicable):

           a. Product is ready for approval for use in adults before pediatric studies are
              complete;

           b. Pediatric studies should be delayed until additional safety or effectiveness
              data have been collected; or
                                                                                 Page 6 of 19
Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research                                     SOPP 8421


           c. There is another appropriate reason for deferral; and the applicant submits
              to the Secretary (i) certification of the grounds for deferring the
              assessments; (ii) a description of the planned or ongoing studies; and (iii)
              evidence that the studies are being conducted or will be conducted with
              due diligence and at the earliest possible time; (iv) a timeline for
              completion of such studies.

   F. Deferred studies identified in the agreed PSP may become PREA PMRs with
      specific milestones at the time of BLA or supplement approval. If unanticipated
      difficulties arise, applicants may later request revised (new) final study report
      (FSR) milestones for their deferred PREA PMRs under a deferral extension (DE)
      request (which must be submitted not less than 90 days prior to the date the
      original deferral FSR milestone and no earlier than one year prior to the date the
      original deferral FSR milestone).

   G. Generally, products designated as orphan under the Orphan Drug Act are
      exempt from meeting the requirements of PREA. However, Section 505B of the
      FD&C Act, as amended by FDA Reauthorization Act (FDARA), requires that any
      original NDA or BLA submitted on or after August 18, 2020, for a new active
      ingredient, must contain reports on the molecularly targeted pediatric cancer
      investigation described in section 505B(a)(3) of the FD&C Act, unless the
      requirement is waived or deferred, if the drug that is the subject of the application
      is: (1) intended for the treatment of an adult cancer, and (2) directed at a
      molecular target that the Secretary determines to be substantially relevant to the
      growth or progression of a pediatric cancer in accordance with the Guidance for
      Industry: FDARA Implementation Guidance for Pediatric Studies of Molecularly
      Targeted Oncology Drugs: Amendments to Sec. 505B of the FD&C Act. FDA
      maintains a current list of pediatric molecular targets, The Relevant Molecular
      Target List & The Non-Relevant Molecular Target Leading to Waiver List on
      FDA’s Pediatric Oncology web page.

   H. Under section 505B(d)(1) of the FD&C Act, FDA may issue a PREA Non-
      Compliance letter to an applicant who failed to submit a required pediatric
      assessment (i.e., FSR containing data to support pediatric labeling of the
      product) or report of a molecularly targeted pediatric cancer investigation (usually
      in the form of a supplemental application that includes proposed labeling). FDA
      may also issue a letter if a sponsor failed to request approval for a pediatric
      formulation. FDA Non-Compliance letters and applicant’s response are made
      public on the CBER PREA Non-Compliance Letters web page.

   I. PREA PMRs cannot be fulfilled only by the submission of FSRs as product
      correspondence or in an annual report. PREA requires the results of all required
      pediatric studies be in the label (whether negative, positive, or inconclusive data);
      therefore, a labeling or efficacy supplement is required as a condition of
      fulfillment of a PREA PMR.

   J. Applicants are required to annually report the progress of all PREA PMRs as part
      of the requirements under 21 CFR 601.70 “Annual progress reports of
      postmarketing studies.”

   K. FDA has tracking and reporting requirements under PREA.

       1. All PREA PMRs (i.e., deferred pediatric assessments) are annually reported
          in the Federal Register and quarterly on FDA’s Postmarketing Requirements
          and Commitments Searchable Database web page. FDA is required to report
          on the status of each milestone for all PREA PMRs. The applicant and FDA
          reporting requirements are detailed in the Guidance for Industry: Reports on
          the Status of Postmarketing Study Commitments – Implementation of Section
          130 of the Food and Drug Administration Modernization Act of 1997. Refer to
          SOPP 8413: Postmarketing Requirement/Commitment Related Submissions
          – Administrative Handling, Review, and CBER Reporting for more information
          and procedures.

       2. Section 508 of FDASIA requires that the Secretary of Health and Human
          Services report by July 9, 2016, and every 5 years thereafter, on various
          activities resulting from the implementation of sections 505A and 505B of the
          Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. This report, “Status Report to
          Congress July [insert dates] - Best Pharmaceuticals for Children Act and
          Pediatric Research Equity Act” is submitted in accordance with that provision
          and contains a brief discussion of various pediatric drug development laws,
          regulations, and guidances; an assessment of the pediatric programs; as well
          as suggestions for improving pediatric research. CBER contributes to these
          Congressional reports.

       3. FDAAA requires that the FDA track and make publicly available certain
          pediatric information resulting from pediatric clinical trials. CBER provides
          data at the end of each calendar year to both the Office of Pediatric
          Therapeutics (Office of Commissioner level) and the Division of Pediatric
          Maternal Health in the Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER) to
          satisfy annual reporting requirements under PREA. CBER tracks & reports
          the following data on the “Pediatric Tracking Requirements Under FDAAA”
          web page:

           a. Waivers requested and granted.

           b. Deferrals requested and granted.

           c. Deferral extensions requested and granted.

           d. Products approved with a pediatric indication.

   L. For submissions subject to PREA and PeRC review, the Product Office will:

       1. Review the sponsor’s pediatric plans under the IND/pre-IND to determine the
          adequacy of the proposed studies.

       2. Review the adequacy of a completed pediatric assessment in the applicant’s
          original application to provide pediatric data in the label.

       3. Review the adequacy of an applicant’s efficacy/labeling supplement
          containing pediatric data (submitted in support of an outstanding PREA PMR)
          to provide sufficient pediatric data in the label and to fulfill the PREA PMR.

   M. Product Offices will utilize their Associate Director for Pediatrics (OTP) or Review
      Division Senior Advisor for Pediatrics (OVRR/DCTR) and office representatives
      to the CBER PWG for clinical questions pertaining to PREA, and consult with the
      Senior Advisor for Pediatric Regulatory Review (CBER) for questions that could
      affect consistency of PREA oversight across CBER Product Offices with regard
      to regulatory and policy questions related to INDs and applications that are
      subject to PREA and other relevant pediatric topics. If warranted, the
      questions/topics will be discussed at the monthly PWG.

   N. CBER will utilize PeRC/Oncology subcommittee PeRC to review all Pediatric
      Plans, Assessments, Deferrals, and Waivers prior to the approval of an
      application or supplement for which a pediatric assessment is required.

   O. CBER will utilize the PMR/PMC Annual Report Review Form (PARRF) to review
      the applicant’s PMR/PMC Annual Reports to monitor that PREA PMRs are
      proceeding on schedule.

   P. Studies conducted under the BPCA are optional because sponsors have the
      option of declining to undertake them in response to a written request (WR).
      While sponsors may qualify for pediatric exclusivity under the BPCA for
      completed PREA studies when those studies are described in a WR issued by
      FDA before the sponsor submits any study reports, FDA does not expect to issue
      WRs solely for PREA-required studies. In general, a WR that includes PREA-
      required studies will also encompass additional indications or populations that
      may produce health benefits in the pediatric population If FDA determines that
      “information relating to the use of a new drug in the pediatric population may
      produce health benefits in that population” and issues a WR, a sponsor may
      qualify for 6 months of exclusivity under the BPCA for conducting studies that are
      required under PREA.

   Q. For submissions under BPCA and PeRC review, the Product Office will:

       1. Review Proposed Pediatric Study Requests (PPSRs) submitted to the IND
          and determine whether to issue an inadequate letter or issue a Written
          Request (WR) for pediatric studies. The Product Office will communicate the
          planned action to PeRC to facilitate appropriate scheduling and discussion
          time allocation.

       2. Review and discuss amendments to the Written Request at PeRC to ensure
          alignment on study design, endpoints, and pediatric study requirements.

       3. Review completed pediatric studies submitted to the (s)BLA, in response to a
          Written Request to determine whether:

           a. All objectives outlined in the WR have been met.

           b. The data are adequate to support pediatric labeling.

           c. The sponsor has fulfilled requirements for pediatric exclusivity
              consideration.

       4. Present at the Pediatric Exclusivity Board (currently housed in CDER) after
          the BLA/sBLA midcycle meeting, to evaluate whether the sponsor has met all
          requirements for pediatric exclusivity, including completion of all WR
          objectives and timely submission of study reports.

       5. Present pediatric exclusivity determinations to PeRC following the pediatric
          exclusivity board meeting for awareness and to solicit additional comments or
          considerations.

   Responsibilities

   A. Regulatory Project Manager (RPM)

       1. Ensures the timely review of all incoming PREA and BPCA related
          submissions.

       2. Ensures sponsors are provided standard language regarding the submission
          of an iPSP within 60 days of the End-of-Phase 2 (EOP2) meeting. When
          there are clinical development topics at meetings (i.e., EOP2, and pre-BLA),
          ensures that timelines for completion of pediatric studies are included.

       3. Schedules PeRC presentations with the PeRC RPMs in a timely manner
          (based on the availability of the relevant review staff) and communicates the
          PeRC meeting date to the Associate Director for Pediatrics (OTP), Review

           Division Senior Advisor for Pediatrics (OVRR/DCTR), and Senior Advisor for
           Pediatric Regulatory Review (CBER).

       4. Ensures relevant review team members and senior leadership are aware of
          the PeRC meeting.

       5. Assists the clinical reviewer in completing the required PeRC documentation,
          as needed.

       6. Submits the required PeRC/Oncology Center of Excellence Pediatric Review
          Committee (OCE PeRC) documents and follows up if there are scheduling
          delays or issues.

       7. Attends the PeRC meetings, and after internal review team concurrence,
          follows up (telecon, email, letter) with the sponsor/applicant based on the
          meeting outcome.

       8. Notifies the Pediatric Exclusivity Board (PEB) as soon as a PPSR is
          submitted.

       9. Keeps the Senior Advisor for Pediatric Regulatory Review (CBER), Associate
          Director for Pediatrics (OTP), Division Senior Advisor for Pediatrics
          (OVRR/DCTR), and the Product Office Representatives to the CBER
          Pediatric Working Group informed of high priority communications with the
          PeRC.

       10. Drafts relevant PSP communications (emails/letters), Deferral Extension (DE)
           granted/denial letters, release/replace letters, and non-compliance letters for
           delayed PREA PMRs when applicable. Drafts relevant Inadequate PPSR
           letters or WR following PeRC recommendations.

       11. Ensures the correct PREA letter template language is used in the approval
           letter for new applications or relevant supplements.

       12. Ensures the correct BPCA letter template language is used in Inadequate
           PPSR letters or WRs.

       13. Follows applicable CBER procedures for the web posting of PREA non-
           compliance letters in accordance with FDASIA requirements.

   Procedures

   A. IND Phase (Review of PSPs)

       1. For processing of all PSPs received under the IND, follow JA 851.05:
          Instructions for Processing Pediatric Study Plans. [RIB, RPM, Reviewers]

           NOTE:

               •   In rare situations in which there is no active U.S. IND, a PTS
                   submission should be created to house the PSP. RPMs should contact
                   RIB and the CBER Senior Advisor for Pediatric Regulatory Review for
                   guidance. Pediatric study plan submissions received under an IND are
                   characterized as an “iPSP” (e.g., initial, agreed, or amended, as
                   applicable). The iPSP should be provided no later than either 60

Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research                                    SOPP 8421


                   calendar days after the date of the end-of-phase 2 meeting or such
                   other time as agreed upon between FDA and the sponsor. In the
                   absence of an end-of-phase 2 meeting, the sponsor should submit the
                   iPSP as early as practicable but before the initiation of any phase 3
                   studies, or any combined phase 2 and phase 3 studies, of the drug that
                   is the subject of the iPSP.

               •   The sponsor should submit the iPSP no later than 210 calendar days
                   before it submits a marketing application or supplement. See the
                   Guidance for Industry: Pediatric Study Plans: Content of and Process
                   for Submitting Initial Pediatric Study Plans and Amended Pediatric
                   Study Plans.

       2. For PeRC scheduling and preparation, refer to the “PeRC Information Page”
          SPO site for necessary instructions and guidance on scheduling a PeRC
          meeting, preparing and submitting the required information. [RPM, Clinical
          Reviewer]

   B. BLA/NDA and Supplement Phases

           NOTE: The “iPSP” becomes the “PSP” upon submission of the marketing
           application (BLA or NDA). Any planned requests for waivers and/or deferrals
           included in the agreed iPSP serve as the official request with the application
           submission.

       1. For applications and supplements subject to PREA and/or postmarketing
          submissions submitted in response to an outstanding PREA PMR: [RPM,
          Clinical Reviewer]

           a. Refer to the FDA/CDER/OND “PeRC Information Page” SPO for
              necessary instructions and guidance on scheduling a PeRC meeting,
              preparing and submitting the required information.

           NOTE: For a BLA or BLA Supplement, PeRC review should generally occur
           after the mid-cycle meeting (6-8 weeks prior to the Goal Date).

   C. Post-Approval Phase (i.e., PMR/PMC Annual Reports, Requests for PREA
      PMR changes such as Deferral Extension Requests or Request for
      Release/Replace, and Issuance of Non-Compliance Letters)

       1. For specific instructions on completing the PARRF to review the applicant’s
          PMR/PMC Annual Reports (to determine whether PREA PMRs are
          proceeding on schedule), refer to JA 860.03: Instructions for Completing the
          PMR/PMC Annual Report Review Form (PARRF). [RPM, Office PMR/PMC
          Coordinator]

       2. For specific procedures on the management of applicant submitted Deferral
          Extension Request, refer to JA 860.08: Instructions for Processing Applicant
          Deferral Extension Requests for Pediatric Postmarketing Requirements.
          [RPM, Clinical Reviewer]

       3. Schedule a PeRC Meeting to present applicant’s substantive changes to an
          open PREA PMR (i.e., major protocol changes), Deferral Extension Requests
          (i.e., request to change the established PMR final study report milestone), or
          a request from the applicant to release and replace a PREA PMR. Refer to
          the CDER/OND “PeRC Information Page” SPO for necessary instructions and
          guidance on scheduling a PeRC meeting, preparing and submitting the
          required information. [RPM, Clinical Reviewer]

           Note: For 45-Day Deferral Extension Requests and 90-day Correspondence
           Status Updates (CSU) submission requests for Release/Replace, PeRC
           review should be scheduled as soon as possible and presented to PeRC no
           later than 7 days prior to the Goal Date.

       4. Contact the Office PMR/PMC Coordinator and Associate Director for
          Pediatrics (OTP)/ Review Division Senior Advisor for Pediatrics
          (OVRR/DCTR) to discuss whether a non-compliance letter should be issued if
          an applicant fails to meet deadlines specified in postmarketing requirements
          (PMRs). Notify the Senior Advisor for Pediatric Regulatory Review (CBER) for
          awareness. [RPM, Clinical Reviewer]

   D. Pediatric Exclusivity (PPSR & WR)

       1. For guidance on the review of requests for pediatric exclusivity, refer to the
          Guidance for Industry: Qualifying for Pediatric Exclusivity Under Section
          505(A) if the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act and consult the Associate
          Director for Pediatrics (OTP)/Senior Pediatric Advisor (OVRR/DCTR) and the
          Senior Advisor for Pediatric Regulatory Review (CBER). [RPM, Clinical
          Reviewer]

           NOTE: PPSRs and WRs are distinct from and serve a different statutory
           purpose than PSPs. PPSRs are submitted by sponsors to request a WR from
           FDA, which, if issued, may qualify the sponsor for an additional six months of
           pediatric market exclusivity under the BPCA. FDA must respond to a PPSR
           within 120 days. All PPSRs, WRs, and Inadequate PPSR Letters must be
           reviewed by the PeRC prior to issuance.
