15   I.         INTRODUCTION
16
17   The purposes of eligibility criteria for cancer clinical trials are to select the intended patient
18   population and reduce potential risks to trial subjects. However, eligibility criteria are sometimes
19   more restrictive than necessary, and expanding eligibility criteria to be more representative is one
20   trial design consideration that may improve the applicability of clinical trial data to U.S.
21   populations. This guidance is one in a series of guidances that provide recommendations
22   regarding eligibility criteria for clinical trials of investigational drugs or biological products 2
23   regulated by CDER and CBER for the treatment of cancer, although many of the concepts may
24   apply more broadly to other clinical areas. 3 Specifically, this guidance includes
25   recommendations regarding the appropriate use of washout periods and concomitant medication
26   exclusions. This guidance is intended to assist interested parties, including sponsors, clinical
27   investigators, and institutional review boards (IRBs), who are responsible for the development
28   and/or oversight of clinical trials.
29
30   A clinical trial’s eligibility criteria (inclusion and exclusion) are essential components of the trial,
31   defining the characteristics of the study population. 4 Because there is variability in
32   investigational drugs and trial objectives, eligibility criteria should be developed taking into

53   II.     BACKGROUND
54
55   Washout periods and concomitant medication exclusions are commonly included in cancer
56   clinical trials. However, these exclusions often vary across trials for similar therapeutic classes
57   and diseases and should be appropriate for the trial under consideration.
58
59   A washout period is a treatment-free period between the most recent anti-cancer treatment and
60   treatment with the investigational drug. This treatment-free period is intended to allow a prior
61   therapy and/or its effects on the body to be eliminated or reduced to acceptable levels preventing
62   additional toxicity and/or interfering with the pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics (PK/PD) of
63   the investigational drug when a new therapy is started. For clinical trials, this is also important to
64   prevent misinterpreting safety or efficacy observations about study-related treatments that could
65   be attributed to prior therapies. While washout periods are most often intended to allow clinical
66   or laboratory adverse events to resolve that may be related to the prior treatment, there may be

 70   Concomitant medications are any prescription or non-prescription medications (i.e., over-the-
 71   counter drugs) a subject may be taking in addition to the investigational drug product(s).
 72   Although this guidance is focused on concomitant medications, certain products such as dietary
 73   supplements that are not regulated as drugs or biological products may also interfere with drug
 74   clearance. Patients receiving anticancer therapies often have comorbidities that require drug
 75   therapy or cancer-related issues that require supportive care (e.g., prophylactic antimicrobials or
 76   treatment of symptoms related to cancer therapy). Undue restrictions to concomitant medications
 77   may result in hindered trial enrollment, as the average patient with cancer takes five chronic non-
 78   cancer medications, not including those that may be used to manage adverse events associated
 79   with anticancer therapy. 8 The prevalence of comorbidities and associated polypharmacy are
 80   more common in older patients, and exclusion of specific concomitant medications could result
 81   in preferentially excluding older patients from cancer clinical trials. 9 Exclusion of specific
 82   concomitant medications should be scientifically and clinically justified in the context of the
 83   known drug profile. Sponsors should consider removing exclusions carried over from earlier
 84   trials as increased drug metabolism, clearance, and drug-drug interaction information becomes
 85   available and suggests certain concomitant medications should no longer be prohibited. Sponsors
 86   may, if applicable, provide alternatives to prohibited concomitant medications in trial protocols.

 89   III.    RECOMMENDATIONS
 90
 91   Eligibility criteria should be tailored to the population being studied and the goals of the clinical
 92   investigation. 10 For that reason, the recommendations in this guidance reflect a general approach
 93   to broadening eligibility criteria related to washout periods and concomitant medications, rather
 94   than providing specific or prescriptive criteria. Exclusion criteria should be justified with a
 95   disease- and drug-specific scientific rationale as opposed to vague statements. For example,
 96   sponsors should avoid statements such as, “Exclude patients taking concomitant medications
 97   expected to increase the risk for a clinically significant adverse event.”
 98
 99   Information about the PK/PD of the expected previous treatments could inform the appropriate
100   duration of the washout period. In addition, accumulated pharmacologic information for the
101   investigational drug should be incorporated as soon as possible in subsequent clinical trials to

108          A.      Washout Periods
109
110          •    Relevant clinical and laboratory parameters, based on the characteristics of preceding
111               therapy, should be used in place of time-based washout periods to address safety
112               considerations (e.g., “[laboratory test value] must have returned to within normal
113               limits or acceptable baseline prior to enrollment/initiation of study treatment”).
114
115          •    Time-based washout periods (e.g., “at least 14 days must have elapsed since last
116               treatment with [therapy] before the patient may be enrolled”) should be scientifically
117               justified and relevant PK/PD of the prior therapy should be taken into consideration.
118               If time-based washout periods are included in trial eligibility criteria, justification
119               should be clearly specified in the protocol (e.g., provide data or justification
120               indicating that a washout is needed so that a subject is not exposed to specific
121               unreasonable risks). A washout period may be appropriate if prior therapy can result
122               in delayed anti-tumor effects, if one of the objectives of a trial is an estimate of anti-
123               tumor effects of an investigational drug.
124
125          •    Depending on its relevance to the investigational drug (e.g., potential overlapping
126               toxicity), candidate trial subjects should have recovered from clinically significant
127               adverse events resulting from their most recent anti-cancer therapy/intervention prior
128               to enrollment.
129
130          B.      Concomitant Medications
131
132          •    Patients using concomitant medications should only be excluded from trial
133               participation when clinically relevant known or predicted drug-drug interactions and
134               potential overlapping toxicities will impact the safety of trial subjects.
135
136          •    Alternatively, instead of excluding patients using interacting concomitant
137               medications, sponsors may also consider modification of the dosage or regimen of the
138               investigational anti-cancer agent or the concomitant medications. These approaches
139               should be justified and clearly specified in the protocol and other study materials.
140               Subjects and caregivers should be adequately informed of these changes.
