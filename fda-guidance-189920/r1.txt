16   I.      INTRODUCTION
17
18   The purpose of this guidance is to assist sponsors in implementing streamlined approaches for
19   nonclinical safety assessments of monoclonal antibodies that recognize a single molecular target,
20   referred to as monospecific antibodies. Most antibodies are pharmacologically active in
21   nonhuman primates (NHPs) only, and this guidance is intended to facilitate drug development
22   for monospecific antibodies while avoiding unnecessary use of animals, particularly NHPs,
23   consistent with the 3R principles of reducing, refining, and replacing animal testing. By reducing
24   animal testing and incorporating an integrated knowledge-based risk assessment, this guidance is
25   anticipated to facilitate greater efficiencies in product development.
26
27   The streamlined approaches recommended in this guidance are intended to apply broadly to the
28   development programs for monospecific antibodies in any indication except those reviewed by
29   the Office of Oncologic Diseases (OOD). Oncology-specific guidances provide additional
30   flexibility appropriate for oncology indications specifically. This flexibility is not described in
31   this guidance. 2
32
33   This guidance supplements the ICH guidances for industry S6(R1) Addendum to Preclinical
34   Safety Evaluation of Biotechnology-Derived Pharmaceuticals (May 2012) (ICH S6(R1)), S11
35   Nonclinical Safety Testing in Support of Development of Pediatric Pharmaceuticals (May 2021)
36   (ICH S11), and S5(R3) Detection of Reproductive and Developmental Toxicity for Human
37   Pharmaceuticals (May 2021) (ICH S5(R3)). This guidance also supplements the ICH guidance
38   for industry M3(R2) Nonclinical Safety Studies for the Conduct of Human Clinical Trials and
39   Marketing Authorization for Pharmaceuticals (January 2010), which provides guidance with

57   II.     BACKGROUND
58
59   Clinically relevant toxicities of antibodies are primarily due to their exaggerated
60   pharmacological effects. Antibodies are large proteins with low tissue distribution and high
61   specificity for their molecular targets; these features reduce the potential for off-target toxicities.
62   Because monospecific antibodies are typically metabolized through protein catabolism and
63   degradation rather than hepatic biotransformation, the metabolite safety concerns and species-
64   specific metabolic differences that are relevant for small molecule drugs are generally not
65   applicable to these products. Because of these product characteristics, knowledge of target
66   biology and expression profile may inform approaches for assessing patient safety, and studies in
67   animals (when warranted) could be streamlined.
68
69   Consistent with the Agency’s commitment to reducing, refining, and replacing animal testing,
70   this guidance provides broadly applicable recommendations for streamlined approaches to assess
71   long-term safety from monospecific antibodies; describes when general toxicology studies are
72   not warranted or may be limited to a short-term study; and addresses alternative approaches for
73   reproductive, developmental, and juvenile toxicity assessments. Sponsors are encouraged to
74   discuss their approach to nonclinical safety assessments of monospecific antibodies with the
75   FDA review divisions before initiating their nonclinical programs.
76
77   To help advance the principles of the 3Rs, sponsors may propose nonclinical tests, including new
78   approach methodologies (NAMs), as appropriate, and study designs that reduce the number of
79   animals in toxicology studies. Sponsors are encouraged to discuss their nonclinical programs
80   during appropriate meetings, for example, Type B meetings. 4 The Agency will evaluate whether

 85   III.       RECOMMENDATIONS
 86
 87              A.     Chronic Toxicology Studies
 88
 89              1.     Evaluating Safety From Chronic Administration of Monospecific Antibodies
 90
 91   In general, studies longer than 3 months in nonrodent species (e.g., NHPs, dogs, and mini-pigs)
 92   are not warranted to evaluate toxicities from chronic administration of monospecific antibodies
 93   when data from 3-month studies are supplemented with a weight-of-evidence (WoE) risk
 94   assessment.
 95
 96   A WoE risk assessment may include the following data:
 97
 98          •   Mechanism of action and pharmacology data generated with the monospecific antibody.
 99
100          •   A literature-based assessment of potential toxicities associated with the molecular target,
101              (e.g., based on the expression profile or roles of the molecular target in physiological
102              processes).
103
104          •   Results of toxicology and pharmacokinetic (PK) data in pharmacologically relevant
105              species (also see sections III.A.2 and III.B).
106
107          •   Results of an assay to detect human-relevant off-tissue binding and potential secondary
108              effects. This is particularly recommended when a pharmacologically relevant species has
109              not been identified and thus no toxicology studies are conducted.
110
111          •   Clinical safety and PK data generated with the monospecific antibody (e.g., phase 1 or 2
112              data).
113
114          •   Toxicity findings in animals and humans, such as when extensive information is
115              published on toxic effects based on other monospecific antibodies against the same
116              target.
117
118          •   Other nonclinical data as scientifically justified (e.g., NAMs, transgenic models, data
119              using surrogates).
120
121          For exceptional cases when a 3-month study and WoE risk assessment may be inadequate ,
122          sponsors should consult the appropriate FDA review division regarding whether a 6-month
123          toxicology study is warranted. For duration of general toxicology studies in severely
124          debilitating or life-threatening hematologic disorders see the guidance for industry Severely
125          Debilitating or Life-Threatening Hematologic Disorders: Nonclinical Development of
126          Pharmaceuticals.

128       2.      Examples of When 3-Month (or Longer Duration) Toxicology Studies Are Not
129               Warranted
130   When an assessment of long-term safety is warranted (e.g., a monospecific antibody being
131   administered weekly continuously to humans over many months), sponsors should decide
132   whether a 3-month (or longer duration) toxicology study in animals will generate relevant
133   information. Below are examples of when 3-month studies (or studies of longer duration) are
134   not warranted. The concept may be applied to other safety studies, as appropriate, and
135   sponsors should discuss their nonclinical development plans with the FDA review divisions
136   before initiating a study in NHPs.
137
138   •   When results of additional studies are likely to be confounded, for example, due to
139       formation of neutralizing or clearing anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) observed in a
140       completed short-term toxicology study conducted with the investigational monospecific
141       antibody.
142
143   •   When a 3-month toxicology study in animals is not feasible, for example, because severe
144       immune suppression or anemia in a short-term toxicology study suggests that a longer
145       duration study is likely to lead to mortality.
146
147   •   When pharmacology data indicate that the monospecific antibody does not bind to the
148       target in any nonclinical species or binding does not elicit pharmacological activity. In
149       these cases, no toxicology studies are warranted (see section III.B).
150
151   •   When substantial animal data with other monospecific antibodies against the same
152       molecular target indicate that the animal data have not been predictive of human
153       toxicities. In these cases, no toxicology studies are warranted.
154
155       B.     Other Considerations for Nonclinical Safety Studies
156
157   •   Animal toxicology studies should use pharmacologically relevant species. Pharmacology
158       studies should demonstrate binding of the monospecific antibody to the molecular target
159       and elicit the intended functional effects. In the absence of a pharmacologically relevant
160       species, the safety assessment could be based on a WoE risk assessment in lieu of animal
161       toxicology studies.
162
163   •   For antibodies that have pharmacological activity similar to humans in both rodent and
164       nonrodent species, general toxicology studies (short- and long-term) conducted in a
165       single rodent species may provide sufficient and appropriate nonclinical data.
166
167   •   A WoE based decision should be made first to determine whether additional nonclinical
168       studies with the monospecific antibody are warranted in support of pediatric studies,
