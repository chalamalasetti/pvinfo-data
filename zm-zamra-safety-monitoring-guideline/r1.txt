ii... Definitions
In this guideline, unless the context otherwise states:

“Adverse Event” means any untoward medical occurrence that may be present during
treatment with a pharmaceutical product but which does not necessarily have a causal
relationship with the treatment. An adverse event can be any unfavorable and unintended sign

“(including an abnormal laboratory finding), symptom, or disease temporarily associated with _

the use of the product whether or not related to the product;

“Adverse Reaction” is a response to a medicinal product/product which is noxious and
unintended. This includes adverse reactions arising from: |
* The use of product within the terms of the marketing authorisation
* The use of product outside the terms of the marketing authorisation, including
overdose, off-label use, misuse, abuse and medication errors;
* Occupational exposure.

In this guideline, Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR), Adverse Reaction and adverse event are used

interchangeably.

“Authority” means the Zambia Medicines Regulatory Authority;

“Dear Healthcare Professional Letter” is a correspondence usually in the form of a mass
mailing from the Marketing Authorisation Holder (MAH), the local responsible person or the
Authority addressed to doctors, pharmacists, nurses and other healthcare professionals

regarding important new information;

“Identified risk” is an untoward occurrence for which there is adequate evidence of an

association with the medicinal product of interest.

1. Introduction

This guideline on Safety Monitoring of Medicines and Allied Substances is issued by the
Authority in pursuant to Sections 47 and 68 of the Medicines and Allied Substances Act No.3
of 2013. The guideline defines the general norms and principles, set standards for continuous
safety monitoring of medicines and allied substances granted Marketing Authorisation and,

ensure that they continue to be safe for patients and the general public.

* “Medicines are approved based on clinical trials data available at the time of authorisation and
in most cases on few volunteers. The limited number of participants included in clinical trials,
the exclusion of certain patient groups, the limited duration of the clinical trials and the
limitation of concomitant therapies do not allow a thorough evaluation of the safety profile.
Under such circumstances, the detection or confirmation of rare adverse reactions is
particularly difficult, if not impossible. This guideline therefore provides the requirements for
safety monitoring during the life cycle of medicines and allied substances granted Marketing
Authorisation and communication of safety information related to medicines and allied
substances. Compliance with this guideline provides the public with assurance that the
medicines and allied substances being made available on the Zambian market continue to meet

the set standards of quality, safety and efficacy.

The Authority may rely on and use relevant decisions, reports or information from well-
resourced regulatory authorities (or WHO-listed, Maturity level 3 or 4 Authorities) or from
relevant regional and international bodies. That notwithstanding, it is equally important to note
that the Authority may request information or material, or define conditions not specifically
described in this Guideline or the Reliance guideline, to allow for adequate assessment of the
safety, efficacy or quality of a medicine or allied substance. The Authority is committed to

ensuring that such requests are justifiable and that decisions are clearly documented.

The Authority may from time to time amend any part of this guideline.

Objective

This guideline sets out the general norms, principles and standards for continuous safety
monitoring of medicines and allied substances on the Zambian market to ensure that their

favorable benefit-risk balance is maintained for the protection of patients and the public.

Scope

This guideline provides safety monitoring requirements for all medicines and allied
substances. It provides for monitoring and reporting of adverse reactions/events, medication
errors, product quality defects and other safety issues as they may apply to medicines and allied
substances. It applies to marketing authorisation holders, local responsible persons, qualified
persons for safety monitoring, health professionals, patients/consumers and all persons
involved in safety monitoring of medicines and allied substances. This guideline is intended to

be applied during the period that a medicine or allied substance is marketed in Zambia.

2.1. Roles and responsibilities
2.1.1. Marketing Authorisation Holder

2.1.1.1.

2.1.1.2.

2,1.1.3.

2.1.1.4.

The Marketing Authorisation Holder (MAH), or their appointed Local Responsible
Person (LRP), shall ensure that any medicine or allied substance made available on
the Zambian market is safe for human or animal health when used under normal or
reasonably foreseeable conditions of use, taking account of its presentation,
packaging and labelling, instructions for use and disposal, and any other indications
or promotional information they provide. Therefore, the MAH shall implement an
appropriate safety monitoring system to monitor the safety of each medicine or allied
substance they place on the market in Zambia.

For new chemical entities, in addition to spontaneous monitoring and reporting, the
MAH shall put in place pharmacovigilance measures to actively monitor the safety
of the product in clinical practice during the first five (5) years following grant of
Marketing Authorisation. Passive surveillance will be required for the entire life-
cycle of the product.

The Authority may also request the MAH to conduct formal post-authorisation safety
studies, when necessary.

The Marketing Authorisation Holder shall employ, appoint and continuously have an
appropriately qualified person responsible for pharmacovigilance resident in Zambia,

referred to as the local qualified person for pharmacovigilance.

Healthcare workers are encouraged to report all adverse reactions, product quality

from patients/consumers. The following will guide reporters on a general approach

3.1. Periodic Safety Update Reports or Periodic Benefit-Risk Evaluation Reports

3.1.1. Requirement to submit Periodic Safety Update Reports (PSUR) or Periodic
Benefit-Risk Evaluation Reports (PBRER)

3.1.1.1. PSURs/PBRERs will be submitted only for medicinal products granted Marketing
Authorisation in Zambia.

3.1.1.2. The main objective of a PSUR is to present a comprehensive, concise and critical
analysis of the benefit-risk balance of the medicinal product considering new or
emerging information in the context of cumulative information on benefits and risks.
The PSUR is a tool for post-authorisation evaluation at defined time points in the life-
cycle of a product.

3.1.1.3. The PBRER is aimed at presenting a comprehensive and critical analysis of new or
emerging information on the risks of the medicinal product, and, where pertinent, on
its benefit in approved indications, to enable an appraisal of the product’s overall

benefit-risk profile.
