14   I.         INTRODUCTION
15
16   The purposes of eligibility criteria for cancer clinical trials are to select the intended patient
17   population and reduce potential risks to trial subjects. However, eligibility criteria are sometimes
18   more restrictive than necessary, and expanding eligibility criteria to be more representative is one
19   trial design consideration that may improve the applicability of clinical trial data to U.S.
20   populations. This guidance is one in a series of guidances that provide recommendations
21   regarding eligibility criteria for clinical trials of investigational drugs 2 regulated by CDER and
22   CBER for the treatment of cancer, although many of the concepts may apply more broadly to
23   other clinical areas. 3 Specifically, this guidance includes recommendations regarding expanding
24   eligibility criteria to include patients with a wider range of performance status . This guidance is
25   intended to assist interested parties, including sponsors, clinical investigators, and institutional
26   review boards (IRBs), who are responsible for the development and/or oversight of clinical trials.
27
28   A clinical trial’s eligibility criteria (inclusion and exclusion) are essential components of the trial,
29   defining the characteristics of the study population. 4 Because there is variability in
30   investigational drugs and trial objectives, eligibility criteria should be developed taking into
31   consideration the mechanism of action of the drug, the targeted disease or patient population, the
32   anticipated safety of the investigational drug, the availability of adequate safety data, and the

51   II.      BACKGROUND
52
53   Performance status (PS), a measure of how well a patient is able to perform ordinary tasks and
54   carry out activities of daily living, 7 is one of the most common eligibility criteria in oncology
55   trials. Many trials are limited to high-functioning subjects (i.e., “good” PS) and exclude lower-
56   functioning subjects (i.e., “poor” PS) 8 based on one of two main scales: Eastern Cooperative
57   Oncology Group (ECOG) and Karnofsky (KPS). Because PS may be based on a different scale
58   for younger pediatric patients (e.g., Lansky) and inclusion of pediatric patients may also be
59   subject to unique considerations, the recommendations in this guidance are specific to inclusion
60   of adult patients in oncology trials. However, many of the general considerations and
61   recommendations in this guidance may be applicable to pediatric oncology trials.
62
63   PS is included as a common eligibility criterion because low PS (i.e., ECOG PS2-4 and KPS
64   ≤70) has been reported to correlate with worse survival, and subjects with low PS may not be

65   well enough to receive investigational treatment or tolerate its potential toxicities. 9, 10, 11
66   However, excluding patients with low PS may prevent trial enrollment for many patients and
67   limit generalizability of trial results. The underlying etiology for low PS is important. For
68   patients whose low PS is due to disease burden, cancer-directed treatment may result in
69   improved PS with tumor control and symptom alleviation, especially with highly effective
70   treatments. However, current PS scales do not differentiate between causes of low PS.
71
72   Additionally, there are limitations to PS assessments. PS determination is inherently subjective,
73   which can affect inter-rater reliability 12 and invite potential bias, particularly for patients at the
74   borderline between PS categories. For example, studies demonstrate that clinicians assign
75   patients aged ≥65 years higher numeric ECOG PS 13 scores than patients aged <65 years, despite
76   no difference in objectively measured physical activity. 14 Additionally, PS is less predictive of
77   cancer-related outcomes for older adults 15, 16 and may be less relevant for more recently
78   developed anticancer treatments that have different toxicities than cytotoxic chemotherapy. 17

 84   When considering inclusion of patients with low PS in clinical trials, sponsors should consider
 85   the following potential advantages and disadvantages:
 86
 87            A.      Potential Advantages
 88
 89        •   More rapid trial accrual. Increased number of eligible patients may lead to more rapid
 90            accrual. Studies have demonstrated that, of patients deemed ineligible for a clinical trial,
 91            exclusion was related to low PS in a significant proportion of patients, with variability
 92            across disease type, investigational therapy, and therapy line. 18, 19
 93
 94        •   Improved external validity of trial results. Restrictive eligibility criteria may result in a
 95            group of trial subjects who do not reflect the clinical and demographic characteristics of
 96            patients with the indicated disease. As a result, the efficacy and safety outcomes
 97            experienced by subjects with high PS may not adequately predict the outcomes for
 98            subjects with low PS. 20, 21 Expanding eligibility to include patients with low PS can
 99            mitigate this issue.
100
101            Including a broader group of subjects could offer additional benefits, such as additional
102            information in drug labeling to inform clinicians and patients and/or reduce the need for
103            post-marketing commitments.
104
105            B.      Potential Disadvantages
106
107        •   Increased adverse events. Rates of adverse events (AEs) may be greater in ECOG PS2
108            subjects as compared to PS0 and PS1 subjects, and this may influence subjects’ ability to
109            complete the intended course of treatment, their outcomes and/or their ability to comply
110            with study procedures necessary to assess their outcomes. The risk of overestimating
111            harm or underestimating benefit may be mitigated in randomized trials given baseline PS
112            is often a stratification factor and the effect of low PS on AE rate and other outcomes will
113            impact both arms.
114
115        •   Impact on trial retention. Low-functioning PS subjects may require greater reliance on
116            family members or caregivers to travel to and attend study visits and procedures, which
117            can influence clinical trial retention. Sponsors should consider the potential effect of
118            inclusion of patients with low PS on trial retention and necessary sample size. Sponsors

129   IV.       RECOMMENDATIONS
130
131   Patients with lower PS should be included in clinical trials in a way that contributes to a greater
132   understanding of the efficacy and safety profile of the investigational drug while maintaining
133   subject safety. In cases where there is a strong rationale for exclusion, the rationale should be
134   described in the trial protocol.
135
136   Baseline ECOG and Karnofsky PS should be complemented by emerging patient-reported
137   outcomes, and other assessment tools (e.g., digital health technologies) that can provide a more
138   refined and/or longitudinal understanding of performance status across populations, including
139   older patients with cancer.
140
141             A.      Recommendations for Inclusion Based on PS
142
143   Patients with ECOG PS2 (or KPS 60-70) should be included unless there is a scientific and/or
144   clinical rationale for exclusion justified by established safety considerations. Given the potential
145   for differences in AE rates, including PS2 patients could provide important safety data to
146   facilitate decision-making for patients in the post-approval setting.
147
148         •   PS eligibility criteria should be based on the patient population in which the treatment is
149             expected to be applied in clinical practice.
150
151         •   PS eligibility criteria should be re-evaluated and modified throughout the drug
152             development process to reflect accumulated safety data of the investigational treatment.
153             Decisions about PS eligibility criteria should be based on early clinical safety and
154             efficacy data about the specific investigational agent or based on known data from other
155             drugs in the same class with similar mechanism of action. Later phase trials (e.g., phase
156             2/3) should generally mirror the intended use population and ECOG PS2 (or KPS 60-70)
157             patients should be included, unless safety concerns have manifested in earlier phase trials.
158             The rationale for exclusion should be justified and stated explicitly in the protocol.

169              B.       Recommendations for Alternative Trial Designs
170
171   Where there are concerns regarding low PS based on the particular trial context, consider
172   alternative trial designs, such as pre-specified cohorts with low PS (ECOG ≥2) that are exempt
173   from the primary analysis, to encourage inclusion of these patients and collect safety data. These
174   cohorts would generally be small in size and exploratory in nature and could be enrolled
175   incrementally to enable an early stopping rule based upon safety data. Consideration of the data
176   analysis approach for the broader eligibility cohort and subgroup analysis should be determined
177   during the study design phase and its implications for marketing and post-marketing
178   requirements discussed with FDA when appropriate. Refer to FDA guidance for industry
179   Adaptive Design Clinical Trials for Drugs and Biologics (December 2019) when considering a
180   prespecified assessment of cohorts with low PS.
181
182              C.       Recommendations for Additional Assessments of Functional Status
183
184   Additional assessments of functional status should be considered to better characterize the
185   functional status of patients at baseline and over time. As with PS, these assessments should not
186   limit trial eligibility unless there is a scientific and/or clinical rationale for exclusion justified by
187   established safety considerations.
188
189          •   Patient-generated physical function and activity data. Assessment of patient reported
190              physical function and role function, is recommended in FDA’s core patient-reported
191              outcomes in cancer clinical trials guidance 24 and can provide both baseline and
192              longitudinal data that can complement clinician-assessed PS. 25 Digital health
193              technologies (e.g., activity trackers) can also be explored to add additional objective
194              activity data to compare with clinician and patient report.
195
196          •   Assessment of patients’ overall health status, particularly in older adults. Existing
197              PS scales are suboptimal for most patients with cancer aged ≥65. 26 Multiple studies have
