15   INTRODUCTION
16
17   This draft guidance provides answers to common questions from prospective applicants and
18   other interested parties regarding the abbreviated licensure pathway in section 351(k) of the
19   Public Health Service Act (PHS Act) (42 U.S.C. 262(k)), which was added by the Biologics
20   Price Competition and Innovation Act of 2009 (BPCI Act). The question and answer (Q&A)
21   format is intended to inform prospective applicants and facilitate the development of proposed
22   biosimilar products and proposed interchangeable products,2 as well as describe FDA’s
23   interpretation of certain statutory requirements.
24
25   Section 351(k) of the PHS Act provides an abbreviated licensure pathway for biological products
26   shown to be biosimilar to, or interchangeable with, an FDA-licensed biological reference product
27   (see sections 7001 through 7003 of the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act (Public Law
28   111-148) (ACA)). FDA believes that guidance for industry that provides answers to commonly
29   asked questions regarding FDA’s interpretation of section 351(k) of the PHS Act will enhance
30   transparency and facilitate the development and approval of biosimilar and interchangeable
31   products. In addition, these Q&As respond to questions the Agency has received from
32   prospective applicants regarding the submission of biologics license applications (BLAs) for
33   proposed biosimilar and interchangeable products. FDA may provide additional Q&As through
34   draft guidance as appropriate.

66   BACKGROUND
67
68   The BPCI Act
69
70   The BPCI Act was enacted as part of the ACA on March 23, 2010. The BPCI Act amended the
71   PHS Act and other statutes to create an abbreviated licensure pathway for biological products
72   shown to be biosimilar to, or interchangeable with, an FDA-licensed biological reference product
73   (see sections 7001 through 7003 of the ACA). Section 351(k) of the PHS Act, added by the
74   BPCI Act, sets forth the requirements for the licensure of a proposed biosimilar or proposed
75   interchangeable product.

 77   Section 351(i) of the PHS Act defines biosimilarity to mean “that the biological product is highly
 78   similar to the reference product notwithstanding minor differences in clinically inactive
 79   components” and that “there are no clinically meaningful differences between the biological
 80   product and the reference product in terms of the safety, purity, and potency of the product” (see
 81   section 351(i)(2) of the PHS Act).
 82
 83   A BLA submitted under section 351(k) (known as a 351(k) BLA) of the PHS Act must contain,
 84   among other things, information demonstrating that the biological product is biosimilar to a
 85   reference product based upon data derived from analytical studies, an assessment of toxicity, and
 86   a clinical study or studies (see section 351(k)(2)(A)(i)(I) of the PHS Act), unless FDA has
 87   determined that an element described in section 351(k)(2)(A)(i)(I) is unnecessary (see section
 88   351(k)(2)(A)(ii) of the PHS Act). To meet the standard for interchangeability, an applicant must
 89   provide sufficient information to demonstrate biosimilarity to the reference product and also to
 90   demonstrate that the biological product can be expected to produce the same clinical result as the
 91   reference product in any given patient, and if the biological product is administered more than
 92   once to an individual, the risk in terms of safety or diminished efficacy of alternating or
 93   switching between the use of the biological product and the reference product is not greater than
 94   the risk of using the reference product without such alternation or switch (see section 351(k)(4)
 95   of the PHS Act). Interchangeable products may be substituted for the reference product without
 96   the intervention of the prescribing health care provider (see section 351(i)(3) of the PHS Act).

139                       [Revised in Draft March 2026]
140
141          A.I.8.a      Yes, under certain circumstances. If a sponsor seeks to use data from a clinical
142                       study comparing its proposed biosimilar product to a non-U.S.-licensed product
143                       (“non-U.S.-licensed comparator product”) to address, in part, the requirements
144                       under section 351(k)(2)(A) of the PHS Act, the sponsor should provide adequate

155                                technologies; and

158                                product has the same or only minor differences in quantitative and/or
159                                qualitative composition of inactive ingredients as the U.S.-licensed
160                                reference product that would not be expected to affect clinical
161                                performance between the U.S.-licensed reference product and the non-
162                                U.S.-licensed comparator product; and
163
164                            •   There is an adequate scientific rationale for why analytical differences (if
165                                any) identified in product quality attributes between the non-U.S.-licensed
166                                comparator product used in the clinical study and the U.S.-licensed

208       A.I.8.b      Reference Product and Non-U.S.-Licensed Comparator Products7
209
210                    For purposes of the comparative analytical assessment and comparison between
211                    the non-U.S.-licensed comparator product and the U.S.-licensed reference
212                    product, any lots used in a clinical study supporting a demonstration of
213                    biosimilarity should be included in the comparative analytical assessment
214                    intended to justify the relevance of comparative data with the non-U.S.-licensed
215                    comparator product to a demonstration of biosimilarity.8 Specifically, if a clinical
216                    study compares the proposed biosimilar to a non-U.S.-licensed comparator
217                    product, sponsors should ensure that data generated in that clinical study are
218                    relevant and applicable to the demonstration of biosimilarity by providing
219                    sufficient comparative analytical data based on: (1) the lots of the non-U.S.-
220                    licensed comparator product used in the clinical study; and (2) the lots of the
221                    U.S.-licensed reference product used in the comparative analytical assessment to
222                    support a demonstration that the proposed biosimilar is highly similar to the U.S.-
223                    licensed reference product. If analytical differences are observed between the
224                    clinical lots of the non-U.S.-licensed comparator product and the U.S.-licensed
225                    reference product lots used in the comparative analytical assessment with the
226                    proposed biosimilar, the sponsor may provide information (e.g., a risk assessment
227                    and relevant analytical data) to scientifically justify why these differences do not
228                    preclude a conclusion that the data from the clinical study comparing the
229                    proposed biosimilar to the non-U.S.-licensed comparator product are relevant to
230                    the demonstration of biosimilarity.
231
232                    The acceptance criteria used to support a demonstration that a proposed biosimilar
233                    product is highly similar to the U.S.-licensed reference product should be derived
234                    from data generated from a sponsor’s analysis of the U.S.-licensed reference
235                    product. The comparative analytical assessment should be based on a direct
236                    comparison of the proposed biosimilar product to the U.S.-licensed reference
237                    product. As a scientific matter, combining data from the U.S.-licensed reference
238                    product and non-U.S.-licensed comparator product to determine the acceptance
239                    criteria or to perform the comparative analytical assessment for the proposed
240                    product would not be acceptable to support a demonstration that the proposed
241                    product is biosimilar to the U.S.-licensed reference product.

252   Q.I.10.   How long and in what manner should sponsors retain reserve samples of the
253             biological products used in comparative clinical PK and/or PD studies intended
254             to support a 351(k) application?
255             [Revised in Draft March 2026]
256
257   A.I.10.   Reserve samples establish the identity of the products tested in the actual study,
258             allow for confirmation of the validity and reliability of the results of the study,
259             and facilitate investigation of further follow-up questions that arise after the
260             studies are completed. FDA recommends that the sponsor of a proposed
261             biosimilar product retain reserve samples for at least 5 years following the date on
262             which the 351(k) application is licensed, or, if such application is not licensed, at
263             least 5 years following the date of completion of a comparative clinical
264             pharmacokinetic (PK) and/or pharmacodynamic (PD) study of the reference
265             product and the proposed biosimilar product (or other clinical study in which PK
266             or PD samples are collected with the primary objective of assessing PK or PD
267             similarity) that is intended to support a submission under section 351(k) of the
268             PHS Act. Contact FDA for specific advice if an alternative approach is being
269             considered. For a PK similarity study, FDA recommends that samples of the
270             proposed biosimilar and the reference product or, if applicable, non-U.S.-licensed
271             comparator product be retained.
272
273             For most protein therapeutics, FDA recommends that a sponsor retain the
274             following quantities of product and dosage units, which are expected to be
275             sufficient for evaluation by state-of-the-art analytical methods:
276
277                •   A minimum of 10 dosage units each of the proposed biosimilar product,
278                    reference product and, if applicable, non-U.S.-licensed comparator
279                    product, depending on the amount of product within each unit; in general,
280                    this should provide for a total product mass of equal to or greater than 200
281                    milligrams (mg) in a volume equal to or greater than 10 milliliters (mL).
282
283             FDA recommends that the sponsor contact the review division to discuss the
284             appropriate quantities of reserve samples in the following situations:
285
286                •   A product mass of equal to or greater than 200 mg in a volume equal to or
287                    greater than 10 mL requires a large number of dosage units

293   Q.I.12.   How can an applicant demonstrate that its proposed injectable biosimilar product
294             or proposed injectable interchangeable product has the same “strength” as the
295             reference product?
296             [Draft December 2018]
297
298             A.I.12. Under section 351(k)(2)(A)(i)(IV) of the PHS Act, an applicant must
299             demonstrate that the “strength” of the proposed biosimilar product or proposed
300             interchangeable product is the same as that of the reference product. Data and
301             information generated as part of the analytical similarity assessment may inform
302             the determination that a proposed biosimilar product or proposed interchangeable
303             product has the same strength as its reference product. As a scientific matter, there
304             may be a need to take into account different factors and approaches in
305             determining the strength of different biological products. Sponsors should discuss
306             their proposed approach with FDA and provide an adequate scientific basis for
307             their approach to demonstrating same strength.
308
309             In general, a sponsor of a proposed biosimilar product or proposed
310             interchangeable product with an injection dosage form (e.g., a solution) can
311             demonstrate that its product has the same strength as the reference product by
312             demonstrating that both products have the same total content of drug substance (in
313             mass or units of activity) and the same concentration of drug substance (in mass
314             or units of activity per unit volume). In general, for a proposed biosimilar product
315             or proposed interchangeable product that is a dry solid (e.g., a lyophilized
316             powder) from which a constituted or reconstituted solution is prepared, a sponsor
317             can demonstrate that the product has the same strength as the reference product by
318             demonstrating that both products have the same total content of drug substance (in
319             mass or units of activity).
320
321             Although not a part of demonstrating same strength, if the proposed biosimilar
322             product or proposed interchangeable product is a dry solid (e.g., a lyophilized
323             powder) from which a constituted or reconstituted solution is prepared, the 351(k)
324             application generally should contain information that the concentration of the
325             proposed biosimilar product or proposed interchangeable product, when
326             constituted or reconstituted, is the same as that of the reference product, when
327             constituted or reconstituted.
328
329             A sponsor should determine the content of drug substance for both the reference
330             product and the proposed biosimilar product or proposed interchangeable product
331             using the same method. The strength of the proposed product generally should be
332             expressed using the same units of measure as the reference product.

336   Q.I.19.    If a non-U.S.-licensed comparator product is proposed for importation and use
337              in the United States in a clinical investigation intended to support licensure of a
338              proposed product under section 351(k), is a separate IND required for the non-
339              U.S.-licensed product?
340              [Revised in Draft March 2026]
341
342    A.I.19. A sponsor may submit a single investigational new drug application (IND) for a
343            development program that is intended to support licensure of a proposed product
344            under section 351(k) of the PHS Act and includes use of a non-U.S.-licensed
345            comparator product. The sponsor should submit information supporting the
346            proposed clinical investigation with the non-U.S.-licensed comparator product
347            under the IND. This scenario may occur if a sponsor seeks to use data from a
348            clinical study comparing its proposed biosimilar product to a non-U.S.-licensed
349            comparator product to address, in part, the requirements under section
350            351(k)(2)(A) of the PHS Act. For example, a sponsor may propose to conduct a
351            clinical PK study in the United States with the proposed biosimilar product and a
352            non-U.S.-licensed comparator product.
353
354             A non-U.S.-licensed comparator product is considered an investigational new drug
355             in the United States, and thus would require an IND for importation and use in the
356             United States (see 21 CFR 312.110(a)). If a sponsor intends to conduct a clinical
357             investigation in the United States using a non-U.S.-licensed comparator product,
358             the IND requirements in 21 CFR part 312 also would apply to this product (see,
359             e.g., 21 CFR 312.2).
360
361             With respect to chemistry, manufacturing, and controls (CMC) information, a
362             sponsor should submit to the IND as much of the CMC information required by 21
363             CFR 312.23(a)(7) as is available. However, FDA recognizes that a sponsor may
364             not be able to obtain all the CMC information required by 21 CFR 312.23(a)(7) for
365             a non-U.S.-licensed comparator product for which it is not the manufacturer. In
366             these circumstances, the sponsor can request in an IND submission that FDA
367             waive the regulatory requirements related to CMC information on the non-U.S.-
