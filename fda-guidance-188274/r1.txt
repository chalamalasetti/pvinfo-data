15   I.      INTRODUCTION
16
17   The purpose of this guidance is to provide recommendations to sponsors on the assessment of
18   overall survival in randomized oncology clinical trials conducted to support marketing approval
19   of drugs and biological products, with an emphasis on the analysis of overall survival as a pre-
20   specified safety endpoint. 2 The recommendations in this guidance are based in part on
21   discussions held at the joint FDA, American Association for Cancer Research (AACR), and
22   American Statistical Association (ASA) public workshop: Overall Survival in Oncology Clinical
23   Trials held in July 2023. 3
24
25   Additional information on the related topic of choice of endpoints in oncology clinical trials can
26   be found in guidance for industry Clinical Trial Endpoints for the Approval of Cancer Drugs and
27   Biologics (December 2018). 4 Pre-specification of a statistical analysis plan (SAP) is a basic
28   statistical principle for clinical trials; refer to guidance for industry E9 Statistical Principles for
29   Clinical Trials (September 1998). The estimand framework is useful to describe the plans for
30   evaluation of overall survival and other primary and key secondary endpoints; refer to guidance
31   for industry E9(R1) Statistical Principles for Clinical Trials: Addendum: Estimands and
32   Sensitivity Analysis in Clinical Trials (May 2021).

49   II.    BACKGROUND
50
51   Overall survival is both an efficacy and a safety endpoint; it can be favorably impacted by the
52   therapeutic benefits of a specific drug and negatively impacted by the drug’s toxicity. Overall
53   survival is also an objective, clinically meaningful endpoint that can be measured easily and
54   precisely. It is considered a gold standard endpoint in oncology, as prolongation of life in the
55   setting of a life-threatening disease is of clear inherent value.
56
57   Overall survival should be prioritized as the primary endpoint when feasible. It is most
58   appropriate for consideration in randomized trials of oncologic diseases with a short natural
59   history (e.g., metastatic pancreatic cancer), late-line disease settings, or in disease settings where
60   there are other therapeutics known to prolong overall survival and it is important to demonstrate
61   retention or improvement of this survival advantage.
62
63   In certain oncology settings, it is often not feasible or practical to include overall survival as the
64   primary endpoint. In some indolent diseases or those with extremely efficacious therapeutics that
65   result in long survival times, the follow-up time needed to show superiority of overall survival as
66   the primary endpoint may be impractical. Additionally, as mentioned, overall survival may not be
67   appropriate in single-arm trials. Furthermore, although overall survival is an objective
68   measurement, the results and their interpretation can be impacted by crossover, receipt of
69   subsequent therapy, and other intercurrent events. These additional factors that impact the
70   interpretation of overall survival are discussed in Sections III.A and III.B.
71
72   When overall survival is not the primary endpoint and other endpoints such as response rate,
73   progression-free survival, or event-free survival are the primary endpoint in an oncology
74   randomized trial, FDA recommends collection and submission of overall survival data. In some
75   situations, overall survival may be a secondary endpoint included in a pre-specified hypothesis
76   testing plan to permit a formal statistical test for an efficacy evaluation, which includes control of

 77   the overall study-wise Type I error rate. 5,6 When it is not a secondary endpoint with an
 78   appropriate formal testing plan, FDA has historically evaluated the overall survival results in a
 79   descriptive manner, assessing overall survival as a safety measurement. There have been several
 80   drug development programs in which discordant results have been observed between early
 81   efficacy endpoints (e.g., response rate, minimal residual disease, or progression-free survival)
 82   and overall survival. 7 In some of these instances, the drug demonstrated anti-tumor activity, but
 83   the benefits of the anti-tumor activity were outweighed by drug-related toxicity that was
 84   evidenced by the observed decrements in overall survival. These examples underscore the
 85   importance of evaluation of overall survival as a safety assessment.
 86
 87   There are, however, inherent limitations in the assessment of overall survival in a descriptive
 88   manner, even if done as a safety assessment. Without a formal pre-specified plan for evaluation,
 89   the interpretation of the overall survival results can be challenging, especially if the study was
 90   not designed to collect sufficient overall survival data to provide estimates of treatment effect
 91   with a pre-specified level of precision.

 94   III.       RECOMMENDATIONS
 95
 96   When overall survival is not an alpha-controlled primary or secondary efficacy endpoint with a
 97   formal testing plan in a randomized oncology clinical trial, sponsors should include a pre-
 98   specified plan to assess overall survival, as a safety endpoint, with an aim to evaluate for
 99   potential harm due to the therapeutic intervention. Sponsors should consult with the FDA review
100   division on their plans to include overall survival analyses in the clinical protocol and SAP.
101
102              A.        Trial Design Considerations
103
104   This section includes an overview of clinical trial design considerations as it pertains to the
105   assessment of overall survival in oncology clinical trials.
106
107          •   Overall survival is generally defined as the time from randomization until death from any
108              cause.
109
110          •   All randomized oncology clinical trials should be designed to assess overall survival in
111              order to adequately evaluate the potential for harm (see also Section III.B.2).

155          — In most trials, interim analyses of overall survival would be appropriate to
156            include; however, interim analyses of overall survival may not be appropriate in
157            some small randomized clinical trials or clinical trials in diseases with a low
158            overall survival event rate (i.e., anticipated long overall survival times).

160   •   Crossover
161
162          — Protocol-specified crossover, is when trial participants who have disease
163            progression on one arm are allowed to initiate the therapeutic intervention of
164            another arm. Protocol-specified crossover has been included in clinical trials.
165
166          — Inclusion of crossover in clinical trials can improve enrollment and help with
167            retention of enrolled participants.
168
169          — Crossover can impact the interpretation of overall survival results because it can
170            confound treatment effect estimates. In general, its use should be limited but it
171            would be most appropriate to include in clinical trials of disease settings that have
172            no other or very limited therapeutic options.
173
174   •   Unequal randomization scheme (e.g., 2:1 randomization)
175
176          — Sponsors should consider the utility of using unequal randomization in the
177            protocol, as this may reduce the precision in the assessment of the difference in
178            overall survival between arms and the ability to adequately contextualize safety
179            findings.
180
181   •   Sponsors should include in their clinical trial protocols adequate plans for collecting data
182       to minimize the amount of missing data for the assessment of overall survival and other
183       long-term outcomes, including details on how data will be collected with regards to
184       capturing long-term safety and efficacy information.
185
186          — In general, treatment discontinuation should be distinguished from study
187            withdrawal, and all randomized participants, including those who discontinue
188            treatment and/or use subsequent therapies, should continue to be followed for
189            overall survival through the end of the controlled period.
190
191          — Participants and investigators should be counseled regarding the importance of
192            obtaining adequate follow-up information, even after discontinuation of study
193            drug treatment or after conclusion of active trial participation.
194
195          — Diverse approaches to minimize loss to follow-up may be specified, such as
196            increased efforts to maintain contact.
197
198          — Participants should be adequately followed after any intercurrent events to capture
199            important safety and efficacy information. If there is subsequent therapy use,

208           B.       Statistical Analysis Considerations
209
210   This section includes considerations for pre-specification of statistical analyses, assessments for
211   harm, and post hoc analyses of overall survival. Sponsors should, a priori, specify the statistical
212   analyses of overall survival, prior to knowledge of any study results or data unblinding, in a
213   clinical trial protocol or accompanying separate document (e.g., SAP). If the protocol and SAP
214   do not adequately characterize plans for assessing, measuring, or analyzing overall survival,
215   evaluations of overall survival may be considered post hoc. FDA also considers additional
216   analyses conducted on overall survival outside the scope of those pre-specified in the SAP to be
217   post hoc and for exploratory purposes (e.g., for hypothesis generation).
218
219           1.       General Considerations for Specification of Statistical Analyses
220
221       •   The SAP should prospectively detail assessment timing and methods for analyzing
222           overall survival.
223
224       •   The primary analysis (or main analysis) of overall survival should be specified in the
225           study protocol in order to define the primary metric for decision-making based on overall
226           survival, even if overall survival is not a primary endpoint. The choice of primary
227           analysis (and primary estimand) for overall survival should be based on the stated study
228           objectives.
229
230                — The primary analysis for overall survival should generally be based on the
231                  intention-to-treat (ITT) principle, that is, all randomized participants with
232                  comparisons based on randomized treatments.
233
234                — FDA generally recommends the hazard ratio (HR) obtained from a Cox
235                  proportional hazard model as a summary measure, along with its 95% confidence
236                  interval. In some cases, pre-specified covariates may be justified to be included in
237                  the Cox proportional hazards model. 9
238
239                — FDA generally recommends the log-rank test for the primary analysis of overall
240                  survival when there is alpha allocation, and either a stratified or unstratified test
241                  should be pre-specified.

289                — All-cause mortality should be considered for the primary evaluation of overall
290                  survival, rather than an endpoint excluding specific causes of death.
291
292                — The primary analysis should include all survival information, including survival
293                  times and deaths after subsequent therapy (i.e., a treatment policy approach within
294                  the estimand framework).
295
296                — Alternative strategies to handle certain intercurrent events may be justified.
297                  Note that strategies to handle intercurrent events should be supported by a
298                  rationale for the plausibility of the underlying assumptions (such as non-
299                  informative censoring).
300
301        •   Sponsors should provide supplementary and/or sensitivity analyses based on different
302            methods and/or assumptions to inform the evaluation of overall survival and the
303            robustness of results, particularly if the intercurrent event rate is high, uncertainty is high,
304            or results are marginal. Appropriate sensitivity analyses should evaluate the robustness of
305            results, and tipping point analyses could help evaluate how sensitive the observed results
306            are to various assumptions. Supplementary analyses should target different estimands and
307            can provide supportive information regarding overall survival.
308
309                — If non-proportional hazards are anticipated, sponsors should specify in the SAP
310                  additional sensitivity analyses to evaluate the robustness of the overall survival
311                  results. Supplementary analyses, such as landmark survival rates, restricted mean
312                  survival time, or other metrics, should be specified in the SAP and may provide
313                  supplementary information to further characterize overall survival.
314
315                — Sponsors should evaluate narratives and toxicity data to understand the causality
316                  of deaths, such as if deaths are due to progression or toxicity. Sensitivity analyses
317                  that account for attribution of the death to treatment can be performed to assess
318                  the impact of deaths potentially due to causes other than treatment, or other types
319                  of competing risk analysis can be performed. For example, COVID-related
320                  mortality may be a concern for some studies. Participants with COVID-19 could
321                  be more vulnerable to drug risks, and risk of death could be increased on
322                  treatment. For this reason, COVID-19 deaths should be included in all-cause
323                  mortality.10 However, a supplementary analysis may be performed where
324                  COVID-19 deaths are censored.
325
326                — Using different approaches to handling subsequent therapy or crossover can
327                  evaluate different estimands via supplementary analyses. Common approaches
328                  include evaluating restricted mean survival time or utilizing causal models and
329                  methods to adjust for treatment switching. However, many of these methods rely
330                  on unverifiable assumptions and generally are not suitable as the sole or primary
331                  evaluation of overall survival.

333            2.      Assessment of Harm
334
335        •   To adequately inform regulatory decision-making, any trial should be designed a priori to
336            adequately capture the number of events needed to rule out harm based on a specified
337            threshold(s). Sponsors should specify and justify in the SAP a threshold or a range of
338            thresholds to indicate the potential for harm for the overall survival summary measure.
339
340                 — Trials should be designed to collect sufficient overall survival data in order to rule
341                   out a clinically relevant degree of harm with a pre-specified degree of precision
342                   (e.g., based on the number of expected overall survival events, the power to rule
343                   out a pre-specified threshold of harm can be provided). Different scenarios based
344                   on a variety of plausible assumptions (e.g., varying number of events, overall
345                   survival effect size, or thresholds for harm) can provide support that the study is
346                   adequately designed to rule out harm.
347
348        •   When determining the pre-specified threshold(s) for harm and proposed degree of
349            precision to rule out harm, consider the following: disease setting, known safety profile
350            of the product or drug class, input from patients and physicians, expected number of
351            events, length of expected survival, duration and severity of expected adverse events, use
352            of subsequent therapies or crossover, potential for non-proportional hazards, evidence of
353            benefit from other endpoints, expected benefit of the control, acceptable level of
354            uncertainty, feasibility of follow-up, and other available data.
355
356        •   There are multiple approaches 11 to assess if study data can rule out harm, and the
357            approach will depend on the degree of precision that is feasible (or degree of uncertainty
358            that is acceptable) and the overall survival summary measure used. If there is high
359            uncertainty and/or marginal overall survival results, additional follow-up may be required
360            and may be the only reliable way to adequately assess harm. In cases where a study is not
361            designed to test for overall survival superiority, uncertainty in the estimate of the overall
362            survival HR is expected, and there may still be uncertainty even with long-term data
363            collection in diseases with long survival times.
364
365                 — When the sponsor expects that sufficiently mature overall survival data will be
366                   collected or the study is designed to demonstrate superiority of overall survival,
367                   one option that can be used is to evaluate if a 95% confidence interval for the
368                   overall survival HR excludes a clinically relevant threshold. The threshold should
369                   be justified, and the study should be designed to estimate the overall survival HR
370                   with a sufficient degree of precision.

384              follow-up time (which may or may not be feasible). Various methodologies can be

390              future data. However, the uncertainty in these methods generally increases for
391              longer looks into the future, and evaluation of harm cannot solely be based on
392              hypothetical future data.

394       3.      Post Hoc Analyses
395
396   •   Sponsors can conduct post hoc analyses to supplement their pre-specified analyses. Post
397       hoc analyses of overall survival can provide additional insight into the potential for harm.
398
399   •   For studies lacking pre-specification of overall survival analyses (as recommended in this
400       guidance), analysis of overall survival should still be performed. In this case, sponsor
401       should perform additional supplementary analyses and simulations to investigate the
402       robustness of results under diverse assumptions.
403
404   •   Post hoc thresholds for harm can be based on similar considerations for a pre-specified
405       threshold; however, in the post hoc setting, multiple measures that reflect the full survival
406       distribution should be considered, including the overall survival HR, and multiple
407       credible thresholds, or grid of thresholds should be explored under a variety of scenarios.
408       However, it may not be possible to detect harm based on studies not adequately designed
409       to assess overall survival.
410
411   •   There are several caveats regarding assessment of overall survival in the post hoc setting,
412       including:
413
414            — FDA considers post hoc analyses exploratory and suitable for hypothesis
415              generation but not suitable for statistical testing with alpha allocation.

436          C.      Subgroup Considerations
437
438   This section includes considerations for evaluation of overall survival in subgroups. Based on
439   prior information, there may be known subgroups of patients where the overall survival effect
440   may be expected to vary across subgroups, such as biomarker-defined subgroups or populations
441   vulnerable to adverse events. When those subgroups are not identified a priori in the study
442   protocol or SAP, FDA considers subsequent analyses of these subgroups to be post hoc.
443
444      •   Known or biologically plausible subgroups should be prospectively identified in a study,
445          and a plan for adequate overall survival data collection within the subgroups should be
446          pre-specified. This includes provisions for extended follow-up periods to enable a robust
447          evaluation.
448
449      •   Depending on the intended use, analyses of overall survival in subgroups can be
450          exploratory and need not be alpha-controlled; however, these analyses should be pre-
451          specified.
452
453               — Pre-specified analyses should include subgroup analyses by demographic
454                 characteristics (age, sex, race, and ethnicity), region, and anticipated prognostic or
455                 predictive characteristics or biomarkers.
456
457               — Sponsors should account in the study design for subgroups known to potentially
458                 experience differential treatment effects on survival, and analyses of these
459                 subgroups should be pre-specified. The importance of enrolling subgroups of
460                 sufficient size and conducting formal hypothesis tests for overall survival within
461                 these subgroups depends on the expected difference in survival or potential for

                                      Draft — Not for Implementation

462              harm in each subgroup, and the expected contribution of each subgroup to the
463              entire overall survival treatment effect.
464
465          — Additionally, if there are known subgroups and potential for using the overall
466            survival subgroup results for labeling purposes, pre-specified study-wise control
467            of Type I error is strongly recommended for hypothesis testing within these
468            subgroups (and for all primary and key secondary analyses).
469
470   •   The following should be included in the study protocol when designing a trial with where
471       the overall survival effect is expected to vary across pre-specified subgroups:
472
473          — Biological or mechanistic rationale for expected differential treatment effects on
474            survival based on subgroups;
475
476          — Anticipated survival benefit or detriment, within each of the subgroups;
477
478          — Expected contribution of the subgroups to the overall treatment effect;
479
480          — Prevalence and plans for adequate enrollment of patients in subgroups;
481
482          — Feasibility of and justification for any planned stratified randomization for
483            prognostic or predictive subgroups;
484
485          — Comprehensive analysis plan for the subgroups, detailing objectives of each
486            planned overall survival analysis (whether for safety or efficacy), and pre-
487            specified thresholds for harm;
488
489          — If a formal hypothesis test for overall survival is planned within subgroups, the
490            number of events that would be required to detect a clinically meaningful
491            difference in survival in each of the subgroups.
492
493   •   Subgroup results should be interpreted with caution, especially if overall survival data are
494       immature or analyses were conducted post hoc. Findings based on subgroups defined
495       post hoc or post hoc analyses of pre-specified subgroups may result from random patterns
496       and are considered hypothesis generating, not evidence of benefit. However, such
497       findings may be sufficient to raise safety concerns, warranting further investigation.
498
499          — There should be consistent results across subgroups, any observed differences
500            should be further evaluated (i.e., additional post hoc analyses should be
501            conducted).

503                 — Subgroup results cannot be used to salvage a trial that was not successful based on

546          •   Accelerated Approval
547
548                  — The accelerated approval pathway is intended for drugs that address an unmet
549                    medical need in the treatment of a serious or life-threatening disease or condition.
550                    Specifically, FDA can approve a marketing application on the basis of, among
551                    other things, adequate and well-controlled clinical trials establishing that the drug
552                    product has an effect on a surrogate endpoint that is reasonably likely to predict
553                    clinical benefit, or on an intermediate clinical endpoint that can be measured
554                    earlier than irreversible morbidity or mortality that is reasonably likely to predict
555                    an effect on irreversible morbidity or mortality or other clinical benefit, taking
556                    into account the severity, rarity, or prevalence of the condition and the availability
557                    or lack of alternative treatments. 13,14,15
558
559                  — Often in oncology, accelerated approval is based on a clinical trial that evaluated
560                    response rate or another intermediate clinical endpoint (e.g., progression-free
561                    survival, etc.).
562
563                  — If there is significant uncertainty in the overall survival results, but efficacy is
564                    supported by an intermediate clinical endpoint, accelerated approval may be the
565                    most appropriate approval pathway. Traditional approval can then be considered
566                    at a later time when there are sufficiently robust and interpretable overall survival
567                    results available.

570

573                    may enter into a written agreement with the sponsor to collect postapproval data
