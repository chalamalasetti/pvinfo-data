13   I.      INTRODUCTION
14
15   This guidance document provides recommendations for developing assays 1 to assess potency 2 as
16   a part of a potency assurance strategy for active immunotherapy products (ACTIMPs) 3. Active
17   immunotherapies aim to treat a pre-existing disease or condition by inducing, stimulating, or
18   modulating immune effector cells through introduction of an antigen (or antigens) associated
19   with that disease or condition to the immune system. This guidance does not apply to preventive
20   vaccines for infectious disease indications, bacteriophage products for infectious diseases, live
21   biotherapeutic products, fecal microbiota for transplantation (FMT) products or allergenic
22   products. Considerations related to potential safety concerns associated with ACTIMP product
23   design are outside the scope of this guidance. Sponsors are therefore encouraged to consult with
24   the appropriate product office regarding such matters.
25
26   When finalized, this guidance will describe the FDA’s current thinking regarding design,
27   validation, and evaluation of potency tests for ACTIMPs. This guidance is intended to
28   supplement the guidance for industry, when finalized, on Potency Assurance for Cellular and
29   Gene Therapy Products (Ref. 1) with additional advice and considerations specifically for
30   ACTIMPs, including peptide- and-protein based ACTIMPs that are not cellular or gene therapy
31   products (Ref. 2).
32

40   II.      BACKGROUND
41
42   This guidance is limited to providing recommendations to assure the potency of ACTIMPs
43   regulated as biological products under section 351 of the Public Health Service Act (PHS Act)
44   (42 U.S.C. § 262 (a)). Preventive vaccines for infectious disease indications, bacteriophage
45   products for infectious diseases, live biotherapeutic products, fecal microbiota for transplantation
46   (FMT) products and allergenic products are beyond the scope of this guidance.
47
48   The goal of active immunotherapies is to treat a pre-existing disease or condition by inducing,
49   stimulating, or modulating immune effector cells by introducing an antigen (or antigens)
50   associated with that disease or condition to the immune system. ACTIMPs may be vectors that
51   express antigens, 4 peptide or protein antigens, 5 or cells that present or express antigens (e.g., cell
52   lysates or antigen-pulsed antigen presenting cells (APCs)). A subset of ACTIMPs are intended
53   to treat cancer by activating immune cells that target tumor-specific antigens and/or tumor-
54   associated antigens (TAAs), including neoantigens and viral antigens expressed by virus-
55   transformed cancer cells. Recent advances in genomics and new knowledge regarding mutation
56   load in tumor cells have led to rapid improvements in ACTIMPs intended to treat cancer through
57   induction of anti-cancer immune responses. ACTIMPs also include products intended to treat
58   autoimmune disease by inducing immune tolerance against specific antigens associated with that
59   autoimmune disease.
60
61   Potency assays for cell and gene therapy products often focus on direct evaluation of biological
62   activity of the product, for example by evaluating biological activity of expressed proteins,
63   activity of recombinant protein-expressing cells, or a function of the cells themselves. However,
64   assessment of ACTIMP’s potency is challenging because the biological activity relies on host
65   immune responses. The challenge can be even greater for individualized (personalized)
66   therapeutic ACTIMPs, where the use of patient-specific neoantigens raises unique considerations
67   for designing potency assays, including limited availability of specific reagents for each product
68   lot (e.g., where there is no readily available antibody to measure expressed antigen). In this
69   guidance, we discuss a science- and risk-based approach (Ref. 3) to developing suitable potency
70   assays for ACTIMPs. We also provide specific examples of assays that can help assure the
71   potency of various types of ACTIMPs.

 75   III.       REGULATORY FRAMEWORK
 76
 77   All biological products regulated under section 351 of the PHS Act must be evaluated to assure
 78   that the product is safe, pure, and potent, 6 that the continued safety, purity, and potency is
 79   assured, 7 and that quality is intact. 8 Potency assays are critical in ensuring that a product is
 80   capable of functioning as intended and are performed during lot release testing, comparability
 81   studies (Refs. 4 - 5), and stability testing (Ref. 6). Potency assays help assure that only product
 82   lots that meet defined criteria (Ref. 7) are administered during clinical investigations and after
 83   marketing authorization.
 84
 85          •   Potency Expectations for Licensed ACTIMPs: Biologics license applications (BLAs)
 86              for products regulated under section 351 of the PHS Act must contain, among other
 87              things, data demonstrating that the product is potent, and that the continued potency of
 88              the product is assured. 9 FDA regulations allow for considerable flexibility in
 89              determining the appropriate measurement(s) of potency for each product. Potency refers
 90              to the ability or capacity of the product to achieve the intended therapeutic effect;
 91              therefore, the suitability of assays to measure potency is evaluated on a case-by-case
 92              basis to account for product-specific attributes. All potency assays used for lot release
 93              testing of licensed biological products must comply with applicable regulations,
 94              including applicable Biological Product and Current Good Manufacturing Practice
 95              (CGMP) regulations. 10, 11 Further, such assays must be validated. 12 Potency assay(s)
 96              should be used as a part of the stability determining tests to establish product expiration
 97              date for licensure.
 98
 99          •   Potency Expectations for Investigational ACTIMPs: In early-phase clinical
100              investigations, it may not be possible to meet all of the requirements for potency
101              described above for licensed biological products (Refs. 8 - 10). Nonetheless, sponsors
102              must submit as part of the Investigational New Drug Application (IND) data to assure the
103              identity, quality, purity and strength, as well as stability, of products used during all
104              phases of clinical study. 13 “[T]he amount of information needed to make that assurance
105              will vary with the phase of the investigation, the proposed duration of the investigation,
106              the dosage form, and the amount of information otherwise available.” 14. To facilitate the
107              development of ACTIMPs, we recommend a progressively iterative approach to potency

119   IV.     GENERAL RECOMMENDATIONS ON POTENCY ASSAYS FOR ACTIVE
120           IMMUNOTHERAPY PRODUCTS
121
122           A.       Assessing Potency of ACTIMPs
123
124                •   A thorough understanding of the ACTIMP’s active ingredient(s) and mechanism
125                    of action (MOA) should be used to identify potency-related critical quality
126                    attributes (CQAs) that should be measured for lot release, stability, and
127                    comparability. The relationship between MOA (e.g., stimulation of an antigen-
128                    specific T cell response) and potency-related CQAs (e.g., epitope properties)
129                    should be supported (e.g., by published medical or scientific information that is
130                    generally accepted by experts qualified by scientific training and experience in the
131                    relevant field including FDA experts, product characterization studies, nonclinical
132                    studies, or clinical studies.) 17 Additional knowledge about the product’s MOA
133                    and CQAs can also be gained through monitoring immune responses in study
134                    subjects who have received the ACTIMP.
135
136                •   Potency assays should be quantitative and should have adequate precision to
137                    reliably discriminate between a product that is sufficiently active to achieve its
138                    intended therapeutic effect and a product that has insufficient activity, to identify
139                    sub-potent lots and allow for their rejection.
140
141                •   For ACTIMPs, potency assays should directly or indirectly measure the ability or
142                    capacity of the product to mediate immune responses to achieve the intended
143                    therapeutic effect.

188           B.       Establishing Potency-Related CQAs for ACTIMPs
189
190                •   An understanding of how the product mediates a therapeutic effect is important
191                    for identifying the product’s potency-related CQAs. We recommend that
192                    sponsors evaluate data from various sources, including preclinical or proof-of-
193                    concept data (in vitro or in vivo), previous human clinical trial experience (your
194                    prior knowledge), and in vitro cellular or biochemical characterization studies.
195
196                •   Potency-related CQAs should be identified for each product by considering the
197                    following:
198
199                        -    Knowledge of the ACTIMP’s MOA;
200                        -    Product characterization data;
201                        -    How the CQA(s) affect the product’s biological activity i.e., by assessing
202                             the relationship between candidate potency-related CQAs and the
203                             product’s ability or capacity to achieve the intended immune response; and
204                        -    Data from studies of candidate potency-related CQAs.
205
206           C.       Approaches for Potency Measurement of ACTIMPs
207
208                •   Bioassays: The most direct assessment of ACTIMP potency is a quantitative
209                    bioassay that measures the product’s effect on the immune system using living
210                    cells, tissues, or animals. Bioassays can help mitigate risks to a product’s potency
211                    that may not be fully addressed through physicochemical assays alone. Bioassays
212                    may be either in vitro or in vivo assays or a combination of these methods. We
213                    encourage methods that replace or reduce the use of animals whenever possible. 19
214                    Examples of in vitro bioassays relevant for measuring ACTIMP potency include,
215                    but are not limited to, cell culture-based assays that quantitate the ability of the
216                    ACTIMP to stimulate antigen-specific T cell responses such as proliferation,
217                    cytokine production, or cytotoxicity. Examples of in vivo assays include
218                    induction of an antigen-specific immune response in appropriate animal models,
219                    collection of samples from animals, and measurements that reflect the proposed
220                    MOA, such as tetramer staining, intracellular cytokine staining, Enzyme-Linked
221                    Immunospot (ELISPOT), or antibody responses.
222
223                •   Physicochemical assays: If you have established that the potency of an ACTIMP
224                    can be adequately assured without a bioassay, you can perform lot release testing
225                    of potency-related CQAs using physicochemical assays alone. Physicochemical

236           PRODUCTS
237
238   The following recommendations are listed by type of ACTIMP:
239
240           A.       Non-personalized 20 Peptide- and Protein-based ACTIMPs
241
242                •   In the case of ACTIMPs where each product batch contains the same peptide(s),
243                    peptide concatemers, purified proteins, protein conjugates, or recombinant
244                    protein(s), we recommend that the potency assay and the necessary assay-specific
245                    reagents (e.g., assay specific cell lines, antibodies, tetramers) be developed and
246                    qualified during early product development to ensure assay consistency
247                    throughout the product life cycle.
248
249                •   When the physicochemical attributes of a non-personalized protein or peptide
250                    product have been established as sufficient to assess the product’s ability or
251                    capacity to achieve the intended immunological response, the physicochemical
252                    attributes alone may be used to evaluate the product’s potency for lot release
253                    testing and stability studies.
254
255                •   When physicochemical tests alone are not sufficient to define the product’s ability
256                    or capacity to achieve the intended immunological response, a bioassay may be
257                    needed to assure the product’s immunological activity. For example, a bioassay
258                    may be needed to assess potency if the product consists of multiple proteins that
259                    cannot be adequately quantitated through physicochemical assays alone, or if the
260                    product is susceptible to chemical or structural changes that may affect antigen
261                    processing or epitope presentation and these changes cannot be reliably
262                    quantitated using physicochemical tests.
263
264                •   If you are designing a bioassay for a product that consists of multiple proteins or
265                    multiple antigenic epitopes concatemerized into a single polypeptide chain, it may
266                    be sufficient for the bioassay to quantitate the activity of one or more individual

283            B.       Non-personalized Vectored ACTIMPs
284
285                 •   Vectored ACTIMPs, including those based on viral vectors, bacterial vectors,
286                     DNA, or RNA, depend on two biological activities to induce their intended effect:
287                     the ability to transfer a genetic sequence to a cell, and the intended biological
288                     effect of the expressed gene sequence. Therefore, potency assays for these types
289                     of ACTIMPs should incorporate a quantitative potency bioassay either through a
290                     protein expression assay (when justified by evidence of a direct relationship
291                     between the level of expressed protein and its immunological activity), or through
292                     a bioassay that measures immune responses to the expressed protein (for example,
293                     the ability of vector-transduced APCs to activate target antigen-specific T cells).
294
295                 •   Because of concerns related to induction of off-target immune responses and the
296                     possibility of antigenic competition and reduction of the therapeutic effect, we do
297                     not recommend the introduction and use of reporter genes or irrelevant epitopes in
298                     the vector, unless their functional role in rendering a therapeutic benefit has been
299                     demonstrated in proof-of-concept studies. 21

375   E.       Cell-based ACTIMPs
376
377   Examples of cell-based ACTIMPs include products composed of living cells (e.g.,
378   antigen-pulsed dendritic cells), irradiated cells (e.g., non-dividing cancer cells), or cell-
379   derived products (e.g., tumor lysates) that are used to induce antigen-specific immune
380   responses in vivo or to modulate immune responses in the patient. The cells may be
381   genetically modified or unmodified cells.
382
383        •   Potency assays for live cell-based ACTIMPs should measure an activity of the
384            cellular product that can be used to evaluate its ability to perform as intended.
385
386        •   The potency of cell-based ACTIMPs that express known antigens and/or
387            immunomodulatory molecules (e.g., cytokines) should be assessed by evaluating
388            potency-related CQAs such as the expressed antigen and/or immunomodulator
389            along with additional potency-related CQAs of the cells (e.g., assessment of co-
390            stimulatory molecule expression by the cell).
391
392        •   Cell viability is an important potency-related CQA and should be a part of the
393            overall potency assessment.
394
395        •   For ACTIMPs consisting of different cell types, where the contributions of each
396            cell type to the activity of the ACTIMP are either unknown or are interdependent
397            with other cell types in the product, it may be appropriate to assess potency on the
398            product as a whole.
399
400        •   When two or more cell populations contribute to product function via the same
401            MOA (e.g., two types of APCs or a product containing two autologous cell
402            lysates), a combined potency assay that measures the total biological activity may
403            be sufficient.
404
405        •   When two or more cell populations are intended to contribute to product activity
406            via distinct and independent MOAs, the contribution of each cell population to the
407            potency of the product should be evaluated independently or in a combined assay
408            that can measure all distinct and independent contributions.
