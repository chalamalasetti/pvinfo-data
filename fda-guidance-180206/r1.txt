I.       INTRODUCTION

This guidance provides answers to commonly asked questions from applicants and other
interested parties (collectively referred to as applicants throughout this guidance) regarding
postapproval manufacturing changes (referred to as manufacturing changes throughout this
guidance) made to licensed biosimilars and licensed interchangeable biosimilars. 2 This
question-and-answer (Q&A) guidance is intended to inform prospective and current applicants of
the nature and type of information that applicants should provide in support of manufacturing
changes to licensed biosimilars and licensed interchangeable biosimilars in different reporting
categories. The recommendations in this guidance are intended to be consistent with the
principles outlined in the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) guidance for industry
Q5E Comparability of Biotechnological/Biological Products Subject to Changes in Their
Manufacturing Process (June 2005) (ICH Q5E). 3

Under § 601.12 (21 CFR 601.12), applicants must inform FDA about each change in the product,
production process, quality controls, equipment, facilities, or responsible personnel, established
in the approved biologics license application (BLA). Before distributing a product made using a

II.        BACKGROUND

           A.       Section 351 of the PHS Act

Section 351 of the PHS Act provides an abbreviated licensure pathway for biological products
shown to be biosimilar to or interchangeable with an FDA-licensed biological reference product.
Section 351(k) of the PHS Act sets forth the requirements for licensure of such biosimilar
products and interchangeable biosimilar products.

Section 351(i)(2) of the PHS Act defines biosimilarity to mean “that the biological product is
highly similar to the reference product notwithstanding minor differences in clinically inactive
components” and that “there are no clinically meaningful differences between the biological
product and the reference product in terms of the safety, purity, and potency of the product.” To
meet the standard for interchangeability, the applicant must: (1) demonstrate biosimilarity to the
reference product; (2) demonstrate that the biological product “can be expected to produce the
same clinical result as the reference product in any given patient”; and (3) if the biological
product “is administered more than once to an individual, the risk in terms of safety or

           B.       Recommendations for Product Quality Data

An applicant who intends to make a manufacturing change to a licensed biosimilar product or a
licensed interchangeable biosimilar product should follow the principles outlined in ICH Q5E.
When changes are made to the manufacturing process, the applicant should evaluate the
comparability of the biosimilar or interchangeable biosimilar product before and after the
manufacturing change (comparability exercise). The extent of data and information necessary to
establish comparability should be commensurate with the type of manufacturing change and its
overall potential to adversely affect the quality, safety, and efficacy of the product. The design
of the comparability exercise, including the quality attributes 26 to be compared and analytical
methods to be used, should address the risks of the manufacturing change(s) and should provide
sufficient data and information to demonstrate the comparability of the biosimilar or
interchangeable biosimilar product before and after the manufacturing change. In addition to
comparability data, other product quality data and information, such as process validation data,
should be included in the supplement, as applicable. 27

Data and information submitted in support of manufacturing changes should demonstrate that
quality attributes remain comparable among the prechange and postchange products and should
demonstrate that consistency in the quality, safety, and efficacy of the postchange product is
predictable. The postchange biosimilar or interchangeable biosimilar product should be
evaluated at the process step most appropriate to detect a change in the quality attributes. This
may entail comparisons at multiple stages of manufacture. For instance, if the change occurred

The goal of the comparability exercise is to ensure that manufacturing process changes do not
lead to an adverse impact on the quality, safety, and efficacy of the licensed biosimilar product or
the licensed interchangeable biosimilar product. The comparability exercise should include a
side-by-side analytical comparison of a sufficient number of lots of prechange and postchange
materials, 28 including stability data, as appropriate. As described in ICH Q5E, for certain
manufacturing process changes, even slight modifications of the production procedures might
cause changes in the stability of the postchange product, and appropriate studies should be
considered to confirm that suitable storage conditions and controls are selected. Comparative
stability studies conducted under relevant storage conditions (e.g., accelerated testing, stress
testing) among prechange and postchange materials can detect subtle differences that may not be
readily detectable by characterization studies and may, therefore, provide greater insight into
differences between the prechange and the postchange product. These comparative stability
studies are especially important when the proposed manufacturing changes can alter protein
structure or purity and impurity profiles. 29 The selection of the conditions for the stability
studies should be justified based on relevance to the product and risks associated with the
manufacturing change.

A comparison of analytical data generated on the postchange material to the historical analytical
data (i.e., data generated on the prechange material) may be sufficient to support a manufacturing
change if the quality attributes of the prechange and the postchange materials are comparable.
The historical analytical data should include results from biosimilar or interchangeable
biosimilar lots used in the comparative analytical assessment (CAA), 30 biosimilar or
interchangeable biosimilar lots used in the clinical development of the biosimilar or the
interchangeable biosimilar product, lots used to support process consistency, and commercial
materials manufactured after approval, as applicable. When a subset of all available historical
data is selected for the comparison, a scientific justification should be provided. If an analytical
assay has changed since licensure of the biosimilar or the interchangeable biosimilar product,
adequate assay bridging data on assay performance should be provided.

Q2.      What reference materials should applicants include in the comparability exercise?

In general, in addition to the prechange and postchange product, an applicant should include a
well-qualified, in-house reference material in the comparability exercise to support that the
postchange biosimilar or interchangeable biosimilar remains comparable to the prechange
product. The in-house reference material serves as an important calibration point for the
evaluation(s) conducted in a comparability exercise. 31

If applicants submit sufficient data to demonstrate comparability, supporting that the
manufacturing change(s) will have no adverse impact on the quality, safety or efficacy, including
immunogenicity, of the postchange product, FDA will generally consider the data adequate to
support the change without the inclusion of reference materials beyond the in-house reference
material in the comparability exercise. When differences in the quality attributes are observed
between the prechange and the postchange materials that may preclude a demonstration of
comparability based solely on the comparison of the prechange and postchange materials, one
approach to assessing the potential impact and acceptability of these differences would be to use
the CAA data from the reference product that were submitted to support the original licensure of
the biosimilar or interchangeable biosimilar product to inform a justification for the
comparability of the postchange product.

The extent of comparability data and CAA data provided in the supplement should be
commensurate with the level of risk associated with the proposed change. Applicants should
include a risk assessment of the differences between the licensed product and the proposed
product with a new dosage form or new strength to support the justification of the comparability
and CAA data provided in the supplement. Based on the differences observed between the
licensed product and the proposed product, and the associated risk assessment, targeted CAA
data may be needed; however, in some cases, it may be reasonable to leverage the CAA data
previously submitted in the 351(k) BLA, potentially without additional, risk-based, targeted
CAA studies comparing the proposed product to its reference product. For example, if an
applicant submits a supplement proposing a new strength or dosage form, and the same drug
substance is used to manufacture the licensed product and the proposed product, leveraging the
CAA previously submitted in the 351(k) BLA along with a risk-based comparability exercise
