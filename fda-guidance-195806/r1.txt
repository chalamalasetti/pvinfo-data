1.0     INTRODUCTION

        1.1 Purpose

This document provides technical specifications for submitting data from a clinical trial to
evaluate the ability to operate a motor vehicle (herein referred to as a driving study) to support a
marketing application for drug or biological product. As presented in FDA’s guidance for
industry Evaluating Drug Effects on the Ability to Operate a Motor Vehicle (November 2017),2
driving studies are primarily conducted to evaluate the effects of psychoactive drugs on the
ability to operate a motor vehicle, though driving studies can also be conducted to evaluate
effects of certain non-psychoactive drugs. These technical specifications should be referenced
when a driving assessment is conducted as part of a clinical drug development program.

        1.2    Background

Driving assessments are functional tests that are used to collect objective findings of a
participant’s ability to operate a motor vehicle by using a measurement tool such as a computer
simulator or a specially instrumented on-road vehicle. The measurement tool produces data
relevant to the clinical investigation but is not under study, and example measurements collected
during a driving assessment can include Standard Deviation of Lateral Position (SDLP), lane
exceedance or lane drifts, response time, and number of collisions. When the drug under study
has significant safety issues where the risk of assessing a participant on the road using a specially
instrumented vehicle is determined to be too high, a computer simulator may be preferable.

Driving studies typically employ a crossover design to compare the effects of an investigational
drug at one or multiple doses against both a positive control and a placebo. The timing of the
planned driving assessment in each period can vary depending on the intended therapeutic use of
the drug under study.3 For instance, to evaluate the effect that an investigational sleep drug has
on the ability to operate a motor vehicle, a next-day driving assessment can be conducted nine
hours postdose. Alternatively, to evaluate the effects of an investigational migraine drug the
driving assessment can be performed 90 minutes postdose to assess an immediate effect on the
operability. It is recommended that before initiating the trial, the sponsor consult with the FDA
for specific guidance on designating the primary and secondary endpoints that align with the
study objectives.

        1.3      Scope

This document provides specifications for the submission of Clinical Data Interchange Standards
Consortium (CDISC) Study Data Tabulation Model (SDTM) and Analysis Data Model (ADaM)
datasets and specifications for tables and figures. These technical specifications aim to provide
general guidelines for standardized content and structure to facilitate FDA review of the
marketing application that the submitted data and analysis outputs are intended to support. The
SDTM and ADaM dataset specifications outlined in section 3.0 Overview of Dataset
Specifications emphasize clinical outcome assessment (COA) data, including the driving
assessment data and questionnaires data used to evaluate the primary and secondary endpoints
defined within the driving study. The dataset specifications are not prescriptive and do not
include an exhaustive list of all datasets, variables, and controlled terminologies to be submitted
for FDA review. Further, the recommended tables and figures within section 4.0 Specifications
for Tables and Figures do not comprise all information critical to support FDA review of a
marketing application. Sponsors should discuss with FDA which specifications are applicable for
their particular clinical development program.

Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act);4

corresponding analyses should be discussed with FDA as early as possible in a clinical drug or
biologic development program (e.g., the End-of-Phase 2 Meeting). Sponsors are strongly
encouraged to use the resources described in section 1.4 Relationships to Other Documents and
to seek Agency input for confirmation and clarification as appropriate. Sponsors should consult
with the Agency to determine which requested displays defined in section 4.0 Specifications for
Tables and Figures apply to the driving study data used to support their marketing application.

3.0     OVERVIEW OF DATASET SPECIFICATIONS

These dataset specifications detail the CDISC datasets that are used to support the submission of
driving assessment data and questionnaires data. Sponsors should implement the CDISC SDTM
standard when submitting data collected during the clinical trial and the CDISC ADaM standard
when submitting analysis data. Throughout this document, the terms domain and dataset are used
interchangeably and refer to the same concept. This section supplements existing guidance by
providing specifications for submitting driving assessment data and questionnaires data but does
not cover all data to be submitted for a clinical trial. As documented in the FDA sdTCG,18 both
SDTM and ADaM datasets should be accompanied by informative metadata provided in a
compliant data definition file (i.e., Define-XML) and are expected to be accompanied by a
Clinical Study Data Reviewer’s Guide (cSDRG) and an Analysis Data Reviewer’s Guide
(ADRG), respectively. Standard CDISC Controlled Terminology19 developed and maintained by
CDISC and National Cancer Institute Enterprise Vocabulary Services (NCI EVS) should be used
where applicable, and codelists can be extensible by adding controlled terminology that is not
previously defined. Sponsor-extended codelists and use of alternative (e.g., non-CDISC or
sponsor-defined) terminologies should be indicated in the study metadata (e.g., Define-XML file,
cSDRG). In addition, software programs (source code) used to create the ADaM datasets and to
analyze the driving study data should be submitted with the marketing application for FDA
review.

        3.1      Driving Assessment Datasets

This section specifies the datasets used to submit driving assessment data from a measurement
tool such as a computer simulator or a specially instrumented on-road vehicle. These datasets
include the SDTM Functional Tests (FT) dataset and the ADaM dataset containing analysis-
ready driving assessment data (herein referred to as the Functional Tests Analysis Dataset
(ADFT)).

          3.1.1.3 Handling of Missing and Partial Driving Assessment Data in SDTM

Understanding the reasons for and prevalence of missing and partial driving assessment data are
critical to support FDA review and regulatory decision-making. FDA is especially interested in
understanding the safety and/or tolerability issues that prevented a participant from completing
an assessment in full, which can affect FDA’s assessment of benefit-risk. To aid in making this
determination, the study protocol and statistical analysis plan (SAP) should contain the definition
and rationale for the criteria that compose a ”complete” driving assessment.

When the reason for the missed assessment is collected, missing driving assessment data are
represented in the FT dataset with FTSTAT = ‘NOT DONE’ and the reason for the missed
assessment entered within ‘Reason Not Done’ (FTREASND), when the reason is collected in the
source data (e.g., collected on the CRF). When a result is produced for a driving assessment
measurement based on a partial driving assessment (i.e., a driving assessment begins as planned
but ends prematurely), the partial driving assessment data are submitted in the FT dataset with
FTORRES, FTDTC, and FTENDTC populated, and FTSTAT and FTREASND set to empty. In
this scenario, Reason for Premature End (FTREASPE) of the driving assessment is encouraged
to be collected and subsequently reported as a supplemental qualifier in the SUPPFT dataset as
shown in Table 2. Conversely, when a result is not produced for a driving assessment
measurement based on a partial driving assessment, this scenario is treated like missing data in
the FT dataset with FTSTAT and FTREASND populated and FTORRES set to empty. Capturing
details for missing and partial data on the CRF supplements the collected driving assessment

        3.1.2    ADaM Functional Tests Analysis Dataset (ADFT)

This section provides specifications for the ADaM dataset containing analysis-ready driving
assessment data within the Functional Tests Analysis Dataset (ADFT). The ADFT dataset
follows the ADaM Basic Data Structure (BDS) and is created from data in the SDTM FT dataset
discussed in section 3.1.1 SDTM Functional Tests (FT) Dataset in conjunction with other SDTM
and ADaM data.

             3.1.2.1    General Considerations

Table 4 contains specifications for a subset of ADFT variables, some of which are standard
variables (included to clarify how they should be completed for driving assessments to foster
industry consistency, standardization, and traceability) and some of which are newly defined.
Table 4 does not represent a complete list of ADFT variables to be submitted. Additional
variables important for analysis (such as indicator variables and analysis flags; variables to aid in

            3.1.2.2   Joining Driving Assessment Data to Other Data

As presented in Table 4 Specifications for a Subset of ADFT Variables, joining non-driving
assessment data to the driving assessment data reported within the ADFT dataset is
recommended for certain data such as pharmacokinetic (PK) concentrations data, which can be
joined from the SDTM Pharmacokinetics Concentrations (PC) dataset or the ADaM ADPC
dataset. Providing PK data within the ADFT dataset supports exposure-response analysis, which
is conducted in addition to the primary statistical analysis done for driving endpoints such as
SDLP (See section 4.0 Specifications for Tables and Figures). Questionnaires data are an
example of other non-driving assessment data that can be joined to the ADFT data depending on
analysis needs of the study. When the protocol specifies the joining of non-driving assessment
data to the driving assessment data during data collection, the Related Records (RELREC)
dataset is employed in SDTM as described in section 3.1.2.2.1 Linking Data in SDTM via
RELREC. Alternatively, if the joining is performed retrospectively for analysis purposes and not
while the driving assessment data are collected, data are linked directly in the ADFT dataset as
described in section 3.1.2.2.2 Joining Data in ADaM.

Joining adverse event (AE) data and driving assessment data can be beneficial when assessing
whether an AE affects a participant’s performance during a driving assessment. It is understood
that the occurrence of an AE during a driving assessment may not result in missing or partial
data. FDA is interested in knowing whether an AE occurred during the time of the driving
assessment, and if so, which AE. However, joining AE data from the Analysis Data Adverse
Event (ADAE) dataset to the driving assessment data in the ADFT dataset may not be feasible,
given that multiple AEs can be present for a single driving assessment timepoint. Furthermore,
the same AE can be ongoing and span multiple driving assessment timepoints. Thus, when
applicable, submission of a standalone additional ADaM dataset that joins AE and driving
assessment data and contains corresponding AE(s) for each driving assessment timepoint for a
given participant is encouraged. This may result in repeated AEs when an AE spans multiple
driving assessment timepoints, as well as repeated driving assessments when multiple AEs occur
at a single driving assessment timepoint. Note that including a row for each individual driving
assessment measurement at a given assessment timepoint is not critical within this dataset. This
dataset is used to create the AE summary described in section 4.0 Specifications for Tables and
Figures. It is recommended that the sponsor consider how to capture the AEs experienced at the
time of the driving assessment during study planning and before conducting the driving study.

           3.1.2.2.1    Linking Data in SDTM via RELREC

The RELREC dataset is an SDTM special-purpose dataset used to identify relationships between
records in disparate SDTM datasets. If the source data (e.g., data collected using a CRF) indicate
a relationship between the driving assessment data in the FT dataset and data in another SDTM
dataset at the time of data collection, this association is shown using the RELREC dataset. FDA
is interested in the completeness and clarity with which RELREC variables ─ including Related
Domain Abbreviation (RDOMAIN), Identifying Variable (IDVAR), Identifying Variable Value
(IDVARVAL), and Relationship Identifier (RELID) ─ are populated to illustrate these
relationships. An example of an annotated CRF (aCRF) is shown in Figure A1. Example
Annotated Case Report Form, and an example of a RELREC dataset that links driving
assessment data in the SDTM FT dataset to adverse events data in the SDTM AE dataset is
provided in Table A5 Subset of Sample RELREC Dataset. If the measurement tool used to
capture driving assessment data directly outputs an identifier to link measurement tool events,
properties, settings, or other findings to the driving assessment measurements (e.g., SDLP), the
RELREC dataset should be used to illustrate the linkage of the driving assessment records in the
FT dataset to the records submitted within other measurement tool dataset(s) as described in
section 3.2. Datasets for Measurement Tool-Level Review.

           3.1.2.2.2    Joining Data in ADaM

When the relationship between records across SDTM datasets is not captured in the source data
and thus not represented using the RELREC dataset, the algorithm used to link driving
assessment data within the ADFT dataset to the other data should be defined in the study
metadata such as the ADaM Define-XML file. For example, ADaM Analysis Visit (AVISIT) or
another analysis timing variable can be used to join records for a subject at each specified driving
assessment timepoint. In addition, the software programs (source code) used to join data to the
ADFT dataset should be provided, which can be included in the program used to create the
ADFT dataset.

           3.1.2.3     Handling of Missing and Partial Driving Assessment Data in ADaM

As discussed in SDTM section 3.1.1.2 Handling of Missing and Partial Driving Assessment Data
in SDTM, understanding the reasons for and prevalence of missing and partial driving
assessment data are critical to support FDA review and regulatory decision-making. Within the
ADFT dataset, it is ideal that a record be provided for every planned (per protocol) driving
assessment timepoint for each participant, regardless of whether the driving assessment is
complete, partial, or missing. Considerations for approaches to represent missing data within the
ADFT dataset are provided in section 3.1.2.3.1 Approaches to Represent Missing Data and
approaches to represent partial data within the ADFT dataset are provided in section 3.1.2.3.2
Approaches to Represent Partial Data.

           3.1.2.3.1   Approaches to Represent Missing Data

Approaches to represent rows for missing driving assessment data within the ADFT dataset
where AVAL is empty include the following:

•   Copying records from the SDTM FT dataset when collected, where FT.FTSTAT = ‘NOT
    DONE’ and FT.FTREASND is populated with the reason the driving assessment is missing

•   Deriving phantom records in the ADFT dataset, where DTYPE = ‘PHANTOM’

It is encouraged that rows for missing driving assessment data in the ADFT dataset be originally
represented in the SDTM FT dataset with FTSTAT and FTREASND populated to support
traceability, rather than derived as new phantom records in ADaM. When FT.FTREASND is
populated within the SDTM FT dataset for a record, the value should be copied into the
FTREASND variable within the ADFT dataset for each affected driving assessment
measurement. Alternatively, phantom records can be added to the ADFT dataset if rows for the
missing data do not exist in the SDTM FT dataset (e.g., records are missing due to participant
discontinuation). Adding rows for phantom records is one solution to represent missing data
when the missing data are not originally present in the SDTM FT dataset; however, based on
analysis needs specified in the SAP, values can be imputed within AVAL instead. The sponsor
should consult with the Agency to determine whether phantom records should be added within
the ADaM dataset for the clinical trial.

In addition, a sponsor-defined analysis variable for Reason Not Done (e.g., ADFT.AREASND)
may be useful to indicate the reason (if known and/or collected) that a driving assessment was
not done when FT.FTREASND is not populated and/or not informative (see AREASND in Table
4 Specifications for a Subset of ADFT Variables). For example, AREASND can be derived from
other sources when phantom records are used to represent missing driving assessment data.
When populated, values for AREASND should be distributed across all measurements for a
given driving assessment timepoint within the ADFT dataset.

           3.1.2.3.2   Approaches to Represent Partial Data

Partial data are represented in the SDTM FT dataset and the ADFT dataset when driving
assessment measurements such as SDLP are calculated, even though the driving assessment ends
prematurely. Within the ADFT dataset, the resulting values are provided in AVAL for each
driving assessment measurement, and supporting variables are recommended within the ADFT
dataset as defined within Table 4 Specifications for a Subset of ADFT Variables. In this
scenario, the Premature End of Driving Assessment Flag (APREFL) is populated as ‘Y’, and the
duration is reported within the Analysis Duration (N) (ADURN) and Analysis Duration Units
(ADURU) variables. Note that if duration were originally collected in lieu of start and end
datetime variables as source data, either on the CRF or by the measurement tool, the duration
would be represented within the Duration of Functional Test (FTDUR) variable in International
Organization for Standardization 8601 (ISO 8601) duration format within the FT dataset.
Appendix 5.1.2 shows an example of these supporting variables within the ADFT dataset.

             3.1.2.4   Handling of ‘Out of Window’ Driving Assessment Data in ADaM

As presented in Table 4 Specifications for a Subset of ADFT Variables, variables to assess
whether a driving assessment was conducted outside of the planned window are useful for
analysis. The windows used for analysis should be prespecified in the SAP, and the target time
for a driving assessment to be conducted can be dependent on the indication for the drug under
review. For example, the driving assessment for an investigational insomnia drug can be planned
to occur 9 hours post-dose, whereas the driving assessment for an investigational migraine drug
can be planned to occur 90 minutes post-dose.

Consider the example for an insomnia drug where a driving assessment is planned to occur 9
hours post-dose (i.e., the following morning) and the window is defined as 8 to 10 hours such
that it is permissible for the participant to perform the driving assessment at any time within this
window. If the driving assessment is performed either before 8 hours or after 10 hours, then the
driving assessment is outside of the planned window. Note that empty values for variables such
as the primary analysis timing variable used in the study (e.g., AVISIT) will aid in the
determination of records that are outside of the planned window.

In addition to the variables defined in Table 4, the suite of Analysis Visit Windowing Variables
for BDS Datasets as well as Analysis Flag zz (ANLzzFL) prespecified in the ADaMIG can
further aid in the understanding and selection of records in the ADFT dataset that are used in
analysis. These variables can be used to select the appropriate record(s) when multiple driving
assessments are conducted within the same planned window. Further, they can be used to
populate the appropriate value for the primary analysis timing variable used in the study (e.g.,
AVISIT, ATPT) based on the protocol schedule compared to what is captured in the CRF or via
eDT for a given assessment timepoint.

       3.2      Datasets for Measurement Tool-Level Review

Within a driving study, the measurement tool used to capture driving assessment data and
generate measurement results can include a driving simulator or a specially instrumented on-road
vehicle. The submission of additional data for the measurement tool(s) used in a driving study is
recommended to enable measurement tool-level review and analysis. These data can include
measurement tool identifier information, properties, settings, software version(s), and other
participant interaction data (e.g., error codes generated by the measurement tool while it is in use
by the participant). When multiple measurement tools are in use for a single driving study, this
additional data can be useful in assessing which measurement tool was used by each participant
at each assessment timepoint and can enhance the overall traceability of the driving assessment
data. Note that the metadata reported within the additional datasets described in the subsections
below can be collected in the protocol, collected on the CRF, or output by the measurement tool.

       3.2.1   Measurement Tool Identifiers

A dataset containing the identifiers or characteristics used to uniquely identify each measurement
tool used in a driving study should be submitted. In the dataset, Sponsor Measurement Tool
Identifier (SPDEVID) denotes the single identifier for each measurement tool and is used to link
data. For example, the identifiers used to uniquely identify a driving simulator can include
manufacturer, model, and serial number. Similarly, identifiers for a specially instrumented on-
road vehicle can include the vehicle’s manufacturer, model name, and model year. If this is not
enough information to identify the vehicle uniquely, then additional identifiers such as Vehicle
Identification Number (e.g., DIPARMCD = ‘VIN’), can be included and represented by an
alphanumeric string (e.g., DIVAL = ‘ABC123…789XYZ’). Sponsors should supply the
minimum number of identifiers appropriate to uniquely identify each measurement tool within
the DI dataset. As shown in Table 1 Specifications for a Subset of FT Variables and Table 4.
Specifications for a Subset of ADFT Variables, FDA recommends that SPDEVID be submitted
within the SDTM FT dataset and the ADFT dataset.

       3.2.2   Measurement Tool -Subject Relationships

A measurement tool used in a study can be associated with multiple participants over the course
of the study; conversely, multiple measurement tools can be associated with an individual
participant. For example, a participant can use one driving simulator at one visit and a different
driving simulator at a later visit, depending on the number of driving simulators available at the
clinical trial site. To facilitate review, it is recommended that all distinct pairs of SPDEVID and
USUBJID values observed in the study data be presented in a dataset.

       3.2.3   Properties and Settings Pertaining to Measurement Tools

Driving assessment data depicting the properties and settings of a measurement tool such as
software name and/or version are recommended to be submitted in separate findings dataset.
Example properties captured for a driving simulator can include the frame rate or resolution of
the simulator display, the course difficulty level, or the name and/or version of the software used
to generate driving assessment measurements (e.g., SDLP). Note that representing properties and
settings of each measurement tool are useful within a dataset when there are differences observed
across measurement tools, participants, and/or driving assessment timepoints. Alternatively,
when properties and settings are identical throughout the study, these details can instead be
presented directly in the study protocol.

       3.2.4   Events Pertaining to Measurement Tools

Driving assessment data depicting events that occur to a measurement tool during a driving study
are recommended to be submitted, especially when the events can affect the results and
interpretation of a driving assessment. Relevant events can include application program issues,
electrical issues, software upgrades or downgrades, sensor issues when using a specially
instrumented on-road vehicle, calibration issues, or other events that result in loss of data. When
a relationship between the driving assessment and a corresponding measurement tool event is

of this is provided in Figure A1. Example Annotated Case Report Form.

       3.3     Comments Dataset

The Comments (CO) domain is a special-purpose dataset prespecified within the SDTM that
provides a solution for submitting free-text comments (e.g., voluntary free text or unsolicited
observations). Within a driving study, an observer or evaluator can collect observations while the
participant is performing the driving assessment at a distinct assessment timepoint, either while
using a driving simulator or a specially instrumented on-road vehicle. Observations can be
related to behavior (e.g., participant seemed upset or withdrawn), performance (e.g., participant
drove erratically), the measurement tool (e.g., driving simulator had to be restarted before drive
due to a technical issue), compliance (e.g., participant was not following clear directions), or the
protocol (e.g., participant started the assessment after the target postdose time specified in the
protocol), but they can also be more general in nature. FDA is interested in the consistency and
accuracy with which variables ─ including Related Domain Abbreviation (RDOMAIN),
Identifying Variable (IDVAR), Identifying Variable Value (IDVARVAL), and Comment Text
(COVAL) ─ are populated within the CO dataset to effectively join comments data with other
driving assessment data.

It is recommended that the comments data in the CO dataset related to the driving assessment are
joined to the driving assessment data within the ADFT dataset as shown in Table 4
Specifications for a Subset of ADFT Variables. Incorporating related comments directly within
the ADFT dataset can aid in an overall understanding and interpretation of the data, including for
driving assessment measurement results where outliers are present. Providing the comments data
directly in the ADFT dataset also allows for the comment(s) related to each participant and
driving assessment timepoint to be easily viewed. Within the ADFT dataset, the sponsor can
consider creating classification flags to categorize the comments for analysis if applicable for the
driving study, given the CO dataset can contain multiple categories of comments related to the
driving assessment.

To improve the submission of standardized data within a driving study, the sponsor should
consider how data will be collected (e.g., values captured as free text, in a structured field, or
from a pre-defined list of values) during initial study planning and CRF design. For example, if
environmental factors such as room temperature are to be collected for all participants before
conducting the driving assessment adding a field on the CRF to capture this value in specified
units helps ensure that room temperature will be collected consistently across participants and
driving assessment timepoints. With the incorporation of these structured fields on the CRF, the
resulting data can be represented within the SUPPFT dataset rather than as free text in the
Comments dataset (see section 3.1.1.1 General Considerations for the FT dataset).

        3.5      Questionnaires Datasets

This section provides dataset specifications for submitting questionnaires data (i.e., other COA
data separate from the driving assessment) in CDISC format and includes other considerations
for questionnaires data, such as the handling of missing data. Like driving assessments,
questionnaires have documented methods for administering and scoring a test using a valid
instrument and can have continuous or categorical outcomes. Within a driving study,
questionnaires data can be collected to support study objectives as separate study endpoint(s),
i.e., to supplement the objective driving assessment endpoint(s) (e.g., SDLP), and to measure
related constructs. Questionnaires data can also be used to aid in the clinical interpretation of the
objective driving assessment results and the participant’s performance. The sponsor should
consider whether capturing questionnaire data before and/or immediately after a driving
assessment is relevant to its clinical interpretation and to evaluate a participant’s pre-driving and
post-driving outcomes. For example, before performing a driving assessment, a PRO can be used
to ask questions related to readiness to drive and participant safety (e.g., ‘Do you feel safe to
drive?’). Similarly, immediately after a driving assessment, a PRO can be used to ask questions
related to the participant’s awareness of their recent performance (e.g., ‘How well did you drive
during the driving assessment?’, ‘How motivated were you to drive to the best of your ability
during the driving assessment?’).

Consider a participant with a poor SDLP result who reported in a questionnaire that they felt safe
to drive immediately before performing the driving assessment and rated both their driving
performance and their motivation to drive well as high immediately after the driving assessment.
In this scenario, impairment without awareness can be more concerning than a participant with
an extremely poor SDLP result who documented in a questionnaire that they did not feel safe to
drive and/or gave low ratings to their driving performance and their motivation to drive well. To
better compare the questionnaires data to the driving assessment data for a given participant and
assessment timepoint, the sponsor can consider joining the questionnaires data to the driving
assessment data within the ADFT dataset or creating a separate summary table to compare the
data. The sponsor can consult with the Agency to discuss whether this analysis is recommended
for the driving study.

        3.5.2    ADaM Specifications for Questionnaires Data

the SDTM QS dataset in conjunction with other SDTM and ADaM data. The ADQS dataset

originally submitted within the SDTM QS dataset if they are considered ‘source data summary

rules defined within the SAP. Within the ADQS dataset, it is recommended that sponsors include
the SDTM variables critical to provide traceability to the source SDTM QS dataset, including the
SDTM variables that support the understanding of missing questionnaires data (i.e., QSSTAT
