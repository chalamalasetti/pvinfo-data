14   I.       INTRODUCTION
15
16   The purpose of this guidance is to help sponsors in the clinical development of drugs 2 for the
17   treatment of erosive esophagitis (EE) in adults.
18
19   Specifically, this guidance addresses the Food and Drug Administration’s (FDA’s) current
20   recommendations on clinical trials for drugs intended for the healing of EE and maintenance of
21   healed EE in adults, including considerations for eligibility criteria, trial design features, efficacy
22   evaluations, and safety assessments. 3
23
24   This guidance does not address the development of drugs for the treatment of symptomatic
25   nonerosive gastroesophageal reflux disease, 4 Barrett’s esophagus, pathological hypersecretory
26   conditions (e.g., Zollinger-Ellison syndrome), peptic ulcer disease, or the development of drugs
27   for healing of EE and maintenance of healed EE in pediatric patients.

36   II.        BACKGROUND
37
38   EE is caused by reflux of acidic stomach contents into the esophagus and is included in the
39   spectrum of acid-related disorders known as gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD). EE is
40   defined as the presence of superficial esophageal erosions on endoscopic evaluation in patients
41   with or without the typical symptoms of GERD (e.g., heartburn, regurgitation). GERD affects
42   males and females in nearly equal proportions; however, males develop EE more often than
43   females. 5,6 Complications of untreated EE include the development of esophageal strictures,
44   perforation, and progression to Barrett’s esophagus.
45
46   In patients with EE, the goals of therapy include healing of erosions and maintenance of healed
47   erosions. Accordingly, pharmacologic therapy for EE typically has two phases: an initial
48   treatment period to heal existing erosions, followed by continued treatment to ensure healing is
49   maintained (i.e., erosions do not recur).

52   III.       DEVELOPMENT PROGRAM
53
54              A.     Trial Population
55
56   Sponsors should enroll subjects who are representative of the population that will use the drug if
57   approved and should consider clinical trial sites that facilitate this goal. Sponsors developing
58   drugs for the healing of EE and maintenance of healed EE should also consider the following:
59
60              1.     Inclusion Criteria
61
62          •   Trials evaluating drugs for the healing of EE and maintenance of healed EE should enroll
63              subjects with EE, as defined by the Los Angeles (LA) classification system (see
64              Appendix) 7 at the baseline endoscopy, to ensure subjects meet endoscopic eligibility
65              criteria and establish disease severity before administration of the investigational product.
66
67          •   Trials should enroll subjects across the whole spectrum of disease severity (i.e., LA
68              Grade A-D).

 74          2.     Exclusion Criteria
 75
 76      •   Subjects who test positive for Helicobacter pylori during screening should be excluded.
 77          However, subjects with a history of H. pylori who have received treatment and who have
 78          negative confirmatory testing may be included if they continue to meet the inclusion
 79          criteria after H. pylori eradication.
 80
 81      •   Subjects with the following should also be excluded:
 82
 83          – Evidence of Barrett’s esophagus and/or definite dysplastic changes on endoscopic
 84            evaluation of the esophagus
 85
 86          – History of dilation of esophageal strictures, other than a Schatzki’s ring (i.e., a ring of
 87            mucosal tissue near the lower esophageal sphincter)
 88
 89          – Presence of gastric or duodenal ulcers
 90
 91          – Coexisting diseases affecting the esophagus (e.g., eosinophilic esophagitis,
 92            esophageal varices, scleroderma, viral or fungal infection, esophageal stricture)
 93
 94          – History of radiation therapy, cryotherapy, sclerotherapy, or other caustic, thermal, or
 95            physiochemical trauma to the esophagus
 96
 97          3.     Concomitant Medications
 98
 99      •   With the exception of protocol-specified rescue medications, concomitant use of acid-
100          reducing medications (e.g., proton pump inhibitors, histamine H2-receptor antagonists) or
101          other drugs found to be effective for the treatment of GERD or other acid-related
102          conditions (e.g., sucralfate, prokinetics, misoprostol) should not be permitted.
103
104      •   As drugs with significant anticholinergic effects (e.g., tricyclic antidepressants,
105          antispasmodics) may impact the occurrence of reflux of stomach contents through their
106          action on the lower esophageal sphincter, subjects who require treatment with these drugs
107          should maintain stable doses for at least 4 weeks before screening and throughout the
108          duration of the trial.

110          B.     Trial Design
111
112   Sponsors developing drugs for the healing of EE and maintenance of healed EE should consider
113   the following:
114

115       •    Sponsors should conduct a randomized, double-blind, active comparator trial design for
116            trials of drugs for the healing of EE and maintenance of healed EE to demonstrate
117            noninferiority or superiority to an approved therapy. 8
118
119       •    Sponsors should discuss their proposed noninferiority margin(s) with the Division. The
120            characteristics of the trial population (e.g., disease severity by LA grade) and choice of
121            active comparator may affect the suitability of a noninferiority margin.
122
123       •    Sponsors seeking to conduct a placebo-controlled trial of their drug should discuss the
124            specifics of the proposal (e.g., population, eligibility criteria, trial design, clinical and
125            laboratory monitoring, escape criteria) with the Division.
126
127       •    The trial duration and timing of efficacy assessments for healing of EE should be guided
128            by the mechanism of action of the drug, its expected onset of action, and the time frame
129            in which a clinical benefit is expected to be observed.
130
131            – If healing is anticipated to occur before the time point identified for primary efficacy
132              assessment (i.e., completion of the healing phase of the trial), trials may include
133              prespecification of an additional endoscopic assessment at an earlier time point.
134
135                    If a subject’s EE is observed to have healed before the prespecified primary
136                     efficacy assessment, they may be considered to have completed the healing phase
137                     and be eligible for direct rerandomization into the maintenance phase. 9
138
139                    If the subject’s EE has not healed at an earlier assessment, they should continue in
140                     the healing phase, and eligibility for the maintenance phase should be determined
141                     by the results of endoscopic evaluation at the completion of the healing phase.
142
143       •    Subjects who have achieved complete healing of erosions during the healing phase
144            should undergo rerandomization into the maintenance phase to either study drug or the
145            comparator for a treatment duration of at least 6 months to assess maintenance of healed
146            EE.
147
148       •    Subjects should be instructed that diet, lifestyle, or behavioral modifications designed to
149            mitigate symptoms of EE (e.g., avoiding caffeine, eating smaller portions, elevating the
150            head of the bed) should not be altered (i.e., initiated, discontinued, or modified from
151            those utilized at baseline) throughout the trial.

156          C.      Efficacy Considerations
157
158   Sponsors developing drugs for the healing of EE and maintenance of healed EE should consider
159   the following:
160
161          1.      Efficacy Assessments
162
163   Sponsors should consider the following to establish efficacy for the healing of EE:
164
165      •   A primary efficacy endpoint of complete healing (i.e., no erosions present) on endoscopic
166          evaluation
167
168      •   Secondary efficacy endpoints of the following:
169
170          – Complete healing of EE in subjects with LA Grade A or B at baseline
171
172          – Complete healing of EE in subjects with LA Grade C or D at baseline
173
174          – Relief of heartburn associated with EE, as assessed by the proportion of heartburn-
175            free days during the prespecified assessment period, where a heartburn-free day is
176            defined as a 24-hour period with no heartburn
177
178   Sponsors should consider the following to establish efficacy for the maintenance of healed EE:
179
180      •   A primary efficacy endpoint of the maintenance of complete healing of all erosions (i.e.,
181          no erosions present) on endoscopic evaluation
182
183      •   Secondary efficacy endpoints of the following:
184
185          – Maintenance of complete healing of EE in subjects with LA Grade A or B at baseline
186
187          – Maintenance of complete healing of EE in subjects with LA Grade C or D at baseline
188
189          – Relief of heartburn associated with EE, as assessed by the proportion of heartburn-
190            free days during the prespecified assessment period, where a heartburn-free day is
191            defined as a 24-hour period with no heartburn
192
193   Sponsors should also consider the following:
194
195      •   Sponsors can explore the effects of a drug on additional symptoms identified by patients
196          as important (e.g., regurgitation), when present, using a fit-for-purpose patient-reported
197          outcome measure.
198

199        •   Sponsors should use patient-reported outcome instruments with a maximum recall period
200            of the past 24 hours for all symptomatic assessments (e.g., heartburn, regurgitation).
201            Respondents should complete the instruments at the same time each day (e.g., evening
202            before bedtime).
203
204        •   Additional information and recommendations regarding the assessment of clinical
205            outcomes in drug development through patient-reported outcome assessments are
206            included in FDA’s patient-focused drug development guidance series. 10
207
208            2.      Statistical Considerations
209
210        •   Sponsors should analyze the primary endpoint for the healing of EE phase (i.e., complete
211            healing of EE) and the primary endpoint for the maintenance of healed EE phase (i.e.,
212            maintenance of complete healing of EE) by evaluating the difference in the proportions
213            across treatment arms.
214
215        •   Sponsors should analyze the secondary endpoint of the relief of heartburn associated with
216            EE by evaluating the difference in the average proportions of heartburn-free days across
217            treatment arms.
218
219        •   Statistical analyses should adjust for patient characteristics at baseline that may impact
220            efficacy outcomes (e.g., advancing age, obesity, smoking, alcohol and caffeine
221            consumption) to gain precision in evaluating overall treatment effects. Sponsors should
222            also consider exploring subgroup analyses and potential treatment interactions based on
223            these factors.
224
225        •   Sponsors should prespecify the approach to ensure strong control of the type I error rate
226            when testing multiple endpoints (i.e., primary and secondary endpoints) that are clinically
227            meaningful and for which labeling claims may be of interest. If an endpoint will be tested
228            for both noninferiority and superiority, each test should be prespecified in the multiple
229            testing procedure and appropriate methods should be used to control the type I error rate
230            across both tests.
231
232        •   Sponsors should prespecify a primary estimand of interest for each endpoint and justify
233            that it is meaningful and that it can be estimated with minimal and plausible assumptions
234            with the proposed analysis. The estimand is a precise description of the treatment effect,
235            reflecting the clinical question posed by a given clinical trial objective. 11

261          D.       Safety Considerations
262
263   Sponsors developing drugs for the healing of EE and maintenance of healed EE should consider
264   the following:
265
266      •   Multiple potential risks have been identified with long-term acid suppression (e.g.,
267          Clostridioides difficile enteric infections, osteoporosis-related bone fractures, vitamin
268          deficiencies). Sponsors should consider these potential risks, as well as known adverse
269          events associated with the therapeutic class of the drug, to inform the overall extent and
270          duration of treatment provided in the program’s overall safety database.
271
272      •   A sufficient number of subjects should be exposed to the to-be-marketed dosing
273          regimen(s) for healing of EE, as well as for maintenance of healed EE, during controlled
274          treatment periods of sufficient duration (e.g., at least 8 weeks for healing of EE, at least
275          24 weeks for maintenance of healed EE) to characterize the safety of the drug for healing
276          of EE and for maintenance of healed EE.
277
278      •   Drug-specific considerations may alter the minimum acceptable size of the safety
279          database and duration of exposure, including whether the drug in question is a new
280          molecular entity or has relevant supportive safety data from other populations, the known
281          and anticipated adverse events of the drug and drug class, and nonclinical findings.

       A                   One (or more) mucosal break no longer than 5 mm that does not extend
