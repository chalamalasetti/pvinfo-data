15   I.      INTRODUCTION
16
17   The purpose of this guidance is to help sponsors in the clinical development of drugs to treat
18   adults with ulcerative colitis (UC). 2 This guidance addresses the Food and Drug
19   Administration’s (FDA’s) current recommendations on clinical trials for drugs being developed
20   under section 505 of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (21 U.S.C. 355), section 351 of
21   the Public Health Service Act (42 U.S.C. 262) and 21 CFR parts 312, 314, and 601 for treating
22   UC. Specifically, this guidance addresses FDA’s current thinking about the necessary attributes
23   of clinical trials for drugs being developed for treating UC, including trial population, trial
24   design, efficacy considerations, and safety assessments. 3,4
25
26   This guidance does not address extraintestinal manifestations of UC, pediatric drug development,
27   or the treatment or prevention of long-term complications of UC (e.g., this guidance is not
28   intended to discuss endpoints for prevention or reduction in risk of colorectal cancer).
29
30   This guidance replaces the withdrawn draft guidance for industry Ulcerative Colitis: Clinical
31   Trial Endpoints (August 2016).

41   II.        BACKGROUND
42
43   UC is a chronic, relapsing, and remitting inflammatory bowel disease characterized by diffuse
44   mucosal inflammation of the colon.
45
46   UC involves the rectum, and it may extend proximally in a contiguous pattern to affect part of
47   the colon or the entire colon. Clinical manifestations of active UC include bloody diarrhea (with
48   or without mucus), urgency, tenesmus, abdominal pain, weight loss, fever, and malaise. In
49   patients with extensive or severe inflammation, acute complications such as severe bleeding and
50   toxic megacolon, which can lead to perforation, may occur (Danese and Fiocchi 2011).
51
52   The treatment goals of UC include resolution or reduction of the signs and symptoms of active
53   disease to provide relief to the patient and healing or control of the underlying mucosal
54   inflammation and its complications.

58
59              A.      Trial Population
60
61   Sponsors developing drugs to treat UC should consider the following:
62
63          •   Subjects should have a confirmed diagnosis of UC based on documented findings on
64              endoscopy and histopathology.
65
66          •   For clinical trials for drugs intended to treat moderately to severely active UC:
67
68              ‒    Subjects should have a score of 5 to 9 on the modified Mayo Score (mMS), 5
69                   including an endoscopy subscore of at least 2.
70
71              — Sponsors should enroll subjects across the whole range of both moderately and
72                severely active disease categories.

 87              B.       Trial Design
 88
 89   Sponsors developing drugs to treat UC should consider the following:
 90
 91          •   We recommend a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial design that would
 92              be able to demonstrate that beneficial effects observed initially with treatment are
 93              continued long term to support chronic administration. This goal may be achieved
 94              through various study designs, and the overall design of a program should be agreed upon
 95              with the appropriate review division before trial initiation.
 96
 97              — One approach (induction followed by randomized withdrawal maintenance) is to
 98                conduct a randomized, placebo-controlled induction trial to assess clinical benefit in
 99                the short term, followed by a maintenance trial in which all subjects who achieve
100                initial clinical response 7 to active drug at the end of induction are re-randomized to
101                receive either active treatment or placebo, and efficacy is evaluated again at the end
102                of the maintenance phase (e.g., 52 weeks). 8
103
104              — Another approach (treat-through design) is to randomize subjects once at the start of
105                the trial to one of the treatment arms (i.e., a dosing regimen or placebo), and subjects
106                are then treated continuously without rerandomization through 52 weeks. Sponsor
107                should assess the primary endpoint at the end of treatment (e.g., 52 weeks). Earlier
108                periodic assessments throughout the trial are useful to characterize the time to onset
109                of initial clinical improvement. Early escape criteria should be incorporated to ensure
110                that subjects who are worsening or not improving after a reasonable time frame have
111                the opportunity to receive active treatment.

127            C.       Efficacy Considerations
128
129            1.       Efficacy Assessments
130
131   Sponsors developing drugs to treat UC should consider the following:
132
133        •   We recommend evaluating the proportion of subjects achieving clinical remission as the
134            primary endpoint.
135
136            — Clinical remission: Defined as an mMS score of 0 to 2, including the following three
137              components: 10
138
139                         1) Stool frequency subscore = 0 or 1 11
140
141                         2) Rectal bleeding subscore = 0
142
143                         3) Centrally read endoscopy subscore = 0 or 1 (score of 1 modified to
144                            exclude friability)
145
146                    Sponsors should explore the proportion of subjects in clinical remission who had
147                     a stool frequency subscore of 0 versus 1. Although a stool frequency subscore of
148                     0 or 1 is allowable for individual subjects, a subscore of 1 in a significant number
149                     of subjects may not be considered adequate evidence of stool normalization, and
150                     this limitation may be noted in the label.

152                    Although historically sponsors have used sigmoidoscopy for the endoscopic
153                     assessment, we recommend that colonoscopy be used to document disease
154                     activity in all involved segments of the colon.
155
156                    We recommend using centralized reading of endoscopies as the primary approach
157                     to scoring the endoscopic component of the primary and secondary endpoint
158                     assessments. Both the endoscopist performing the procedure and the central
159                     readers reviewing high-definition video recordings of the procedure should be
160                     blinded to treatment assignment and should document the endoscopic findings.
161                     The protocol should specify clearly how discrepancies between the findings by
162                     the endoscopist and the central reader will be handled in the efficacy analyses
163                     (e.g., adjudication by a third reader). Efforts should be made to minimize bias and
164                     standardize reading of endoscopy across trial sites and among investigators
165                     through training and education on the definition of each item described in the
166                     scale. Sponsors should draft charters that standardize procedures, video
167                     recordings/equipment, and endoscopy assessment early in drug development and
168                     share them with FDA for comment.
169
170                    For calculation of the mMS stool frequency and rectal bleeding subscores (at
171                     baseline and prespecified timepoints for efficacy assessment), the following
172                     considerations apply:
173
174                     o To calculate the stool frequency subscore and the rectal bleeding subscore, we
175                       recommend defining a 7-day period during which daily subscores are
176                       collected before the specified study visit when the mMS (or partial MS) is
177                       calculated.
178
179                     o The subscores should be calculated by averaging the daily subscores from
180                       within this 7-day period, excluding the day of bowel preparation and day of
181                       endoscopy (for visits that include an endoscopy).
182
183                     o A minimum of 3 consecutive days of completed diary entries or 4
184                       nonconsecutive days are necessary (otherwise the score should be considered
185                       missing and the subject’s result imputed as nonresponder).
186

188

191              in rectal bleeding subscore of greater than or equal to 1 or an absolute rectal bleeding
192              subscore of 0 or 1.

194       — Corticosteroid-free remission: Defined as subjects who are in clinical remission at
195         the conclusion of the controlled trial (e.g., 52 weeks) and having no corticosteroid
196         exposure during a prespecified period (e.g., at least 8 to 12 weeks) before that
197         assessment.
198
199          The proportion of subjects achieving corticosteroid-free remission, of those who were
200          using corticosteroids at enrollment, is of interest and should be reported.
201
202       — Endoscopic improvement: Defined as a centrally read endoscopy subscore of 0 or 1
203         (score of 1 modified to exclude friability).
204
205       — Endoscopic remission: Defined as a centrally read endoscopy subscore of 0.
206
207          We do not recommend the use of the term mucosal healing at this time, as there is no
208          consensus as to how best to define this concept.
209
210       — Maintenance of remission: We recommend the following to demonstrate the
211         durability of benefit:
212
213             For trial designs in which subjects who achieve clinical response at the end of the
214              induction phase are rerandomized in the maintenance phase, we recommend that
215              sponsors assess remission within the subset of subjects who enter the maintenance
216              phase in remission to support the ability of the therapy to maintain a durable state
217              of remission.
218
219             For trial designs in which subjects are treated continuously without
220              rerandomization (treat-through design), sponsors should assess the proportion of
221              subjects who individually achieve clinical remission at both early (e.g., 8 weeks)
222              and late (e.g., 52 week) time points to demonstrate that a clinical benefit was
223              attained and was durable.
224
225   •   We recommend the following exploratory endpoints, each of which should be discussed
226       with FDA before trial initiation:
227
228       — Histologic response/remission: At this time, there is no scientific consensus on a
229         definition of, or scoring system for, histologic resolution of mucosal inflammation in
230         subjects who achieved endoscopic remission in UC. Sponsors should provide
231         adequate justification for the proposed endpoint definitions, grading scales, and
232         scoring techniques.
233
234       — Interim clinical assessments based on noninvasive measures: Sponsors should
235         incorporate interim clinical assessments based on the noninvasive components of the
236         mMS (such as stool frequency and rectal bleeding subscores) at prespecified time
237         points during the trial, up until and including the last visit (e.g., 52 weeks), to support
238         maintenance of remission.

246              2.       Statistical Considerations
247
248   Sponsors developing drugs to treat UC should consider the following:
249
250          •   To gain precision in evaluating overall treatment effects (e.g., the overall difference in
251              remission rates), we recommend statistical analyses adjust for subject characteristics at
252              baseline that may affect efficacy outcomes (e.g., duration of disease, disease severity,
253              concurrent use of corticosteroids, prior biologic use).
254
255          •   Sponsors should conduct efficacy analyses in all randomized subjects.
256
257          •   Sponsors should prespecify methods to handle intermittent missing data (e.g., lack of at
258              least 3 consecutive diary days, or 4 nonconsecutive diary days, during the 7 days before a
259              visit).
260
261          •   Subjects who drop out before the end of treatment should be considered treatment
262              failures.
263
264          •   Sponsors should prespecify a primary estimand of interest for each endpoint and justify
265              that it is meaningful and that it can be estimated with minimal and plausible assumptions
266              with the proposed analysis. The estimand is a precise description of the treatment effect,
267              reflecting the clinical question posed by a given clinical trial objective. See the
268              International Council for Harmonisation harmonized guideline E9 R1 Addendum on
269              Estimands and Sensitivity Analysis in Clinical Trials to the guideline on Statistical
270              Principles for Clinical Trials. 13 The following considerations apply:
271
272              — The important intercurrent events that should be considered when defining the
273                estimand include treatment discontinuation attributable to lack of efficacy or adverse
274                events, use of rescue medication, and UC-related surgery.
275
276              — Potential strategies for defining and handling intercurrent events include the
277                following:
278
279                      A treatment policy strategy in which outcomes are collected after the intercurrent
280                       event and used in analyses.
281
282                      A composite strategy in which subjects who experience the intercurrent event are
283                       considered to have an unfavorable outcome (e.g., to have not achieved remission).

296            3.      Future Patient-Reported Outcome Instrument Development 14,15
297
298        •   Sponsors wishing to develop additional novel PRO instruments (or adapt existing
299            instruments for use in UC patients) to assess concepts that are relevant to UC patients but
300            not captured within the mMS can submit a PRO instrument development proposal for
301            FDA review.
302
303            — Sponsors pursuing PRO instrument development may need to collect additional
304              qualitative information from patients to support the relevance of the selected
305              symptom(s), and document that patients understand and can use the instrument’s
306              proposed items.
307
308            — To support potential labeling claims, an adequate number of patients should
309              demonstrate the presence of the additional symptom(s) at baseline, with sufficient
310              degree of severity in order to be able to measure a clinically meaningful improvement
311              over the course of treatment.
312
313            — Additionally, sponsors may need to collect evidence that captures clinically important
314              improvement at the individual patient level to inform the definition of response using
315              the PRO instrument, preferably by including anchor-based analyses but also by other
316              methods.
