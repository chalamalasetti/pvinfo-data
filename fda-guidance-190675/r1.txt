14   I.     Introduction
15   This draft guidance provides recommendations for clinical performance testing and evaluation to
16   support premarket submissions for cuffless non-invasive blood pressure measuring devices. A
17   cuffless blood pressure measuring device generally is a non-invasive blood pressure
18   measurement system that provides a signal from which systolic, diastolic, mean, or any
19   combination of the three pressures can be derived through sensors. This guidance does not
20   include the totality of information that should be included in a premarket submission.
21
22   For the current edition of the FDA-recognized consensus standard(s) referenced in this
23   document, see the FDA Recognized Consensus Standards Database. If submitting a Declaration
24   of Conformity to a recognized standard, we recommend you include the appropriate supporting
25   documentation. For more information regarding use of consensus standards in regulatory
26   submissions, refer to the FDA guidance titled “Appropriate Use of Voluntary Consensus
27   Standards in Premarket Submissions for Medical Devices.”
28
29   In general, FDA’s guidance documents do not establish legally enforceable responsibilities.
30   Instead, guidances describe the Agency’s current thinking on a topic and should be viewed only
31   as recommendations, unless specific regulatory or statutory requirements are cited. The use of
32   the word should in Agency guidances means that something is suggested or recommended, but
33   not required.

37   The scope of this document includes non-invasive blood pressure measuring systems that are
38   cuffless. Generally, these devices have been classified as class II devices and regulated under 21
39   CFR 870.1130 with the product code listed in Table 1:

45
46   Although the product code listed above is current as of the date of issuance of this guidance, new
47   product codes or classification regulations may be created and could fall within the scope of this
48   guidance. We recommend that you reference the product code database or contact OHT2: Office
49   of Cardiovascular Devices if you are uncertain whether this guidance applies to your device and
50   the product code for your device is not already identified in this guidance.

52   As part of a premarket submission, you should clearly describe the device’s intended use, device
53   technology, and principles of operation. For cuffless devices with a new intended use or new
54   technological characteristics that introduce different questions of safety and effectiveness, as
55   compared to previously cleared cuffless non-invasive blood pressure measuring devices, a De
56   Novo request or premarket approval application (PMA) may be required.
57
58   Indications for Use/Intended Use:
59   Cuffless non-invasive blood pressure measuring devices are intended to measure an individual’s
60   blood pressure. For more specific cleared indications for use for currently marketed cuffless non-
61   invasive blood pressure measuring devices, see FDA’s 510(k) Premarket Notification Database.
62   To ensure a clear understanding of your device and the appropriate regulatory pathway, the
63   premarket submission should clearly describe, for example, if the device is intended to provide:
64      • intermittent/spot-checking measurements or provides continuous measurements;
65      • autonomous initiation of measurement acquisition or only on-demand initiation of
66          measurements;
67      • measurements only under resting conditions or other conditions (e.g., ambulation,
68          exercise);
69      • measurements when the user is at one or more specific body positions and what positions
70          those are or provides measurements regardless of body position; and/or
71      • measurements during sleep.

 84   In the device description of a premarket submission, we recommend that you identify and
 85   describe:
 86       • location/types of the sensor(s) (e.g., photoplethysmogram, electrocardiogram) and how

 88       • any parameters derived from the sensor(s) (e.g., pulse arrival time (“PAT”), pulse wave
 89           transit time (“PWTT”));
 90       • how the signal(s) or parameter(s) are used to compute the device measurement(s);
 91       • if the device requires initialization or otherwise requires memory of a previous state in
 92           order to calculate its output(s) (e.g., a device which uses a cuff-based
 93           sphygmomanometer to provide a known blood pressure measurement at initialization
 94           and, if so, how often re-initialization should be performed);
 95       • if the device requires input of personal information (e.g., age, weight, height) to calibrate
 96           its output(s);
 97       • how the device is able to identify conditions (e.g., body position, motion) in order to
 98           determine when measurement(s) are acquired, if applicable;
 99       • the overall algorithm including:
100                • the scientific principles underlying how the device achieves its intended use;
101                • a description of how the input(s) are used to calculate the output(s);
102                • if the device incorporates signal quality checks to remove or correct poor quality
103                    signal, an explanation of how this is achieved (e.g., identify filtering thresholds,

114   III. Clinical Performance Testing
115   The recommendations for clinical performance testing to support a premarket submission depend
116   in part on the technological characteristics of the cuffless blood pressure measuring device. FDA
117   recommends that the clinical performance testing should represent the intended use of the device
118   including, for example, device calibration.
119
120          A.        Clinical Validation Study Protocol
121   We recommend that you conduct clinical study(ies) to evaluate the performance of the cuffless
122   non-invasive blood pressure measuring device. Clinical validation study recommendations are
123   based on current FDA-recognized consensus standards and the specific intended use and
124   technological characteristics of the device. This section contains examples of device attributes
125   that may affect these considerations, which should be addressed in the protocol and/or report that
126   is submitted in a premarket submission. Note FDA generally considers these studies to be
127   considered Nonsignificant Risk Device Studies. 3
128
129   Due to the breadth of devices covered by this guidance, the considerations described herein
130   should not be considered exhaustive. To assist in determining the appropriate performance
131   evaluation for your device, we strongly recommend early engagement with the Agency through
132   the Q-Submission Program 4 where detailed feedback specific to your device can be obtained
133   prior to initiating studies. For example, there may be additional clinical performance evaluation
134   considerations for devices incorporating artificial intelligence/machine learning techniques, or if
135   the intended use of the device includes measurement(s) in the presence of multiple or non-
136   standard body positions (e.g., ambulation, sleep, vehicular transport, etc.), or if the device is
137   capable of autonomously initiating measurement(s). In general, given the novelty and variety of
138   this device technology, FDA recommends utilizing the Q-Submission Program to obtain
139   feedback for all new devices of this type.
140
141   We recommend that you include adequate description of the design characteristics and intended
142   use in these early engagements (i.e., Q-Submissions) since FDA feedback represents our best

146   Sample size considerations
147      • We recommend that at least 85 participants between the ages 21 years and 50 years old
148         be tested if the device is intended for use in adults between the ages 21 years and 50 years
149         old. 5 This should include at least 30% females and 30% males. 6
150      • There may be additional sample size considerations for devices incorporating Artificial
151         Intelligence/Machine Learning techniques.
152
153   Special patient populations and/or Confounding factors
154   Special patient populations and/or confounding factors should be identified based on the device’s
155   technological characteristics and intended use. We recommend these be evaluated to understand
156   their impact on the accuracy of the device. We typically recommend that performance evaluation
157   should be conducted using at least 85 additional participants of each special population based on
158   the intended use population. 7 An increased sample size may be needed to determine confounding
159   factors do not affect the performance. We recommend adequate sampling across the applicable
160   populations. For example, for intended populations including adults over 50 years, we
161   recommend that you enroll at least 85 subjects over 50 years and at least 20% of this sub-
162   population in the upper most decade.

165              • > 50 years of age
166              • any pediatric sub-population
167              • individuals with arrhythmias
168              • individuals undergoing vasoactive drug therapy

171   Patient demographics
172   We recommend that you identify patient demographics consistent with the device’s
173   technological characteristics and intended use. Increased sample size may be needed to
174   demonstrate patient demographics and/or sub-populations do not affect the performance. We

199            B.       Performance Evaluation Considerations
200   FDA’s recommendations for clinical performance testing to be submitted in a premarket
201   submission depend on the technological characteristics and intended use of the cuffless non-
202   invasive blood pressure measuring device.
203
204   Typically, for cuffless non-invasive blood pressure measuring devices, FDA recommends that
205   you submit clinical performance data from Static, Stability, and Change tests, when applicable,
206   to support device accuracy consistent with the intended use of the device for users in a seated,
207   resting position, as described in ISO 81060-2, unless otherwise stated. We recommend that each
208   study population identified based on the device’s intended use remain consistent throughout all
209   the following applicable tests. Additionally, the Agency recommends that these tests be
210   evaluated independently (i.e., no pooling). We generally recommend that devices with
211   intermittent measurements be evaluated against manual auscultation through the method
212   described in ISO 81060-2. We recommend that devices with continuous measurements be
213   evaluated against arterial line measurements as described in ISO 81060-3. Table 2 provides
214   information on the importance of each performance test and testing considerations.

215                  Table 2. Clinical Performance Testing Description and Considerations
            Test                                    Description and Considerations

       Static Test        Intended to demonstrate that the device can provide accurate measurements for users
                          following device setup. We recommend that you conduct a static test for all cuffless
                          devices that measure one or more absolute blood pressure parameters. For devices
                          that require initialization or otherwise rely on memory of a previous state to operate,
                          this testing should be performed shortly after initialization of the device.
       Stability Test     Intended to demonstrate that the device can provide accurate measurements for users
                          throughout the duration of the labeled window of accuracy. We recommend that you
                          conduct a stability test for all devices that rely on initialization with a known blood
                          pressure measurement or otherwise use memory of a previous device state. We
                          recommend that a finite time window be included in the device labeling, and the
                          stability test should cover the duration of this window.

                          For example, if a device is initialized with a known cuff-based blood pressure
                          measurement and is labeled to be accurate for 28 days, we recommend that a static
                          test be performed on Day 1 and that a stability test be performed first around Day 14
                          (i.e., approximately midpoint) and again on Day 28. Where possible, we recommend
                          that the time of day that subjects are tested be varied so as to introduce circadian
                          variation.
       Change Test        Intended to demonstrate that the device can provide accurate measurements for users
                          following a change in blood pressure. We recommend that you conduct an induced
                          change test for all devices that rely on initialization with a known blood pressure
                          measurement, otherwise use memory of a previous device state, or rely on calibration
                          using personal input parameters.

                          We recommend that testing demonstrate maintained accuracy at blood pressure
                          values both above the point of initialization and below the point of initialization. We
                          recommend that large changes are defined for systolic blood pressure values at least
                          15 mmHg above or below the initialization point and for diastolic blood pressure
                          values at least 10 mmHg above or below the initialization point. To ensure a
                          sufficient number of changes is obtained, we recommend that at least 30% of all
                          measurements for systolic and diastolic blood pressure included in the induced
                          change test be large increases and that at least 30% of all measurements be large
                          decreases.

                      Natural changes can often be more representative of the intended use of the device
                      but may be less predictably obtained in clinical studies. We recommend that you
                      consider how you intend to evaluate natural changes and that you discuss planned
                      assessments with FDA. 10

       Considerations includes other body positions, autonomous measurements, ambulation, exercise,
                      sleep, transport, etc.

217            C.      Recommended Acceptance Criteria
218   FDA recommends the following acceptance criteria for each test – Static, Stability, and Change,
219   and each intended patient population. We recommend that the performance demonstrated by the
220   device be consistent with criteria below. The acceptance criteria for the Change test should be
221   evaluated only on the subset of subjects that achieve the necessary change in blood pressure.

222        •   Criterion #1: A mean error less than or equal ±5.0 mmHg and a standard deviation less
223            than 8.0 mmHg. 11
224        •   Criterion #2: Is determined by the interaction of mean error and standard deviation – i.e.,
225            the maximum allowable standard deviation is a function of the measured mean error. 12
