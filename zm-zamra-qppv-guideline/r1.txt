ii, Definitions
In these guidelines, unless the context otherwise states:

“Adverse Reaction Report” means a detailed record of all relevant data associated with the use of

a product ina patient.

“Adverse Drug Reaction / Adverse Reaction” means a response to a medicinal product which is noxious

and unintended including lack of efficacy and which occurs at any dosage and can arise from: ~
a)  Theuse of product within the terms of the marketing authorisation
b) The use of product outside the terms of the marketing authorisation, including

overdose, off-label use, misuse, abuse and medication errors;

c) Occupational exposure.

In this document, adverse drug reaction, adverse reaction and adverse event will be used

interchangeably.

“Adverse event/experience” means a reaction which is harmful and unintended, and which
occurs at doses normally used in humans for the prophylaxis, diagnosis or treatment of disease

or the modification of physiological function.
“Authority” means the Zambia Medicines Regulatory Authority.
“Local responsible person” means a person residing in Zambia and appointed by a foreign based

applicant to—

a) submit an application, document and sample of products to the Authority on behalf of the

applicant or holder of the marketing authorisation; and

1. Introduction

Pursuant to Section 68 of the Medicines and Allied Substances Act No. 3 of 2013, this guideline is
hereby developed by the Authority for information, guidance and compliance by the Marketing
Authorisation Holder (MAH) whose product has been granted Marketing Authorisation in Zambia on

the requirements and responsibilities of the local Qualified Person for Pharmacovigilance (QPPV).
2. Requirements _
2.1. General Requirements

The MAH shall continuously have at its disposal an appropriately qualified local person who will be
responsible for pharmacovigilance. The local QPPV shall be a natural person resident in Zambia and

meeting the qualifications as outlined in Section 2.4.1 of this guideline.

2.2. Responsibilities of the Marketing Authorisation Holder

The MAH shall:

2.2.1. Provide comprehensive training in Pharmacovigilance to the local QPPV.

2.2.2. Ensure that the local QPPV has sufficient authority to:
a) Implement pharmacovigilance activities on their behalf.
b) Access the Pharmacovigilance System Master File.
c) Provide inputs into Risk Management Plan, when necessary.
d) Provide inputs into the preparation of regulatory documents to emerging
safety concerns (e.g. variations, urgent safety restrictions, and, as

appropriate, communication to patients and healthcare professionals).

Qualifications and training of Local Qualified Person for Pharmacovigilance

a) The local QPPV shall:

i. Possess a degree in Pharmacy, Clinical Pharmacy, Pharmacology,
Clinical Pharmacology, Pharmaceutical Sciences, or other related health
sciences;

ii. Possess a postgraduate diploma/degree in Pharmacovigilance, as an
added advantage;

iii. Possess adequate theoretical knowledge and at least one year of practical

experience in safety monitoring;

iv. Demonstrate competence in the implementation and management of
safety monitoring systems; and

v. Be resident in Zambia and appropriately licensed to practice in Zambia.

b) The MAH shall provide the local QPPV with training in relation to its Safety
Monitoring System, appropriate for the role prior to the local QPPV taking up the
position. This training shall be appropriately documented and supplemented with

appropriate annual training.

c) In addition to the above, both the local QPPV and the Back-up QPPV shall have
knowledge on applicable Zambian safety monitoring legislation and guidelines;
international standards for Pharmacovigilance; and demonstrate (e.g. through
qualifications and training) that he/she has knowledge of the key safety
monitoring activities performed as part of the MAH’s safety monitoring system

and how to implement them.

2.4.2. Back-up Local Qualified Person for Pharmacovigilance

The Back-up local QPPV shall meet the minimum requirements of a local QPPV and have
expertise or access to expertise for the duration of the absence of the local QPPV to maintain a

functional pharmacovigilance system.

2.4.3. Responsibilities of local Qualified Person for Pharmacovigilance

The local QPPV shall not be a freelancer, but an employee of the MAH. The responsibilities
of the local QPPV shall include, but not be limited to, the following:

a) Act as a single point of contact for the Authority on all matters relating to

pharmacovigilance and safety of a product granted Marketing Authorisation, including

pharmacovigilance inspections.

Appendix I: Key Information to be included in agreement/contract between the MAH and the
local QPPV

1. The local QPPV shall act as a single point of contact for the Authority on all matters relating

to pharmacovigilance and safety of a product granted Marketing Authorisation.

2. The local QPPV shall establish and maintain a system which ensures that information about all
suspected adverse drug reactions/events which are reported to the personnel of the MAH,
including medical representatives, is collected, collated, processed and evaluated and submitted

to the Authority in line with the timelines stipulated in the Guidelines.

3. The local QPPV shall serve as a point of contact and be available during pharmacovigilance

inspections.

4. The local QPPV shall provide input on regulatory documents relating to the safety of a product

granted Marketing Authorisation.
