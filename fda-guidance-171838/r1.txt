15   I.      Introduction
16   FDA developed this document to provide guidance to industry and FDA staff about best
17   practices in selecting a predicate device for premarket notification [510(k)] submissions.
18   Specifically, this guidance recommends four (4) best practices to employ when selecting a
19   predicate device used to support a 510(k) submission. The recommendations provided in this
20   guidance are not intended to propose any changes to applicable statutory and regulatory
21   standards, such as how FDA evaluates substantial equivalence, or the applicable requirements,
22   including the requirement for valid scientific evidence. FDA developed this guidance to improve
23   the predictability, consistency, and transparency of the 510(k) premarket review process. This
24   guidance and associated recommendations are consistent with and are intended to be used in
25   conjunction with the FDA guidance “The 510(k) Program: Evaluating Substantial Equivalence in
26   Premarket Notifications [510(k)]”1 (hereinafter, 510(k) Program Guidance) and other relevant
27   FDA guidances on 510(k) submissions.
28
29   In general, FDA’s guidance documents do not establish legally enforceable responsibilities.
30   Instead, guidances describe the Agency’s current thinking on a topic and should be viewed only
31   as recommendations, unless specific regulatory or statutory requirements are cited. The use of
32   the word should in Agency guidances means that something is suggested or recommended, but
33   not required.

35   II. Background
36            A.       The 510(k) Process
37   The framework under which FDA regulates medical devices was put into place when Congress
38   enacted the Medical Device Amendments (Pub. L. 94-295) to the Federal Food, Drug, and
39   Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act) on May 28, 1976. Under section 510(k) of the FD&C Act, a
40   manufacturer must submit a premarket notification (often referred to as a 510(k)) to FDA at least
41   90 days before introducing, or delivering for introduction, a device into interstate commerce for
42   commercial distribution so the Agency can determine whether or not the device meets the criteria
43   for market clearance (sections 510(k), 510(n), and 513(i) of the FD&C Act). A 510(k) is required
44   for devices intended for human use, for which a premarket approval application (PMA) is not
45   required, unless the device is exempt from the 510(k) requirements of the FD&C Act and does
46   not exceed the relevant limitations of exemptions in the device classification regulations.
47
48   A 510(k) is a marketing submission made by a manufacturer to FDA to demonstrate that the
49   device to be marketed is substantially equivalent to a “predicate device” (section 513(i) of the
50   FD&C Act and 21 CFR 807.92(a)(3)-(6)). Substantial equivalence is rooted in a comparison
51   between the “new device”2 and predicate device(s).3
52
53   The Agency bases its decision on whether the device is substantially equivalent (SE) to a
54   predicate device using the statutory criteria in section 513(i) of the FD&C Act. For FDA to find a
55   new device SE to a predicate device, FDA must first find that the new device and predicate
56   device have the same intended use. FDA must then find that the new device and predicate device
57   have the same technological characteristics, or if they do not, that the different technological
58   characteristics4 of the new device do not raise different questions of safety and effectiveness and
59   that the new device is as safe and effective as a predicate device. FDA conducts this evaluation
60   by reviewing the proposed scientific methods for evaluating new/different technological
61   characteristics’ effects on safety and effectiveness and accompanying performance data to
62   determine whether the methods are acceptable and whether the data demonstrates SE. A new
63   device requiring premarket notification cannot be introduced into interstate commerce for

67           B.       510(k) Modernization
68   In April 2018, CDRH issued the Medical Device Safety Action Plan: Protecting Patients,
69   Promoting Public Health6 (herein referred to as the “Safety Action Plan”) to communicate
70   CDRH’s vision for modernizing measures to improve the safety of medical devices while
71   continuing to create more efficient pathways to bring critical devices to patients. The Safety
72   Action Plan describes the efforts underway to modernize the 510(k) program.
73
74   In November 2018, FDA announced transformative new steps to modernize FDA’s 510(k)
75   program to advance the review of the safety and effectiveness of medical devices. In connection
76   with this announcement, FDA also requested public feedback on these steps to continue to
77   modernize the framework for 510(k) review while promoting innovation and improving safety
78   by driving innovators toward reliance on more modern predicate devices or objective
79   performance criteria when they seek to bring new devices to the market and ultimately to
80   patients. FDA indicated that it is looking at ways to promote the use of more recent predicates
81   because it believes that newer devices should be compared to the benefits and risks of more
82   modern technology.
83
84   To advance these goals, FDA discussed several potential options for 510(k) modernization. The
85   statement discussed that the Agency considered making public on its website those cleared
86   devices that demonstrated substantial equivalence to older predicate devices. FDA also
87   considered focusing on predicates that were more than ten (10) years old as a starting point, so
88   the public was made aware of those technologies. FDA’s goal in focusing on older predicates
89   was to encourage manufacturers to continually offer patients devices with the latest
90   improvements and advances. FDA issued a public notice on January 22, 20197 on FDA’s website
91   that requested public comment on this proposal.
92
93   FDA reviewed all comments submitted to the docket and acknowledges that the initial proposal
94   of focusing only on older predicates may not optimally promote safer and more effective
95   devices. For example, if selecting a predicate for an implant, older devices may potentially have
96   long-term safety and effectiveness data that establishes a history of safe and effective use.
97   Conversely, when selecting a predicate for a device that includes software, a more recently

101   After considering the docket comments, FDA believes that it may be more appropriate to
102   modernize the 510(k) process with respect to the use of predicate devices by focusing on
103   utilizing best practices when selecting a predicate device rather than just their age. Therefore,
104   FDA is issuing this draft guidance to propose ways to encourage the use of best practices when
105   selecting a predicate device.
106
107   FDA developed this draft guidance to propose factors for consideration as best practices for
108   choosing a predicate device. These best practices include consideration of the characteristics of
109   predicate devices rather than focusing on the age of the predicate. FDA believes that this will
110   encourage the evolution of safer and more effective medical devices in the 510(k) program over
111   time. Additionally, FDA believes that identification of the characteristics of predicate devices
112   used to support a 510(k) submission in the accompanying 510(k) Summary may provide
113   additional transparency to the public for devices subject to 510(k) requirements.8

115   III. Scope
116   This guidance provides recommendations to industry and FDA staff about the best practices of
117   choosing a predicate device for a 510(k) submission. This guidance is intended to be used in
118   conjunction with the 510(k) Program Guidance.9 The recommendations provided in this
119   guidance are not intended to propose any changes to applicable statutory and regulatory
120   standards, such as how FDA evaluates substantial equivalence, or the applicable requirements,
121   including the requirement for valid scientific evidence. FDA developed this guidance to improve
122   the predictability, consistency, and transparency of the 510(k) premarket review process.
123
124   This guidance is also not intended to supplant existing device-specific guidance but may cover
125   broader areas not addressed in device-specific guidances.
126

161   V.         Best practices for selecting a predicate device
162   FDA identifies all devices cleared through the 510(k) process in the publicly available FDA
163   510(k) Premarket Notification Database.15 This online database is updated monthly by FDA.
164   Most submitters likely start with basic administrative information to identify valid predicate
165   device(s), including but not limited to the:
166      · Trade names of similar devices;
167      · Manufacturer(s) of similar devices;
168      · 510(k) numbers for similar devices; and
169      · Searching of classification information (e.g., product codes, classification regulation) for
170          similar devices.

172   Once legally marketed devices have been identified, FDA recommends reviewing the publicly-

178        A. Predicate devices cleared using well­established methods
179   FDA recommends selecting a valid predicate device that was cleared using well-established
180   methods.17 These methods include those from a currently FDA-recognized voluntary consensus
181   standard,18 an FDA guidance document,19 a qualified medical device development tool
182   (MDDT),20 or a widely available and accepted method published in the public domain or
183   scientific literature for the context of use, or found acceptable through the submitter’s own
184   previous premarket submission. FDA recommends prioritizing predicate devices with methods
185   developed within a consensus environment, and those subject to public comment or peer review.
186   FDA believes that when selecting a valid predicate device, submitters should consider how much
187   information is available regarding the test method(s) used in support of the predicate device’s
188   510(k) clearance and whether those methods continue to be appropriate for evaluating the subject
189   device. For example, voluntary consensus standards periodically undergo revisions and the
190   methods used to evaluate devices can change with both industry and FDA experience with a
191   device.
192

204        B. Predicate devices meet or exceed expected safety and
205           performance
206   FDA considers it a best practice to select a valid predicate device that continues to perform
207   safely and as intended by the manufacturer during use in its intended environment of use
208   whenever possible. FDA recommends selecting a valid predicate device after considering how
209   any reported medical device-related adverse events, malfunctions, or deaths may have a role in
210   the safety and effectiveness of the device. New information about a device’s safety and/or
211   effectiveness, including unanticipated adverse events, may become available once the device is
212   more widely distributed and used commercially. Also, subsequent changes made to the device,
213   including material changes, or its manufacturing process may lead to unanticipated effects that
214   cannot be comprehensively captured during premarket review.22 This new information may
215   include, but is not limited to, a newly recognized type of adverse event associated with a medical
216   device, an increase in the severity or frequency of a known adverse event, new product-product
217   interactions, or device malfunctions.
218
219   Once the submitter has identified a list of valid predicate devices, FDA recommends conducting
220   a search for any reported injury, deaths, or malfunctions using the following FDA databases:
221       · Manufacturer and User Facility Device Experience (MAUDE) Database;23
222       · Medical Device Reporting (MDR) Database;24 and
223       · MedSun Reports Database.25
224
225   FDA recommends searching each of the above databases for any reports of unexpected injury,
226   deaths, or malfunctions associated with the available valid predicate devices. For example, when
227   selecting a predicate device for an infusion pump, if the database search reveals a high frequency
228   of reports of battery failures related to the predicate device that resulted in serious injuries to the
229   operator, such events could suggest fundamental design issues with this valid predicate device

235        C. Predicate devices without unmitigated use­related or design­
236           related safety issues
237   FDA recommends selecting a valid predicate device that does not have unmitigated use-related
238   or design-related safety issues, including consideration of emerging signals or safety
239   communications.26 New information about a device’s safety and/or effectiveness can become
240   available once the device is more widely distributed and used. This new information could
241   represent a signal and may include information related to device malfunctions or patient injuries
242   potentially related to improper device use or design.
243
244   Consistent with the FDA guidance “Public Notification of Emerging Postmarket Medical Device
245   Signals (“Emerging Signals”),”27 an emerging signal is new information about a device that
246   supports a new causal association or a new aspect of a known association between a device and
247   an adverse event or set of adverse events and for which FDA has conducted an initial evaluation
248   and determined that the information has the potential to impact patient management decisions
249   and/or the known benefit-risk profile of the device. An emerging signal may be associated with
250   one product from one manufacturer, one type of product or similar products from multiple
251   manufacturers, or multiple different product types from multiple different manufacturers (e.g.,
252   materials issues). Information about emerging signals and safety communications is available on
253   the Medical Device Safety28 and CBER Safety & Availability (Biologics)29 websites. FDA
254   recommends reviewing any safety signals, emerging signals, or other safety information
255   available prior to selecting a valid predicate device to support the 510(k) submission.
256
257   Once the submitter has identified a list of valid predicate devices, FDA recommends conducting
258   a search of the Medical Device Safety and CBER Safety & Availability (Biologics) websites to
259   assess whether any of the valid predicate devices have an associated use-related or design-related
260   safety issue. For example, a signal was reported for duodenoscopes describing challenges in the
261   adequacy of reprocessing instructions that resulted in a potential for disease transmission. 30 This

268        D. Predicate devices without an associated design­related recall
269   FDA recommends selecting a valid predicate device that has not been subject to a design-related
270   recall.31 Recalls are typically voluntary actions taken by a manufacturer or may be requested by
271   FDA to correct or remove a violative product from the market.32 A violative product is one in
272   violation of the laws that FDA administers and against which FDA would initiate legal action.
273   Recalls can occur due to design defects, manufacturing defects, or labeling defects.
274
275   Design-related recalls can indicate a fundamental flaw with the design of the device as cleared
276   and commercially distributed. Design controls under 21 CFR 820.30 include a framework that
277   requires manufacturers subject to these requirements to establish and maintain procedures to
278   control the design of the device in order to ensure that specified design requirements are met.33,34
279   When a design-related recall has been conducted for a device, adequate design control
280   procedures, including but not limited to design input, output, verification, validation, and transfer
281   may not have been adequately implemented through the design process. In some instances, the
282   underlying root cause of the design related issues identified as part of a design-related recall may
283   not be available or a correction of these design-related issues may not be possible. Further,
284   although the methods and performance data provided in the 510(k) submission for the valid
285   predicate device subject to a subsequent design-related recall were sufficient to support a
286   substantial equivalence determination at that time of 510(k) clearance, utilization of such a valid
287   predicate device may not be ideal to use for future 510(k) submissions.
288

300   VI. Improving the Transparency of Predicate Devices
301   The 510(k) Summary is a document that provides an adequate summary of any information
302   respecting safety and effectiveness and must include all the elements identified in 21 CFR
303   807.92.35 A 510(k) Summary must be in sufficient detail to provide an understanding of the basis
304   for a determination of substantial equivalence (21 CFR 807.92(a)). In Appendix B of the 510(k)
305   Program Guidance, FDA describes the requirements of the content to be included in a 510(k)
306   Summary, in accordance with 21 CFR 807.92, and provides recommendations on the
307   information to be included in a 510(k) Summary to ensure compliance with 21 CFR 807.92 and
308   consistency in the level of information conveyed and captured in the 510(k) Summaries that are
309   available to the public on FDA’s website.
310
311   In an effort to improve the transparency and predictability of the 510(k) program and to ensure
312   that the 510(k) Summary reflects the information provided in a 510(k) submission to support a
313   substantial equivalence determination, FDA stated in the 510(k) Program Guidance that the
314   Agency intends to verify the accuracy and completeness of the information included in a 510(k)
315   Summary.
316
317   Although the 510(k) Summary is a document drafted by the submitter and is included in the
318   510(k), revisions to the 510(k) Summary may be necessary to accurately reflect FDA’s decision-
319   making process. As stated in the 510(k) Program Guidance, and consistent with 21 CFR
320   807.92(b)(1), 510(k) Summaries shall include a brief discussion of the nonclinical tests
321   submitted, referenced, or relied on in the premarket notification submission for a determination
322   of substantial equivalence.
323
324   FDA recommends that submitters include a narrative explaining their selection of the predicate
325   device(s) used in support of the 510(k) submission in their draft 510(k) Summary submitted with
326   their original 510(k).36 FDA recommends this narrative include a discussion of how the best
327   practices described in Section V of this guidance were used to select the predicate device(s)
328   proposed for use in the 510(k) submission. This recommendation is intended to promote
329   transparency to the public regarding the process of selecting a predicate device using these best
330   practices.
331

333   best practices discussed in Section V of this guidance, FDA recommends that the submitter
