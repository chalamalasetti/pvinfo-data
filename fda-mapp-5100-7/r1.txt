PURPOSE

This Manual of Policies and Procedures (MAPP) establishes good review practices
(GRPs) for the reviews of new drug applications (NDAs) and biologics license
applications (BLAs) in the Office of Clinical Pharmacology (OCP), Office of
Translational Sciences (OTS), Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER). In
general, the principles outlined herein may also be applicable to other submissions, as
appropriate.

This MAPP describes OCP’s integrated review procedure to evaluate NDAs and BLAs.

The GRPs and procedures described apply for all templates used to document the review.


BACKGROUND

Clinical pharmacology is a multidisciplinary science. OCP reviews of NDAs and BLAs
are expected to synthesize information from all relevant clinical pharmacology
knowledge areas, including drug disposition, pharmacology, biomarkers, quantitative
methods, drug safety, pharmacotherapy, and clinical trial methods to inform regulatory
decisions (e.g., approvability, labeling, and postmarketing requirements (PMRs) or

POLICY

The OCP review is based on the following guiding principles:

    •   Review recommendations and decisions will be clinically relevant, pragmatic, and
        take into consideration the specific therapeutic context (e.g., nature of disease,
        size of population, unmet medical need, available treatments). Reviewers will
        utilize a risk-based approach that incorporates the totality of evidence.

    •   The following four guiding review questions are important to inform the safe and
        effective use of all drugs in the general indicated population as well as in specific
        populations. These questions will be addressed during the clinical pharmacology
        review (refer to Attachment 1 for more details on the content expected in the
        response for each of these questions):

        1. To what extent does the available clinical pharmacology information provide
           evidence of effectiveness?

        2. Is the proposed dosage appropriate for the general patient population for
           which the indication is being sought?

        3. Is an alternative dosage and/or management strategy required for
           subpopulations based on intrinsic factors?

        4. Are there clinically relevant food-drug or drug-drug interactions, and what are
           the appropriate management strategies?

    •   Reviewers from different units in OCP with the expertise needed for the particular
        NDA or BLA submission will work collaboratively to review the submission and
        incorporate feedback.

    •   Reviewers will use the most current scientific and regulatory information
        available to ensure the highest quality reviews.

    •   Equal voice principles as outlined in MAPP 4151.8 Rev.2 will be employed for
        collaborative review and regulatory decision-making.

The following describe documentation principles:

    •   The OCP review team will use the review templates that are agreed upon by the
        CDER review team.

    •   Important findings and recommended regulatory actions will be clearly and
        succinctly communicated in reviews. The review will logically guide the reader
        through the thought process used to identify and address scientific, clinical, and
        regulatory questions and issues.

ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

All OCP Review Team Members:

    •   All members of the OCP review team will work collaboratively and are jointly
        responsible for the stated OCP positions and recommendations.

    •   Review team members must participate in the following three ways to be listed as
        signatories:

        o Contribute to the formulation and/or design of the review questions, approach
          to addressing the review questions, and/or the analysis and interpretation of
          the data.

        o Draft the review or critically revised it for important content.

        o Approve the final version of the review to be entered into the regulatory
          record.

    •   Review teams consist of staff from various units in OCP, depending on the type
        of knowledge needed for the review. These areas may include drug- and disease-
        related clinical pharmacology, pharmacometrics, genomics and other biomarkers,
        pediatrics, biologics, labeling, life cycle management, guidance and policy
        implementation and development, and regulatory project management. Not all
        knowledge areas will be needed for every review.

OCP review team roles are listed below.

OCP Regulatory Project Manager:

    •     OCP Regulatory Project Managers (OCP RPMs) are the regulatory experts for
          the application. They are responsible for notifying the relevant review
          divisions within OCP of new molecular entity NDA and original BLA
          submissions. OCP RPMs coordinate OCP reviewer assignments and
          communicate the assignments to the Office of New Drugs, Office of
          Regulatory Operations RPMs (OND/ORO RPMs). OCP RPMs will ensure that
          appropriate experts, including labeling, pediatrics, life cycle management, as
          well as experts in biologics programs, guidance, and policy are consulted as
          required. OCP RPMs are responsible for scheduling and managing the OCP
          scoping and briefing meetings, capturing minutes of the discussion, and
          archiving minutes in accordance with current Records Management practices.
          They provide reviewer tools, templates, and knowledge management, and they
          promote consistent communication throughout the review of the application.

OCP Primary Reviewers:

    •   OCP primary reviewers are responsible for assessing information in the
        submission, analyzing and interpreting data, and completing individual study
        report reviews or integrated summaries of individual study reviews. The primary
        reviewers are responsible for authoring their respective parts of the integrated
        review and jointly communicating the overall OCP recommendations derived
        from the review. They will participate in internal FDA meetings, external
        meetings with applicants, and advisory committee meetings.

OCP Lead Author of the Integrated Review:

    •   The OCP review team will identify the lead author based on the clinical
        pharmacology issues identified for the submission. The lead author is responsible
        for integrating information from primary reviews and leading the completion of
        the issue-based, interdisciplinary review with input from other OCP reviewers.
        The lead author is usually the lead reviewer representing the OCP team at the
        CDER multidisciplinary review team meetings. The lead author will also ensure
        accurate representation of all OCP knowledge areas in the OCP review team in
        reviews, presentations, and communications related to the application.

OCP Team Lead (TL) and/or Secondary Reviewer:

    •   The OCP TLs or secondary reviewers are responsible for conducting secondary
        review to provide scientific and regulatory advice and input to primary reviewers,
        having appropriate discussions of key review issues, ensuring that a high-quality
        review is completed within agreed-upon timelines, ensuring that the summary of
        OCP’s findings and regulatory recommendations are comprehensive and clear,
        and communicating with relevant parties within and outside OCP.

OCP Application TL:

    •   The OCP application TL is responsible for leading the review and overseeing the
        integrated review process to ensure overall review efficiency, quality, and
        consistency. The TLs and/or secondary reviewers work with each other to identify
        the application TL.

PROCEDURES

    1. Reviewers in OCP will adhere to established goal dates.

    2. Review teams will follow the most recent and up-to-date OCP Internal
       Quality Procedures.

    3. Regulatory project management staff or other members of the review
       team should schedule additional formal meetings as needed (e.g., pre-
       filing meetings to gain alignment on potential filing issues from OCP’s
       perspective, pre-mid-cycle/pre-briefing meetings to discuss clinical
       pharmacology issues, content, and deliverables before presenting these
       findings outside OCP).

    4. Informal meetings and communication among OCP review team members and
       other discipline reviewers (e.g., clinical, biostatistics, pharmacology and
       toxicology, chemistry, manufacturing) are encouraged. Because participation in
       meetings is issue-based, keeping the entire OCP review team informed of key
       findings is important throughout the review process. Thus, important review
       findings that might influence approval or labeling decisions should be discussed
       among the OCP review team to gain alignment prior to engaging with other
       members of the CDER review team. Relevant outcomes of interactions with other
       disciplines outside of OCP that may be relevant to the OCP review should be
       shared with the rest of the OCP review team.

    5. Reviewers in OCP will follow the review principles outlined in the
       POLICY section when documenting their reviews.

    6. Differences in opinions with respect to review and/or process will be
       resolved among the OCP review team members. For unresolved issues,

ATTACHMENT 1: OCP-SPECIFIC ISSUE-BASED REVIEW GENERAL
INFORMATION AND TEMPLATE

General Information

The purpose of the review template is to consistently ensure that critical information is
presented by OCP review teams in a prioritized, issue-driven, and logical manner. The
template provides standardization and consistency in the format and content of clinical
pharmacology reviews and ensures that critical presentations and analyses will not be
inadvertently omitted. The standardized structure also enables readers to readily locate
specific information. As such, headings and subheadings are named, numbered, and
ordered as stipulated in the template unless there is a compelling reason not to for a
specific application.

Information should generally not be repeated within the review. Reviewers may refer the
reader to relevant sections and use hypertext links where appropriate. Text, tables, and
figures may be used to summarize information, but redundancy should be avoided. The
approach that best ensures clarity and understanding should be used. Proper attribution
regarding data sources and analyses is expected. Reviews should be clear as to which
assertions, conclusions, and recommendations are those of the applicant and those of the
review team. Coherent and concise writing is expected for all draft and final reviews.

The template may be modified by OCP review teams if deemed necessary to ensure
clarity and cohesion for a given application; however, this scenario is expected to be a
rare occurrence. The content of this template can easily be incorporated into other CDER
integrated review team templates as appropriate.

Review Structure

The OCP integrated review consists of four sections. An overview of these four sections
with a description of their target audiences is provided in Table 1.

    •   Section 1. Executive Summary: Summarizes OCP’s position and final
        recommendations on key regulatory actions (e.g., approvability, dosing, labeling,
        need for PMR and/or PMC).

    •   Section 2. Summary of Clinical Pharmacology Assessment: Summarizes major
        clinical pharmacology information and analyses used to address issue-specific
        review questions.

    •   Section 3. Comprehensive Clinical Pharmacology Review: Contains more
        detailed information and explains the thought process for clinical pharmacology
        questions that are relevant to the application.

3.3.1 Do the available clinical pharmacology data contribute to the evidence of
effectiveness?

Clinical pharmacology information often provides substantial evidence of
effectiveness in situations that involve extrapolation of findings of an approved product
to a new population (e.g., adults to pediatric patients), a different dose, dosing regimen,
or dosage form. In other situations, clinical pharmacology data may be considered
confirmatory evidence of effectiveness or may otherwise contribute to the body of
data providing substantial evidence of effectiveness. For more information, see the
guidance for industry Providing Clinical Evidence of Effectiveness for Human Drug
and Biological Products (May 1998). 2 See also the draft guidances Demonstrating
Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness for Human Drug and Biological Products
(December 2019) and Demonstrating Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness Based on
One Adequate and Well-Controlled Clinical Investigation and Confirmatory Evidence
(September 2023). 3

In general, a recap of efficacy results is not warranted, except in situations in which
compelling clinical pharmacology information can be used to provide additional
evidence of effectiveness for an NDA or BLA. Situations where the available clinical
pharmacology data contribute to the evidence of effectiveness include, but may not be
limited to:

       •   Providing mechanistic evidence of a drug’s treatment effect that may be
           appropriate to be considered as confirmatory evidence to a single adequate and
           well-controlled trial (e.g., because of an effect on a relevant pharmacodynamic
           biomarker).

       •   Identifying subsets of patients with notably large treatment effects or favorable
           risk/benefit balance for a drug with significant toxicity or otherwise marginal
           average treatment effects.

       •   Contributing to evaluation of a biomarker as a fully validated surrogate or one
           reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit.

       •   Providing exposure-response information contributing to the understanding of
           drug effect in the intended population.

       •   Determining the individual contributions of components of a combination therapy
           (e.g., in the absence of a full factorial clinical trial).

3.3.2 Is the proposed dosage appropriate for the general patient population for
which the indication is being sought?

Provide an assessment of and final dosage recommendations for the general patient
population for which the indication is being sought. This section could also include the
details on the dose finding approach as well as the integrative approach taken
(including pharmacokinetic, pharmacodynamic, efficacy, and safety information) to
support the proposed dosing regimen. If necessary, this section should include revised
dosing recommendations to be incorporated into labeling or proposal for further
studies to resolve any unresolved issues related to dosing for the indication sought.

Information needed to address this question may include (but is not limited to):

    •   Dose-exposure-response analyses for drug activity, safety and/or efficacy, where
        available

    •   Therapeutic index of the drug if known

    •   Applicant’s rationale to support dosing or labeling claims

    •   Additional analyses if OCP’s conclusion is different from that of the applicant

3.3.3 Is an alternative dosing regimen and/or management strategy required for
subpopulations based on intrinsic factors?

Provide an assessment of and final recommendations on dosage and/or the
appropriateness of treatment in relevant patient subsets based on various intrinsic
factors. Intrinsic factors include age, sex, race/ethnicity, genetics, body weight, organ
impairment, etc.

If necessary, this section should include revised dosage recommendations to be
incorporated into labeling or proposals for further studies to resolve any unresolved
issues related to dosage in specific populations. The basis for alternative dosage
recommendations in these populations should be explicitly described (e.g., average
exposure matching, interpolation or extrapolation based on exposure-response

3.3.4 Are there clinically relevant food-drug or drug-drug interactions, and what
is the appropriate management strategy?

Provide assessment of and final recommendations on dosage and/or therapeutic
management strategies needed to address important food-drug (including beverage-
drug) or drug-drug interactions. If necessary, this section includes revised management
strategies to be incorporated into the labeling or proposals for further studies to resolve
such clinically relevant interactions. The basis for alternative dosages under these
conditions is explicitly described (e.g., average exposure matching, interpolation or
extrapolation based on exposure/response analyses).

This section provides information on:

    •   The influence of food/diet, alcohol use, herbal supplements, and concomitant
        drugs on the exposure (pharmacokinetics of parent and/or relevant metabolites)
        and/or pharmacodynamics of the new drug.

    •   The new drug’s effect on the exposure and/or pharmacodynamics of concomitant
        drugs.

    •   Whether a dosage adjustment is warranted and the nature of the data that supports
        those conclusions.

4 Appendixes

This section is organized to include key individual study report reviews or integrated
summaries of individual study reviews and analyses on various clinical pharmacology
areas that inform major decisions and recommendations. Subsections should be divided
into informative topical subheaders.

Reviewers should take into account whether pivotal clinical pharmacology studies are fit
for their intended purposes based on study design, data quality, and the appropriateness
of the data analysis. Reviews may include independent OCP data analyses. This section,
as with other sections of the review, should not include non-essential information. The
review team should exercise judgment on the breadth and depth of information to be
included in this section.
