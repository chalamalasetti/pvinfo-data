31   II.     BACKGROUND
32
33           A.       Overview of Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategies (REMS)
34
35   The Food and Drug Administration Amendments Act of 2007 (FDAAA) 6 created section 505-1
36   of the FD&C Act, which authorizes FDA to require a REMS if FDA determines that a REMS is
37   necessary to ensure that the benefits of the drug outweigh its risks. 7 A REMS is a required risk
38   management strategy that employs tools beyond prescribing information to ensure that the
39   benefits of a drug outweigh its risks. A REMS may require a Medication Guide (or patient
40   package insert) to provide risk information to patients 8 and/or a communication plan to
41   disseminate risk information to health care providers. 9 FDA may also require certain Elements
42   To Assure Safe Use (ETASU) when such elements are necessary to mitigate specific serious
43   risks associated with a drug. 10 ETASU may include, for example, requirements that health care
44   providers who prescribe the drug have particular training or experience, that patients using the
45   drug be monitored, or that the drug be dispensed to patients with evidence or other
46   documentation of safe-use conditions.
47
48   Certain REMS with ETASU may also include an implementation system through which the
49   applicant is able to monitor and evaluate implementation of the ETASU and work to improve
50   their implementation. 11 Finally, REMS generally must have a timetable for submission of
51   assessments of the strategy. 12
52
53   FDA can require a REMS before initial approval of a new drug application (NDA) or, should
54   FDA become aware of new safety information 13 about a drug and determine that a REMS is
55   necessary to ensure that the benefits of the drug outweigh its risks, after the drug has been
56   approved. 14

58            B.       Overview of Shared System REMS
59
60   A shared system REMS encompasses multiple prescription drug products and is developed and
61   implemented jointly by two or more applicants. 15 A REMS that includes more than one product
62   but only a single applicant is not considered a shared system REMS. A shared system REMS
63   can be a program shared by a drug that is the subject of an abbreviated new drug application
64   (ANDA) and the listed drug, as required in section 505-1(i)(1)(B) of the FD&C Act (described in
65   subsection I.D below). It can also involve multiple NDAs or ANDAs, submitted under section
66   505(b)(1), (b)(2), or (j) of the FD&C Act, or multiple biologics license applications (BLAs),
67   submitted under section 351(a) or (k) of the PHS Act, that form a shared system voluntarily
68   (described in subsection I.E below).
69
70   A shared system REMS uses a single REMS document, 16 supporting document, and REMS
71   materials, agreed to by all applicants and approved as part of each application. Applicants
72   generally share in the implementation and maintenance of any database and infrastructure (e.g.,
73   call center) used for the program, and are jointly responsible for carrying out all or most
74   components of the REMS assessments (see section IV below). There may be product-specific
75   materials (e.g., Medication Guide) that will not be shared across applicants.
76
77            C.       Benefits of a Shared System REMS
78
79   A shared system for REMS with ETASU may offer benefits to drug applicants and stakeholders
80   in the health care delivery system. The benefits may vary, but generally stem from opportunities
81   for increased efficiencies for stakeholders and applicants.
82
83   Impact on Drug Applicants. Use of a shared system REMS may benefit the applicants by
84   providing opportunities for sharing the cost of developing and implementing the program,
85   analyses of adverse events or other safety data, and periodic assessments to determine whether
86   the REMS is meeting its intended risk management goal(s). A shared system may also make
87   modifying a REMS that encompasses multiple products and adding other applicants to the
88   REMS more efficient.
89
90   Impact on Health Care Providers and Patients. A shared system REMS generally uses a shared
91   infrastructure for all of the products in the REMS. This generally provides a single portal for
92   REMS participants 17 to engage in and undertake the activities of the program. For example,

 97               D.     Required Use of a Single, Shared System REMS for ANDAs and RLD
 98
 99   As noted above, section 505-l(i)(l)(B) of the FD&C Act requires that a holder of an ANDA
100   approved under section 505(j) use a “single, shared system” with the reference listed drug (RLD)
101   for any ETASU, unless FDA waives this requirement and permits the ANDA to use a separate,
102   comparable aspect of the ETASU. 18
103
104               E.     Recommended Use of a Shared System REMS in Other Situations
105
106   The requirement under section 505-1(i)(1)(B) regarding a “single, shared system” only applies to
107   ANDAs. However, FDA recognizes that it may be in the interest of public health to have a
108   shared system REMS in other cases because it may increase efficiencies for applicants and
109   stakeholders.
110
111   For example, we recommend the applicants for a product submitted under section 505(b)(2) of
112   the FD&C Act or section 351(k) of the PHS Act work together with the applicant for the product
113   they are referencing to establish a shared system REMS. If an applicant of a product submitted
114   under section 505(b)(2) of the FD&C Act or section 351(k) of the PHS Act seeks to develop a
115   shared system REMS, we encourage the applicant to contact the applicant of the product they are
116   referencing and begin joint development of the shared system REMS as early as possible.
117
118   Additionally, FDA may request that applicants for a class of products with a similar risk profile
119   develop a shared system REMS. Applicants that are interested in developing a shared system
120   REMS for a class of products should seek the advice of FDA. 19

128            A.       Notification From FDA That a Shared System REMS Is Required or
129                     Recommended
130
131   FDA will initiate discussions about the formation of a shared system REMS under the following
132   circumstances:
133
134       •    When FDA has received an ANDA that references an RLD that has a REMS with
135            ETASU 20
136
137       •    When FDA has received an NDA or a BLA for a product that references an NDA or a
138            BLA that is subject to REMS with ETASU, and has determined that the proposed product
139            also would require the same ETASU 21
140
141       •    When FDA has determined for drugs and biologics in a class with similar, serious risks
142            that a shared system REMS may minimize the burden of having multiple unique REMS
143            programs 22
144
145   The Agency will, where appropriate, request that applicants of the pending applications submit
146   an authorization for disclosure that will allow the Agency to hold meetings between multiple
147   applicants. FDA will provide contact information for the product being referenced by the
148   pending applications to facilitate the development of a shared system REMS.
149
150            B.       Formation of an Industry Working Group
151
152   Applicants may consider forming a working group, commonly referred to as an industry working
153   group (IWG). Some applicants have used an IWG to facilitate negotiations and agreements
154   among the applicants to develop a shared system REMS on issues such as confidentiality,
155   governance, voting structure, cost-sharing, any potential changes to the drug distribution model,
156   and any other issues that may come up during the collaboration. Some applicants have used
157   third-party vendors to aid in implementation and management of the program. The applicants
158   must comply with the requirements of the REMS regardless of whether they use a third-party
159   vendor.
160
161   If an IWG is formed, the IWG applicants should identify a single point of contact (POC) to
162   represent the IWG in communication with FDA. This POC will facilitate communication
163   between the Agency and the IWG about the status of developing the shared system. FDA
164   generally requests that the Agency receive updates from the IWG POC on the status of
165   negotiations and on any barriers to developing the shared system REMS based on timelines that
166   have been communicated by the Agency early in the process. The updates are typically
167   submitted to each respective application as a REMS correspondence.

169              C.     FDA Role
170
171   FDA does not advise on the business arrangements being negotiated or arbitrate substantive
172   disputes about the terms of contracts. FDA will set forth expectations of the applicants for the
173   development of the shared system REMS, which may include suggested timeframes for various
174   milestones in the process of developing the shared system, and the process for submission of the
175   proposed REMS. FDA may facilitate collaborations between applicants when necessary to
176   achieve a shared system REMS. For example, FDA may host teleconferences to encourage
177   communications between applicants.
178
179   FDA will identify an Agency POC for a shared system REMS that is under development and will
180   provide this information to the IWG. If obstacles arise that impede the development of a shared
181   system REMS, the Agency POC should be notified.

184        IV.        SHARED SYSTEM REMS ASSESSMENTS
185
186   Information about the performance of REMS programs is important in determining whether the
187   program is meeting its intended risk mitigation goals. All approved REMS for NDA and BLA
188   products are required to include a timetable for submission of assessments of the REMS. 23 The
189   proposed REMS assessment plan for a shared system REMS, which specifies the nature and
190   content of the periodic assessments, is generally included in the REMS supporting document.
191
192   The assessments for shared system REMS apply collectively to all products that are subject to
193   the REMS. The Agency generally does not require submission of the shared REMS assessment
194   reports for ANDAs that are part of a shared system REMS with an RLD. However, FDA may
195   require that ANDA applicants submit REMS assessment reports when product-specific
196   utilization information or adverse event data is necessary to evaluate whether the program should
197   be modified to ensure the benefits of the drug outweigh the risks or to minimize the burden on
198   the health care delivery system of complying with the REMS. 24
199
200   Generally, FDA expects to assess any separate REMS for ANDAs at the same intervals and with
201   the same metrics as the RLD REMS to ensure that the separate REMS is meeting its goals.

237           C.      RLD Submission of a “Bifurcated REMS”
238
239   In some cases, the approval of a single, shared system REMS between an ANDA and its RLD
240   may coincide with tentative approval of the ANDA. 30 Tentative approval for an ANDA is a
241   notification to an applicant that an ANDA meets the requirements for approval but cannot be
242   approved because of unexpired patents or exclusivity. 31 A drug product that is granted tentative
243   approval is not an approved drug and will not be approved until FDA issues an approval letter
244   after any necessary additional review of the ANDA. In such cases, it would not be appropriate
245   for the single, shared system to be operational yet, because the generic product would not yet be
246   approved.
247
248   For the Agency to approve the single, shared system but postpone its operation until the approval
249   of the ANDA(s), FDA will request that the RLD holder submit a REMS modification that
250   proposes a single, two-part REMS, sometimes referred to as a "bifurcated REMS." Part A would
251   consist of the current REMS that applies only to the RLD and would be operational before the
252   first ANDA receives approval. Part B would be the single, shared system REMS that would be
253   operational once the first ANDA receives approval. In this circumstance, the approval letter for
254   the RLD holder’s REMS modification, as well as the REMS document itself, would state that the
255   single, shared system REMS (Part B) becomes operational automatically upon approval of the
256   first ANDA. If approval of the single, shared system REMS coincides with approval of the first
257   ANDA, rather than tentative approval, the single, shared system REMS (Part B) would
258   automatically be operational.
259
260           D.      Submission to FDA Using a Type V Drug Master File
261
262   To improve the efficiency of the submission and review process for shared system REMS, FDA
263   recommends that applicants use a Type V 32 Drug Master File (DMF) for their shared system
264   REMS submissions. Using a DMF allows applicants that are part of a shared system REMS to
265   submit one set of files on behalf of the application holders to the Agency and eliminates the need
266   for each applicant to submit identical REMS-related documents to its own application. The DMF
267   holder of the shared systems REMS DMF is jointly designated by the application holders. The
268   DMF holder will make submissions to the DMF on behalf of the application holders.
269
