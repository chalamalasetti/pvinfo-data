PURPOSE

This Manual of Policies and Procedures (MAPP) outlines the consistent application of the
United States Pharmacopeia (USP) policy entitled, Monograph Naming Policy for Salt
Drug Substances in Drug Products and Compounded Preparations 1 (henceforth referred
to as the USP Salt Policy) by the Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER) to
prescription drug products. 2 The USP Salt Policy became effective on May 1, 2013, and
CDER’s guidance for industry, Naming of Drug Products Containing Salt Drug
Substances (June 2015) (henceforth referred to as the Salt Guidance) describes CDER’s
implementation of the USP Salt Policy. This MAPP outlines the process for assessing the

BACKGROUND

As of May 1, 2013, CDER implemented the USP Salt Policy for prescription drug
products approved under section 505 of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act
(FD&C Act). This policy provides guidelines for the naming and labeling of drug
products containing an active ingredient that is a salt.

The USP Salt Policy stipulates that the name of the active moiety will be used for drug
product monograph titles, instead of the name of the salt. Additionally, the strength of the
drug product will be determined based on the active moiety. The policy also allows for
exceptions under specified circumstances (see the Policy section below). The policy’s
scope includes the following:

        A. The USP Salt Policy applies exclusively to the monograph titles of drug
           products and does not extend to the monograph titles of drug substances
           (active ingredients). The names of active ingredients, including salts, remain
           unaffected by this policy.

        B. The USP Salt Policy is applicable to all new drug product monographs that
           contain a salt as the active ingredient. Titles of drug product monographs
           published prior to May 1, 2013, should remain unchanged, unless a
           modification is necessary for safety reasons. 5 USP and CDER have agreed to
           collaborate on any retrospective name changes that may arise.

        C. The USP Salt Policy impacts the development of new drug products, as the
           title of a USP drug product monograph typically serves as the established
           name 6 for related drug products. A drug product that features a label or
           labeling that contains an established name that is not consistent with the
           applicable monograph title 7 may be at risk of being considered misbranded. 8

POLICY

        A.       USP Salt Policy 9

             1. When an active ingredient in a drug product is a salt, the drug product
                monograph title will contain the name of the active moiety (or neutral
                form), and not the name of the salt (e.g., “newdrug oral solution instead of
                “newdrug hydrochloride oral solution”).

             2. The strength will be expressed in terms of the active moiety (e.g.,
                “newdrug 100 mg,” rather than the salt strength equivalent (e.g., “newdrug
                hydrochloride 136.5 mg”).

             3. For drug products for which the name and strength are expressed in terms
                of the active moiety, the name and strength (or proportion, if CDER has
                determined that the proportion is more appropriate) of the active
                ingredient (salt) will also appear on the drug product label and labeling as
                an equivalency statement to indicate the amount of active moiety and
                equivalent amount of active ingredient (salt). 10, 11

             4. The USP Salt Policy provides for exceptions to the active moiety naming
                approach when the name of the salt conveys vital information from a
                clinical perspective. In these cases, the drug product monograph title will
                include the name of the salt, and the strength of the drug product will be
                expressed in terms of the salt form (active ingredient). In these cases, the
                label and labeling will also include an equivalency statement to indicate
                the amount of active ingredient (salt) and equivalent amount of active
                moiety. 12

        B.       CDER Implementation of the USP Salt Policy

             1. CDER has published the Salt Guidance to help applicants understand how
                products with active ingredients that are salts may be impacted by
                CDER’s implementation of the USP Salt Policy.

             2. CDER applies the USP Salt Policy only to prescription drug products
                under development whose approval is sought under section 505 of the
                Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act).

             3. CDER’s application of the USP Salt Policy, in conjunction with the
                application of other labeling policies and requirements, is intended to help
                avoid medication errors that could result from a mismatch of the drug
                product established name and strength (e.g., the name includes the salt, but
                the drug product strength is based on the active moiety). In addition, the
                Policy is intended to make it easier for practitioners to determine an
                equivalent dose when converting patients from one dosage form to another
                (e.g., converting a patient from an injection to a tablet), even when the
                drug products contain active ingredients in different salt forms, as the
                strengths and names will be determined based on the active moiety.

        C.       Application of Exceptions as Described in the Salt Guidance

             FDA, and not the applicant, will determine whether the USP Salt Policy
             exceptions apply; early discussions with the applicant will assist the FDA in
             making this determination. As CDER applies the USP Salt Policy, additional
             grounds for exceptions may be identified.

             1. When applying the USP Salt Policy exception that the name of the salt
                may be retained if it conveys vital information from a clinical perspective,
                CDER has determined that the name of the salt could be retained, if any of
                the following is true:

                 a) The active ingredient is a relatively simple salt and administration of
                    the entire salt is therapeutically important. Examples include lithium
                    carbonate; iron sulfate, and other oral and intravenous iron salts;
                    calcium gluconate and other calcium salts; potassium chloride;
                    magnesium sulfate; sodium or potassium phosphate; and sodium
                    citrate.

                 b) Scientific evidence demonstrates that the salt form affects the
                    absorption, distribution, metabolism, and/or excretion (ADME) of the
                    drug in a manner that influences drug product selection.

                 c) Clinically significant amounts of cations such as sodium, potassium,
                    magnesium, or calcium accompany the active moiety of a drug
                    product. Clinical significance may be related to the recommended
                    maximum daily amount of an electrolyte intake in special patient
                    populations. Examples include recommended daily intake of sodium in
                    patients with heart failure or recommended daily intake of potassium
                    in patients with chronic kidney disease.

                 d) There is significant evidence-based safety concern that the counter-ion
                    part of the salt could cause acid-base disturbances; hepatic, renal, or
                    other organ damage; or hypersensitivity reactions.

             2. In addition to the application of the exception specified in the USP Salt
                Policy, the name of the salt could be retained if any of the following safety
                or historical conditions are met:

                 a) The name of the salt is necessary to maintain consistency with other
                    dosage forms of the same active ingredient (salt). For example, if a
                    tablet dosage form that was approved prior to May 1, 2013, included
                    the salt in its drug product established name and the drug product’s
                    strength is based on the salt form, then the naming convention would
                    not change for a new capsule dosage form with the same active
                    ingredient (salt) that is approved after May 1, 2013.

                 b) There are relevant, documented safety reasons (e.g., documented
                      medication errors related to the name or strength) in a closely related
                      drug product.

        If a drug product is named according to the USP Salt Policy (i.e., the name and
        strength of the drug product are based on the active moiety), and there are post-
        approval reports of medication errors or safety concerns, CDER will consider
        whether a retrospective name change is appropriate. In keeping with the provision
        that a retrospective name change can be made for safety reasons after drug
        product approval, CDER may request such a change and will coordinate with
        USP to have any applicable USP monograph updated.

PROCEDURES

I.      Within the Office of Pharmaceutical Quality (OPQ) it is the responsibility of drug
        product Quality Assessors in the Office of Product Quality Assessment (OPQA I
        and II) to determine whether the USP Salt Policy applies to a new drug product. 13
        The following procedures should be followed:

        A. OPQA I and II Quality Assessors will work with the applicant at the earliest
           stage possible (e.g., investigational new drug (IND) phase 2) in the
           development of a new drug product that contains an active ingredient that is a
           salt. During the assessment of such a drug product, the Quality Assessors will:

             1. Determine whether the drug product contains an active ingredient that is a
                salt.

             2. Determine whether the drug product may qualify for an exception to the
                USP Salt Policy (see CDER Application of Exceptions as Described in the
                Salt Guidance in the Policy section above)

             3. Refer all applicants of new drug products that contain an active ingredient
                that is a salt to the Salt Guidance and the USP Salt Policy.

             4. Confer, as appropriate, with OPQ labeling experts (and
                CDER/OPQ/Office of Regulatory Practice of Pharmaceutical
                Quality/Compendial Operations and Standards Staff, as needed) and
                members of the extended review team (e.g., Office of New Drugs, Office
                of Surveillance and Epidemiology) in the following situations:

                 a. The OPQA I and II Quality Assessors (in consultation with their
                    management) recommend that a drug product qualifies for an
                    exception to the USP Salt Policy.
                 b. The applicant asserts that their drug product qualifies for an exception
                    to the USP Salt Policy.

        B. If CDER determines that an exception does not apply, inform the applicant
           that the USP Salt Policy applies to their drug product via standard
           communication methods appropriate to the application type:

II.     Confirmation of labeling compliance

        As part of the discipline-specific assessment, confirm that the drug product's
        labeling complies with all applicable statutory and regulatory requirements. The
        following confirmation checks apply:

             1. General Labeling Requirements

                 Confirm that the labeling correctly displays the established names of both
                 the drug product and active ingredients as required by FDA statute or
                 regulations. 14

                 a. Confirm that the active ingredient (salt) established name and the
                    amount appear on the container label, carton labeling, and other
                    labeling as required by statute and regulations even when the active
                    moiety is used in the drug product established name and strength. 15

             2. Active Ingredient Requirements

                 The use of an active moiety in the drug product established name and in
                 the expression of strength does not impact the need to meet other statutory
                 and regulatory requirements related to the active ingredient. For example,
                 an applicant for an abbreviated new drug application (ANDA) must
                 demonstrate that the generic drug product has the same active ingredient
                 as the reference listed drug (RLD). OPQA I and II Quality Assessors
                 confirm that other statutory and regulatory requirements related to the
                 active ingredient have been met.

        As part of the labeling assessment of the prescribing information, OPQA I and II

 DEFINITIONS

        1. Active ingredient - Any component that is intended to furnish pharmacological
           activity or other direct effect in the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or
           prevention of disease, or to affect the structure or any function of the body of man
           or other animals. The term includes those components that may undergo chemical
           change in the manufacture of the drug product and be present in the drug product
           in a modified form intended to furnish the specified activity or effect. 21

        2. Active moiety - The molecule or ion, excluding those appended portions of the
           molecule that cause the drug to be an ester, salt (including a salt with hydrogen or
           coordination bonds), or other noncovalent derivative (such as a complex, chelate,
           or clathrate) of the molecule, responsible for the physiological or pharmacological
           action of the drug substance. 22 Note: the USP Salt Policy definition for “active
           moiety” no longer includes “esters.” For additional details, please refer to USP
           General Chapter <1121>.

          A.      ATTACHMENT 1:

Monograph Naming Policy for Salt Drug Substances in Drug Products and
Compounded Preparations 23

The titles of USP monographs for drug products and compounded preparations
formulated with a salt of an acid or base use the name of the active moiety, as defined
below. The strength of the product or preparation is also expressed in terms of the active
moiety.

An active moiety is the molecule or ion, excluding those appended portions of the
molecule that cause the drug to be a salt (including a salt with hydrogen or coordination
bonds), or other noncovalent derivative (such as a complex, chelate, or clathrate) of the
molecule. The active moiety is responsible for the physiological or pharmacological
action of the drug substance, without regard to the actual charged state of the molecule in
vivo. For example, the active moiety of a hydrochloride salt of a base is the free base and
not the protonated form of the base. The active moiety of a metal salt of an acid is the
free acid.

This Policy is followed by USP in naming drug products and compounded preparations
that are newly recognized in the USP. Revising existing monographs to conform to this
Policy is not intended, except where the USP Council of Experts determines that, for
reasons such as safety, a nomenclature change is warranted.
