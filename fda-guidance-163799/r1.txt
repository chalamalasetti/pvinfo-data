16   I.       INTRODUCTION
17
18   Aluminum toxicity from prolonged use of parenteral nutrition (PN) represents a major safety
19   concern, particularly in patients with renal impairment or pediatric patients with immature renal
20   function. This guidance is intended to assist sponsors and applicants of small volume parenteral
21   (SVP) drug products 2 and pharmacy bulk packages (PBPs) 3 in meeting the requirements in 21
22   CFR 201.323 to control aluminum contamination in PN drug products. Although 21 CFR
23   201.323 uses the term “total parenteral nutrition” (TPN), meaning PN providing 100 percent of a
24   patient’s nutritional needs, the same PN products are used whether providing all of a patient’s
25   nutritional needs or only a portion of their nutritional needs. This guidance clarifies the key
26   factors in calculating the aluminum content to ensure that the total aluminum exposure (TAE)
27   from PN does not exceed an acceptable threshold. 4 It also provides FDA’s recommendations
28   regarding the aluminum concentration limits (ACLs) for SVPs. The recommendations for TAE
29   and ACL in this guidance apply to SVPs that are the subject of new drug applications (NDAs),
30   abbreviated new drug applications (ANDAs), and supplements to those applications.

31
32   Aluminum content of large volume parenteral (LVP) drug products 5 must not exceed 25
33   micrograms per liter (mcg/L). 6 However, the limits for the aluminum content of SVPs are not
34   specified by statute or regulation. Further, the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) has
35   not established a permitted daily exposure (PDE) for aluminum. 7
36
37   Due to the potential health risks from long-term exposure to aluminum, as explained in the
38   Background section below, FDA aims to have sponsors and applicants limit the individual
39   aluminum exposure (IAE) in each drug product to ensure that the TAE from all components of
40   PN in an admixture (LVPs plus SVPs) does not exceed 4 to 5 micrograms/kilogram/day
41   (mcg/kg/day) of aluminum, a level above which toxicity may occur (as described in 21 CFR
42   201.323(e)). To achieve these aims, FDA recommends sponsors and applicants develop
43   mitigation and control strategies to reduce aluminum contamination in their drug products
44   through formulation design optimization, raw material and component control, manufacturing
45   equipment and process optimization, and selection of appropriate container and closure
46   system(s).
47
48   This guidance does not alter concentration limits for aluminum content in LVPs because those
49   are addressed in 21 CFR 201.323. However, LVPs and SVPs are often used together in PN;
50   therefore, this guidance considers the aluminum content in LVPs when calculating the
51   recommended ACL in an SVP.
52
53   Additionally, this guidance is intended to assist sponsors and applicants in determining the
54   appropriate placement of information on aluminum toxicity in SVP and LVP Prescribing
55   Information and container and carton labeling. 8 In particular, this guidance aims to ensure that
56   this information in labeling is clear to health care practitioners and guides the safe and effective
57   use of the drug product.

66   II.     BACKGROUND
67
68   Parenteral drug products are those intended for injection through the skin or other external
69   boundary tissue, rather than through the alimentary canal, so that the drug products’ active
70   substances are administered directly into a blood vessel, organ, tissue, or lesion. PN is a source
71   of calories, protein, electrolytes and essential fatty acids in patients for whom oral or enteral
72   nutrition is not possible, is insufficient, or is contraindicated. SVPs may be components of PN
73   admixtures.
74
75   Aluminum, one of the most abundant metallic elements on earth, occurs naturally in several
76   minerals, ores, oxides, and silicates. Humans are exposed to aluminum through drinking water,
77   foods, and drugs. Aluminum’s oral bioavailability is poor, so healthy individuals typically face
78   little risk of toxicity. The gastrointestinal tract allows less than one percent of ingested aluminum
79   to be absorbed into the bloodstream, and renal excretion removes 99 percent of that aluminum.
80   However, in the settings of chronic kidney failure 9 or prolonged PN treatment in neonates, 10,11
81   aluminum toxicity has manifested as osteomalacia and reduced bone mineralization, neurological
82   dysfunction including dialysis encephalopathy, microcytic hypochromic anemia, and cholestasis.
83
84   PN ingredients may be contaminated with aluminum from raw materials as well as from
85   byproducts generated during the manufacturing and packaging process, where aluminum may
86   leach from the manufacturing equipment and/or container closure components (e.g., glass vials,
87   stoppers) during autoclave terminal sterilization and shelf-life storage. Therefore, PN can be a
88   source of aluminum exposure. Patients with underlying renal impairment who receive prolonged
89   courses of PN are at greatest risk of exposure to toxic levels of aluminum from PN. Preterm
90   neonates and infants, 12 who have immature kidneys that are incapable of excreting aluminum
91   efficiently and who may require weeks of PN before transitioning to oral nutrition, are at
92   particularly high risk.

 93
 94   In one controlled trial,10 preterm neonates were randomized to receive PN with standard
 95   aluminum content (mean of 45 mcg/kg/day) or aluminum-depleted PN (mean of 4 to 5
 96   mcg/kg/day). Patients treated with standard PN for more than 10 days had lower scores on
 97   developmental assessments at 18 months compared with the aluminum-depleted PN group. A
 98   follow-up study that evaluated a subset of survivors at age 13 to 15 years showed lower bone
 99   mineral content in the lower lumbar spine and hips in the group treated with standard PN.11
100   Accordingly, FDA recommends that the TAE from PN should not exceed 4 to 5 mcg/kg/day to
101   protect the safety of all patients.
102
103   Multiple sources of LVPs and SVPs comprise PN, and each drug product contributes to the total
104   aluminum content of PN. Sponsors and applicants should consider the recommended aluminum
105   limit in an individual SVP because the aluminum limit determines the drug product’s
106   contribution to the total daily aluminum exposure from PN. The individual SVP aluminum limit
107   is needed to support the calculations for determining whether addition of the SVP will cause the
108   TAE to exceed 5 mcg/kg/day (see Figure 1).

117   III.    STEPS TO DERIVE THE RECOMMENDED ACL IN THE SVP
118
119   There are two major steps in deriving the ACL in an SVP. First, the sponsor or applicant selects
120   the IAE expressed as mcg/kg/day of the individual SVP (see section IV.A., Calculation of
121   IAESVP Based on the Known or Labeled Aluminum Concentration for an Approved SVP); then
122   the sponsor or applicant can use the IAE to calculate the ACL for each specific SVP (see section
123   IV.B., Calculation of the ACL Based on a Selected IAESVP for SVPs Under Development).

125          A.     Selection of the IAE of Individual SVP
126
127   The first step in calculating the ACL for the specific SVP is the selection of the IAE. The IAE
128   should be selected to ensure that the addition of the SVP to a PN admixture containing multiple
129   SVPs and LVPs will not cause the TAE to exceed 5 mcg/kg/day. The number of SVP additives
130   will vary for individual patients based on their nutritional needs. A practical approach for
131   ensuring that the addition of SVPs to PN admixtures does not result in TAE values greater than 5
132   mcg/kg/day is to select a single (constant) IAE. Selection and application of the IAE should
133   occur during the early developmental stages of SVPs.
134
135   Figure 2 provides a conceptual overview of the aluminum exposure contributed by the multiple
136   LVPs and SVPs that comprise PN as a basis for the Agency’s recommended approach for
137   selecting an IAE for SVPs (described after Figure 2).

152   For this approach, the recommended IAE for an individual SVP is 0.6 mcg/kg/day or lower.
153   Selection of 0.6 mcg/kg/day as the constant IAESVP allows for the addition of up to five SVPs
154   with reasonable assurance that the TAE will not exceed 5 mcg/kg/day, based on the expectation
155   that the total aluminum exposure from LVPs (IAELVPtotal) will not exceed 2 mcg/kg/day
156   (aluminum is controlled at 25 mcg/L in LVPs, per 21 CFR 201.323). Further, if use of more than
157   five SVPs in a PN admixture is anticipated, then the selection of an IAESVP should be
158   substantially lower than 0.6 mcg/kg/day to ensure that the TAE will not exceed 5 mcg/kg/day.

164                   (mcg/L).

166                   b. The aluminum concentration in each LVP component used in a PN admixture
167                      must not exceed 25 mcg/L. 13 Therefore, this guidance assumes a maximum
168                      aluminum concentration of 25 mcg/L (or 0.025 mcg/mL) to determine each
169                      IAELVPtotal.
170
171                      Example: For a 3 kg infant receiving a daily dose volume of 80 mL/kg/day of
172                      LVP1+2+n, the maximum aluminum contribution from LVPs (X mcg/kg/day or
173                      IAELVPtotal) would be 2 mcg/kg/day (i.e., 0.025 mcg/mL times 80 mL/kg/day). The
174                      infant would be exposed to a maximum of 6 mcg/day (i.e., IAELVPtotal times 3 kg)
175                      of aluminum from the LVP.
176
177              2.      TAE from SVP (Y mcg/kg/day or IAESVPtotal)
178
179                   a. TAE from SVPs can be calculated by subtracting the IAELVPtotal aluminum
180                      contribution from the TAE for the total amount of PN (e.g., 5 mcg/kg/day) or Y
181                      mcg/kg/day equals 5 mcg/kg/day minus X mcg/kg/day.
182
183                      Example: If TAE from the LVP (IAELVPtotal) is X equals 2 mcg/kg/day, given that
184                      the TAE for the total amount of PN is 5 mcg/kg/day, Y should be no more than 3
185                      mcg/kg/day (IAESVPtotal).
186
187                   b. SVPs can be used alone or in combination with other SVPs as additives (i.e.,
188                      electrolytes, trace elements, vitamins, amino acids), which will all contribute
189                      toward the IAESVPtotal.
190
191                      As described above in section III.A., the IAE from an individual SVP (IAESVP)
192                      should be selected as 0.6 mcg/kg/day or lower. This approach takes into
193                      consideration the number of SVPs intended to be used in the PN admixture and
194                      the known IAESVP of each individual SVP. A typical PN admixture can include
195                      approximately four to six SVP additives, but this can vary depending on the
196                      specific SVP indication and an individual’s nutritional needs.
197
198                      Selection of 0.6 mcg/kg/day as the default IAESVP allows for the addition of up to
199                      five total SVPs in PN admixtures with reasonable assurance that the TAE will not
200                      exceed 5 mcg/kg/day. Selection of an IAESVP less than 0.6 mcg/kg/day may
201                      enable the use of more than five SVPs in PN admixtures, with a similar assurance
202                      that the TAE will not exceed 5 mcg/kg/day.

327   V.         MANUFACTURING CONSIDERATIONS FOR THE CONTROL OF
328              ALUMINUM CONTAMINATION IN SVPS
329
330   Control of elemental impurities to ensure that the levels do not exceed the PDE is one part of the
331   overall control strategy for a drug product. ICH Q3D(R2) provides general recommendations for
332   risk assessment and control of elemental impurities. ICH Q3D(R2) does not provide
333   recommendations of the actual values of the established PDE for some elemental impurities
334   including aluminum for several reasons, including the differences in regulations and practices
335   among geographic regions. Applicants and manufacturers must use validated assay methods to
336   determine the aluminum content in PN. The assay methods must comply with current good
337   manufacturing practice requirements. 16 Sponsors and applicants should establish the appropriate
338   acceptance criterion of the aluminum content in an SVP based on the following two factors:
339
340          1) The historical experience of the manufacturing capability, such as pharmaceutical
341             development, batch records, and results from release and stability studies of the
342             registration batches
343
344          2) The dosing regimen for patients with renal impairment including preterm neonates
345
346   Sponsors and applicants should include information on these factors and how they inform the
347   acceptance criteria in the product specifications of SVP at release and at expiry. Changes to
348   product specifications generally must be submitted in a prior approval supplement (see section
349   VI below).
350
351   For each SVP intended to be added to the PN admixture, the aluminum exposure to patients with
352   renal impairment should not exceed the IAE. Therefore, the concentration of the aluminum
353   impurity of each SVP should be controlled at or below the recommended ACL (see the
354   calculation of ACL in section IV.B.). This information can be used to guide the establishment of
355   the acceptance criterion for aluminum content in the SVP specification. If the historically
356   observed maximum level of aluminum exceeds the calculated ACL using the methods shown in

      16
           21 CFR 201.323 (f).

357   this guidance, applicants and sponsors should develop mitigation and control strategies to reduce
358   the aluminum contamination in the drug product (e.g., formulation design optimization, raw
359   material and component controls, manufacturing process improvement, selection of appropriate
360   container and closure system).
361
362   Regarding ANDAs, applicants should provide adequate justification to demonstrate that the
363   proposed acceptance criterion is appropriate. Differences in the acceptance criterion of the
364   aluminum content in the drug product specifications between a proposed ANDA and its
365   reference listed drug product may be appropriate if adequately justified.
366
367   Manufacturers should work to minimize aluminum contamination in SVPs during the drug
368   product development and product life cycle. For example, minerals are commonly added into
369   USP Type I glass as modifiers and stabilizers to produce glass containers with desired physical
370   properties and durability. Aluminum and other elemental impurities could leach into the SVP
371   from the glass containers over time, especially for drug products with an extreme pH. Therefore,
372   the pH of the formulation should be considered when performing risk assessment to identify the
373   source and control of aluminum and other elemental impurities in SVPs.
374
375   As part of the risk mitigation, the control of aluminum should be considered in the proposed
376   quality target product profile (e.g., route of administration, patient population, drug product
377   formulation design, strength, primary packaging materials). As illustrated in the SVP ACL
378   calculation formula in section III.B. and examples in section IV.B., the ACL is proportional to
379   the active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) concentration for an individual SVP if the maximum
380   daily dose of the SVP and its IAE remains unchanged. Under such a circumstance, a higher
381   aluminum concentration resulting from a higher ACL will be anticipated when an applicant has
382   selected a higher API concentration during the SVP formulation design. FDA encourages the
383   applicant to discuss the aluminum control strategy with FDA’s review divisions when
384   developing SVPs intended to be a component of PN. 17 Finally, the applicant should also
385   implement an adequate control strategy for postapproval changes that could affect aluminum
386   content in the drug product during its life cycle.

559
560   Total aluminum exposure (TAE) (microgram/kilogram/day (mcg/kg/day)): The daily patient
561   exposure to aluminum, from all components used in parenteral nutrition (PN) (SVPs and LVPs)
562   therapy, not to exceed 5 mcg/kg/day (see Figure 1).
563
564   Individual aluminum exposure (IAE) (mcg/kg/day): The maximum daily patient exposure to
565   aluminum from an individual component of PN (SVPs and LVPs) therapy; the value not to
566   exceed is variable among individual drug products and is dependent on the component and
567   composition of the PN admixture prescribed or intended for clinical use.
568
569   Aluminum content (mcg): The amount of aluminum present in a single dose of the individual
570   drug product. It is derived from the aluminum concentration in the drug product.
571
572   Aluminum concentration (mcg/Liter (L)): The amount of aluminum per liter of the individual
573   drug product determined from batch analyses.
574
575   Aluminum concentration limit (ACL) (mcg/L): The highest aluminum concentration
576   established in each individual drug product that will ensure compliance with its individual IAE.
577   It is the basis for the establishment of the acceptance criteria for elemental impurity aluminum in
578   drug product specifications. The acceptance criteria should not exceed the recommended
579   aluminum concentration limit for each drug product.
580
581   Drug product concentration (milligram/milliliter (mg/mL)): The amount of the drug
582   expressed in milligram per milliliter of the individual drug product defined in an application. 1
583
584   Large volume parenteral (LVP): A terminally sterilized aqueous drug product packaged in a
585   single-dose container with a capacity of 100 milliliters or more, for use in PN in a human.
586
587   Maximum daily dosage (max. daily dosage) (mg or mL/kg/day): The prescribed maximum
588   daily dosage of the specific drug 2 expressed per kilogram of the patient body weight.
589
590   Specification for drug product: A specification is defined as a list of tests, references to
591   analytical procedures, and appropriate acceptance criteria, which are numerical limits, ranges, or
