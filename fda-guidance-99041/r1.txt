15   I.      INTRODUCTION
16
17   This guidance describes a process for updating labeling for abbreviated new drug applications
18   (ANDAs) in cases where FDA has withdrawn approval of the new drug application (NDA) 2 for
19   the ANDA’s reference listed drug (RLD) 3 for reasons other than safety or effectiveness. Where
20   approval of the NDA for the RLD has been withdrawn by FDA under these circumstances
21   (referred to in this guidance as a “withdrawn RLD”), and ANDAs are pending or generic drugs
22   continue to be marketed under one or more ANDAs that rely on the withdrawn RLD, the
23   labeling of those pending or marketed ANDA products may need to be updated to reflect
24   changes that would have been necessary had the NDA for the RLD not been withdrawn.
25
26   This guidance sets forth ways in which FDA may seek to facilitate the revision of labeling to
27   reflect updated information for ANDAs that rely on withdrawn RLDs. This guidance will be of
28   interest to the holders of pending or approved ANDAs that rely upon a withdrawn RLD, as well
29   as to applicants seeking to submit ANDAs relying on withdrawn RLDs. We anticipate that this
30   guidance will help facilitate labeling updates for approved ANDAs as well as the approval of
31   certain pending ANDAs where the NDA for the RLD has been withdrawn.
32

34   Instead, guidances describe the Agency’s current thinking on a topic and should be viewed only
35   as recommendations, unless specific regulatory or statutory requirements are cited. The use of

40   II.    BACKGROUND
41
42   A generic drug is required to have the same labeling as the RLD at the time of approval, except
43   for changes required because of differences approved under a suitability petition (see section
44   505(j)(2)(C) of the FD&C Act and 21 CFR 314.93) or because the generic drug and the RLD are
45   “produced or distributed by different manufacturers” (see section 505(j)(2)(A)(v) of the FD&C
46   Act). FDA regulations provide examples of permissible differences in labeling that may result
47   where a proposed generic drug and the RLD are “produced or distributed by different
48   manufacturers,” including the omission of an indication or other aspect of labeling protected by
49   patent or exclusivity and “labeling revisions made to comply with current FDA labeling
50   guidelines or other guidance.” 21 CFR 314.94(a)(8)(iv).
51
52   As a general matter, all holders of marketing applications for drug products (both NDAs and
53   ANDAs) have an ongoing obligation to ensure their product labeling is accurate, and not false or
54   misleading. When new information becomes available that causes the labeling to become
55   inaccurate, false or misleading, the application holder must take steps to update its labeling (see,
56   e.g., 21 CFR 201.56(a)(2)). Any drug is misbranded if its labeling is false or misleading, or does
57   not provide adequate directions for use and adequate warnings (sections 301(a) and (b), and
58   502(a), (f), and (j) of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act) (21 U.S.C. 331(a)
59   and (b), and 352(a), (f), and (j))).
60
61   Where the NDA for the RLD has not been withdrawn, RLD holders often propose changes to the
62   labeling by submitting them to the NDA. ANDA holders are expected to update their labeling
63   after FDA has approved relevant changes to the labeling for the corresponding NDA RLD. FDA
64   may withdraw approval of an ANDA if it finds that the labeling for the drug product that is the
65   subject of the ANDA is no longer consistent with that for the RLD (section 505(e) of the FD&C
66   Act and 21 CFR 314.150(b)(10)).
67
68   FDA believes ANDA applicants are familiar with the mechanisms for updating labeling for
69   pending and marketed ANDAs where the NDAs for the RLDs have not been withdrawn.
70   However, there has been confusion regarding the process for updating labeling for ANDAs
71   referencing RLDs where the NDAs have been withdrawn.
72
73   NDAs for RLDs may be withdrawn voluntarily, at the NDA holder’s request, for reasons other
74   than safety and effectiveness. Specifically, FDA will withdraw approval of an NDA at the
75   applicant’s request where the drug that is the subject of the application is no longer being
76   marketed, and if certain other conditions are satisfied, including the absence of any FDA finding
77   that the drug is unsafe or ineffective for its approved conditions of use (see, e.g., § 314.150(c)

 78   (21 CFR 314.150(c))). 4 As noted above, withdrawal of an NDA RLD under these circumstances
 79   is referred to in this guidance as a “withdrawn RLD.” 5
 80
 81   Where an RLD has been withdrawn, ANDA products that were approved in reliance on the
 82   withdrawn RLD may continue to be marketed, and new ANDAs must refer to the withdrawn
 83   RLD as the basis for ANDA submission (provided that there has been a determination that the
 84   NDA RLD was not withdrawn for reasons of safety or effectiveness) (see § 314.122(c) (21 CFR
 85   314.122(c))).
 86
 87   Under these circumstances, where approval of the NDA for an RLD has been withdrawn, the
 88   NDA holder can no longer update labeling for the withdrawn RLD. 6 Yet as a drug is used over
 89   time, the scientific community’s understanding of the drug may evolve based on data from
 90   various sources, including published literature and postmarketing data. Therefore, the labeling
 91   of ANDAs that rely on the withdrawn RLD might eventually become inaccurate and outdated,
 92   resulting in labeling that is false and/or misleading, for example. Likewise, new original
 93   ANDAs that rely on the withdrawn RLD might include proposed labeling based on the last
 94   approved RLD labeling that includes outdated information that is false and/or misleading.
 95
 96   If an NDA for a certain drug has been withdrawn, there may be other drugs that contain the same
 97   active ingredient (or an active ingredient in the same pharmacologic or therapeutic class) for
 98   which approval of the NDA has not been withdrawn. The labeling of those other drugs, as well
 99   as the labeling of any corresponding ANDAs, may have been updated to reflect any new
100   scientific information that is needed for the safe and effective use of the drug. This creates a
101   situation in which certain NDAs and ANDAs for a given active ingredient have up-to-date
102   labeling, while other ANDAs do not, simply because those other ANDAs rely on an RLD for
103   which approval of the NDA has been withdrawn. In such cases the labeling of pending ANDAs
104   or marketed ANDAs products may need to be updated to reflect changes that would have been
105   necessary had the NDA for the RLD not been withdrawn.

111   III.    SCOPE OF GUIDANCE
112
113   This guidance is limited to labeling updates for generic drugs that are the subject of new original,
114   pending ANDAs or approved ANDAs that rely on an RLD for which approval of the NDA has
115   been withdrawn for reasons other than safety or effectiveness.
116
117   Consistent with this guidance, FDA may seek to facilitate certain updates to the labeling for a
118   new original, pending ANDA or an already-approved ANDA that relies on a withdrawn RLD
119   when the previously approved labeling for the withdrawn RLD has become outdated and such
120   changes would have been necessary had the RLD not been withdrawn.
121
122   The process described in this guidance is intended to complement existing Agency authorities
123   and processes, including FDA’s authority under the Food and Drug Administration Amendments
124   Act of 2007 (FDAAA) to require certain safety labeling changes (SLCs) to the labeling of certain
125   prescription drug products. The relationship of this guidance to certain existing FDA authorities
126   and processes is further discussed in section IV.C below.
127

129   IV.     UPDATING LABELING OF ANDAs THAT RELY ON WITHDRAWN RLDs
130
131   ANDA holders are responsible for reviewing postmarketing data and published literature as
132   appropriate to satisfy applicable reporting requirements (e.g., 21 CFR 314.81, 314.98).
133   However, FDA (as well as ANDA holders and applicants) may be aware of data relevant to
134   labeling updates in a variety of situations. For example, relevant data may already be reflected in
135   the updated labeling of other drugs in the same pharmacologic or therapeutic class (e.g., class
136   labeling); in non-product specific literature; or in postmarketing information. The updates
137   contemplated by this guidance generally would not involve reliance on product-specific,
138   proprietary information about another drug. Any changes proposed under this guidance must be
139   consistent with the requirement that an ANDA include sufficient information to show that the
140   conditions of use have been previously approved for the RLD (section 505(j)(2)(A)(i)).

142           A.       Examples of Labeling Updates for ANDAs That Rely on Withdrawn RLDs
143
144   In general, the labeling changes contemplated under this guidance include changes necessary to
145   ensure that labeling adequately describes information essential for safe and effective use; that
146   labeling is accurate and meets current standards; and that labeling is not false or misleading
147   under section 502 of the FD&C Act. Revisions described by this guidance may include changes
148   based on data that have become available since the RLD was withdrawn, including published
149   literature and other data that emerge after products have entered the market.
150
151   More specifically, the labeling changes contemplated under this guidance may be needed:
152
153       •   To achieve consistency with the labeling of other products that have the same active
154           ingredient or an active ingredient in the same pharmacologic or therapeutic class, or with
155           the labeling of other products approved for the same indication, where appropriate;
156       •   To correct outdated information related to a previously approved indication; and/or
157       •   To achieve consistency with applicable regulations and current FDA labeling guidelines
158           or other guidance (as already contemplated under § 314.94(a)(8)(iv)).
159
160   Examples of the updates contemplated by this guidance might include the following (this list is
161   not intended to be exhaustive): 8
162
163       •   Updating the indication statement in the INDICATIONS AND USAGE section to reflect
164           current disease terminology (e.g., changing “Juvenile Rheumatoid Arthritis” to “Juvenile
165           Idiopathic Arthritis”) or to modify the description of an outdated restriction on the use of
166           the drug in specific situations.
167
168       •   For systemic antibacterial drug products, including the required statement about
169           antimicrobial resistance in the INDICATIONS AND USAGE section (see 21 CFR
170           201.24(b)).
171
172       •   For parenteral products, including the following required statement or appropriate
173           modification in the DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION section: “Parenteral drug
174           products should be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to
175           administration, whenever solution and container permit” (see 21 CFR 201.57(c)(3)(iv)).
176
177       •   Removing a risk from the CONTRAINDICATIONS section if the benefit outweighs the
178           risk of use in the situation or subpopulation (e.g., the risk is theoretical) (see 21 CFR
179           201.57(c)(5)).

226            B.       Process for Labeling Updates
227
228   ANDA holders have the ability to submit any of the labeling updates contemplated by this
229   guidance, i.e. those changes needed to ensure that labeling is accurate and meets current
230   standards for an ANDA whose RLD is withdrawn, through the submission of a prior approval
231   supplement (PAS). If FDA determines that a change proposed in this manner is appropriate and
232   approves the supplement, the Agency may request that other ANDA holders and any ANDA
233   applicants relying on the same withdrawn RLD make the same updates, where appropriate. This
234   latter step is intended to ensure that labeling remains uniform across generic drugs that rely on
235   the same RLD.
236
237   FDA may also request a changes being effected (CBE-0) supplement from ANDA holders (see
238   21 CFR 314.70(c)(6)(iii)(E)) if FDA becomes aware of labeling updates that are needed to
239   ensure that labeling is accurate and meets current standards for an ANDA or ANDAs whose
240   RLD is withdrawn. 11 Under these circumstances, the Agency would also solicit an amendment
241   reflecting the needed updates from any ANDA applicants seeking initial approval that rely on the
242   same RLD. Such an amendment would be necessary prior to ANDA approval.
243

245
246   The process described in this guidance is intended to complement existing Agency authorities
247   and processes. Two of these are described below.

249            1.       Safety Labeling Changes Under Section 505(o)(4) of the FD&C Act
250

252   authority to require certain safety labeling changes (SLCs) to the labeling of prescription drug
253   products marketed under NDAs and under ANDAs whose RLD is not currently marketed (i.e.,
254   ANDAs with a “withdrawn RLD” or “discontinued RLD” as described in the Background
255   section of this guidance). If FDA becomes aware of “new safety information” 12 that it believes

267   This guidance addresses certain situations in which needed updates to the labeling of ANDAs
268   that rely on withdrawn RLDs may not be captured by the FDAAA SLC process. For example:

269          •   The FDAAA SLC authorities apply only to approved NDAs and ANDAs. When new
270              original ANDAs are submitted that rely on a withdrawn RLD, the FDAAA SLC
271              authorities do not extend to the draft labeling submitted as part of those pending
272              applications. Under this guidance, FDA will consider the FDAAA SLC notification that
273              is sent to the holders of approved applications to be “FDA labeling guidelines or other
274              guidance” within the meaning of § 314.94(a)(8)(iv). Applicants with pending ANDAs
275              will be able to submit amendments to their applications with labeling that reflects the
276              safety labeling change made by the holders of approved ANDAs relying on the same
277              RLD, and this will be considered a change made to comply with “current FDA labeling
278              guidelines or other guidance” within the meaning of the regulation.
279
280          •   Before FDAAA came into effect on March 25, 2008, certain labeling updates had been
281              accomplished through other regulatory procedures, including class labeling changes.
282              Those updates were typically carried out in the first instance by updates to NDA RLD
283              labeling that would then have been reflected in the labeling of ANDAs relying on that
284              RLD. Where an RLD affected by the update was withdrawn, however, the corresponding
285              ANDAs may have never adopted these updates. Where appropriate under the process
286              described in this guidance (for example where labeling changes are not implemented
287              through the FDAAA SLC process), FDA will ask ANDA holders that rely on withdrawn
288              RLDs to make these updates to their labeling consistent with this guidance.
