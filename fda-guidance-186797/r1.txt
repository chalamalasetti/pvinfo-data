65   I.      Introduction
66   The Food and Drug Administration (FDA or Agency) is issuing this guidance to provide labeling
67   recommendations for hernia mesh devices. These labeling recommendations are being issued
68   because suboptimal device selection associated with these devices has resulted in serious adverse
69   events, including deaths. 1 The package labeling recommendations in this guidance, including the
70   example in Appendix A below, are intended to help promote the safe and effective use of hernia
71   mesh. When finalized, this guidance is intended to supplement the labeling recommendations in
72   section XI of “Guidance for the Preparation of a Premarket Notification Application for a
73   Surgical Mesh,” for hernia mesh devices.
74
75   In general, FDA’s guidance documents do not establish legally enforceable responsibilities.
76   Instead, guidances describe the Agency’s current thinking on a topic and should be viewed only
77   as recommendations, unless specific regulatory or statutory requirements are cited. The use of
78   the word should in Agency guidance means that something is suggested or recommended, but
79   not required.
80

81   II. Background
82   Hernia meshes represent a diverse group of medical devices featuring a wide variety of
83   compositions, form factors, shapes, sizes, materials, and mechanical properties. They can be used
84   in many different anatomic regions for different types of hernia repair. These factors can make it

 85   challenging, even for experienced healthcare providers, to choose the most appropriate mesh
 86   based on patient-specific considerations and surgical approach. Since January 1, 2019, FDA has
 87   received over 86,000 adverse event reports related to hernia mesh, of which the most common
 88   reported adverse events include adhesions, recurrence, pain, and infection. Based on FDA’s
 89   review of these adverse event reports as well as the published literature, FDA believes that
 90   concise, consistent, and easily understandable package labeling of hernia meshes that contains
 91   certain clinically relevant information may facilitate the safe and effective use of these devices. 2
 92   While the labeling of many hernia meshes currently on the market provides information on the
 93   composition, physical properties, mechanical properties, and intended uses, it can be difficult for
 94   healthcare providers to find the desired information quickly and easily. For example, some of the
 95   information may be provided in the more detailed labeling that is included inside the hernia mesh
 96   package (such as the package insert) rather than the package labeling. Standardized informative
 97   package labeling for hernia meshes may provide a more consistent format for disseminating
 98   certain clinically relevant information, making it easier for healthcare providers to find certain
 99   information needed to use the device safely and for the purposes for which it is intended.

112   IV. Labeling Recommendations
113   Hernia meshes are prescription devices and are exempt from having adequate directions for use 3
114   required under section 502(f)(1) of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act) (21
115   U.S.C. 352(f)(1)) as long as the conditions in 21 CFR 801.109 are met. 4 Specifically,
116   manufacturers of prescription medical devices, such as hernia meshes, are required as per 21
117   CFR 801.109 to provide labeling that “bears information for use, including indications, effects,
118   routes, methods, and frequency and duration of administration, and any relevant hazards,
119   contraindications, side effects, and precautions under which [healthcare providers] can use the
120   device safely and for the purpose for which it is intended . . . .” To help manufacturers develop
121   informative package labeling consistent with the requirement of 21 CFR 801.109 to provide
122   labeling that bears “information for use . . . under which [healthcare providers] can use the
123   device safely and for the purpose for which it is intended,” and to mitigate the safety issues for
124   hernia meshes, FDA is providing the following recommendations. We intend for the package
125   labeling recommendations below to supplement and enhance the information that is often already
126   included in labeling for these device types. The Agency will continue to monitor hernia mesh
127   adverse event reporting and update the labeling recommendations as warranted.
128
129

131   believe may facilitate the safe and effective use of the device:

163   FDA believes that the device description and instruction information identified above will help
164   mitigate the safety issues associated with hernia meshes and help manufacturers develop
165   informative package labeling consistent with the labeling requirements under 21 CFR 801.109.
166   Since these recommendations are based on known safety issues, FDA recommends that this
167   information be considered for inclusion as current product labeling is updated, and that labeling
168   included as part of future premarket submissions for hernia meshes incorporate the
169   recommendations. For currently marketed hernia meshes, manufacturers should evaluate their
170   labeling changes as recommended in FDA’s guidance, “Deciding When to Submit a 510(k) for a
171   Change to an Existing Device.”

172   Appendix A. Example Package Labeling
173   The following is an example package labeling for a hernia mesh containing the recommended
174   device description and instruction information as described in Section IV.A.
175
176   As stated in Section IV.A., FDA recommends that the package labeling be presented as shown in
177   the following example for ease of comprehension. 5
178
           Device Facts                                                                               Rx Only
           Mesh Components
           Base Material: Polypropylene (synthetic, non-absorbable)
           Barrier Material: Expanded Polytetrafluoroethylene
           Additional Materials: Polytetrafluoroethylene

           Mesh Physical Properties
           Mesh Size: 20cm x 30cm
           Mesh Pore Size: 0.100mm2
           Mesh Thickness: 1.1-1.2mm
           Mesh Density: 90grams/m2
           Fiber: Monofilament
           Mesh Mechanical Properties
           Tear Strength: 55.0 lbf
           Ball Burst Strength: 90.0 lbf
           Anatomic Site of Application for Hernia Repair: Intraperitoneal Onlay Mesh (IPOM)

           Method of Fixation: Tacks (permanent or absorbable) with or without Transfixing sutures

           Other Information:
